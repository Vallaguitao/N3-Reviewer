1. ～に対して

Set two sides beside each other and show the difference

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A に対して B means “in contrast to A, B…” in this lesson. Identify the two sides and the feature being compared: Osaka and Tokyo on weather; women and men on after-work habits; two brothers on where they like to play.

The first side supplies a comparison point. The second gives a clearly different fact or tendency on a relevant dimension. The difference need not be an exact logical opposite.

This often sounds more deliberate and explanatory than a simple “but.” It can compare people, places, groups, products, or different times. The important point is the contrast being drawn, not a rule that the sentence must contain two different people.

Think of it this wayName side A, side B, and the comparison point. If you cannot say what differs between them, recheck the connection.

How to form it

Noun phrase + に対して
長男に対して、次男は… · in contrast to the older son, the younger son… A describing clause can be part of that noun phrase: 外遊びが好きな長男.
Verb clause + のに対して
姉はよく話すのに対して、弟は静かだ · the older sister talks a lot, whereas the younger brother is quiet. の turns the whole first statement into the comparison point.
い-adjective + のに対して
高いのに対して／高かったのに対して · the plain adjective form stays intact before の.
な-adjective: な + のに対して
静かなのに対して · change the present affirmative だ to な before の. The formal variant is 静かであるのに対して.
Noun predicate: な + のに対して
姉は学生なのに対して、兄は会社員だ · the sister is a student, whereas the brother is an employee. Compare a direct noun target, 姉に対して: “in contrast to the sister.”
Past and negative clauses
大雨だったのに対して／行かないのに対して · a past noun predicate keeps だった; a negative verb keeps ない. Do not change every first clause to な.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

きのうはではだったの、はいいだった。

Yesterday it rained heavily in Osaka, whereas Tokyo had fine weather.

Clue Osaka and Tokyo are compared on the same day’s weather.

How to read this example

大雨だった is a past noun predicate. Add のに対して to that whole clause: ［大阪では大雨だった］のに対して［東京はいい天気だった］. Past だった stays unchanged.

Example 02

うちのはがよくにの、はまっすぐに。

In our section, the women often go out for drinks, whereas all the men go straight home.

Clue The groups differ in what they do after work.

How to read this example

飲みに行く means “go out for drinks.” The dictionary verb 行く is followed by のに対して. 男性は皆まっすぐ家に帰る supplies the contrast: all the men go straight home.

Example 03

がな、はのでことがだ。

In contrast to the older son, who likes playing outside, the younger son likes playing indoors.

Clue The first comparison point ends in the noun 長男.

How to read this example

Bracket ［外遊びが好きな長男］に対して. 好きな describes 長男, so no additional の is required before に対して. The younger son’s preference for playing indoors completes the comparison.

Do not confuse it with…

Contrast ↔ “toward” with the same form
弟に対して厳しい means “strict toward the younger brother.” There is a target of an attitude, not necessarily a contrasting second person. Decide from the sentence, not from に対して alone.
に対して ↔ 反面
反面 presents an opposing aspect of a topic. に対して explicitly places two sides in comparison. “Two brothers’ preferences” fits the first approach; “the appeal and stress of city life” fits the second.
に対して ↔ 一方で
一方で can also contrast groups, so the patterns may overlap. It additionally presents parallel roles or activities without requiring a sharp difference.
のに対して ↔ のに
安いのに売れない means “It is cheap, yet it does not sell”: an unexpected result. In 安いのに対して, read の + に対して as a comparison construction. Do not stop reading at のに.
Contrast ↔ variation · Lesson 2
人によって好みが違う says preferences vary by person. に対して can spell out two sides: one person likes A, whereas another likes B.

JLPT approach

  1. Check whether A is a noun or a whole statement. A noun phrase can take に対して directly; a clause needs のに対して. This is a frequent form clue.
  2. Follow the comparison already introduced. “Previous apartment” naturally invites “current apartment.” A workplace could be compared with an apartment in a different context, but do not invent that missing context just to rescue an option.
  3. Compare the same relevant feature. Strong sales versus low popularity is a clear contrast. A launch date alone does not directly tell you how well the other product sells.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

兄は静かなのに対して、弟はよく話します。

あにはしずかなのにたいして、おとうとはよくはなします。

My older brother is quiet, whereas my younger brother talks a lot.

静か is a な-adjective: 静かだ → 静かな + のに対して.

2. ～反面

Show the opposing aspect of the same topic

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A 反面 B means “A is true, but there is also the other side: B.” Look at one topic from two opposing angles. City life is interesting and stressful; solo travel is carefree but inconvenient; leaving a job can bring free time and a loss of tension.

An advantage paired with a disadvantage is a useful starting point. However, the essential relationship is opposing aspects, not a compulsory “good first, bad second” formula. The order may be reversed, and a contrast can be descriptive rather than a moral judgment.

The two sides belong to a shared topic or situation. They do not have to happen at the same instant: one town may have a busy summer and a quiet winter.

Think of it this wayFinish this thought: “That is one side of this situation; the contrasting side is…” Both parts should help describe the same overall topic.

How to form it

Verb in a plain form + 反面
増える反面／増えた反面／増えている反面 · dictionary, past, and ongoing forms can describe the relevant side. Choose the tense from the situation.
い-adjective + 反面
面白い反面／忙しかった反面 · attach directly to the plain adjective. Do not insert の.
な-adjective: な／である + 反面
気楽な反面／便利である反面 · the textbook gives both the ordinary な form and the more formal である form.
Noun predicate: である + 反面
責任者である反面 · “on the other hand, being the person in charge…” For this pattern, use the book’s noun + である connection, not noun + の.
How to read the word
反面（はんめん） · 反 suggests an opposing side; 面 means a face or aspect. This helps recall the meaning but does not replace reading the whole sentence.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

のはことが、ストレスも。

City life has many interesting things, but on the other hand it also brings a lot of stress.

Clue Interesting experiences and stress are opposite aspects of city life.

How to read this example

都会の生活 is the shared topic. 多い is an い-adjective and attaches directly to 反面. The second も adds stress to the overall picture; it does not turn the sentence into simple addition without contrast.

Example 02

はな、でやらなければならないので、だ。

Traveling alone is relaxing, but it is also inconvenient because you must do everything by yourself.

Clue Being carefree is balanced against having to manage everything alone.

How to read this example

気楽な connects the な-adjective to 反面. The clause 何でも一人でやらなければならないので explains why the trip is 不便: everything must be handled alone.

Example 03

をなが、もなくなってしまった。

After quitting my job, I gained more free time, but I also lost the sense of tension that had kept me alert.

Clue The same life change has a welcome and an unwelcome consequence.

How to read this example

辞めて links quitting the job to what followed. 増えた is the past form before 反面. なくなってしまった presents the loss of 緊張感, a sense of tension or alertness, as regrettable.

Do not confuse it with…

反面 ↔ 一方で
一方で is broader: it can show contrasting aspects or simply two concurrent roles. When the sides genuinely contrast, both may work. The textbook explicitly notes overlap; do not declare them mutually exclusive.
Opposing aspects ↔ parallel duties
研究をする一方で、学生の世話もする presents two duties. Student care is not automatically a drawback of research. 反面 would need a context that actually presents the second fact as an opposing aspect.
反面 ↔ かわりに
かわりに can frame a disadvantage as balanced by a benefit: “demanding work, but good pay in return.” 反面 simply presents the reverse aspect. No deliberate bargain is required for either to describe a trade-off.
反面 ↔ に対して
に対して foregrounds an explicit comparison between sides. 反面 foregrounds the contrasting aspects of an overall topic. Counting subjects is a useful first clue, not a universal decision rule.
Contrasting aspects ↔ an unexpected result
便利なのに使われない says it is convenient, yet surprisingly unused. 反面 asks the reader to consider another aspect; it does not by itself require the speaker to be surprised.

JLPT approach

  1. Look for a real contrast. “Busy in summer, few people in winter” gives opposing seasonal aspects. “Busy in summer, busy in winter too” mainly adds the same tendency.
  2. Do not translate every “on the other hand” as 反面. If the sentence adds another responsibility without presenting an opposing aspect, examine 一方で.
  3. Check the word before the connector. 気楽 is a な-adjective, so 気楽な反面. 多い is an い-adjective, so 多い反面. Neither takes the の required by a clause before に対して.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

一人暮らしは自由な反面、寂しいときもあります。

ひとりぐらしはじゆうなはんめん、さびしいときもあります。

Living alone gives you freedom, but it can also be lonely.

Both sides describe living alone. ときもあります limits the loneliness to some occasions.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 4, printed pages 24–25.

3. ～一方（で）

Present another side, role, or activity alongside the first

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A 一方（で） B means “A, while also B” or “A; on the other hand, B.” It can introduce another role or duty, another feeling about an event, or a contrasting group in society.

Research and caring for students are two responsibilities. Joy and a sense of responsibility can coexist. Some people enjoy conversation, while others find it difficult. The relationship may be contrasting, but it need not be a benefit followed by a drawback.

“At the same time” can mean within the same life, period, or situation. It does not require someone to speak and listen, or research and teach, at exactly the same second.

Think of it this wayAsk whether B adds a distinct side of the picture. It might be a contrasting tendency, a second role, or another feeling—not necessarily something bad.

How to form it

Verb in a plain form + 一方（で）
働く一方で／働いている一方で · while working / while also being employed. A plain clause connects directly; do not add の.
い-adjective + 一方（で）
うれしい一方で／うれしかった一方で · happy while also… The past form can describe the reaction to a past event.
な-adjective: な／である + 一方（で）
便利な一方で／便利である一方で · convenient, while also… Keep the な-adjective connection.
Noun predicate: である + 一方（で）
教師である一方で、作家でもある · a teacher and also a writer. This shows two roles, without judging either as a disadvantage.
で is optional in this construction
人がいる一方、それが苦手な人もいる · the book’s fourth example omits で. 一方 alone can still connect two sides; it does not automatically mean a continuing trend.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

ではのをで、ほかののもよくください。

In meetings, state your own opinion while also listening carefully to what others say.

Clue Speaking and listening are both part of participating in meetings.

How to read this example

言う is the dictionary form before 一方で. 聞いてください asks the listener to listen too. The advice does not require talking over other people while they speak.

Example 02

はにで、しっかりのもしなければならない。

A professor pursues new research while also having to take proper care of students.

Clue Research and student care are two professional responsibilities.

How to read this example

取り組む means to engage in or work on something. The second side adds 世話もしなければならない: one must also take care of students. It is not automatically the negative consequence of doing research.

Example 03

がうれしかったで、もじた。

I was happy when my child was born, while at the same time I felt a heavy responsibility.

Clue A birth brings both happiness and a sense of responsibility.

How to read this example

うれしかった is the past form of the い-adjective. The second clause adds 重い責任も感じた. These feelings coexist; the sentence does not say that happiness ended before responsibility began.

Example 04

にはとことがながいる、それがなも。

Some people in the world like talking with others, while many people are not good at it.

Clue The topic is society’s different responses to conversation.

How to read this example

The first side ends in いる一方, with no で. それ refers to talking with others. 苦手 means finding it difficult or not being good at it; it does not necessarily mean actively disliking people.

Do not confuse it with…

一方で ↔ 反面
反面 specifically emphasizes an opposing aspect. 一方で also allows parallel roles. If the two aspects contrast, either may be possible; choose from the complete wording and intended emphasis.
Parallel roles ↔ ながら
音楽を聞きながら勉強する describes one person doing two actions together. 一方で can describe two roles held over a period, even if the activities happen at different times. Compare the ながら discussion in Lesson 2.
一方（で） ↔ 一方だ
物価は上がる一方だ means prices keep rising: one continuing direction of change. 物価が上がる一方で、給料は変わらない contrasts rising prices with unchanged pay. Read the following clause and meaning; the presence or absence of で is not enough.
Two sides ↔ two linked scales · Lesson 2
～ば～ほど links degrees: the more one thing changes, the more another changes. 一方で places facts alongside each other; it does not itself state a proportional relationship.
Alongside ↔ an extra opportunity · Lesson 2
ついでに makes B a convenient extra to a main task. 一方で does not rank B as an incidental errand. A professor’s student care is another duty, not a spare-time add-on.

JLPT approach

  1. Do not invent a “second clause must be negative” rule. It can express a welcome role or activity. In a question about a harmful side effect, the negative meaning comes from that context.
  2. Use も as supporting evidence. It often helps signal “also,” as in 学生の世話も. But も alone cannot distinguish 一方で from other constructions.
  3. Check whether the options add distinct aspects. Football enthusiasm and quiet reading show two sides of one person. Library visits and buying books largely reinforce the same reading interest.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

会社で働く一方で、日本語も勉強しています。

かいしゃではたらくいっぽうで、にほんごもべんきょうしています。

Alongside my company job, I also study Japanese.

The sentence presents two activities in your life. It does not claim that you study during the exact moments you perform your job.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 4, printed pages 24–25.

4. ～というより

Choose a more accurate description: “I would call it B”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A というより B means “not so much A as B” or “A—or rather, B.” The speaker considers an initial description and replaces or refines it with a better one. “Friends” becomes “rivals”; “walking” becomes “practically running”; “asked” becomes “ordered.”

The first description may be partly appropriate, too weak, or something the speaker rejects. B need not always be a stronger adjective on the same scale—it may name a different category or a more accurate explanation.

Think of two descriptions competing to explain one situation. The sentence is not necessarily comparing two separate objects or choosing which action to perform.

Think of it this wayPut quotation marks around A and B. Does the sentence mean “B describes this better than A does”?

How to form it

Noun or label + というより
友だちというより競争相手 · rivals rather than friends. No の is needed before というより.
Verb or い-adjective in a plain form
歩くというより／涼しいというより · rather than call it walking / cool. The tense of the final description may place the whole situation in the past.
な-adjective description
静かというより寂しい · more lonely than merely quiet. A quoted label can appear without だ; a fuller plain statement can retain だ, as in 静かだというより…. Do not use な just because the word is a な-adjective.
A fuller quoted statement
壊れているというより、使い方の問題だ · rather than being broken, it is a matter of how it is being used. というより can revise a whole explanation.
A repeated fragment in conversation
頼まれて、というより… repeats the other speaker’s wording, pauses, and corrects it. The book treats the phrase being compared as the unit; do not “fix” every quoted fragment into dictionary form.
Optional emphasis
というより、むしろ… · “rather, I would say…” むしろ reinforces the preference for the second description; it is not required.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

ぼくとが？　いや、ぼくたちはというよりいいなんだよ。

He and I are friends? No, rather than friends, we are good rivals.

Clue The speaker corrects the label “friends” to “good rivals.”

How to read this example

The opening question supplies 友だち, and いや introduces the correction. The same two people are being described throughout. 競争相手 means a rival or competitor, not necessarily an enemy.

Example 02

はのが。というよりというじだ。

Michiko walks fast. It feels more like she is running than walking.

Clue The second description communicates just how fast Michiko walks.

How to read this example

The first sentence supplies the speed clue, 歩くのが速い. 走るという感じだ means “it feels like running”; the sentence need not claim a literal change from walking to running.

Example 03

A「へえ、この、にれてかいたんですか。」B「れて、というよりされたんだよ。」

A: Oh, did the company president ask you to paint this? B: “Asked” is not quite the word—he ordered me to.

Clue The reply repeats part of the question, then corrects its force.

How to read this example

頼まれて is the passive て-form of 頼む: “being asked.” B quotes that fragment before the pause. 命令された means “was ordered.” The difference is the pressure of the request, not who painted the picture.

Do not confuse it with…

Description ↔ numerical comparison
来週は今週より忙しい compares two weeks. というより would compare how to describe something: “busy—or rather, overwhelmed.” An ordinary “more than” comparison does not need という.
“Or rather” ↔ “I would rather do” · Lesson 3
くらいなら rejects an unwanted action and chooses an alternative. というより chooses a better description. English “rather” appears in both translations, but does different work.
Rewording ↔ substitution
かわりに can mean using B instead of A. というより does not replace one real object with another: calling a dog “family” changes its description, not the dog.
Rewording ↔ opposing aspects
反面 and 一方で can acknowledge two aspects as true together. というより tells you that the second wording fits better; it is not merely adding another trait.

JLPT approach

  1. Read B first. It is the description the speaker prefers. Then ask why A might have been suggested and why B improves it.
  2. Use the available context to judge alternatives. After a sudden temperature drop, “cool—rather, cold” naturally corrects an understatement. Other corrections may be possible in a different conversation, but must be supported here.
  3. Avoid a rule that A must remain partly true. The speaker may qualify A or reject it. The essential point is choosing B as the better description.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

これは仕事というより、趣味です。

これはしごとというより、しゅみです。

I would call this a hobby rather than work.

The speaker chooses the label that better reflects how they see the activity.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 4, printed pages 24–25.

5. ～かわりに

A substitute, something in return, or a balancing benefit and cost

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

かわりに connects two things through replacement or compensation. Its translation depends on what happens to A. Do not automatically translate every example as “instead of.”

Replacement: B takes A’s place
The usual hometown visit is not chosen; an overseas trip takes its place. Here “instead of A, B” works.
Exchange: A and B both happen
Lee teaches me English; I help Lee with work in return. One contribution answers the other.
Trade-off: both sides are true
Freelance work offers free time, but financial uncertainty comes with it. A benefit and a cost balance the picture. There need not be a literal agreement or a mathematically equal exchange.

A trade-off can start with the drawback and then name the benefit, or the other way around. The speaker presents the sides as compensating for one another.

Think of it this wayFirst ask “Does A still happen?” If no, look for a substitute. If yes, look for something given in return or a benefit balanced against a cost.

How to form it

Verb in a plain form + かわりに
帰るかわりに／教えてもらっているかわりに · instead of returning / in return for being taught. Dictionary and ongoing forms can fit; the relationship decides the meaning.
Past verb + かわりに
手伝ってもらったかわりに · in return for receiving help. Past tense is possible when A has already happened.
い-adjective + かわりに
高いかわりに／きついかわりに · expensive but with a compensating benefit / demanding but with something in return. Attach directly.
な-adjective + なかわりに
不便なかわりに · inconvenient but with a balancing good point. Do not put だ immediately before かわりに.
Noun + のかわりに
砂糖のかわりに／先生のかわりに · instead of sugar / in place of the teacher. This familiar substitution form adds の after the noun.
Separate sentence: そのかわり（に）
少し高いです。そのかわり、長く使えます。 · “It is a little expensive. In return, it lasts a long time.” その points back to the previously stated side.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

フリーのはながかわりに、のことがいつもだ。

Freelance work gives you a lot of free time, but in exchange money is always a worry.

Clue The free time is balanced against ongoing money worries.

How to read this example

多い is an い-adjective, so it attaches directly to かわりに. Both facts are presented as true about freelance work; the sentence does not replace free time with money worries.

Example 02

のは、があるかわりに、ことはしない。

Chairman Yamada is decisive, but in exchange he does not think things through deeply.

Clue A strength is paired with a corresponding weakness.

How to read this example

実行力がある means having the ability to put things into action. 深く考えることはしない says he does not think deeply. This is a trade-off in the speaker’s evaluation, not a literal exchange negotiated by Yamada.

Example 03

リーさんにをもらっているかわりに、リーさんのをいる。

In exchange for Lee teaching me English, I help with Lee's work.

Clue Lee’s teaching and the speaker’s help are reciprocal contributions.

How to read this example

リーさんに英語を教えてもらっている means the speaker receives English teaching from Lee. The speaker helps with Lee’s work in return. Both ている forms describe an ongoing arrangement.

Example 04

のはいつものようにふるさとにかわりに、とをしたい。

This New Year, instead of returning to my hometown as usual, I want to travel abroad with my parents.

Clue The overseas trip replaces the usual New Year visit.

How to read this example

いつものように identifies the usual activity: returning home. 帰る is the dictionary form before かわりに. The planned alternative is traveling abroad with the parents; this does not mean avoiding the parents.

Do not confuse it with…

かわりに ↔ 反面
反面 presents opposing aspects. かわりに emphasizes a balancing cost or compensation, and also has exchange and substitution uses. In a benefit-and-cost sentence, both may be possible with different emphasis.
かわりに ↔ 一方で
一方で can simply present another role or fact. かわりに asks you to understand the second part as replacing or compensating for the first. Two duties do not automatically form an exchange.
Replacement ↔ an extra task · Lesson 2
ついでに adds B while doing A anyway. In the replacement use of かわりに, B takes A’s place. In the exchange use, both happen, but B is given in return.
Substitute ↔ a rejected option · Lesson 3
くらいなら presents A as undesirable and B as preferable. かわりに can describe a neutral replacement without disliking A.
Compensation ↔ sequence · Lesson 1
てから places one action after another. かわりに adds a replacement or balancing relationship. “After helping” alone does not mean “in return for helping.”

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the use before translating. In the English-lessons example, both people provide help. Translating it as “instead of being taught” would reverse the situation.
  2. Look for the balancing side. After “the job is demanding,” good pay supplies compensation. Another disadvantage merely adds a complaint unless further context explains the balance.
  3. Do not invent a deliberate bargain. A personality’s strength and weakness can be described with かわりに. The speaker frames a balance; the person need not have consciously chosen the weakness.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

今日は私が料理するかわりに、皿を洗ってくれますか。

きょうはわたしがりょうりするかわりに、さらをあらってくれますか。

If I cook today, could you wash the dishes in return?

An exchange of contributions: cooking and washing the dishes are both intended to happen.
