1. 尊敬語（そんけいご）

Respect the person who performs the action or has the state

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

尊敬語 presents another person’s action or state respectfully. In 先生がお帰りになります, the teacher goes home. The teacher is the person being honored; the speaker is not the person going home.

Start with the ordinary verb: 帰る, する, 食べる, 言う. Then choose its respectful form. The subject is often omitted in conversation, so look at the question, the previous speaker, and the setting. A clerk asking a customer about a ticket is still describing the customer’s possession.

Politeness and direction of respect are separate. 帰ります is polite; お帰りになります also honors the person going home. In standard conversation with an outsider, describe your own family or company as your side, even when that person is older or your manager. Age alone does not select the answer.

Think of it this wayFirst complete “_____ does / is _____.” If that person is the one being honored, look for a respectful verb. Do not choose the longest expression just because it sounds formal.

How to form it

Regular pattern: お + ます-stem + になる
帰ります → 帰り → お帰りになる → お帰りになります · Remove ます, not just its final す. Past: お帰りになりました. Do not put 帰る or 帰って after お in this pattern.
Many する-verbs: ご + action noun + になる
卒業する → ご卒業になる · The action noun replaces the whole する-verb. Prefix choice is conventional: お is common with Japanese verbs, ご with many Sino-Japanese action nouns. This is a tendency, not a rule for every word.
Respectful れる / られる
書く → 書かれる／食べる → 食べられる／する → される／来る → 来られる · Same shape as the passive. For Group 1 use the あ-row + れる; Group 2 drop る + られる. Context decides whether it is respect, passive, or—in some forms—ability.
Requests: お / ご + stem or action noun + ください
お書きください／ご連絡ください／ごらんください · The listener performs the action. Do not mechanically insert して: ご連絡ください is the request pattern here.
Being and continuing: で / ていらっしゃる
会長でいらっしゃる／元気でいらっしゃる／住んでいらっしゃる · で for a noun or な-adjective description; a verb’s て-form for ている. お探しです is an established お + stem + です pattern for what someone is looking for.
Special verbs: movement and presence
来る・行く・いる → いらっしゃる／おいでになる · These can mean come, go, or be. 見える can respectfully mean come, as in the arrival example. Recover the basic meaning from location, time, and context.
Special verbs: actions and knowledge
する → なさる／見る → ごらんになる／食べる・飲む → 召し上がる／言う → おっしゃる／知っている → ご存じです · Learn these as pairs. ご存じです is an established expression; do not create ご存じます.
Special ます-forms to memorize
いらっしゃいます／なさいます／おっしゃいます／くださいます · These use い before ます, not the expected り. Compare regular 召し上がります. Past forms end in ました: おっしゃいました.
Receiving a kindness from the other side
先生が書いてくださった · くださる is respectful くれる. The teacher writes; the speaker or their side receives the benefit. This is different from a bare request ending ください.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

A. General respectful forms and requests

Example 01

はごろおになりますか。

About what time will you go home today?

Clue The listener is the person going home.

How to read this example

帰ります → 帰り → お帰りになる. 何時ごろ asks for an approximate time. The respectful form changes how the actor is presented, not the meaning of 帰る.

Example 02

にをごになったのですか。

In what year did you graduate from university?

Clue The question respectfully asks about a completed graduation.

How to read this example

卒業する becomes ご卒業になる. なった is past, and のですか asks for the information in an explanatory frame. Do not read になった here as “became a graduation.”

Example 03

のにはされますか。

Will you attend tomorrow's meeting?

Clue The person being asked will attend the meeting.

How to read this example

出席する → 出席される → 出席されます. Here される respectfully describes attendance; nobody is being passively “attended.” 明日 sets the future time.

Example 04

こちらにおをおください。

Please write your name here.

Clue The other person is asked to write their own name.

How to read this example

書き is the ます-stem of 書く. お書きください is the respectful request. こちらに gives the place to write; お名前 refers politely to the listener’s name.

Example 05

ぜひごください。

Please be sure to contact us.

Clue The speaker wants the listener to contact them.

How to read this example

連絡 is the action noun before ご and ください. ぜひ adds encouragement or emphasis. ご連絡します would instead announce contact from the speaker’s side.

B. Identity, states, and continuing actions

Example 06

のでいらっしゃいます。

He is the chairperson of the study group.

Clue This respectfully states someone’s role, rather than their location.

How to read this example

会長 is a noun. 会長でいらっしゃいます corresponds to 会長です. The で belongs to the identity expression, not a location of an activity.

Example 07

おでいらっしゃるそうです。

I hear that they are doing well.

Clue The person’s good health is reported secondhand.

How to read this example

元気 is a な-adjective, so 元気でいらっしゃる corresponds to 元気だ respectfully. The following そうです reports what was heard. Compare the reported-information expressions in Lesson 7.

Example 08

にいらっしゃいます。

They live in Kyoto.

Clue The other person’s continuing residence is being described.

How to read this example

住んでいる becomes 住んでいらっしゃる. 京都に is the residence location. The ている meaning remains: this is living there, not traveling to Kyoto.

Example 09

をおですか。

What are you looking for?

Clue The question concerns what the listener is looking for now.

How to read this example

探す → 探し; お探しです corresponds here to 探しています. Compare お持ちです for someone’s possession in the bank exercise. The ending です differs from humble お探しします.

Example 10

をしておいでになります。

They are conducting research on new energy.

Clue The research is ongoing; おいでになる supports the ている meaning.

How to read this example

研究している becomes 研究しておいでになる. After て, this does not mean the researcher goes somewhere. 新エネルギー is what they are researching.

C. A kindness received from the respected person

Example 11

をくださいました。

They kindly wrote a letter of recommendation for me.

Clue The respected person wrote something helpful for the speaker.

How to read this example

書いてくれた becomes 書いてくださいました. The teacher or another respected giver is the understood subject. This is a past statement of kindness, not the instruction “please write.”

D. Coming, going, and being somewhere

Example 12

いつわたしのへいらっしゃいましたか。

When did you come to my country?

Clue Here いらっしゃる means come, with a past visit in question.

How to read this example

いつ and いらっしゃいました ask when the visit happened. わたしの国へ gives the destination relative to the speaker. The speaker is not the traveler simply because わたし appears.

Example 13

いつわたしのへおいでになりますか。

When will you come to my country?

Clue おいでになる is another respectful way to ask about coming.

How to read this example

The destination is the speaker’s country. いつ asks when the visit will occur. Unlike the preceding past example, になります is nonpast.

Example 14

さきほどよ。

They arrived a little while ago.

Clue A recent arrival makes 見える mean come here.

How to read this example

さきほど means a little while ago. 見えました is a respectful arrival expression in this context, not a report that something became visible.

Example 15

アメリカへいらっしゃいます。

They will go to America next month.

Clue The destination and next-month time make this going to America.

How to read this example

いらっしゃる can replace 行く as well as 来る and いる. 来月 supplies the future time; アメリカへ supplies the destination.

Example 16

アメリカへおいでになるようです。

It appears they will go to America next month.

Clue The speaker presents the planned trip as an apparent situation.

How to read this example

おいでになる respectfully means go here. ようです adds “it appears / seems,” so do not turn the sentence into an unqualified confirmation of the trip.

Example 17

はにいらっしゃるでしょう。

They will probably be at home tonight.

Clue A home location and tonight’s time indicate presence.

How to read this example

お宅にいらっしゃる means be at home respectfully. でしょう adds an expectation or probability. There is no movement verb meaning required here.

Example 18

はにおいでになりますか。

Will you be at home tonight?

Clue The question asks whether the person will be at home.

How to read this example

おいでになります can express presence as well as movement. 今晩 and お宅に support the presence reading. The final か makes this a question.

E. Special verbs for everyday actions

Example 19

ゴルフをなさいますか。

Do you play golf?

Clue The listener is the person who plays golf.

How to read this example

ゴルフをする becomes ゴルフをなさる. なさいます is the special polite form, not なさります. The question can ask about the person’s golfing practice.

Example 20

このをごらんになりますか。

Will you look at this magazine?

Clue The other person will look at or read the magazine.

How to read this example

見る becomes ごらんになる. 雑誌 is the object marked by を. If the speaker were offering to look at it, the corresponding humble verb would be 拝見する.

Example 21

を。

They eat Japanese food.

Clue The respected person is eating Japanese food.

How to read this example

召し上がる replaces 食べる here. It is a regular Group 1 verb for conjugation: 召し上がります. Do not use it to describe your own eating in an ordinary self-report.

Example 22

も。

They also drink sake.

Clue The same respectful verb also covers drinking.

How to read this example

お酒 tells you to interpret 召し上がる as drink. も means also. The お in お酒 alone does not tell you who is being honored; the verb and context do.

Example 23

おはとおっしゃいますか。

What is your name?

Clue This is a respectful name question using the verb for saying.

How to read this example

何と asks “as what / by what name.” おっしゃいます is respectful 言います. Compare the speaker’s answer ～と申します in the third topic.

Example 24

あのをですか。

Do you know that person?

Clue The question concerns the listener’s knowledge of a person.

How to read this example

知っています becomes ご存じです. あの方 is a respectful way to say that person. ご存じ is followed by です here, not directly by ます.

Do not confuse it with…

Their action ↔ my action involving them
拝見します means I look respectfully at something associated with the other person. ごらんになります describes that person looking. Both concern seeing; the actor changes.
Their speech ↔ my introduction
山中と申します introduces the speaker. お名前は何とおっしゃいますか asks another person’s name respectfully. Memorize 言う → おっしゃる / 申す as a direction contrast.
Who gives the kindness? · Lesson 9
先生が教えてくださる / 私は先生に教えていただく can describe the same teaching. The first makes the teacher the giver; the second makes me the recipient. The teacher teaches in both.
Respectful request ↔ direct instruction · Lesson 10
書け directly commands writing. お書きください requests the same action respectfully. Do not transfer the imperative ending into the お + stem pattern.
Respectful form ↔ passive / potential
先生が食べられます can need context to distinguish “the teacher eats” respectfully from “the teacher can eat.” 召し上がります clearly identifies the respectful eating/drinking verb. Do not label every られる passive.

JLPT approach

  1. Mark the actor before reading the choices. Teacher, customer, or listener’s father often signals another person to honor; “my parents” in speech to an outsider signals the speaker’s side.
  2. Reduce each option to its ordinary verb. いたす = する on the speaker’s side; なさる = する respectfully for the other person. Both are formal, but they point in different directions.
  3. Check the final construction. お持ちですか asks respectfully whether the customer has something. お持ちします offers to carry or bring something for them. One short ending changes the roles.
  4. Avoid inventing extra layers. For the regular pattern, use お帰りになる or 帰られる, rather than お帰りになられる. Some established expressions have special histories; “more than one polite-looking piece is always wrong” is not a reliable rule.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

先生はもうお帰りになりましたか。

せんせいはもうおかえりになりましたか。

Has the teacher already gone home?

The teacher goes home. もう and the past ending check whether that action has happened; the respectful form does not change its timing.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 12, printed pages 52–53.

The Agency for Cultural Affairs’ 敬語の指針, printed p.30, explains why redundant honorific forms are generally avoided while recognizing established exceptions.

2. 謙譲語1（けんじょうご）

Present an action humbly in relation to someone you respect

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

謙譲語1 focuses on the respected person involved in an action from your side. You borrow the teacher’s book, guide the teacher, look at their photograph, or visit their home. The teacher may be the recipient, source, owner, or person visited—not necessarily the grammatical subject.

The bracketed phrases in the book make that relationship visible. They may be omitted in real conversation when everyone knows who is involved. Before choosing a humble verb, restore that missing relationship in your mind.

Do not attach お～する to every action you perform. An ordinary statement about watching television alone does not automatically need 拝見する. Also separate the two actions in 教えていただく: the teacher teaches; I receive that help. The humble receiving expression does not mean that I teach.

Think of it this wayAsk “What am I doing, and how does it involve the respected person?” For ていただく, separately identify who performs the helpful action and who receives it.

How to form it

お + ます-stem + する
借りる → 借り → お借りする／見せる → 見せ → お見せする · Borrow something associated with the other person; show something to them. Polite: お借りします / お見せします.
ご + action noun + する
案内する → ご案内する → ご案内します · The speaker guides the respected person. Compare ご案内になる, where the person being honored does the guiding.
Ability: お / ご + stem or action noun + できる
お見せできる → お見せできません · Can / cannot show it to the other person. Keep the humble relationship while changing the ability. The book’s まだ…ません means not yet.
Receive an action: Vて + いただく
私は先生に教えていただく · Humble てもらう. 私 is the recipient; 先生に identifies the person giving the instruction. Past: 教えていただきました.
Special verbs: see and say
見る → 拝見する／言う → 申し上げる · Respectfully look at something associated with the person / say something to them. お礼を申し上げる is a useful fixed phrase for offering thanks.
Special verbs: ask, visit, meet, give
聞く・訪ねる → 伺う／会う → お目にかかる／あげる → さしあげる · Learn the roles with the word, not just its English label. 伺う can mean ask or visit; the complement decides.
Tense and politeness stay at the end
伺う → 伺います → 伺いました／拝見する → 拝見しました · 伺う is Group 1; its て-form is 伺って. 拝見する conjugates through する. Honorific meaning does not erase ordinary tense or negation.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

A. Borrowing, guiding, and showing

Example 01

（の）ごをおします。

I will borrow the teacher's book.

Clue The teacher owns the book; the speaker borrows it.

How to read this example

借りる → お借りする. The teacher is not the borrower. The bracketed 先生の identifies the relationship that licenses the humble expression.

Example 02

（を）へごします。

I will guide the teacher to the venue.

Clue The teacher is the person being guided.

How to read this example

先生を is the object of 案内する. 会場へ is the destination. ご案内します says the speaker will guide; ご案内になります would honor the guide instead.

Example 03

はまだ（に）おできません。

I cannot show the completed product to the teacher yet.

Clue The speaker cannot yet show the finished product to the teacher.

How to read this example

見せる means show, not see. お見せできません combines the humble showing pattern with inability. まだ…ません means not yet; do not translate it as a refusal without further context.

B. Receiving instruction

Example 04

（に）ピアノをいただきました。

The teacher kindly taught me piano.

Clue The teacher teaches; the speaker receives the instruction.

How to read this example

先生に marks the provider of teaching. 教えていただきました is humble 教えてもらいました, with gratitude or benefit in the perspective. The speaker does not perform 教える.

C. Special verbs and their relationships

Example 05

（のを）ちょっとします。

I will take a quick look at the teacher's photograph.

Clue The photograph’s connection to the teacher matters.

How to read this example

拝見します humbly describes the speaker’s viewing of the teacher’s photograph. It does not honor the speaker as a viewer. ちょっと softens the announcement as a brief look.

Example 06

（に）をます。

I offer my thanks to the teacher.

Clue The thanks are directed to the teacher.

How to read this example

お礼を申し上げます is an established expression for offering thanks. 申し上げる is humble 言う with someone addressed; this is not the ordinary self-introduction ～と申します.

Example 07

（に）ちょっとが……。

May I ask the teacher something?

Clue Here 伺う means ask, not visit.

How to read this example

先生に and the unfinished ～ますが frame a courteous lead-in to a question. ちょっと softens the approach. The following question would supply what the speaker wants to ask.

Example 08

あした3に（の）に。

I will visit the teacher's home at three tomorrow.

Clue The teacher’s home is the place being visited.

How to read this example

お宅に and あした3時に make the visiting sense of 伺う clear. The subject is the speaker, even though the respected teacher appears in the sentence.

Example 09

あした3に（に）おにかかります。

I will meet the teacher at three tomorrow.

Clue The speaker will meet the teacher.

How to read this example

お目にかかる is humble 会う. Learn it as a complete phrase rather than translating the parts as eyes and hanging. The teacher is the person marked by に.

Example 10

（に）をさしあげます。

I will give the teacher a book.

Clue The teacher receives the book; the speaker gives it.

How to read this example

さしあげる is the humble giving verb. Do not confuse its direction with いただく, where the speaker receives. The book uses a straightforward transfer of an object.

Do not confuse it with…

I look ↔ they look
ごらんになります honors the person looking. 拝見します humbly presents my looking at something associated with the respected person. The object alone cannot decide the answer.
A respectful target ↔ formal report of my side
参ります formally reports going or coming without requiring the destination to be a respected person’s place. 伺います in a visit emphasizes that relationship to the person visited.
Receive help ↔ ask for permission · Lesson 9
説明していただく / 説明させていただく: someone explains to me / I am allowed to explain. The causative させ changes who performs 説明する.
I receive ↔ they kindly do
先生が教えてくださる and 私は先生に教えていただく describe the benefit from different viewpoints. Do not change に to が while leaving the intended receiving structure unchanged.
Humble offer ↔ respectful request
お持ちします / お持ちください · I will carry or bring it for you / please bring or carry it. する and ください change who is supposed to act.

JLPT approach

  1. Restore an omitted participant. For 伺う, locate the person you ask or visit. For 拝見する, locate whose material you view. A formal-sounding verb still needs a suitable relationship.
  2. Separate teaching from receiving teaching. In 先生に教えていただいた, 先生 teaches and the understood speaker receives. Do not mark the teacher as the subject of いただく.
  3. Check the ordinary verb and its ending. お見せできません expresses inability to show; お見せしません expresses not showing. The polite surface does not make them equivalent.
  4. Choose a useful offer in conversation. お手伝いしましょうか (“Shall I help?”) can be easier to receive than announcing that you will give someone a favor. Respectful wording still needs an appropriate situation.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

資料を拝見してもよろしいですか。

しりょうをはいけんしてもよろしいですか。

May I look at the materials?

The speaker asks permission to look at the other person’s materials. 拝見 is followed by する → して, then the permission expression.

3. 謙譲語2（けんじょうご）

Speak formally about yourself or your side

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

謙譲語2 presents your side’s actions or states modestly and formally to the listener. You state your name with 申します, say you will go with 参ります, or report your presence with おります. The listener does not have to be the destination or recipient of the action.

That is the practical difference from the previous topic: 家におります can describe being at your own home. There is no need to invent a respected person at that location. These forms suit introductions and formal reports; ordinary polite 行きます or います is often sufficient in everyday conversation.

Follow the textbook’s grouping for study, but learn the use of each verb. Here いただく means eat or drink. In the previous topic, ていただく means receive another person’s action. The same spelling does not guarantee the same sentence structure.

Think of it this wayAsk “Am I formally reporting something about myself or my side to this listener?” Then recover the basic meaning: go/come, be, do, eat/drink, say, know, or think.

How to form it

行く / 来る → 参る
参る → 参ります → 参りました · Go or come, selected by context. Group 1: て-form 参って. This form does not by itself tell you which direction the journey takes.
いる → おる
おります／おりました／おりません · Formal presence on the speaker’s side. For continuing actions, 働いております corresponds to 働いています. Keep the distinction between living presence and inanimate ある.
する → いたす
いたします／いたしました · Formal “do” on the speaker’s side. In the tidy-up example, わたしが explicitly identifies the actor. Do not use いたす as respectful なさる for a customer.
食べる / 飲む → いただく
いただきます／いただきました · Eat or drink in this lesson’s example. 食事をいただく names the meal; 教えていただく uses a preceding て-form and means receiving help.
言う → 申す
リーと申します · The fixed introduction “My name is Lee.” Use と before the naming verb. 申す conjugates regularly: 申します / 申しました.
知っている → 存じている
存じております／存じません · Know / do not know, modestly. 存じております combines the knowledge state with formal おる. For knowing a person, 存じ上げております is also used.
思う → 存じる
方法があると存じます · “I think there are methods.” A clause + と gives the content of the thought. Do not force “know” onto every 存じます.
My side includes family in outside conversation
両親が参ります · The subject need not be 私. When formally telling an outsider about your own parents coming, the textbook uses the form for your side. Relationships in the current conversation matter.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

あした3にます。

I will come at three tomorrow.

Clue The speaker formally states a coming time.

How to read this example

参る covers 来る and 行く. This example is translated as coming because that is the intended situation; あした3時に gives the appointment time, not the direction by itself.

Example 02

はにおります。

I will be at home in the evening.

Clue The speaker is at their own home in the evening.

How to read this example

おります is formal います. This is the key contrast with a humble visit to a respected person: the home does not need to belong to someone being honored.

Example 03

はでわたしがいたします。

I will tidy up later.

Clue わたしが makes the person doing the tidying explicit.

How to read this example

いたします is formal します for the speaker’s action. 後で places it later. なさいます would instead honor the person doing the action and would be wrong for this ordinary self-offer.

Example 04

ももいただきます。

I will have both sashimi and sake.

Clue The food and drink identify the eating/drinking use of いただく.

How to read this example

刺身も日本酒も means both sashimi and sake. There is no preceding て-form: this is not receiving another person’s action. The listener is not being asked to eat.

Example 05

と。

My name is Yamanaka.

Clue The speaker is introducing themself.

How to read this example

名前 + と申します is the fixed pattern. 申す corresponds to 言う on the speaker’s side. The respectful name question to someone else uses おっしゃいます.

Example 06

はよくております。

I know the destination well.

Clue The speaker already knows the destination well.

How to read this example

存じております describes an existing knowledge state. 行き先 is what is known; it does not make the verb mean go. よく adds “well.”

Example 07

いくつかがあるとます。

I believe there are several methods.

Clue The speaker is expressing a view that several methods exist.

How to read this example

方法がある is the content before と. 存じます corresponds to 思います here. It presents the speaker’s thought formally, not the other person’s knowledge.

Do not confuse it with…

My presence ↔ another person’s presence
先生はいらっしゃいます honors the teacher. 私はおります formally reports my presence. Both replace いる; choosing the wrong one reverses the respect.
My movement ↔ visiting a respected person
先生のお宅に伺います highlights the visit to the teacher. 明日参ります reports coming or going formally. 参る does not require that particular destination relationship.
Say my name ↔ offer thanks
お礼を申し上げます offers thanks to someone. 山中と申します is the standard self-introduction. A longer verb is not automatically a better introduction.
Eat/drink ↔ receive an action · Lesson 9
お返事をいただく / 説明していただく mean receive a reply / have someone explain. パンをいただく can mean eat bread in a meal context. Identify what comes before いただく.
Formal する with familiar Lesson 11 patterns
行くことにいたしました retains the meaning “decided to go”; only the final する becomes formal. Keep 行く plain before こと, not 行きますこと.

JLPT approach

  1. Track whose family or company is mentioned. The speaker’s own parents belong to the speaker’s side in the textbook’s formal outside conversation. Being older does not automatically require いらっしゃる.
  2. Distinguish the person described from the person listening. A formal report can concern your own action at home while showing consideration to the listener.
  3. Look inside the thought. In お忙しいとは存じますが, the other person is busy, but the speaker does the thinking or understanding. ご存じです would honor the knower instead.
  4. Translate the plain meaning first. 参る has go/come meanings; いただく has eating/drinking and receiving uses. Location, objects, and the preceding て-form settle the reading.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

田中と申します。どうぞよろしくお願いいたします。

たなかともうします。どうぞよろしくおねがいいたします。

My name is Tanaka. It is a pleasure to meet you.

A formal introduction. The second sentence is a conventional request for a good relationship; its natural English wording depends on the occasion.

4. 丁寧語（ていねいご）

Make the statement polite without changing whose action it is

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

丁寧語 makes an utterance polite to the listener. Familiar です and ます already do this. The book adds the more formal ございます, common in service explanations and announcements.

Distinguish two structures: Nでございます means “is N”; Nが場所にございます means “N is located at the place.” The product and computer supplies are things, not people being elevated. The form makes the clerk’s explanation polite.

A sentence can combine polite endings with respectful or humble vocabulary: いらっしゃいます has a respectful verb and a polite ending. Do not assume every expression ending in ます belongs only to this fourth category.

Think of it this wayDecide whether you are identifying something or saying where it is. “This is a new product” needs でございます; “the supplies are on the third floor” uses にございます for location.

How to form it

Polite baseline: です / ます
学生です／行きます／読みました · Politeness toward the listener. These do not by themselves say that the actor is a teacher, customer, or the speaker.
Noun + でございます
新製品でございます · A formal counterpart of 新製品です. Keep で between the identifying noun and ございます.
Inanimate existence: あります → ございます
用品は3階にございます · The supplies are on the third floor. に marks where the things are; do not replace this with でございます after the location unless you intend a different identification structure.
Past and negative forms
ございました／ございません／ございませんでした · Was / is not / was not there. For noun identity: 新製品でございました, 新製品ではございません.
People’s presence uses いる-based forms
私はおります／先生はいらっしゃいます · Compare the inanimate milk or supplies, which use ございます. First choose ある or いる, then the appropriate form for the relationship.
Do not attach it to every dictionary form
説明いたします · “I will explain” formally uses a verb with its own ending. Do not create 説明するでございます by treating でございます as a universal polite suffix.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

これはでございます。

This is a new product.

Clue The clerk identifies what this item is.

How to read this example

新製品 is a noun followed by でございます, a formal version of 新製品です. This is classification, not a location or someone’s action.

Example 02

パソコンは3にございます。

Computer supplies are on the third floor.

Clue The clerk tells the listener where the supplies are.

How to read this example

3階に marks location. ございます replaces あります for these inanimate goods. Do not use おります merely because the shop clerk is a person; the supplies are what the sentence locates.

Do not confuse it with…

Identification ↔ location
受付でございます / 受付にございます · “This is the reception desk” / “It is at reception.” The particle changes the relationship, even though ございます looks the same.
Things exist ↔ people are present
おります corresponds to いる on the speaker’s side. ございます corresponds to ある. Milk and computer supplies are inanimate, so a person’s polite-presence verb is not a substitute.
Polite identity ↔ respectful identity
先生でいらっしゃいます honors the person described as a teacher. これは新製品でございます politely identifies a product. The two are not interchangeable “fancyです.”
Formal ending ↔ grammar relationship · Lesson 11
確認するようにしています / 確認するようにしております retain the effort or routine meaning. The formal ending changes register, not the basic grammar relationship.

JLPT approach

  1. Reduce the sentence to だ, ある, or いる. Is it “is a product,” “a product exists there,” or “a person is there”? This catches many plausible-looking choices.
  2. Check the particle just before the blank. A location followed by に often needs an existence verb. A noun followed by で can introduce the formal identification でございます.
  3. Do not award points for length. いたします is formal, but means do; it cannot state where milk is located. Match meaning before politeness.
  4. Use the situation to choose register. ございます fits a formal shop explanation; あります remains ordinary polite Japanese. N3 practice is about choosing the appropriate form in context.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

お手洗いは奥にございます。

おてあらいはおくにございます。

The restroom is toward the back.

A useful service expression. The restroom is the thing located; 奥に states its location, and ございます makes the explanation formal.
