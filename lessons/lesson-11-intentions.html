1. ～ことにする

Make a decision, or keep a decision as a personal rule

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～ことにする presents an action as a decision the subject makes. 買うことにした means “I decided to buy it.” The deciding is completed; the purchase itself may still be ahead.

ことにする can announce a decision being made now, as when the father chooses to cancel his golf plans. ことにしている presents an adopted decision or personal policy that remains in force, such as choosing not to let a manager’s harsh words bother you.

Do not reduce this to “a one-time action.” A person can decide on a repeated habit. The useful distinction is the focus on choosing or maintaining a decision, rather than on the ongoing effort needed to carry it out.

Think of it this wayAsk “What choice has the person made?” Then distinguish the time of the decision from whether the chosen action has actually happened.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + ことにする
買うことにする／断ることにする · decide to buy / turn down or cancel. Use the plain action before こと, not a ます-form.
Negative action + ことにする
言わないことにする／行かないことにした · decide not to speak / decided not to go. The inner ない describes the chosen nonaction; the decision itself is affirmative.
A continuing action or state + ことにする
黙っていることにした · decided to keep quiet. The book includes ている before ことにする; do not strip it away when it carries the intended continuing state.
Past decision: ことにした
買うことにした · decided to buy. した is the past of the deciding expression; it does not turn 買う into an already completed purchase.
Standing rule: ことにしている
毎日読むことにしている／気にしないことにしている · make a practice of reading daily / make it a rule not to worry about it. The decision continues to guide behavior.
Polite versions
ことにします／ことにしました／ことにしています · decide / decided / follow a standing policy. Keep the chosen verb plain and make the final する polite.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

がこわれたので、のをことにした。

The refrigerator broke, so I decided to buy a new one.

Clue The refrigerator’s breakdown leads to a decision about a replacement.

How to read this example

こわれたので supplies the reason. 新しいの means a new one, with “refrigerator” understood. 買うことにした reports the decision, not necessarily an already completed purchase.

Example 02

とので、もでいることにした。

I would be scolded if I interfered, so I decided to say nothing and keep quiet.

Clue The speaker chooses to remain quiet to avoid being scolded.

How to read this example

口を出す means interfere or put in one’s opinion. 怒られる is passive: be scolded. 何も言わないで describes saying nothing, while 黙っていることにした makes keeping quiet the chosen course.

Example 03

「、のにディズニーランドにいってよ。」「よし、わかった。じゃ、とゴルフにはことにするよ。」

Daughter: Dad, take me to Disneyland next holiday. Father: All right. Then I will cancel my plans to play golf with my friends.

Clue The father announces a decision in response to his daughter’s request.

How to read this example

連れていってよ asks him to take her along. じゃ introduces the resulting choice. 断ることにするよ means he is deciding to cancel or back out of the golf arrangement; it does not report that he has already contacted his friends.

Example 04

のはいつもとてもきびしいが、わたしはことにしている。

The manager's words are always harsh, but I make it a rule not to let them bother me.

Clue Not taking the manager’s harsh words to heart is a standing personal policy.

How to read this example

気にしない is the chosen response. ことにしている says the policy remains in force. It does not prove the speaker never feels hurt; it describes how they choose to handle the remarks.

Do not confuse it with…

Personal policy ↔ continuing effort
毎日歩くようにしている emphasizes trying to walk daily. 毎日歩くことにしている presents daily walking as an adopted practice. Both can describe a habit; they differ in focus, not simply frequency.
Make a decision ↔ report an arrangement
来月引っ越すことにした emphasizes deciding to move. 来月引っ越すことになった presents the move as an arrangement or outcome. ことになる does not always mean the speaker had no role in the decision.
Decide ↔ attempt
買おうとする describes trying or moving toward buying. 買うことにする settles on buying as a choice. Neither alone proves that the purchase was completed.
Decision ↔ written instruction · Lesson 10
読むこと at the end of a notice tells readers to read. 読むことにする describes deciding to read. The words after こと change the job of the expression.
Decision ↔ future intention
行くつもりだ in its familiar planning use states an intention already held. 行くことにする can announce settling on that choice now. This lesson’s main つもり examples use a different, self-assessment meaning.

JLPT approach

  1. Do not confuse a decision with its result. A broken refrigerator explains deciding to buy a replacement; the sentence need not say one has already been bought.
  2. Read the final ending. ことにした reports a decision; ことにしている presents a continuing personal policy.
  3. Keep the negative inside the choice. 行かないことにした means “decided not to go,” not “did not decide whether to go.”

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

寝る前に、明日の予定を確認することにしています。

ねるまえに、あしたのよていをかくにんすることにしています。

I make it a rule to check tomorrow’s schedule before going to bed.

The repeated action is presented as a personal practice the speaker has adopted.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 11, printed pages 50–51.

2. ～ようにする

Make an effort or take care to do something

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～ようにする means to make an effort, take care, or arrange one’s behavior so that something happens or is avoided. The focus is on managing conduct: using resources carefully, coming home earlier, or avoiding lateness.

～ようにしている emphasizes continuing that effort. It can describe a routine, but does not promise success on every occasion. なるべく in the cooking example explicitly means “as much as possible.”

The form is not limited to long-term habits. 遅れないようにしてください can concern one upcoming gathering. It asks the listener to take care not to be late; it is not merely the speaker hoping that punctuality happens by chance.

Think of it this wayAsk “What behavior is the person trying to manage?” Look for effort, precautions, or continued attention rather than only the moment of deciding.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + ようにする
早く帰るようにする／大切に使うようにする · make an effort to return early / take care to use something responsibly. The action stays in plain form.
Negative verb + ようにする
遅れないようにする／忘れないようにする · take care not to be late / forget. Put the negative before ように to identify what is to be avoided.
Continuing effort: ようにしている
自分で料理を作るようにしている · try to cook for oneself regularly. The effort is being maintained; individual occasions can still vary.
Encourage a group: ようにしましょう
大切に使うようにしましょう · let us make an effort to use it carefully. しましょう is the polite volitional of する.
Ask for care: ようにしてください
遅れないようにしてください · please make sure not to be late. This is a request to manage behavior, not a report that punctuality is guaranteed.
Negative effort versus effort to avoid
忘れないようにする means take steps not to forget. 忘れるようにしない would negate trying to forget, which is a different statement. Keep the intended negative with the right verb.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

やはにようにしましょう。

Let us try to use water and electricity carefully.

Clue The speaker encourages everyone to use resources with care.

How to read this example

大切に modifies 使う: use responsibly or avoid waste. ようにしましょう proposes shared effort. It does not mean that water and electricity merely seem valuable.

Example 02

「あなたのがから、たちがさびしがっているわよ。」「そうか。これからはもっとようにするよ。」

Wife: The children miss you because you come home late every day. Husband: I see. From now on I will try to come home earlier.

Clue The husband responds to a recurring problem with a commitment to change his behavior.

How to read this example

毎日遅い explains why the children miss him. これからは marks “from now on.” 帰るようにする focuses on trying to come home earlier, without promising that no exception will ever occur.

Example 03

にようにしてください。

Please make sure you arrive by the appointed gathering time.

Clue The listener is asked to take precautions against lateness.

How to read this example

遅れない is the desired nonoccurrence before ように. してください requests that the listener manage their behavior accordingly. 集合時間 is the appointed gathering time, not necessarily a business meeting.

Example 04

わたしはなるべくでをようにしている。

I try to cook and eat my own meals whenever possible.

Clue The cooking routine is maintained as far as circumstances allow.

How to read this example

なるべく limits the claim to “as much as possible.” 自分で means by oneself, and 作って食べる connects preparing and eating the food. ようにしている describes continuing effort, not an absolute every-meal rule.

Do not confuse it with…

Effort ↔ personal rule
気にしないことにしている presents an adopted policy. 気にしないようにしている emphasizes trying not to let something bother you. They can overlap in a real situation.
Effort ↔ resulting change
早く起きるようにしている describes an effort to get up early. 早く起きられるようになった describes becoming able to get up early. ようにする manages behavior; ようになる reports a change.
Habitual effort ↔ an attempt at this moment
ドアを開けようとしている describes an attempt to open a door. ドアを閉めるようにしている can describe taking care to close it as a practice. The verb forms and particles differ.
Taking care ↔ hoping · Lesson 9
遅れないといい can hope that lateness will not occur. 遅れないようにする says the person takes care to prevent it. The hope alone does not state that precautionary effort.
Careful conduct ↔ looking like something
眠いようだ means someone seems sleepy. ようだ and the fixed pattern ようにする do different jobs. Do not translate every よう as “seems.”

JLPT approach

  1. Use adverbs as clues. なるべく often signals best effort, while これからは can introduce a new commitment.
  2. Do not require perfect success. Making an effort to cook does not mean every meal is homemade.
  3. Keep one-time precautions possible. A request to avoid lateness can apply to one meeting; ようにする is not restricted to repeated actions.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

出かける前に、忘れ物がないか確認するようにしています。

でかけるまえに、わすれものがないかかくにんするようにしています。

I try to check that I have not forgotten anything before going out.

The speaker describes an ongoing precaution, rather than guaranteeing that forgetting never happens.

3. ～（よ）うとする

Try to act, be on the point of acting, or show no willingness to act

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Verb volitional form + とする describes an attempt or the point just before an action starts. A child reaches for a toy; someone tries to fall asleep; a departure is about to begin when rain interrupts.

The pattern does not establish success or failure by itself. Read the rest of the sentence: 眠れなかった explicitly gives failure to sleep, while the reaching child’s result is not stated. With とき or たら, the sentence often focuses on the moment of starting or attempting.

～ようとしない describes someone showing no intention or willingness to act. In the book, it is used about other people: the husband will not go and the father will not listen. This differs from being physically unable to do something. The negative wording often carries frustration or criticism.

Think of it this wayChoose among “is trying,” “was just about to,” and “will not even try” using the situation. Then check separately whether the action was completed.

How to form it

Group 1 volitional: final sound → お-row + う
取る → 取ろう；眠る → 眠ろう；聞く → 聞こう · then add とする. This is not the dictionary form or the imperative.
Group 2 volitional: drop る + よう
出る → 出よう；持ち上げる → 持ち上げよう · 出ようとする / 持ち上げようとする. Learn the verb group before selecting the ending.
Irregular volitional forms
する → しよう；来る → 来よう（こよう） · 説明しようとする means try to explain. The reading of 来よう is こよう.
An attempt in progress
取ろうとしている · is trying to take or reach for it. The ongoing form describes the attempt, not necessarily having the object already.
At the moment of attempting
出ようとしたとき／持ち上げようとしたら · just as one was about to leave / when one tried to lift it. The following event tells you what happened at that point.
No willingness: volitional + としない
行こうとしない／聞こうとしなかった · will not try to go / would not listen. The negative is on する, rather than a negative potential of the underlying verb.
Emphatic は after と
聞こうとはしなかった · would not make the effort to listen. は emphasizes or contrasts the rejected attempt; it does not turn the meaning positive.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

A. Attempt — trying to make an action happen

Example 01

あのはいっしょうけんめい、テーブルののおもちゃをとしている。

That child is stretching out with all their might, trying to take the toy on the table.

Clue The reaching movement shows an attempt to get the toy.

How to read this example

手を伸ばして describes the child stretching out a hand. 取る → 取ろうとしている gives an ongoing attempt. The sentence does not say whether the child actually gets the toy.

Example 02

きのうのはとしてもなかなか。

Last night, even when I tried to sleep, I could not fall asleep easily.

Clue The attempt and the ability to sleep have different forms.

How to read this example

眠ろうとしても means “even though I tried to sleep.” 眠れなかった is negative past potential: could not sleep. なかなか with the negative emphasizes the difficulty; the sentence explicitly contrasts effort with lack of success.

B. Starting point — something happens as the action begins

Example 03

をとしたとき、が。

Just as I was about to leave the house, it suddenly began to pour.

Clue Rain begins at the point when the speaker is about to leave.

How to read this example

出る → 出ようとした. とき locates the sudden rainfall at that attempted starting point. The sentence does not say whether the speaker later went out anyway.

Example 04

をとしたら、がなってしまった。

When I tried to lift the heavy luggage, my lower back started to hurt.

Clue The attempted lift is followed by an unwanted physical result.

How to read this example

持ち上げようとしたら locates the onset of pain at the attempted lift. 痛くなってしまった presents the result as unwelcome. It does not establish that the luggage was fully lifted.

C. Unwillingness — someone will not make the effort

Example 05

いくらもはへとしない。

No matter how much I urge him, my husband will not try to go to the hospital.

Clue The husband shows no willingness to go despite repeated encouragement.

How to read this example

いくら勧めても means “no matter how much I encourage him.” 行こうとしない rejects making the effort. The wording does not establish a lack of transport, money, or physical ability.

Example 06

にをしようとしたが、はをとはしなかった。

I tried to explain the circumstances to my father, but he would not listen.

Clue The speaker tries to explain, while the father refuses to listen.

How to read this example

説明しようとした is the speaker’s attempted action. 聞こうとはしなかった describes the father’s lack of willingness, with は adding emphasis. The two clauses have different actors; the father is not described as unable to hear.

Do not confuse it with…

Intend ↔ attempt
行こうと思っている holds an intention to go. 行こうとしている describes trying or being about to go. A plan does not by itself show that an attempt is underway.
Try to achieve ↔ try as an experiment
ドアを開けようとする describes an attempt to open it. ドアを開けてみる means try opening it to see what happens. てみる emphasizes trying out an action; とする emphasizes attempting or beginning it.
Volitional + とする ↔ dictionary form + ようにする
帰ろうとする tries or is about to return. 早く帰るようにする makes an effort to return early. Do not swap と and に while leaving the verb unchanged.
Unwillingness ↔ inability
聞こうとしない shows no willingness to listen. 聞けない says cannot listen or hear, depending on context. A refusal and a limitation in ability are different explanations.
About to ↔ just before · Lesson 1
出るところだ places the person just before departure. 出ようとしたとき can frame that moment from the attempted start and introduce an interruption.
An approaching event without a deliberate actor
日が沈もうとしている can describe the sun being about to set. In this broader use, “about to” is appropriate; do not invent an intention or effort for the sun.

JLPT approach

  1. Build the volitional first. 説明する → 説明しよう → 説明しようとする. The よう in しよう is part of the verb form, not the separate よう of ようにする.
  2. Look for evidence of outcome. とした alone does not mean succeeded. Pain, rain, or an explicit negative result may interrupt the action.
  3. Separate unwillingness from inability. いくら勧めても plus 行こうとしない signals resistance to encouragement, not inability to travel.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

電話をかけようとしたとき、相手から電話が来ました。

でんわをかけようとしたとき、あいてからでんわがきました。

Just as I was about to call, the other person called me.

The attempt to begin the call provides the timing for the incoming call.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 11, printed pages 50–51.

4. ～つもり

“I thought I had…” / “I consider myself…” — a view of oneself

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

In this lesson, つもり expresses how a person regards their own state or action. Someone still thinks of themselves as young, meant a remark as a joke, sees themselves as senior, or considers the cleaning done.

This differs from the familiar future-plan use in 行くつもりだ, “I intend to go.” 掃除したつもり is an assessment of an action treated as done, not a plan to clean later. The context may show that another person disputes the result.

The person’s view and other people’s assessment can differ, but つもり does not automatically mean deliberate lying or foolishness. It can express a sincere self-image or intended meaning. In a challenging question, however, it can sound critical: “Do you really call that cleaned?”

Think of it this wayMake two short notes: “The person’s own view: …” and “The result or other person’s view: …”. Read が, のに, 本当は, and the question ending for the contrast.

How to form it

Verb た-form + つもり
言ったつもり／掃除したつもり · meant it that way / consider the cleaning done. In this use, the person evaluates an action or result, rather than announcing a future plan.
Verb ている + つもり
気をつけているつもり · believe oneself to be taking care. The ongoing behavior is the person’s own assessment; others may disagree.
い-adjective + つもり
若いつもり · think of oneself as young. Keep い; do not add だ or な.
な-adjective + なつもり
親切なつもり · think of oneself as kind / mean to be kind in context. The adjective modifies つもり with な.
Noun + のつもり
先輩のつもり · regard oneself as the senior person. Use の, not だ, before つもり.
Before another noun
言ったつもりの言葉 · words one meant to say in a particular way. つもり takes の before 言葉 in this noun-modifying expression.
Compare the time of the action and the intention
掃除するつもりだった = had intended to clean. 掃除したつもりだった = had thought one had cleaned. Moving the past changes the meaning; both are different from simply confirming 掃除した.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

わたしは77ですが、まだまだつもりです。

I am 77 this year, but I still think of myself as young.

Clue The speaker’s feeling of youth is set against their stated age.

How to read this example

今年77歳 gives the factual age. まだまだ若いつもり describes the continuing self-image. It does not mean planning to become chronologically young or denying the stated age.

Example 02

じょうだんでつもりのだったが、はようなをした。

I meant the words as a joke, but he looked angry.

Clue The intended joke and the listener’s reaction do not match.

How to read this example

じょうだんで言ったつもり describes the way the speaker meant the words. の links that assessment to 言葉. 怒ったような顔をした reports an angry-looking expression; the sentence does not prove the listener accepted the remark as a joke.

Example 03

にしたはのつもりらしいが、はわたしのがなのだ。

Yumi, who joined the company first, seems to think she is my senior, but I am actually older.

Clue Yumi’s workplace seniority and the narrator’s age are different reference points.

How to read this example

先に入社した explains Yumi’s earlier entry into the company. 先輩のつもりらしい reports how she seems to position herself. 本当はわたしの方が年上 expresses the narrator’s age-based objection, not proof that her workplace seniority is false.

Example 04

こんなにのに、それでもしたつもりですか。

With it still this dirty, do you really think you have cleaned it properly?

Clue Visible dirt makes the speaker challenge the other person’s assessment.

How to read this example

こんなに汚いのに gives the evidence against treating the cleaning as satisfactory. それでも adds “even so.” 掃除したつもりですか asks whether the person really considers the task done, not whether they intend to clean later.

Do not confuse it with…

Self-assessment ↔ future plan
若いつもりだ／掃除したつもりだ give self-assessments. 行くつもりだ and 行かないつもりだ give future intentions. The book separates this lesson’s self-assessment use from the basic dictionary/ない-form planning pattern.
My own view ↔ another person’s apparent view · Lesson 7
先輩のつもりらしい attributes the self-image to Yumi, while らしい marks the narrator’s impression or information about her stance. Reported information and someone’s own belief are different layers.
Thought it was done ↔ tried to do it
掃除しようとした reports an attempt. 掃除したつもり describes regarding the action as done. Neither alone guarantees that the room meets someone else’s standard of cleanliness.
Self-image ↔ admitting a limited result · Lesson 8
掃除したことはしたが admits cleaning and then qualifies it. 掃除したつもり focuses on the person’s own understanding of what they did.
Different standards of seniority
先輩 can refer to having joined a workplace earlier, while 年上 means older in age. In the book’s example, the narrator resists Yumi’s stance by referring to age. Being older does not automatically erase someone else’s longer workplace tenure.

JLPT approach

  1. Look before つもり. A past action, ongoing state, adjective, or noun can signal self-assessment rather than a future plan.
  2. Do not ignore the reaction. An angry face contrasts with the intended joke; visible dirt challenges the claim of adequate cleaning.
  3. Keep the viewpoints separate. The speaker may report another person’s self-image without agreeing with it. Do not turn the narrator’s opinion into an objective fact.
  4. Read questions for tone. それでも掃除したつもりですか challenges the adequacy of the cleaning; it is not politely asking about a future cleaning schedule.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

丁寧に説明したつもりでしたが、うまく伝わっていませんでした。

ていねいにせつめいしたつもりでしたが、うまくつたわっていませんでした。

I thought I had explained it carefully, but I had not got the message across well.

The speaker contrasts their own assessment of the explanation with the communication result.
