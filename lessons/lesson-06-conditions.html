1. ～（の）なら…

Respond to a situation: “If that is the case…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A なら B means “If A is the case, then B.” In this lesson, the speaker takes information from the conversation or situation and responds with advice, a request, an offer, an intention, or a judgment.

Someone no longer needs a box, so you ask for it. Tomorrow’s forecast is rain, so you judge cycling unlikely. A finished book becomes available to borrow. The first part is the premise for the response.

なら does not require A to happen before B. If someone plans to travel, you can suggest buying a ticket beforehand. The speaker responds to the plan rather than waiting for the journey to finish.

Think of it this wayAsk “What information is the speaker responding to?” Then check whether the second clause is an appropriate response to that information.

How to form it

Plain verb + なら／のなら
使わないなら／読み終わったのなら · if you will not use it / if you have finished reading it. Use the plain form appropriate to the premise.
い-adjective + なら／のなら
高いなら／高いのなら · if it is expensive. The い stays; do not add な.
Noun or な-adjective + なら
雨なら／暇なら · if it is rain / if you are free. Drop present affirmative だ before なら.
With explanatory の: なのなら
雨なのなら／暇なのなら are fuller ways of presenting “if that is the situation.” For the book’s basic noun and な-adjective pattern, learn 雨なら and 暇なら first; do not form 雨のなら or 暇のなら.
Past or negative premise
学生だったなら／行かないなら／使わなかったのなら · if someone was a student / will not go / did not use it. Do not strip a needed past or negative meaning just to obtain dictionary form.
Conversational の → ん
行くのなら → 行くんなら · a spoken contraction. The book’s opening 使わないんですか is an explanatory question; the actual conditional comes in the next sentence.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

その、もうんですか。ならわたしにください。

Are you no longer using that box? If not, please give it to me.

Clue The request responds to the box no longer being needed.

How to read this example

The first sentence checks もう使わない, “not going to use it anymore.” 使わないなら accepts that as the premise for ください. It does not mean the other person is unable to use the box.

Example 02

ああ、あしたはか。ならサイクリングにはそうもないね。

Oh, it will rain tomorrow? If it rains, it looks as though we cannot go cycling.

Clue The forecast is the premise for a judgment about cycling.

How to read this example

雨 is a noun, so use 雨なら. 行けそうもない combines potential 行ける with a negative likelihood expression: it does not look possible to go. The rain is tomorrow, not an event already completed.

Example 03

その、しまったのならわたしにくれませんか。

If you have finished reading that book, could you lend it to me?

Clue The finished reading is treated as the condition for lending.

How to read this example

読んでしまった emphasizes finishing the book here. のなら attaches to that complete past clause. 貸してくれませんか requests a favor; it is not a report that the book has already been lent.

Do not confuse it with…

なら ↔ たら: premise versus event timing
日本へ行くなら、切符を買っておこう gives preparation for a planned trip. 日本へ着いたら、電話してください asks for a call after arrival. たら also has hypothetical uses, so “たら always describes a completed past event” is not a valid rule.
なら ↔ のだから · Lesson 5
のだから presents A as an established reason: “Since A is true…”. なら takes A as the condition for a response: “If that is the case…”. Both can support advice, but the speaker’s stance differs.
An accepted premise ↔ a rejected option · Lesson 3
くらいなら presents an unwanted action and a preferable alternative. Ordinary なら does not itself reject the first action.
なら ↔ ては／のでは
ては／のでは focuses on a troublesome consequence of the condition. なら can instead offer a solution: “If you have no time, I will clean up.”
Conditional form ↔ unreal situation
なら can also appear in counterfactual statements, such as imagining being a student when one is not. The context, not なら alone, tells you whether the condition is unreal.

JLPT approach

  1. Use both speakers’ lines. A hurried speaker establishes a lack of time. The reply should respond to that situation, not reverse it with “if you are not in a hurry.”
  2. Translate the negative accurately. 使わないなら means “if you will not use it.” A negative verb does not necessarily express inability.
  3. Keep the timing flexible. Advice prompted by a plan can happen before the planned event. Do not eliminate なら because the second action needs to happen first.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

その本を買うなら、この店がおすすめです。

そのほんをかうなら、このみせがおすすめです。

If you are going to buy that book, I recommend this shop.

The recommendation is useful before the purchase. なら responds to the buying plan.

2. ～ては…・～（の）では…

“With that condition, there will be a problem”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A ては B or A のでは B presents A as leading to an unfavorable result. The condition can be real, anticipated, or imagined. Visiting immediately after surgery may burden someone; an irresponsible attitude may alienate others; a late start may prevent completion.

The second part is a difficulty, negative judgment, or unwelcome consequence. It need not contain a negative verb: 迷惑だ and 困る express problems without ending in ない.

This pattern can function as a warning by explaining what would go wrong. That differs from directly granting permission, making an invitation, or stating a plan.

Think of it this wayComplete the thought “If things are like this, the problem is…”. Then look for the consequence, not merely another action someone could take.

How to form it

Verb て-form + は
行く → 行っては／飲む → 飲んでは · if someone goes / drinks. Verbs whose て-form ends in で naturally use では.
い-adjective: い → くては
悪い → 悪くては／高い → 高くては · if it is bad / expensive. いい uses よい for conjugation: よくては.
Noun or な-adjective + では
そんな態度では／不便では · with that attitude / if it is inconvenient. Do not attach ては directly to the noun.
A whole plain clause + のでは
今から始めるのでは／値段が高いのでは · if one starts only now / if the price is high. の packages the situation being evaluated.
Noun or な-adjective predicate + なのでは
初心者なのでは／危険なのでは · if someone is a beginner / if it is dangerous. Use な before explanatory の in the present affirmative.
Common spoken contractions
行っては → 行っちゃ；飲んでは → 飲んじゃ；それでは → それじゃ · recognize these when reading dialogue. Meaning still depends on the complete construction.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

さんはしたばかりだから、おにはかえってだろう。

Mr. Yamanaka has just had surgery, so visiting him would probably be more of a burden.

Clue The visit could create the opposite of its intended helpful effect.

How to read this example

手術したばかり explains why the timing matters. 行っては introduces the proposed visit as a condition. かえって means “instead / contrary to expectations”; 迷惑だろう predicts that it would probably be a burden.

Example 02

そんななではみんなにきらわれますよ。

With such an irresponsible attitude, everyone will dislike you.

Clue The attitude is presented as leading to others’ dislike.

How to read this example

無責任な describes 態度, the noun directly before では. きらわれます is passive: one will be disliked. よ gives the statement a warning tone.

Example 03

からをのではには。

If construction starts only now, the house will not be finished within the year.

Clue Starting only now is the obstacle to finishing within the year.

How to read this example

Bracket ［今から家を建て始める］のでは. 年内には sets the deadline. でき上がらない means it will not be completed; the statement is not asking someone to start construction.

Do not confuse it with…

Bad consequence ↔ a helpful response
体調が悪くては、仕事が進まない explains an obstacle to progress. 体調が悪いなら、休んでもいい responds with permission to rest. Compare なら.
ては ↔ ても
高くては買えない = “If it is expensive, I cannot buy it.” 高くても買う = “Even if it is expensive, I will buy it.” は presents the condition as an obstacle; も allows the result despite it.
This use ↔ the familiar prohibition
入ってはいけない says entering is not allowed. It belongs to the familiar てはいけない prohibition pattern. In this lesson, a wider range of troublesome consequences can follow ては.
This use ↔ repeated actions
書いては消す means “write and erase repeatedly.” That is another use of ては, not automatically an unwanted-condition statement. Identify what follows.
のでは ↔ のではないか
雨なのではないか can mean “Could it be raining?” as a conjecture. Do not identify every のでは as this lesson’s conditional; read the rest of the ending.

JLPT approach

  1. Check meaning rather than counting ない. 迷惑だ, 無理だ, and 困る can all supply an unwelcome evaluation.
  2. Separate a warning from an instruction. “Work probably will not progress” reports the consequence. “You may rest” gives permission; it calls for a different connection in the textbook item.
  3. Do not turn a tendency into certainty. だろう can make the consequence probable. The pattern frames a problem; it does not prove that the outcome must occur in every real-world case.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

今からでは、間に合いません。

いまからでは、まにあいません。

Starting now, we will not make it in time.

今から names the starting point. では evaluates that late start as insufficient for being on time.

3. ～さえ～ば…・～さえ～なら…

“This one condition is enough”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A さえ…ば／なら B means “As long as A…, B” or “If only this condition is met, B.” The speaker singles out a condition and presents it as sufficient for the result. Other concerns are set aside for the purpose of this statement.

Manga is enough to keep Taro from boredom. Being healthy is presented as enough to take on challenges. A simple apology is expected to be enough for forgiveness.

This is the speaker’s assessment, not an objective guarantee. The book itself uses ようだ and だろう. “If A, that is enough for B” also does not prove that B is impossible by any other route.

Think of it this wayAsk “What does the speaker treat as all that is needed?” Then check whether the result says that condition is sufficient.

How to form it

Noun + さえ + verb ば-form
お金さえあれば／ハンドルさえ直せば · as long as there is money / if the handlebars are repaired. さえ highlights the noun; the later predicate carries the condition.
An ongoing state + ば
漫画さえ読んでいれば · as long as someone is reading manga. 読んでいる → 読んでいれば; this is not itself the potential form “can read.”
Noun + さえ + い-adjective ければ
値段さえ安ければ · as long as the price is low. 安い → 安ければ.
Noun + さえ + な-adjective／noun + なら
体さえ丈夫なら／明日さえ休みなら · as long as one is healthy / tomorrow is a day off. 丈夫 is a な-adjective and takes なら.
Verb ます-stem + さえすれば
言う → 言います → 言いさえすれば；確認する → 確認しさえすれば · if one only says it / checks it. Use the stem before さえ, followed by すれば.
Basic ば-form reminders
直す → 直せば；読む → 読めば；見る → 見れば；する → すれば；来る → 来れば（くれば） · Group 1 changes the final sound to the え-row + ば; Group 2 drops る and adds れば.
What happens to the particle before さえ?

In the book’s patterns, さえ can replace が or を: お金がある → お金さえあれば；漫画を読む → 漫画さえ読んでいれば. Some other particles remain when needed, as in ここにさえいれば (“as long as you are here”). Preserve the noun’s role rather than deleting every particle mechanically.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

はさえいればしないようだ。

As long as Taro is reading manga, he seems not to get bored.

Clue Reading manga is presented as enough to keep Taro occupied.

How to read this example

漫画さえ highlights the activity’s object. 読んでいれば describes the continuing reading condition; 退屈しない is the result. ようだ marks this as the speaker’s impression.

Example 02

さえならどんなことにもできる。

As long as one is physically healthy, one can take on any challenge.

Clue The speaker places the emphasis on physical health.

How to read this example

体さえ highlights the body or health condition. 丈夫なら is the な-adjective conditional. どんなことにも挑戦できる is the speaker’s broad encouraging claim, not a factual guarantee about every possible challenge.

Example 03

「ごめんなさい。」とさえすれば、はくれるだろう。

If you just say 'I'm sorry,' the other person will probably forgive you.

Clue Saying a brief apology is presented as sufficient for forgiveness.

How to read this example

言う becomes the ます-stem 言い before さえすれば. 一言 here means a brief remark, not a strict count of one Japanese word. 許してくれるだろう means the other person will probably forgive; certainty is not claimed.

Do not confuse it with…

Sufficient condition ↔ ordinary condition
お金があれば says “if there is money.” さえ adds that the speaker treats having money as enough for the stated result.
さえ…ば ↔ だけ
これだけで十分だ can mean “this alone is enough,” but だけ by itself simply limits quantity or scope. さえ…ば explicitly combines focus with a condition.
さえ…ば ↔ たとえ…ても
たとえ…ても says a condition does not change the conclusion. さえ…ば instead makes the highlighted condition sufficient to obtain the result.
“As long as” ↔ a time period · Lesson 1
The English phrase “as long as” may describe duration, but here it normally expresses sufficiency. 間 describes a period, without saying that one condition solves the problem.
Conditional さえ ↔ “even”
名前さえ知らない means “I do not even know the name.” There is no ば／なら condition in that sentence. Read the full construction instead of translating every さえ as “as long as.”

JLPT approach

  1. Read the condition and result as a pair. If repairing the handlebars is all that is needed, a result such as “still usable” completes the intended claim.
  2. Locate the highlighted unit. 漫画さえ focuses the thing being read; 言いさえすれば focuses doing the action. The attachment forms differ.
  3. Do not confuse “enough” with “the only possible way.” The speaker may simplify or emphasize. Keep qualifiers such as ようだ and だろう in the English meaning.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

時間さえあれば、手伝えます。

じかんさえあれば、てつだえます。

As long as I have time, I can help.

Time is presented as the condition that would be enough to make helping possible.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 6, printed pages 32–33.

4. たとえ～ても…・たとえ～でも…

“Even under that condition, the conclusion stays the same”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

たとえ A ても／でも B means “Even if A, B.” The speaker considers a condition that might seem to change the result, then says that the stated conclusion still holds.

Opposition does not remove the singer’s ambition; a high price does not remove a work-related obligation; inconvenience does not change the recommendation in the book’s example. The second clause can express a wish, a decision, advice, an obligation, or a judgment.

たとえ emphasizes the hypothetical case. It is often difficult or extreme, but it need not be impossible or objectively the worst imaginable situation. The relationship is what matters: A does not overturn B.

Think of it this wayTry “Even assuming A, that would not change B.” If A simply causes B, you probably need an ordinary condition or reason instead.

How to form it

Verb て-form + も
行っても／反対されても · even if one goes / is opposed. For the passive example, 反対する → 反対される → 反対されても.
い-adjective: い → くても
高い → 高くても；いい → よくても · even if expensive / good. Do not write 高いでも.
Noun or な-adjective + でも
雨でも／面倒でも · even if it rains / it is troublesome. Present だ becomes で before も.
A noun phrase with an adjective
いい案でも · even if it is a good proposal. でも follows the noun 案; this differs from adjective-only よくても.
Negative forms
行かなくても／高くなくても／学生でなくても · even if one does not go / it is not expensive / one is not a student. Preserve what is being conceded.
Emphasis with どんなに
たとえどんなに高くても · no matter how expensive it is. どんなに highlights degree; ても／でも still supplies the concessive connection.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

たとえのたちにどんなにされても、ぼくはプロのになりたい。

Even if everyone around me opposes it, I want to become a professional singer.

Clue The ambition remains despite any degree of opposition.

How to read this example

どんなに intensifies the opposition. 反対されても is passive plus ても: even if others oppose the speaker. なりたい expresses the wish to become a singer; it does not guarantee success.

Example 02

たとえても、になものはばならない。

Even if it is expensive, we must buy what is necessary for the job.

Clue Expense does not remove the stated obligation to buy necessary items.

How to read this example

高い changes to 高くても. 仕事に必要なもの is the complete object being discussed. 買わなければならない means one must buy it; the final clause is an obligation, not merely a preference.

Example 03

たとえでも、はほうがいいですよ。

Even if it is troublesome, you should have a health examination every year.

Clue The speaker maintains the recommendation despite inconvenience.

How to read this example

面倒 is a な-adjective and becomes 面倒でも. 受けたほうがいい gives advice in this book sentence. The た-form in the advice does not claim that the examination already took place.

Do not confuse it with…

Even if ↔ if
雨なら行かない makes rain the condition for not going. たとえ雨でも行く keeps going despite rain. Changing なら to でも changes the relationship.
Concession ↔ troublesome consequence
ては presents the condition as an obstacle leading to a bad result. ても says the conclusion remains despite the condition. The small は／も difference is meaningful.
Concession ↔ sufficient condition
さえ…ば makes one condition enough for a result. たとえ…ても does not say the condition is required or helpful; it considers it without letting it alter the conclusion.
Hypothetical “even if” ↔ factual “although”
ても without たとえ can be interpreted from context as “even if” or “even though.” たとえ explicitly asks the reader to consider a hypothetical case; do not assume A already happened.
Unchanged stance ↔ two aspects · Lesson 4
一方で and 反面 put two aspects alongside each other. たとえ…ても asks whether a condition would change the conclusion.

JLPT approach

  1. Match たとえ with the concessive ending. In these N3 exercises, look for ても or でも rather than choosing any form that translates as “if.”
  2. Do not require a positive final sentence. Even an excellent proposal may be impossible to carry out without support. A negative conclusion can remain true despite a favorable condition.
  3. Find the expectation being overcome. Distance might threaten a relationship, but “I will not forget you” states the promise that remains.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

たとえ失敗しても、もう一度やってみます。

たとえしっぱいしても、もういちどやってみます。

Even if I fail, I will try again.

Failure is considered as a possibility. The decision to make another attempt remains.

5. ～ば…のに／～たら…のに／～なら…のに

Imagine a different reality: regret, an unreal wish, or relief

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

These examples use conditions to imagine something different from reality. The speaker imagines having enough money and time, having overslept, or being eligible for a student discount.

Regret is only one possibility. The missed discount produces disappointment; the imagined missed flight produces relief because that bad outcome was avoided. The unreal wish can remain unfinished with んだけど….

のに is common, but not compulsory. It adds a sense that the imagined result contrasts with reality. The book’s first two examples do not contain のに. Read the surrounding situation to identify what is real and what is imagined.

Think of it this wayMake two short notes: “Reality: …” and “Imagined alternative: …”. Then decide whether the speaker regrets a lost possibility or feels relieved about an avoided problem.

How to form it

Verb ば-form
ある → あれば；する → すれば；行く → 行けば · if there were / if one did / if one went. These forms can express ordinary or unreal conditions; context decides.
Adjectives and nouns
安ければ／便利なら／学生なら · if it were cheaper / convenient / if one were a student. いい becomes よければ.
Negative conditions
行かなければ／高くなければ／学生でなければ · if one did not go / it were not expensive / one were not a student. Check whether the imagined condition is affirmative or negative.
たら: plain past form + ら
行ったら／遅かったら／静かだったら · formed from the plain past shape. This formation does not mean that every たら sentence refers to the past: 明日雨が降ったら concerns tomorrow.
An unreal past action: ～ていたら
寝坊していたら、乗れなかった · if I had overslept, I could not have boarded. The context identifies an alternative to what actually happened.
The imagined result + のに
学生なら、安く買えたのに · if I were a student, I could have bought it cheaply. Put のに after the imagined result, not directly after なら.
A wish left unfinished: ～んだけど…
お金があれば行くんだけど… · “If I had money, I would go, but…” The speaker leaves the limiting reality implied.
Past regret: ～ばよかった
もっと勉強すればよかった · “I should have studied more.” よかった evaluates the unrealized action as what would have been good.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

とひまがあればわたしもするんだけど……。

If I had money and free time, I would travel overseas too.

Clue The speaker imagines having enough money and free time for travel.

How to read this example

お金とひまがあれば supplies the desired circumstances. するんだけど… leaves the obstacle implied: the resources are not available in the needed combination. It does not necessarily mean the speaker has literally no money and no free time.

Example 02

もししていたらこのには。よかった。

If I had overslept, I could not have boarded this plane. I am glad I made it.

Clue The imagined bad outcome contrasts with the successful journey.

How to read this example

寝坊していたら imagines oversleeping. 乗れなかった is the negative past potential: could not have boarded. 間に合ってよかった confirms that the speaker actually made it in time, so the mood is relief rather than regret.

Example 03

ああ、だ。ならでチケットがのに……。

What a shame. If I were a student, I could have bought the ticket with a student discount.

Clue The speaker imagines eligibility for a missed discount.

How to read this example

学生なら is a noun condition. 買えた is the past potential of 買う: could have bought. 残念だ and のに show disappointment that the student-discount possibility did not apply.

Do not confuse it with…

Unreal past ↔ a future condition
明日遅れたら連絡します = “If I am late tomorrow, I will contact you.” 昨日もっと遅れていたら、間に合わなかった imagines a different past. Time words and the final clause determine the timeline.
ば…のに ↔ proportional ば…ほど · Lesson 2
読めば読むほど links changing degrees. A conditional followed by an imagined result and のに is a different construction; do not supply a repeated verb automatically.
Unreal identity ↔ conversational なら
なら can respond to information that might be true. In 学生なら…買えたのに, the complaint indicates the student condition is not met. なら itself does not mean “actually false.”
A future hope ↔ past regret · Lesson 9
～といい／～ばいい／～たらいい can express wishes in context. ～ばよかった looks back at a better action that was not taken. Check 今, あのとき, and 学生時代.
Sentence-final のに ↔ “although”
勉強したのに不合格だった contrasts actual study with an actual disappointing result. 勉強していたら合格できたのに imagines a different past. The whole sentence tells you which contrast is intended.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the reality clue. 残念だ points toward regret; 間に合ってよかった points toward relief. The conditional ending alone cannot supply that context.
  2. Track condition time and result time separately. “If I were a student, I could have bought the ticket cheaply” can combine a status with a missed purchase. Do not force every verb into the same tense.
  3. Keep uncertainty in the imagined result. ～たかもしれない means something might have happened. Do not translate an avoided possibility as a certain disaster.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

もっと早く準備しておけばよかった。

もっとはやくじゅんびしておけばよかった。

I should have prepared earlier.

しておく adds preparation in advance. しておけばよかった expresses regret that this preparation was not done earlier.
