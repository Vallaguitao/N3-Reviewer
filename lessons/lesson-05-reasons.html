1. ～ためだ・～ため（に）…

Present the cause of a result in a formal explanation

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A ため（に） B means “Because of A, B happened / is the case” in this lesson. A explains the result: a report contains mistakes because it was not checked; residents must travel because their village has no doctor.

This cause use is common in reports, notices, and formal explanations. It presents the reason in a relatively detached way. That tone does not prove the cause is certain: the book’s ためだろう means it is probably the cause.

Unwanted results are common, but ため does not itself assign blame. Start by identifying the cause-and-result relationship, rather than deciding only whether the outcome is good or bad.

Think of it this wayAsk “What caused this result?” If the first part instead names a goal someone is working toward, you are reading the purpose use of ために.

How to form it

Noun + のため（に）
出張のため／大雨のために · because of a business trip / heavy rain. Nouns need の; do not write 出張ため.
Verb in a plain form + ため（に）
医者がいないため／見直しをしなかったため · because there is no doctor / because the review was not done. Both present states and past actions can be causes.
い-adjective + ため（に）
忙しいため／忙しかったため · because someone is / was busy. Keep the plain adjective ending.
な-adjective: な + ため（に）
不便なため · because it is inconvenient. The present affirmative だ changes to な. Formal 不便であるため is also possible.
Formal noun predicate: であるため
休日であるため · because it is a holiday. The simpler noun connection is 休日のため.
Put the reason last: ～のは…ためだ
遅れたのは、道が混んでいたためだ · “The reason I was late is that the road was congested.” The result comes first; the cause comes after のは.
Optional に; polite and uncertain endings
ため／ために can both connect cause and result. ためです ends a polite explanation; ためだろう presents a likely cause. に does not determine whether the meaning is purpose or cause.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

にミスがのは、よくをしなかったためだろう。

The report had many mistakes, probably because it was not reviewed carefully.

Clue The mistakes are the result; insufficient checking is the likely cause.

How to read this example

Bracket ［報告書にミスが多かったのは］［よく見直しをしなかったためだろう］. しなかった is the past negative of する. だろう makes this an explanation offered as probable, not a confirmed fact.

Example 02

このにはがいないために、のときはとなりのまでばならない。

Because there is no doctor in this village, residents must go to the next town when they are ill.

Clue No local doctor creates the need to travel elsewhere.

How to read this example

いない is the negative of the existence verb いる. 病気のときは sets the situation when the inconvenience matters. 行かなければならない reports the resulting necessity; it is not the speaker inviting someone to travel.

Example 03

のため、のはさせていただきます。

Because of a business trip, I will be absent from tomorrow's meeting.

Clue The business trip explains the absence from the meeting.

How to read this example

出張 is a noun, so it takes のため. 欠席させていただきます is a deferential way to announce the absence. The speaker is not going on a trip in order to miss the meeting.

Do not confuse it with…

Cause ↔ purpose: the direction changes
留学するために、お金を貯める = save money in order to study abroad: studying abroad is the goal. お金が足りないため、留学を延期した = postpone study abroad because money is insufficient: the shortage is the cause.
ため ↔ から／ので
から gives a reason directly; ので often presents an explanation more gently or as background. Both can report factual reasons. Causal ため sounds more formal. Do not memorize “から = opinions only, ので = facts only.”
Formal cause ↔ credit or blame
おかげで／せいで adds the speaker’s evaluation of the cause. ため can state the cause without explicitly thanking or blaming it.
Reported consequence ↔ advice based on a reason
のだから is useful when the speaker treats A as established and draws advice, a judgment, or a request from it. The textbook’s causal ため pattern primarily explains a consequence.
The absence notice is still causal
In the third book example, 出張 is why the speaker will be absent. The formal announcement 欠席させていただきます does not turn absence into the purpose of the trip. Compare permission-related forms in Lesson 9.
Purpose versus cause: useful clues without absolute rules
Purpose: a goal followed by a chosen action
本を買うために貯金する = save to buy a book. The basic verb pattern uses a dictionary-form goal. Purpose can also use a noun: 健康のために = for one’s health.
Cause: a fact or situation followed by its consequence
A past or negative clause can help identify a cause, but verb form alone is not decisive. 医者がいないため is causal even without past tense. Prevention expressions such as 忘れないためにメモする can express purpose despite the negative verb.
Compare goal ように
日本語が話せるように練習する focuses on reaching an ability: practice so that one can speak Japanese. Do not simply replace every purpose ように with ために. Keep this separate from ようにする, making an effort or habit.

JLPT approach

  1. Locate the result and the cause. With ～のは…ためだ, the order is reversed. Do not assume the first clause always gives the reason.
  2. Check what the final clause is doing. A direct invitation, wish, or command is not the basic causal-ため continuation taught here. A formal notice of a resulting action can still fit, as the book’s absence example shows.
  3. Never use に as the deciding shortcut. Both purpose and cause can appear with or without に. Explain the relationship in the whole sentence.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

電車が遅れたため、到着が遅くなります。

でんしゃがおくれたため、とうちゃくがおそくなります。

Because the train was delayed, I will arrive late.

The past train delay explains the later arrival. A future consequence can still follow causal ため.

2. ～によって…・～による

Identify what caused a result—or the method that makes it possible

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

This topic has two uses: “caused by” and “by means of.” A material shortage causes production to stop; learning a language provides a way to understand people’s thinking.

Cause: why the result occurred
A shortage, accident, decision, or other factor leads to a result. The textbook emphasizes an event or change, rather than simply describing an unrelated state.
Means: how something is done
A method, process, or channel makes something possible. Learning through an activity and paying by a particular method fit this use.

Both uses often sound formal. Do not restrict によって to natural disasters, passive sentences, or things entirely beyond human control. First decide whether the noun names a cause or a method.

Think of it this wayTry two questions: “Why did it happen?” and “How was it done?” The answer tells you which use is active.

How to form it

Noun + によって + clause
材料不足によって、生産できなくなった · production became impossible because of a material shortage. No の goes between the noun and によって.
Action turned into a noun: V + ことによって
外国語を学ぶことによって · by learning a foreign language. こと packages the action as a method; do not attach によって directly to 学ぶ.
Noun + による + noun
ウイルスによる病気／カードによる支払い · illness caused by a virus / payment by card. による modifies the noun that follows, whether the meaning is cause or means.
Cause explanation: ～によるものだ
ウイルスによるものである · it is something caused by a virus. Here もの stands for the phenomenon being explained; である is a formal statement ending.
Formal connecting variant: により
工事により、道路が通れません · the road cannot be used because of construction. により can connect to a following clause; による is the form before a noun.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

うちのでは、によってたたみのはもうできなくなった。

At our factory, tatami production is no longer possible because of a material shortage.

Clue The shortage is the cause; loss of production capability is the result.

How to read this example

材料不足 is a noun and takes によって directly. もうできなくなった means production is no longer possible. うちの工場では provides the setting; the factory is not the grammatical cause.

Example 02

のインフルエンザは、までにないのウイルスによるものである。

This year's influenza is caused by a type of virus not seen before.

Clue による modifies もの and identifies the cause of the illness described.

How to read this example

今までにない型のウイルス is the complete cause phrase: a type of virus not seen before. である closes the formal explanation. This is the book’s example statement, not current information about influenza.

Example 03

をことによってそののたちのもことができる。

By learning a foreign language, one can also understand how people in that country think.

Clue Learning the language is the means of gaining cultural understanding.

How to read this example

学ぶこと turns learning into a noun-like unit before によって. 考え方 means a way of thinking. 知ることができる is “can come to know”; it states what becomes possible through learning.

Example 04

クレジットカードによるおをされるは、のをおください。

Those who wish to pay by credit card should read the following notes.

Clue The method phrase belongs to 支払い, inside a longer description of people.

How to read this example

Read ［クレジットカードによるお支払いを希望される方］は［注意をお読みください］. による marks the payment method. 希望される is respectful wording for customers’ wishes; the request at the end concerns reading the notes.

Do not confuse it with…

Cause／means ↔ variation · Lesson 2
人によって考え方が違う means ways of thinking vary by person. Person is the changing factor. Here, a shortage causes a result or learning serves as a method. The shared spelling does not guarantee the same meaning.
によって ↔ によると
天気予報によると means “according to the weather forecast”: it cites information. It does not mean the forecast caused the weather. Compare reported information in Lesson 7.
Means によって ↔ everyday で
カードで払う is the simple everyday way to say “pay by card.” カードによる支払い suits a formal noun phrase in a notice. Form and register matter; a method does not always require によって.
Cause によって ↔ ため
ため can connect a whole plain-form reason. によって takes a noun or a nominalized action. Both may express formal cause; neither automatically proves that a cause is uncontrollable.
Method ↔ purpose
練習することによって上達する says improvement comes through practice. 上達するために練習する says practice is done with the goal of improvement. Method and goal occupy different roles.

JLPT approach

  1. Inspect the next word. Before a noun such as 支払い or 被害, check による. Before a clause reporting the result, check によって or により.
  2. Keep the two textbook uses separate. The restrictions attached to the cause use are not a blanket ban on requests anywhere near による. The card example contains a method noun phrase inside a sentence that asks readers to do something.
  3. Read the verb accurately. できなくなった means “became unable to,” a change in possibility. It is not simply “did not do it,” and a passive ending is not required.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

工事による遅れが出ています。

こうじによるおくれがでています。

Construction is causing delays.

による modifies the following noun 遅れ, “delay.” The whole noun phrase is the subject of 出ています.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 5, printed pages 30–31.

3. ～から…・～ことから…

Trace a development to its starting point, or a conclusion to its evidence

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Nから can mean “arising from N”; Aことから means “from the fact that A.” The first part supplies a starting cause, an event that sets something in motion, or evidence supporting a conclusion.

A misunderstanding starts a deterioration in a friendship. Sitting beside someone creates the opportunity to become close. Similar faces provide evidence that two people are related.

This often sounds explanatory or analytical. With evidence, distinguish the cause of a situation from the reason you know about it: resemblance does not cause the family relationship; it supports recognizing it.

Think of it this wayAsk “Did A start this development, or did A help someone reach this conclusion?” Both can fit, but they explain different kinds of connection.

How to form it

Noun + から
誤解から／不注意から · from a misunderstanding / through carelessness. These nouns name a starting cause; do not add だ for this noun-source use.
Verb in a plain form + ことから
となりの席になったことから／顔が似ていることから · from becoming seat neighbors / from the fact that their faces resemble each other.
い-adjective + ことから
形が丸いことから · from its round shape. This fact could explain a nickname, classification, or judgment.
な-adjective: な／である + ことから
便利なことから／便利であることから · because of the fact that it is convenient. Use な or the formal である connection, not だことから.
Noun predicate: である + ことから
専門家であることから · from the fact that someone is an expert. Compare 専門家から聞いた, “heard from an expert,” where から simply marks the source of information.
Past noun or な-adjective predicates
学生だったことから／静かだったことから · from having been a student / having been quiet. Past だった stays intact before こと.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

わずかなからとのがなってしまった。

A slight misunderstanding caused my relationship with a friend to deteriorate.

Clue A small misunderstanding is the starting point of the deterioration.

How to read this example

わずかな describes 誤解: a slight misunderstanding. 友だちとの関係 means the relationship with a friend. 悪くなってしまった describes the unwelcome change that grew out of it.

Example 02

のでとなりのになったことから、わたしたちはしくなった。

We became close because we ended up sitting next to each other in Japanese class.

Clue Becoming seat neighbors gave the friendship its starting point.

How to read this example

Bracket ［日本語の授業でとなりの席になったこと］から. The event is presented as the reason they grew close, not simply a date that happened to come before the friendship.

Example 03

がよくことから、はだとすぐにわかった。

Because their faces look very similar, I immediately knew they were parent and child.

Clue Resembling faces are evidence for recognizing the relationship.

How to read this example

似ている describes an existing resemblance. 二人は親子だ is the conclusion placed before とわかった. 顔が似ている does not cause them to be parent and child; it is how the observer recognizes it.

Do not confuse it with…

Nから ↔ clause + から
誤解から関係が悪くなった traces deterioration to a misunderstanding. 誤解だから説明しよう gives an ordinary reason for a suggestion: “It is a misunderstanding, so let’s explain.” The extra だ changes the construction.
Evidence ↔ information source
声が震えていることから uses a trembling voice as evidence. 本人によると cites what the person says. One is observation-based reasoning; the other is reported information.
Starting event ↔ simple sequence · Lesson 1
隣の席になってから親しくなった places becoming close after becoming seat neighbors. ことから additionally presents the seating arrangement as the starting cause. Compare てから.
Evidence or origin ↔ accepted premise for advice
のだから often argues “Given this fact, here is what follows or what you should do.” ことから in this lesson describes a development or conclusion from a fact, rather than functioning as a casual reason for every request.
A naming clue is common, not compulsory
形が星に似ていることから「星パン」と呼ばれている is an added illustrative example: “It is called ‘star bread’ because its shape resembles a star.” Naming is one use; the book also shows friendship and recognition.

JLPT approach

  1. Translate こと as “the fact/event that…” here. It packages the preceding clause; it is not automatically the experience pattern ～たことがある.
  2. Find what the second clause explains. わかった can point to a conclusion; なった can describe a development; 呼ばれている can give a name. These are clues, not required endings.
  3. Use the book’s explanatory focus. For a direct invitation or instruction, ordinary から／ので or the appropriate のだから context is usually a better starting point than mechanically substituting ことから.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

小さな誤解から、けんかになってしまいました。

ちいさなごかいから、けんかになってしまいました。

A small misunderstanding ended up causing an argument.

から traces the argument to its starting cause. てしまいました presents the outcome as regrettable.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 5, printed pages 30–31.

4. ～おかげだ・～おかげで…／～せいだ・～せいで…

Add appreciation or blame to a cause

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

おかげで means “thanks to”; せいで means “because of,” with an unfavorable evaluation. These expressions do more than identify a cause: they show how the speaker feels about its effect.

おかげ: credit for a helpful result
A teacher helps you get a job, or fine weather helps construction finish early. The cause can be a person, an action, or a circumstance.
せい: responsibility for an unwelcome result
Being very busy leaves you tired. せい can blame circumstances or your own behavior; it does not always accuse another person.

Evaluate the result from the speaker’s point of view. A negative verb can still describe something welcome: “Thanks to the umbrella, I did not get wet.” Do not choose by spotting ない alone.

Think of it this wayAsk “Is the speaker giving this cause credit, or holding it responsible for a problem?” Then check the actual outcome.

How to form it

Noun + のおかげ／のせい
先生のおかげで／雨のせいで · thanks to the teacher / because of the rain. Nouns need の.
Verb in a plain form + おかげ／せい
手伝ってくれたおかげで／寝坊したせいで · thanks to someone’s help / because of oversleeping. Connect the clause directly, without adding の.
い-adjective + おかげ／せい
暖かいおかげで／忙しかったせいで · thanks to the warmth / because someone was busy. Keep the plain adjective form.
な-adjective + な
便利なおかげで／不便なせいで · thanks to the convenience / because of the inconvenience. Do not use だ directly before おかげ or せい.
Cause first: ～で + result
先生のおかげで合格できた · “Thanks to the teacher, I was able to pass.” おかげで connects to the result.
Cause last: ～のは…だ
合格できたのは先生のおかげです · “Being able to pass is thanks to my teacher.” 遅れたのは寝坊したせいだ assigns the delay to oversleeping.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

いいにがのはのおかげです。ありがとうございました。

It is thanks to my teacher that I obtained a job at a good company. Thank you very much.

Clue The job outcome is presented first, then the teacher receives the credit.

How to read this example

就職が決まった means the job placement was settled. のは marks that outcome as what is being explained. 先生 is a noun, so it takes のおかげ. The following thanks confirms sincere appreciation.

Example 02

のいいがおかげで、が。

Thanks to the continued fine weather, the construction finished early.

Clue The continued fine weather helped the work finish early.

How to read this example

Bracket ［天気のいい日が続いた］おかげで. 続いた is a past verb attached directly to おかげで. The good result is 工事が早く終わった; a person is not needed as the credited cause.

Example 03

せいで、かなりいる。

Because I have been busy lately, I am quite tired.

Clue Recent busyness is held responsible for current fatigue.

How to read this example

忙しい → 忙しかった is the past い-adjective form before せいで. 疲れている describes being tired now. The cause can be in the past while the resulting state continues.

Do not confuse it with…

Evaluation ↔ neutral cause
ため and causal によって can explain the same cause-and-result relation without explicitly crediting or blaming it. Formal wording does not automatically express gratitude.
Gratitude ↔ something given in return · Lesson 4
かわりに can describe an exchange: I help you in return for your teaching. おかげで credits your teaching for a result; it does not promise repayment.
A helpful result can use a negative verb
地図のおかげで迷わなかった = “Thanks to the map, I did not get lost.” 迷わなかった is negative in form, but avoiding getting lost is welcome.
Uncertain attribution: せいか
寝不足のせいか、集中できない = “Perhaps because of lack of sleep, I cannot concentrate.” か makes the suggested cause uncertain. Compare definite せいで.
A cause is not always a person
天気のおかげ／忙しさのせい can evaluate circumstances. せい can also acknowledge your own mistake; it does not require hostility toward someone else.
Expression note: おかげさまで and sarcastic おかげで

おかげさまで、元気です is a polite appreciative response meaning “I am well, thank you.” It can acknowledge support generally. おかげで may also be used sarcastically for a bad result, but that needs clear tone or context. For the book’s straightforward exercises, begin with genuine credit versus blame.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the result before choosing. Early completion and avoiding trouble usually support おかげ. Fatigue or failure usually supports せい, according to how the speaker presents it.
  2. Watch cause-last sentences. In ～のは先生のおかげです, the teacher is credited for the result mentioned before のは. Do not translate the order literally into confusing English.
  3. Separate an outcome from a wish. These patterns normally explain a result in this lesson. A wish that something will happen does not yet establish a result for which to give credit or blame.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

手伝ってくれたおかげで、間に合いました。

てつだってくれたおかげで、まにあいました。

Thanks to your help, I made it in time.

くれた presents the help as received from someone else. おかげで credits that help for the successful result.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 5, printed pages 30–31.

5. ～のだから…

“Given that this is true, here is what follows”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A のだから B means “Given that A is true, B follows / you should do B.” The speaker treats A as established, obvious, or something the listener should recognize, then draws a judgment, warning, request, intention, or other response from it.

“You are injured” supports a warning not to overdo things. “The world is large” supports the judgment that different customs are natural. A request or command is possible, but not required.

This can sound caring, encouraging, persuasive, or scolding. “Shared” describes how the speaker presents the reason; it does not guarantee that the listener already knew it or agrees with the conclusion.

Think of it this wayTry “Given that…” or “After all…”. Is the speaker using an accepted reason to make the next point feel justified?

How to form it

Verb in a plain form + のだから
勉強しているのだから／約束したのだから · given that someone is studying / has promised. Plain past and ongoing forms keep their endings.
い-adjective + のだから
広いのだから／忙しいのだから · given that it is large / someone is busy. Do not insert な after an い-adjective.
な-adjective: な + のだから
大切なのだから · given that it is important. 大切だ → 大切な + のだから.
Noun predicate: な + のだから
学生なのだから · given that someone is a student. This な connection differs from 学生のため or 学生であることから.
Conversational の → ん
しているのだから → しているんだから；学生なのだから → 学生なんだから · keep な before ん with a present noun or な-adjective predicate.
Polite wording: のですから／んですから
真剣にやっているんですから · “After all, I am taking this seriously.” です gives a polite ending, though the message can still be insistent.
Reason after the request
笑わないでください。真剣にやっているんですから。 gives the request first and the reason afterward. The listener supplies the connection between the two sentences.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

はのだから、いろいろながあるのはだ。

The world is large, so it is natural that many different customs exist.

Clue The size of the world supports a judgment about different customs.

How to read this example

広い is an い-adjective, so it takes のだから directly. いろいろな習慣があるのは当然だ means the existence of varied customs is natural. There is no request or command in this example.

Example 02

あなたはけがをしているんだから、をしてはいけませんよ。

You are injured, so you must not overdo it.

Clue The injury is treated as an established reason for the warning.

How to read this example

けがをしている describes the listener’s current condition. んだから is conversational のだから. 無理をしてはいけません warns against overdoing things; the explanation supports that warning.

Example 03

ください。にやっているんですから。

Please do not laugh. I am doing this seriously.

Clue The speaker gives the request first, then insists on the reason.

How to read this example

笑わないでください asks the listener not to laugh. 真剣にやっている means the speaker is doing it seriously. んですから is the polite conversational reason ending; the two sentences must be read together.

Do not confuse it with…

のだから ↔ ordinary から
忙しいから、帰ります simply gives a reason for leaving. のだから places more emphasis on accepting the premise and what follows from it. It is not merely a longer spelling of から.
Established reason ↔ conditional のなら · Lesson 6
もう読んだのなら、貸してください = “If you have already read it, please lend it to me.” のなら takes that information as a condition. のだから presents the reason as established: “Since you have already read it…”
Reason for a response ↔ formal causal report
ため primarily reports a cause and consequence in this lesson. のだから readily supports advice and requests. Still, the first book example ends in a judgment, not an instruction.
Reason for a judgment ↔ evidence for discovery
ことから can identify evidence from which someone discovered a fact. のだから uses a premise to justify a response or conclusion; it does not have to describe a discovery.
Known condition ↔ despite a condition · Lesson 4
けがをしているのだから休む uses injury as a reason to rest. けがをしているのに働く contrasts injury with working despite it. Compare the のに discussion in Lesson 4.

JLPT approach

  1. Do not demand a request ending. 当然だ, advice, a warning, or a personal intention may all follow, depending on the situation. Read what the speaker is doing with the reason.
  2. Distinguish なの from ordinary の. 学生 and 大切 need なのだから in the present affirmative; 広い and している take のだから directly.
  3. Listen for stance as well as politeness. んですから may sound insistent even with です. In your own speech, consider whether you mean to encourage someone, remind them, or press them to agree.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

せっかく来たんだから、楽しみましょう。

せっかくきたんだから、たのしみましょう。

Since we have made the effort to come, let’s enjoy ourselves.

せっかく highlights the opportunity or effort. Here んだから supports encouragement rather than criticism.
