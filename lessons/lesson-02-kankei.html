1. ～とおり／～どおり

Match an instruction, model, plan, or expectation

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A とおり B means that B matches A. A is the reference: directions someone gave you, a demonstration, written instructions, or an expected result. B is what you do or what actually happens.

Following a model
You follow the route you were told, or cook using the method in a book. The question is “Does the action match the instructions?”
Matching an expectation
A result turns out as expected or a plan proceeds as scheduled. No one has to deliberately copy anything: the result simply matches the reference.

Think of it this wayFind the reference first, then the thing that matches it. “Just as…” should connect the two naturally.

How to form it

Verb, dictionary form + とおり
言うとおりに · as someone says; use the plain verb, not 言います
Verb, た-form + とおり
聞いたとおりに · as I heard; 聞く → 聞いた → 聞いたとおりに
Ongoing or resulting state
説明しているとおりに／書いてあるとおりに · as someone is explaining / as it is written; these plain forms can also come before とおり
Noun + のとおり
説明のとおり／この絵のとおり · as explained / as shown in this picture
Common noun compounds + どおり
予定どおり／期待どおり／計画どおり · as scheduled / as expected / according to plan. Learn familiar compounds; do not attach どおり after a verb.
Choose the ending
とおりに + action · do it that way
とおりの + noun · a noun matching that reference
とおりだ／とおりです · it is just as described

に is sometimes omitted: 言われたとおりにした and 言われたとおりした can both describe doing as instructed. Look at the following word before deciding between に, の, and a sentence ending.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the choice.

Example 01

でもらったとおりにいったので、に。

Because I followed the directions I received at the police box, I reached the venue without getting lost.

Clue The received directions are the model; the walking follows them.

How to read this example

教えてもらった means the speaker received instructions. とおりに links those instructions to 歩いていった. 迷わず means “without getting lost”; it describes the successful result of following the route.

Example 02

だから、このにあるとおりのでみよう。

Since this is my first time making the dish, I will try using the method written in this book.

Clue とおりの comes before the noun やり方, “method.”

How to read this example

書いてある describes what is written in the book. The structure is ［書いてあるとおりの やり方］で作る: use a method that matches the written instructions. The final で belongs to やり方で, “using that method.”

Example 03

サッカーののはわたしたちのどおりだった。

The result of the soccer match was just as we had expected.

Clue The actual match result matches the expectation.

How to read this example

期待 is a noun, and 期待どおり is a familiar compound. だった makes the statement past: “was as expected.” This is a matching outcome, not necessarily an action someone intentionally copied.

Do not confuse it with…

とおり ↔ どおり
思ったとおり follows a verb. 予想どおり is a noun compound. 予想のとおり also works. The sound change is about the connection, not a change from “approximately” to “exactly.”
とおりに ↔ とおりの
教わったとおりに作る = “make it as taught”: に connects to an action. 教わったとおりの方法 = “the method I was taught”: の connects to the noun 方法. Check the word immediately after the blank.
とおり ↔ ように
Both can mean “as” when following instructions, and sometimes both fit. とおり emphasizes matching a specific model. ように has wider uses, including resemblance, goals, and efforts; it does not always mean “exactly as.”
Following a model ↔ making an effort · Lesson 11
ようにする means to try or make sure to do something. 毎日読むようにする is an effort to read daily; 書いてあるとおりにする is following what is written.
Agreement ↔ hearsay · Lesson 7
そのとおりです agrees that something is correct. ということだ can report information received from another source; reporting it does not itself express agreement.

JLPT approach

  1. Check both sides of the blank. A noun + の supports とおり; an established direct noun compound supports どおり. A verb before the blank takes とおり.
  2. Read “matches” even in a negative sentence. 思ったとおりにはいかない means “does not go as I expected.” は highlights that expected outcome for contrast; it does not create a new grammar family.
  3. Do not choose by past tense alone. 聞いた can refer to earlier instructions, while 書いてある describes instructions still written there. Choose the form that describes the reference.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

そのとおりです。

そのとおりです。

That’s exactly right.

A simple way to agree. The more respectful おっしゃるとおりです means “You are quite right / It is just as you say.” Compare the respectful verb おっしゃる in Lesson 12.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 2, printed pages 18–19.

2. ～によって／～によっては

Different cases, different results · A particular case

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

In this lesson, によって means “depending on” or “from one … to another.” The noun before it tells you what varies: the person, country, day, place, or circumstances. The result is not fixed across those cases.

によって · Describe variation
Compare across the cases: customs differ by country; people feel differently. Endings such as 違う, さまざまだ, and 変わる often express this variation.
によっては · Pick out a case
Focus on what happens in some cases or under certain conditions: on some days you get home very late. This can be a possibility or a known fact. The selected case does not have to be rare.

Think of it this wayによって asks “What varies across the cases?” によっては asks “What happens in some of those cases?” Read the scope of the whole statement.

How to form it

Noun + によって
国によって／人によって／日によって · depending on the country / person / day
Noun + によっては
場所によっては／場合によっては · in some places / under some circumstances
Sentence ending: による
人によります · it depends on the person; よります is the polite form of よる
Useful noun connections
感じ方 = “way of feeling”: 感じる → 感じます → 感じ + 方. 日 here is read ひ; 場合 is ばあい.

Do not add の: use 人によって, not 人のによって. This pattern follows the noun directly.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the choice.

Example 01

によってが。

Customs differ depending on the country.

Clue Country is the changing factor; customs are what differ.

How to read this example

Read it as “If you change the country, the customs are different.” 違う supplies the variation. It does not mean “because a country caused the customs” or identify an information source.

Example 02

はによってさまざまだ。

The way people feel about things varies from person to person.

Clue Different people can have different reactions.

How to read this example

感じ方 means the way someone feels about something. さまざまだ means varied or diverse. 人によって connects that variety to the individual, rather than describing one particular person.

Example 03

わたしのは。によってはになることもある。

The time I get home differs every day. On some days, it can be the middle of the night.

Clue The first sentence gives variation; the second selects late-homecoming days.

How to read this example

毎日違う establishes that the arrival time is not fixed. 日によっては then picks out some days, and こともある says that very late arrivals sometimes occur. The first sentence itself does not contain によって.

Example 04

によってはのはになるかもしれない。

Depending on the circumstances, this year’s cultural festival may be cancelled.

Clue Cancellation is one possible outcome under certain circumstances.

How to read this example

場合 means circumstances or case. によっては selects a possible situation; かもしれない marks uncertainty. The festival is not described as already cancelled or as certain to be cancelled in every case.

Do not confuse it with…

Variation ↔ selected cases
店によって値段が違う = “Prices differ from shop to shop.” 店によっては安い = “Some shops are inexpensive.” Notice that the second sentence is a statement, not a possibility ending.
によっては ↔ “everywhere”
In a weather sentence, 所によっては narrows the scope to some places. 雨が降るかもしれない describes what could happen there. An answer describing all places uniformly would not fit that intended contrast.
Other によって meanings · Lesson 5
Cause and means in Lesson 5 use the same spelling. 事故によって can mean “because of an accident”; 話し合いによって can mean “through discussion.” In a passive sentence it can also introduce the creator or agent. Read the noun and predicate together.
によって ↔ によると · Lesson 7
天気予報によると means “according to the weather forecast”: it identifies an information source. Connect this with reported information in Lesson 7. Variation and an information source are different relationships.
Variation ↔ contrast · Lesson 4
に対して can set A against B: “whereas / in contrast to.” によって says that a result depends on a changing factor; it does not need two explicitly named opposing sides.
Extra forms: により and による + noun

により is a more formal connecting form. による can modify a following noun: 地域による違い means “differences by region.” The meaning still depends on context; 事故による遅れ means “a delay caused by an accident.” Keep these other uses separate from the variation focus of this lesson.

JLPT approach

  1. Look for the factor that changes. Country, person, day, and place are common clues, but the noun alone does not prove the meaning.
  2. Possibility endings help; they are not required. かもしれない and こともある often accompany によっては. Do not turn this into “は must always end in might.”
  3. Avoid an overly rigid rule. によって need not end in 違う; it can describe a choice or result determined by circumstances. Explain why the entire option fits the intended scope.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

それは場合によります。

それはばあいによります。

That depends on the circumstances.

Useful when there is no single answer for every situation. You can also say 人によります: “It depends on the person.”

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 2, printed pages 18–19.

3. ～たびに

Every separate occurrence brings a response or change

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A たびに B means “Every time A happens, B happens.” Imagine separate occasions: one phone call, another phone call, then another. B is the response or change associated with each one.

The pattern can describe past experience or a recurring situation generally. It does not mean only “the next time.” A repeated result can also build up: a team can become stronger with each match.

Think of it this wayCan you count separate occasions of A, with B occurring on each occasion? If you only mean two activities happening together once, reconsider.

How to form it

Dictionary-form verb + たびに
会うたびに／見るたびに／電話をかけるたびに · every time we meet / I see it / I call
Action or event noun + のたびに
試合のたびに／旅行のたびに · with every match / on every trip
Irregular verbs
するたびに／来るたびに · every time someone does it / comes; 来る is read くる
Keep the first verb in dictionary form
会うたびに話した · “Every time we met, we talked.” The final 話した gives the past time; this basic pattern does not change to 会ったたびに.

たび is a word, not the た-form. It can be written 度, read たび here. Attach it to the dictionary form or to noun + の.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the choice.

Example 01

このはがたびにのが。

Every time a typhoon comes to this region, damaging floods occur.

Clue Each typhoon brings another occurrence of flood damage.

How to read this example

台風が来る is a repeatable event, not one continuous state. たびに presents the damage as recurring whenever that event happens in this region.

Example 02

はわたしがをかけるたびに、ちゃんとをいるかと。

Every time I call, my mother asks whether I am eating proper meals.

Clue Each call triggers the mother’s familiar question.

How to read this example

The caller is わたし; the person asking is 母. Different subjects are allowed. 食べているかと聞く means “asks whether I am eating”; the embedded か marks the question she asks.

Example 03

このチームはのたびになっていく。

This team gets stronger with every match.

Clue Every match brings another step in the team’s improvement.

How to read this example

試合 is a noun, so it takes のたびに. 強くなっていく expresses continuing change toward greater strength. The result need not reset to the same level after each occasion.

Do not confuse it with…

たびに ↔ ながら
電話するたびにメモを取る = “take notes every time I make a call”: repeated occasions. 電話しながらメモを取る = “take notes while talking on the phone”: simultaneous actions. Both can describe a habit; the relationship being emphasized differs.
たびに ↔ 間／間に · Lesson 1
間／間に set a time period. 旅行の間に means “during the trip”; 旅行のたびに means “on every trip.” One trip containing an event is different from a recurring event across trips.
たびに ↔ うちに · Lesson 1
うちに can describe development over a continuing or repeated experience. たびに highlights each occurrence. Both can describe improvement; ask whether the sentence stresses “over time” or “on each occasion.”
たびに ↔ ごとに
These can overlap with events, such as a change after every match. For fixed intervals, use the interval pattern: 三日ごとに = “every three days.” Do not mechanically replace ごとに with たびに after a number.
たびに ↔ ほど
ほど links degree or amount: more practice, greater skill. たびに links occasions: every practice session brings something. Repetition may underlie either, so identify what the sentence is measuring.

JLPT approach

  1. Use the textbook’s intended focus. This lesson emphasizes a noticeable repeated event or change, rather than a bare ongoing state or a routine action that is simply taken for granted. A recurring daily event can still fit when the repeated response is worth emphasizing.
  2. Check the actor without inventing a same-person rule. In the phone example, I call and my mother asks. The trigger and response can have different subjects.
  3. Do not let English “when” hide the repetition. Translate it mentally as “on every occasion that…” and see whether that matches the context.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

会うたびに、元気をもらいます。

あうたびに、げんきをもらいます。

Every time we meet, you lift my spirits.

元気をもらう is a natural expression for feeling encouraged or energized by someone.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 2, printed pages 18–19.

4. ～（ば）～ほど／～ほど

As one amount or degree changes, another changes with it

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A ば A ほど B means “The more A…, the more B…” The repeated A names one scale, and B tells you what changes along with it: more possessions make organizing harder; more days off bring more happiness.

“More” is an English guide, not a requirement that both numbers increase. 安ければ安いほどいい means “The cheaper, the better.” The first scale is increasing cheapness, which means a decreasing price.

This lesson also uses single ほど for the same kind of relationship. You do not always need the full repeated pair.

Think of it this wayName both scales: “As A becomes more/less…, B becomes more/less….” Then check whether the verb or adjective forms match the pattern.

How to form it

Verb: ば-form + dictionary form + ほど
読む → 読めば読むほど · the more you read
見る → 見れば見るほど · the more you look
する → すればするほど · the more you do
い-adjective: ければ + い + ほど
多い → 多ければ多いほど · the more numerous
高い → 高ければ高いほど · the higher / more expensive
いい → よければいいほど · the better
な-adjective: なら + な + ほど
静かなら静かなほど · the quieter. The first half can be omitted: 静かなほど. Do not put だ immediately before ほど.
Single ほど
時間がたつほど／にぎやかなほど · as time passes / the livelier it is. Look for a corresponding change in the result.
Whole noun phrase + ほど
忙しい人ほど · the busier people are, the more…
経験が多い人ほど · the more experienced people are, the more…; keep the describing phrase attached to 人.
Conjugation help: making the ば-form
Group 1 verbs
Change the final う-row sound to its え-row sound + ば: 書く → 書けば／話す → 話せば／会う → 会えば.
Group 2 verbs
Drop る and add れば: 食べる → 食べれば／見る → 見れば／見える → 見えれば.
Irregular verbs
する → すれば；来る（くる）→ 来れば（くれば）.
い-adjectives
Replace the final い with ければ: 安い → 安ければ. いい uses よい for this change: よければ.
Another な-adjective form
便利であればあるほど also means “the more convenient it is.” This formal-looking variant uses であればあるほど; it does not change 便利 into an い-adjective.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the choice.

Example 01

がほどがになる。

The more things you have, the harder they become to organize.

Clue More possessions correspond to greater difficulty organizing them.

How to read this example

増える is a Group 2 verb: 増えれば is its ば-form, followed by 増えるほど. The repeated part describes the quantity; 整理が大変になる gives the corresponding result.

Example 02

いいはがたつほどが。

Truly good furniture increases in value as time passes.

Clue Elapsed time and value are the two linked scales.

How to read this example

時間がたつほど uses single ほど: as more time passes, the value rises. There is no missing-word error. The sentence makes this claim about truly good furniture, not about every object that ages.

Example 03

のはほどうれしい。

The more days off I have, the happier I am.

Clue A larger number of days off means greater happiness.

How to read this example

多い becomes 多ければ in the first half and stays 多い before ほど. The scale is the number of days off. うれしい is the result, so it does not replace 多い in the repeated pair.

Example 04

がにぎやかなほどではがよくのだ。

The livelier a town is, the better goods sell in its shops.

Clue Greater liveliness goes with stronger sales.

How to read this example

にぎやか is a な-adjective, so use にぎやかなほど. The sentence uses single ほど; 商店では物がよく売れる is the result. よく売れる means goods sell well, not simply that they are visible in the shop.

Example 05

ほどのがだ。

The busier a person is, the better they tend to be at using time.

Clue The comparison moves along a scale of how busy people are.

How to read this example

Read ［忙しい人］ほど as a complete noun phrase plus ほど. It presents a tendency: people with greater busyness are better at managing their time. It does not mean “only busy people” or guarantee this about every individual.

Do not confuse it with…

見る ↔ 見える
For the repeated-verb pattern, keep the same verb: 見れば見るほど or 見えれば見えるほど. 見る means “look at”; 見える means “be visible / look or appear.” A visibility scale is possible when the context supports it; the pattern is not restricted to deliberate actions.
The repeated pair ↔ the final result
見れば見るほど、本物に見える = “The more I look, the more real it looks.” 見れば見るほど is the pair; 本物に見える is the result. Do not put 見える into the second slot just because it appears later.
い-adjective ↔ な-adjective
高いほど and きれいなほど are both valid forms. きれい is a な-adjective despite ending in the sound い. Which one fits depends on whether the sentence needs height/price or beauty/cleanliness.
Proportion ↔ degree · Lesson 3
くらい／ぐらい／ほど can indicate extent: 声が出ないほど驚いた = “I was so surprised I could not speak.” That measures one intense reaction; it is not automatically a “the more…, the more…” pair.
Proportion ↔ negative comparison · Lesson 3
AはBほど高くない = “A is not as expensive as B.” B is the comparison standard. Do not read every ほど followed by a negative as proportional.
ば…ほど ↔ a single condition · Lesson 6
Conditional forms in Lesson 6 can set one condition or an unreal situation. Here, the repeated word plus ほど creates a changing-scale relationship, not merely “if A, then B once.”

JLPT approach

  1. Bracket the pair. ［増えれば 増えるほど］［整理が大変になる］. The conditional form and dictionary form belong together; the result follows them.
  2. Do not force a missing ば half into every sentence. The book’s furniture and lively-town examples use single ほど. The relationship between the scales supplies the meaning.
  3. Check word form and meaning separately. First identify whether the blank needs a verb, an い-adjective, a な-adjective, or a complete noun phrase. Then decide which meaning fits.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

早ければ早いほどいいです。

はやければはやいほどいいです。

The sooner, the better.

Useful when choosing a time. Depending on the situation, 早い can mean early or quick; interpret the scale in context.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 2, printed pages 18–19.

5. ～ついでに

A main task creates a chance to do a useful extra

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A ついでに B means “While doing A / while I’m at it, I’ll do B too.” A is the main task or original reason for the outing. B is an intentional extra that fits the same occasion.

You are already out walking, so you post a card. You are already cleaning the entrance, so you arrange the shoes too. The two actions can happen one after the other; they do not have to happen at exactly the same instant.

Think of it this wayRemove B from the story. Would A still be the reason for the trip or task? If so, A is the main action to put before ついでに.

How to form it

Dictionary-form verb + ついでに
買い物に行くついでに · while going shopping / since I am going shopping
た-form verb + ついでに
本を注文したついでに · taking the opportunity when I ordered a book
Action noun + のついでに
散歩のついでに／掃除のついでに · while out for a walk / while doing the cleaning
Verb versus noun connection
掃除するついでに uses the verb する directly.
掃除のついでに uses the noun 掃除 + の. Do not mix these into するのついでに.
A useful form from the book
注文する → 注文した → 注文したついでに. For Group 1 verbs, remember the た-form sound change: 買う → 買った.

Dictionary form does not automatically mean future: 買い物に行くついでに、手紙を出した can describe a past outing. The main sentence ending and the intended timing both matter.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the choice.

Example 01

のついでにこのはがきをポストにきて。

While you are out for a walk, please mail this postcard.

Clue The walk is the original outing; mailing the postcard is the extra.

How to read this example

散歩 is a noun, so it takes のついでに. 出してきて asks the listener to go and post the card, then return. The sender need not ask for a separate trip just for the postcard.

Example 02

のをするついでにのをしよう。

While cleaning the entrance, let’s organize the shoes too.

Clue Organizing the shoes fits the cleaning task already planned.

How to read this example

掃除をする is a verb phrase and connects directly to ついでに. 整理をしよう is the added action: “let’s organize.” Both tasks use the same area and opportunity.

Example 03

インターネットでをついでにDVDも。

When I ordered a book online, I also looked up newly released DVDs.

Clue Ordering the book creates the chance to check DVDs as well.

How to read this example

注文した is the た-form of 注文する. The later も marks the DVD search as an addition. 調べた means “looked up / checked”; the sentence does not say the speaker bought a DVD.

Do not confuse it with…

ついでに ↔ ながら
歩きながら話す means talking while walking: simultaneous actions. 散歩のついでに手紙を出す makes posting a letter a convenient extra on the walk. Ask whether the sentence emphasizes simultaneity or the extra opportunity.
ついでに ↔ たびに
たびに means “every time.” ついでに does not promise repetition; one shopping trip can be enough. A repeated outing may allow either, but the emphasis changes.
ついでに ↔ うちに · Lesson 1
うちに can highlight a temporary opportunity before a state changes. ついでに highlights the convenience created by an existing task or outing. “Before it gets dark” and “while I’m out shopping anyway” are different reasons to act.
ついでに ↔ てから · Lesson 1
買い物をしてから手紙を出す simply places posting after shopping. ついでに additionally ranks the actions as main task and extra task. Compare the sequence and prerequisite discussion in Lesson 1.
ついでに ↔ かわりに · Lesson 4
かわりに can mean “instead of” or “in exchange for.” ついでに adds B while also doing A; it does not replace A or require compensation.

JLPT approach

  1. Check which action is the main one. Reversing A and B changes the reason for doing them. The “extra” need not be objectively tiny, but the speaker must present it as secondary.
  2. Look for an intentional extra. Posting a card, organizing shoes, and checking DVDs fit. An accidental event such as falling over is not normally the useful extra this pattern expresses.
  3. Use the occasion, not just the word も. “Also” can support the extra-action reading, but the two tasks must fit the same opportunity. も is a clue, not a requirement.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

ついでに、これもお願いします。

ついでに、これもおねがいします。

While you’re at it, could you do this too?

Use it when the extra request really fits the task already being done. Calling a major additional favor “just an extra” can sound inconsiderate.
