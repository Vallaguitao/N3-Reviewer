1. ～くらい／～ぐらい／～ほど

Use an image or comparison to show how strong something is

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

くらい／ぐらい／ほど can mean “to the extent that,” “so…that,” or “about as…as.” Instead of only saying “very,” give the listener a way to picture the degree: pain that makes standing impossible, or wind comparable to a typhoon.

Read the sentence in two parts: the image that measures the degree and the thing being described. Japanese often puts the image first: “cannot speak + ほど + surprised” becomes “so surprised that I could not speak.”

The image need not be an event that actually happens. “Thin enough to look as if it might break” describes an appearance. It does not report a broken arm.

Think of it this wayAsk “How much? How strong?” Then find the image that answers. If two quantities change together, check Lesson 2’s proportional ほど instead.

How to form it

Verb in a plain form
泣くほど／声が出ないほど／泣きたいくらい · enough to cry / so…that no voice comes out / enough to want to cry. Negative and たい forms can supply the degree too.
い-adjective + くらい／ほど
痛いくらい／痛かったほど · retain the plain adjective form; do not insert な or だ.
な-adjective + な
不思議なくらい · to a surprising extent. For the present affirmative, change だ to な before the pattern. The book’s 折れそうなくらい also needs な after the appearance expression そう.
Noun directly + くらい／ほど
台風ぐらい · about the degree of a typhoon. No の is needed between 台風 and ぐらい.
Before a following noun: add の
台風ぐらいの風／立っていられないほどの痛み · typhoon-strength wind / pain so severe one cannot stay standing. の connects the whole degree phrase to the following noun.
Before a description or at the end
泣くほど痛い · so painful it makes one cry
泣きたいくらいだ／泣きたいくらいです · it is enough to make me want to cry. The final だ／です ends the degree statement.
Below a threshold: ほどではない
かさをさすほどではない · not to the point of needing an umbrella. This denies that degree, not necessarily the rain itself.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then explain the choice using the clue.

Example 01

こののパンはおいしい。いくらいだ。

The bread at this shop is delicious—so delicious that I want to eat it every day.

Clue Wanting the bread every day measures how delicious it is.

How to read this example

The first sentence states おいしい. The second supplies an image: 毎日食べたいくらいだ. 食べたい is “want to eat”; this does not claim the speaker already eats it daily.

Example 02

ようさんのはそうなくらい。

Yoko's arms are so thin that they look as if they might break.

Clue 折れそう is an imagined appearance, followed by な.

How to read this example

Read ［折れそうな くらい］［細い］: so thin it looks as if it might break. This is appearance そう, not the reported-information そうだ used in the next example.

Example 03

によると、はぐらいのがそうだ。

According to the weather forecast, winds about as strong as a typhoon’s are expected today.

Clue 台風 is the benchmark; の connects the comparison to 風.

How to read this example

Read ［台風ぐらいの 風］ as one noun phrase. 天気予報によると identifies the source, and 吹くそうだ reports what the forecast says. It is not the speaker’s guess that the wind “might” blow.

Example 04

かさをさすほどではないが、がいる。

It is raining a little, though not enough to need an umbrella.

Clue The rain exists, but the umbrella threshold has not been reached.

How to read this example

ほどではない negates the need for that degree of response. The following が means “but,” and 少し雨が降っている confirms there is still a little rain.

Example 05

いられないほどのをにじた。

I suddenly felt pain in my back so severe that I could not remain standing.

Clue 立っていられない gives a concrete measure of the pain.

How to read this example

立っている means to be standing. 立っていられない means one cannot remain standing. The complete clause modifies 痛み through ほどの; it is not “no pain.”

Example 06

さっきがあった。だながかとほど。

There was an earthquake a moment ago. It shook so violently that I thought the bookshelf might fall.

Clue かと思う shows the speaker’s alarm, not a confirmed collapse.

How to read this example

Read ［本だなが倒れるかと思うほど］［激しく揺れた］. The shaking was so violent that the speaker thought the bookshelf might fall. The sentence does not state that it fell.

Do not confuse it with…

くらい ↔ ぐらい ↔ ほど
くらい and ぐらい are variants with the same core meanings. All three can overlap in degree descriptions. Do not use a rule that ほど is always stronger, or that ぐらい is only for speaking. Fixed expressions and the whole sentence matter.
One degree ↔ two changing scales · Lesson 2
読むほど面白くなる = “The more I read, the more interesting it gets.” Reading and interest change together. 眠れないほど面白い = “so interesting I cannot sleep”: sleeplessness shows the degree of interest.
Extent ↔ cause · Lesson 5
声が出ないほど驚いた measures surprise through its effect. A cause expression explains why something happened. See Lesson 5. Do not translate every English “so…that” as a simple “because.”
Degree ↔ “nothing compares”
声が出ないほど驚いた has a negative inside the degree image. これほど驚いたことはない has a negative about other experiences: “I have never been this surprised.” Find what the negative belongs to.
Not as…as ↔ not enough to…
今日は昨日ほど寒くない = “Today is not as cold as yesterday”: yesterday is the standard. 暖房をつけるほどではない = “It is not cold enough to turn on the heating”: an action defines the threshold.
Other familiar uses: “about” and “at least / even that much”
Approximate amount
三十分くらい／三十分ほど = about thirty minutes. This measures a duration. For a clock time, 三時ごろ is a useful basic pattern: around three o’clock.
A small or minimum amount
名前くらい書ける = “I can at least write my name.” Here くらい presents the task as a small minimum; it is not the same extent use as “so painful that…” Do not automatically replace this くらい with ほど.

JLPT approach

  1. Read past the blank. A following noun may need くらいの／ほどの; a following adjective can take the degree phrase directly. Check the printed の before adding another one.
  2. Separate an image from a fact. 倒れるかと思うほど means “so…that I thought it might fall.” かと思う expresses the thought; かどうか means “whether or not” and does not express the same reaction.
  3. Do not choose by intensity alone. Identify the construction first: approximation, vivid extent, negative comparison, or proportion. More than one form may be grammatical in isolation.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

言葉が出ないほど驚きました。

ことばがでないほどおどろきました。

I was so surprised I was speechless.

The inability to speak makes the degree of surprise vivid.

2. ～くらい…はない／～ほど…はない

Put one thing at the top: “Nothing compares with it”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A ほど…はない means “There is nothing as…as A.” In this lesson, the speaker chooses A as their strongest example of a quality: the most enjoyable activity, the worst thing, or the most anxious moment.

The structure works by saying that no other person, thing, or experience reaches that level. The negative ending helps make a strong positive ranking of A. The quality itself may be pleasant or unpleasant.

The textbook emphasizes personal judgment. “Nothing is as enjoyable as cooking” expresses someone’s feeling; it is not a measurement that every reader must agree with.

Think of it this wayFind A, then the quality, then the category being denied. “No other [person / thing / experience] is this…” should preserve the meaning.

How to form it

Noun + くらい／ぐらい／ほど
テストほどいやなものはない · nothing is as unpleasant as a test. No の goes immediately before ほど.
Action treated as a noun: V + こと
料理を作ることぐらい楽しいことはない · nothing is as enjoyable as cooking. The first こと turns the activity into the comparison standard.
A whole time phrase
病気だとわかったときほど… · as much as at the time I learned I was ill. Keep everything describing とき together.
People: はいない
リーさんぐらい動物好きな人はいない · no person loves animals as much as Lee. 人 uses the existence verb いる, so the negative is いない.
Things and experiences: はない
楽しいことはない／いやなものはない／不安になったことはない · no enjoyable thing / no unpleasant thing / no experience of becoming anxious to that degree. Read the comparison before interpreting the negative.
Adjective before the category noun
楽しいこと／きれいな山／いやなもの · an い-adjective stays in its plain form; a な-adjective needs な. きれい is a な-adjective despite its final い sound.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then explain the choice using the clue.

Example 01

リーさんぐらいなはいない。

No one loves animals as much as Lee does.

Clue The category is 人, so the ending is はいない.

How to read this example

リーさん is the standard. 動物好きな describes 人: “a person who loves animals.” The sentence places Lee at the top for this quality; it does not say Lee dislikes animals.

Example 02

わたしはをことぐらいことはないといます。

I think nothing is as enjoyable as cooking.

Clue The first こと names the activity; the second names comparable enjoyable things.

How to read this example

Bracket ［料理を作ること］ぐらい［楽しいこと］はない. The final と思っています makes the speaker’s personal view explicit.

Example 03

ああ、あしたものテストがある。テストほどいやなものはない。

Oh, there is another kanji test tomorrow. Nothing is as awful as a test.

Clue Tests are ranked highest on an unpleasant scale.

How to read this example

いやな modifies もの. はない denies other things as unpleasant as tests. The first sentence complains about tomorrow’s kanji test; the second generalizes that feeling about tests.

Example 04

２にだとわかったときほどになったことはない。

I have never felt as anxious as when I learned two years ago that I was ill.

Clue The reference is a whole past moment, not just “two years.”

How to read this example

Read ［２年前に病気だとわかったとき］ほど［不安になったことはない］. The speaker did become anxious then; no other experience has reached that degree of anxiety.

Do not confuse it with…

“Nothing compares” ↔ “A is less…than B”
この店はあの店ほど高くない = “This shop is not as expensive as that one”: two named shops. この店ほど好きな店はない = “There is no shop I like as much as this one”: one shop is placed above the rest. Both have ほど and a negative; their structures differ.
Expressive ranking ↔ 一番
料理が一番楽しい directly says cooking is the most enjoyable. This lesson’s pattern expresses a stronger “nothing else compares” feeling. 一番 can also report a measurable ranking, such as the tallest mountain.
A negative experience ↔ no equally strong experience
不安になったことはない alone means “I have never become anxious.” With あのときほど, it means “I have never become as anxious as at that time.” Do not lose the comparison standard.
Highest degree ↔ best choice
に限る recommends a best choice or method. This pattern ranks a quality, including an unpleasant one: “nothing is as awful as tests” is not a recommendation to take tests.
Textbook focus versus an overly strict shortcut

For questions built around this lesson, look for an evaluation or feeling, especially when the speaker says と 思う. But do not memorize “高い, 大きい, and 速い can never appear with ほど…ない.” These words also express impressions, and negative comparisons have wider uses. と 思う is a clue to the speaker’s stance, not a ban on discussing facts.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the ending before deciding. はいない denies comparable people; はない denies comparable things or experiences. ほかにない can emphasize “nothing else.”
  2. Bracket a long standard. In the fourth example, the whole clause ending in とき identifies the anxious moment. The sentence does not deny that the speaker became ill or anxious.
  3. Check both meaning and adjective form. For the book’s personal ranking, ask which quality conveys the intended evaluation, then check い versus な before the following noun. Do not eliminate a word just because its English meaning can be measured.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

家で過ごす時間ほど落ち着くものはありません。

いえですごすじかんほどおちつくものはありません。

Nothing is as relaxing as time spent at home.

A personal preference expressed as “nothing else comes close.” はありません is the polite negative ending.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 3, printed pages 22–23.

3. ～くらいなら／～ぐらいなら

Reject option A and choose B: “I would rather…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A くらいなら B means “Rather than do A, I would choose B.” The speaker treats A as undesirable, too troublesome, or not worth the sacrifice. B is the preferred alternative, even if B is only the less bad choice.

This is more than a neutral “if.” Making lunch every morning is presented as a burden, so a convenience-store lunch will do. The speaker is comparing choices from their own point of view.

It can express your own decision or advice about someone else’s options. Because it rejects A strongly, it can sound blunt when A is someone else’s plan or offer.

Think of it this wayLabel A “the choice being rejected” and B “the preferred alternative.” If B is simply a consequence of A, this pattern probably does not express the intended relationship.

How to form it

Dictionary verb + くらいなら／ぐらいなら
作るくらいなら／行くぐらいなら · rather than make / go. This preference pattern uses the dictionary form, not the ます-stem or た-form.
する verbs and 来る
がまんするくらいなら／来るくらいなら · rather than put up with it / come. 来る is read くる. Keep する after an action noun: 生活するぐらいなら.
Preferred action: ～ほうがいい／～ほうがましだ
参加しないほうがいい · it is better not to attend. まし means “better than the alternative”; it does not necessarily mean good.
Acceptable alternative: ～でいい／～でかまわない
コンビニ弁当でいい／今の体型のままでかまわない · a convenience-store lunch will do / I do not mind staying as I am. These endings show acceptance, not necessarily enthusiasm.
Personal preference: ～たい
独身でいたい · I want to remain single. でいる means to remain in a state; いたい expresses the wish to do so.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then explain the choice using the clue.

Example 01

でをくらいなら、コンビニでいい。

If I had to make my own lunch every morning, a convenience-store lunch would be fine.

Clue Making lunch every morning is the burden; buying lunch is acceptable.

How to read this example

作る is the dictionary form. コンビニ弁当でいい means a convenience-store lunch “will do,” not necessarily that it is the speaker’s favorite food.

Example 02

がといっしょにぐらいなら、このままでいたい。

Rather than live with someone I do not get along with, I would prefer to remain single.

Clue The unwanted option is living with someone incompatible.

How to read this example

気が合わない describes 人: someone the speaker does not get along with. The verb directly before ぐらいなら is 生活する. このまま独身でいたい means the speaker would prefer to remain single.

Example 03

30もにくらいなら、ほうがいい。

If you would arrive thirty minutes late to the information session, it would be better not to attend.

Clue Going thirty minutes late is rejected in favor of not attending.

How to read this example

も presents thirty minutes as a significant delay. 遅れて leads into the dictionary-form 行く. 参加しないほうがいい gives advice about the alternative; it is the speaker’s judgment in this situation.

Example 04

やせるためになケーキをがまんするくらいなら、ののままでかまわない。

If losing weight means giving up the cake I love, I do not mind keeping my current figure.

Clue Giving up a favorite cake is treated as too high a price.

How to read this example

やせるために explains the purpose: to lose weight. The action being rejected is がまんする, giving up or resisting the cake. 今の体型のままでかまわない expresses acceptance of the current state.

Do not confuse it with…

くらいなら ↔ ordinary なら · Lesson 6
行くなら、早く出よう = “If we are going, let’s leave early”: going is accepted as the premise. 遅れて行くくらいなら、行かないほうがいい rejects going late. See conditional forms.
くらいなら ↔ より
作るより買うほうが楽だ compares convenience. くらいなら additionally presents making it as the unwanted option. より itself does not require rejection or dissatisfaction.
“Rather than do” ↔ “Or rather…” · Lesson 4
というより revises a description: “cold—or rather, freezing.” くらいなら compares alternative actions: “rather than wait, I’ll walk.” Similar English wording can hide different jobs.
Rejected option ↔ replacement · Lesson 4
かわりに can state a replacement without criticizing the original option. くらいなら explicitly presents A as undesirable and B as preferable.
Preferred alternative ↔ best recommendation
に限る says which choice is best. くらいなら needs the unwanted alternative A as part of its comparison; B only has to be better than A.

JLPT approach

  1. Check the verb just before くらいなら. Earlier verbs in the same phrase can have other forms. In 30分も遅れて説明会に行くくらいなら, 遅れて connects to 行く; 行く is the dictionary form attached to the pattern.
  2. Look for preference, acceptance, or advice in B. ほうがいい is useful evidence, but not required: the book also uses でいい, いたい, and かまわない.
  3. Keep the direction straight. A is what the speaker would avoid. Do not translate the sentence as “A is better than B.” くらいなら and ぐらいなら have the same role here.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

長く待つくらいなら、歩いて行きます。

ながくまつくらいなら、あるいていきます。

Rather than wait a long time, I’ll walk there.

Walking is chosen as preferable to the unwanted wait; it need not be the easiest possible option.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 3, printed pages 22–23.

4. ～に限る

Recommend your best choice: “Nothing beats…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

に限る means “is the best choice” or “nothing beats…” in this lesson. The speaker confidently recommends a thing, place, or action, often from personal experience. Movies are best at the cinema; this spot is best for photographing the lake.

The recommendation may concern a general preference or one particular situation. It is a strong opinion, not proof that every other option is impossible.

With a negative verb, the recommended choice is avoiding the action: 行かないに限る means “the best policy is not to go.”

Think of it this wayReplace it mentally with “I think this is the best choice here.” If the sentence instead announces who is eligible or allowed, check the separate restriction meaning.

How to form it

Noun + に限る
この場所に限る · this place is the best. No の or だ is needed between the noun and に.
Dictionary verb + に限る
見るに限る／家で休むに限る · watching is best / resting at home is best. Connect the plain verb directly, without adding の.
ない-form + に限る
行かないに限る／無理をしないに限る · it is best not to go / not to overdo it. The negative belongs to the recommended action.
Making the negative verb
行く → 行かない；会う → 会わない；見る → 見ない；する → しない；来る → 来ない（こない） · then add に限る. For Group 1 verbs, change to the あ-row + ない; a final う changes to わない.
Polite ending
に限る（にかぎる）→ に限ります（にかぎります） · the politeness goes at the end: 見るに限ります, not 見ますに限る.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then explain the choice using the clue.

Example 01

やっぱりはでに。

After all, movies are best watched in a cinema.

Clue やっぱり supports the speaker’s confident preference.

How to read this example

映画館で gives the place for watching. The complete recommendation is ［映画館で見る］に限る: watching at a cinema is best, rather than a rule that movies can only be shown there.

Example 02

かぜがはやっているときは、がにはに。

When colds are going around, the best policy is not to go to crowded places.

Clue 行かない is the action recommended: not going.

How to read this example

かぜがはやっているときは sets the situation. 人が多い所 describes crowded places. This sentence expresses the speaker’s avoidance advice; the negative is not denying that avoidance is a good choice.

Example 03

のをこのに。すてきながよ。

If you want to photograph the lake, this is the best place. You can take wonderful pictures here.

Clue 撮るなら sets the goal; この場所 names the recommended choice.

How to read this example

なら accepts taking photographs as the premise. It does not reject photography as くらいなら would reject an unwanted action. 撮れます is the potential form, “can take,” supporting the claim that the spot is good.

Do not confuse it with…

Recommendation ↔ restriction with the same verb
休むなら、この宿に限る recommends this inn. 利用は会員に限る restricts use to members. Notices, eligibility, and rules point to restriction. The exact ending に限る／に限ります alone cannot distinguish them.
に限る ↔ に限り
本日に限り means “today only.” This is a restriction expression, often found in notices. It does not mean “today is best.” Learn the surrounding context along with the ending.
に限る ↔ ～たほうがいい／～ないほうがいい
早く寝たほうがいい gives advice: “You should go to bed early.” に限る adds a confident best-choice judgment. Either may concern a specific situation; do not memorize “ほうがいい = specific, に限る = always general.”
Best choice ↔ obligation · Lesson 10
べきだ can express what someone ought to do as a duty or appropriate action. に限る recommends what the speaker considers best; it does not by itself impose a rule.
Best choice ↔ “not necessarily”
必ずしも高い物がいいとは限らない means “Expensive things are not necessarily good.” とは限らない in Lesson 8 questions whether a statement always holds. It is not the negative-action recommendation 買わないに限る (“best not to buy”).

JLPT approach

  1. Read the whole situation. やっぱり often supports a personal recommendation, but it is not required. An eligibility notice can use exactly the same に限ります ending with the restriction meaning.
  2. Locate the negative. 行かないに限る recommends not going. とは限らない says a proposition is not always true. Similar-looking endings make different claims.
  3. Identify what is being recommended. In the lake example, 撮るなら sets the purpose or situation; この場所 is the best choice. A condition can introduce a recommendation without rejecting that condition.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

週末は、家でゆっくりするに限ります。

しゅうまつは、いえでゆっくりするにかぎります。

On weekends, nothing beats relaxing at home.

A confident personal preference. It does not forbid spending the weekend elsewhere.
