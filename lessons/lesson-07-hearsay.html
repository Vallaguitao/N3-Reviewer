1. ～ということだ・～とのことだ

Pass on a report or message: “According to…” / “They say…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～ということだ and ～とのことだ relay information obtained from a source. That source can be a person, a phone call, an email, or a notice. “Hearsay” here includes ordinary reliable messages; it does not automatically mean gossip.

Both sound more formal than everyday hearsay そうだ. とのことだ is especially useful for passing on a particular person’s message, as in an office phone message. ということだ also suits reports and explanations. These uses overlap: a city notice or email does not force one unique ending.

First identify the reported content, then put the reporting expression around it. The source’s claim and the current speaker’s own opinion are different layers. A report about next week remains about next week even if the message arrived earlier today.

Think of it this wayBracket the sentence as [source] + [what the source says] + [reporting ending]. Do not mistake “according to the notice” for the cause of the event.

How to form it

Verb: plain clause + ということだ／とのことだ
始まるということです／戻れないとのことです · construction will begin / someone cannot return, according to the source. Keep any negative or past meaning inside the clause.
い-adjective: keep い
忙しいとのことです／おいしかったということです · reportedly busy / reportedly was delicious. Do not add だ after an い-adjective.
Noun or な-adjective: だ + reporting ending
休みだということです／元気だとのことです · reportedly a day off / reportedly well. This is a quoted clause, so use だ rather than noun の or adjective な in the basic pattern.
Plain content, polite report
来ます → 来るとのことです；来ません → 来ないとのことです · for an ordinary indirect report, the outer です makes the report polite. A direct quotation can retain the original wording; “polite forms can never appear inside a quote” would be too strong.
Name the information source
メールによると／市のお知らせによれば／店の人の話では · according to the email / city notice / shop worker’s account. The source may also be established in the preceding sentence.
Acknowledge news in a letter: ～とのこと、…
合格したとのこと、おめでとうございます · “I hear you passed—congratulations.” The acknowledgment continues into the greeting; the book’s letter example need not end とのことです.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

のおによれば、このはからがということです。

According to the city notice, construction on this road will begin next week.

Clue The city notice supplies the information; next week supplies the event time.

How to read this example

によれば names the source. 来週から means “starting next week.” 始まる is the reported future event; ということです politely passes it on, without claiming the speaker saw construction begin.

Example 02

ののでは、こののはとてもおいしいということだ。

According to someone at the shop, the rice from this region is very delicious.

Clue The taste judgment belongs to the shop worker’s report.

How to read this example

店の人の話では means “according to what someone at the shop says.” おいしい keeps its い before ということだ. The speaker can relay this claim without having tasted the rice.

Example 03

さっきさんからがありました。はにとのことです。

Mr. Kawamura called a little while ago. He says that he cannot return to the office today.

Clue The first sentence identifies the caller; the second delivers the message.

How to read this example

さっき places the call a little earlier. 今日は belongs to the message about today. 戻る → 戻れる → 戻れない gives return → can return → cannot return. とのことです is useful for this kind of message relay.

Example 04

メールによると、さんははとてもとのことです。

According to the email, Ms. Hayashi will be very busy next week.

Clue Written information also counts as hearsay in this grammar lesson.

How to read this example

メールによると identifies a written source. 来週は establishes next week as the relevant period. 忙しい attaches directly to とのことです; 忙しいだ would be incorrect.

Example 05

【】がとのこと、おめでとうございます。

[Letter] I hear you have secured a new job. Congratulations!

Clue The writer acknowledges news before offering congratulations.

How to read this example

決まった reports that the new job has been settled or secured. とのこと、 connects that received news to おめでとうございます. The meaning includes “I hear that…” even though a natural short English greeting may omit it.

Do not confuse it with…

Hearsay そうだ ↔ appearance そうだ
おいしいそうだ = “I hear it is delicious”; おいしそうだ = “It looks delicious.” Likewise, 雨が降るそうだ reports rain, while 雨が降りそうだ judges that rain looks likely. Check the form immediately before そう.
Reporting news ↔ explaining what something means
明日は休みだということです can report a closure. つまり、明日は来なくてもいいということです explains its implication: “In other words, that means we do not have to come tomorrow.” ということだ has an explanatory use as well as this lesson’s hearsay use.
According to ↔ caused by / by means of · Lesson 5
によって can introduce a cause or means. によると／によれば here identify where information came from. The notice reports the construction; it is not the tool used to construct the road.
A particular message ↔ a circulating view
と言われている in this lesson presents what people commonly say. とのことです naturally relays a specific caller’s message. Look at the information situation, not only the English “it is said.”
Reporting a premise ↔ responding to it · Lesson 6
なら can introduce advice based on reported information. “I hear the shop is closed” supplies information; “If it is closed, let’s go elsewhere” responds to it.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the source, even if it is in the previous sentence. A phone-call introduction can supply it without によると.
  2. Preserve tense and negation inside the report. 戻れない is “cannot return,” not “did not return.” A past phone call does not require every verb in its message to be past.
  3. Check meaning and tone together. Several hearsay endings may be grammatical. A specific office message favors とのことです, but formality alone is not a guaranteed answer key.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

田中さんは少し遅れるとのことです。

たなかさんはすこしおくれるとのことです。

Tanaka says they will be a little late.

A concise way to pass on someone’s message. The report remains polite while 遅れる stays in plain form.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 7, printed pages 36–37.

For the appearance/hearsay distinction, see Niwa Saburoo, Japanese Grammar Overview, section 38.

2. ～と言われている

Report a circulating view: “It is said…” / “People generally say…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～と言われている presents a statement as something people say or believe. In this lesson, it introduces a circulating view, reputation, or prediction without focusing on one particular speaker.

It can concern a current belief, a past event, or a future prediction. The ている ending describes the statement’s status as something being said; it does not force the reported event to be happening now.

A widely repeated statement is still a reported claim. This grammar does not prove scientific truth, unanimous agreement, or certainty. If the reported content includes だろう, preserve that uncertainty in the translation.

Think of it this wayAsk “Is this one person’s message, or a view presented as circulating among people?” Then separate the claim from the act of reporting it.

How to form it

Build the reporting verb
言う → 言われる → 言われている · say → be said → be said / commonly said. In this construction 言われる is passive.
Verb in a plain form + と言われている
流行する／起こった／起こらない + と言われている · is said to become fashionable / to have happened / not to happen. The reported clause supplies its own time and polarity.
い-adjective + と言われている
体にいいと言われている · is said to be good for one’s health. Keep いい; do not use よさそう when the task is to relay what people say.
Noun or な-adjective + だと言われている
名作だと言われている／安全だと言われている · is said to be a masterpiece / safe. Written prose may use である instead of だ.
A prediction inside the report
優勝するだろうと言われている · people say that someone will probably win. Bracket [優勝するだろう] + と + [言われている].
Polite and past reporting forms
と言われています／と言われていた · polite present report / was said or believed. The outer past locates the saying in the past; it does not by itself say whether the belief is still held.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

はがするとれている。

People say that black clothing will be fashionable this year.

Clue The fashion forecast is presented as something people are saying.

How to read this example

今年 sets the year. 流行する describes the predicted trend; と言われている reports its circulation. Do not translate it as proof that everyone already wears black.

Example 02

はにいいとれている。

Natto is said to be good for the body.

Clue The sentence reports a commonly expressed view about natto.

How to read this example

体にいい is the complete quoted assessment. と links that assessment to 言われている. The lesson illustrates how a health-related claim is reported, rather than establishing the claim’s medical accuracy.

Example 03

のではがするだろうとれています。

It is said that Nakagawa will probably win the next tournament.

Clue The report contains a probability judgment, not a guaranteed winner.

How to read this example

中川選手 is the competitor being discussed. 優勝するだろう means “will probably win the championship.” と言われています reports that prediction politely; it does not remove だろう’s uncertainty.

Do not confuse it with…

General report ↔ a named person’s message
とのことだ is useful for a particular message. The passive here shifts attention toward the circulating statement; it does not require an identifiable caller or email.
General belief ↔ being told something personally
先生に静かにするように言われた means “I was told by the teacher to be quiet.” It also uses passive 言われる, but the teacher and recipient define a personal instruction. Not every 言われる means “people generally believe.”
People’s prediction ↔ the speaker’s prediction
優勝するだろう can state a prediction directly. Adding と言われている attributes that prediction to what others say. Keep both layers in mind.
Commonly said ↔ necessarily true · Lesson 8
とは限らない denies that a statement always holds. Reporting a common view and questioning whether it holds in every case are different jobs.

JLPT approach

  1. Look inside the quotation boundary と. A だろう before と belongs to the reported prediction; do not discard it as redundant.
  2. Read passive forms in context. A general reputation differs from “I was told by the teacher,” even though both can contain 言われる.
  3. Separate information status from truth. “It is commonly said” does not mean “everyone agrees” or “this has been proved.”

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

この小説は名作だと言われています。

このしょうせつはめいさくだといわれています。

This novel is widely regarded as a masterpiece.

The sentence reports a reputation rather than directly stating the speaker’s own review.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 7, printed pages 36–37.

3. ～とか

Pass on something unconfirmed: “I hear…” / “Something about…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Sentence-final ～とか presents information heard without firmly confirming it. The speaker may have picked up a rumor or a report whose details are not clear. It leaves some distance between the speaker and the information.

It can open a question, invite a reaction, or lead to a personal comment or plan. The book asks about a business trip, comments on a lively home, and plans to buy bread based on a favorable report.

Do not translate this vagueness as “probably false.” The point is that the speaker is not presenting the information as personally confirmed. It also need not sound rude: the following sentence in the book can be respectful.

Think of it this wayTry “I hear something about A—is that right?” Then check that the rest of the conversation responds to this received information.

How to form it

Verb: plain form + とか
引っ越すとか／飼っているとか／帰ったとか · I hear someone will move / keeps animals / went home. Keep the form required by the reported content.
い-adjective + とか
おいしいとか／忙しかったとか · I hear it is delicious / someone was busy. Keep い in the present affirmative.
Noun or な-adjective + だとか
出張だとか／便利だとか · I hear it is a business trip / convenient. Learn these as a complete reported clause followed by とか.
Negative report
来ないとか／休みではないとか · I hear someone will not come / it is not a day off. Uncertainty does not cancel the negative meaning.
With an explicit reporting verb
来月引っ越すとか聞きました · I heard something about someone moving next month. とか can appear before 聞いた／聞きました as well as at the end.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

まただとか。はどちらにんですか。

I hear you are going on another business trip next month. Where are you going this time?

Clue The tentative report opens a respectful question about the trip.

How to read this example

出張 is a noun, so the report is 出張だとか. また means “again.” どちら asks the destination; 行かれる is respectful here, not a claim about someone’s ability to travel.

Example 02

ではいろいろなをいるとか。にぎやかでしょうね。

I hear you keep various animals at home. It must be lively.

Clue A report about keeping animals prompts a guess about the household.

How to read this example

飼っている describes keeping or raising animals. とか marks the received information. にぎやかでしょうね is the speaker’s resulting comment: “It must be lively.” Distinguish the report from that reaction.

Example 03

あののパンはとてもおいしいとか。、に。

I hear that shop's bread is very good. I will buy some on my way home today.

Clue The speaker plans to test a favorable report by buying the bread.

How to read this example

おいしいとか reports the taste claim without saying the speaker has tried the bread. 買って帰ります means buy it and go home, often naturally “buy some to take home.” The plan follows the report; it is not itself part of what was heard.

Do not confuse it with…

Unconfirmed report ↔ examples in a list
パンとか果物とかを買う lists bread, fruit, and perhaps other things. あのパンはおいしいとか reports that the bread is tasty. Check whether とか follows a whole statement or lists items within the sentence.
Vagueness ↔ casual quotation
って mainly marks a casual quote or report; it can relay exact words. とか here additionally leaves the information unconfirmed or vague. Casual speech and uncertain information are not the same feature.
Unconfirmed report ↔ a straightforward report
そうだ／ということだ can simply pass on acquired information. とか draws attention to a looser report. This is a nuance, not a numerical scale of reliability.
Reported uncertainty ↔ inferred likelihood
来るとか聞いた refers to something heard about coming. 来るかもしれない says coming is possible and need not be based on anyone’s report. Inferences and hearsay can overlap in context, but the source matters.

JLPT approach

  1. Read what follows. A question asking for details can naturally follow a tentative report; it helps explain why the speaker chose とか.
  2. Do not choose it merely because the source is unnamed. と言われている and sentence-final という can also omit a named source. Look for vagueness, context, and style together.
  3. Check for a list before calling it hearsay. Repeated nouns or activities with とか may be examples rather than reported news.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

来月、引っ越すとか聞きました。

らいげつ、ひっこすとかききました。

I heard something about you moving next month.

Useful for bringing up information tentatively, before asking the person for details.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 7, printed pages 36–37.

4. ～って

Quote or share news in conversation: “They said…” / “I heard…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

って is a casual way to mark quoted words or reported content. It can replace quotation と before 言う or 聞く. At the end of a sentence, the reporting verb is often left understood.

It works in ordinary conversation among friends, relatives, and classmates. The quote itself can still contain polite words: the book relays a teacher’s 来てください inside a casual message.

って does not automatically make the information doubtful. You might be relaying exactly what you just heard. For a formal written report, use an appropriate と言いました, とのことです, or other reporting expression.

Think of it this wayFind the words being quoted. Then check whether the reporting verb is visible, as in って言ってた, or understood after sentence-final って.

How to form it

Plain verb + って
休むって／来ないって／帰ったって · says they will be absent / will not come / went home. In these report contexts, preserve the content’s tense and negative meaning.
い-adjective + って
忙しいって／おいしかったって · I hear someone is busy / it was delicious. Do not insert だ after the adjective.
Noun or な-adjective + だって
先生だって／元気だって · I hear someone is a teacher / is well. Here だって is [だ] + [って], not one indivisible expression meaning “because.”
Explanatory clause + んだって
できたんだって／先生なんだって · I hear it has opened / the person is a teacher. ん is conversational の; nouns and な-adjectives use な before it.
Keep the spoken request when quoting it
「来てください」って · says “Please come.” A directly quoted request does not need to be converted into 来る just to follow a plain-form chart.
Spoken contraction: 言っていた → 言ってた
休むって言ってた · was saying / said they would take the day off. The first って marks the quote; 言ってた is the reporting verb.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

さん、はってたよ。

Ogawa said that they would take today off.

Clue 休む is the message; 言ってた tells us it was said.

How to read this example

Bracket ［今日は休む］って［言ってたよ］. The speaker reports Ogawa’s plan for today. The report is past, but that does not turn the intended absence into an already completed event.

Example 02

さんのはのだって。

I heard that Sato's wife is a cooking teacher.

Clue 先生だ is the reported statement before sentence-final って.

How to read this example

料理の先生 means a cooking teacher. The ending is ［先生だ］って. It is not the excuse-giving “because” use of だって. The reporting verb is understood.

Example 03

にタイのレストランができたんだって。みようよ。

I heard a Thai restaurant opened in front of the station. Let's try it.

Clue The new restaurant is reported news; the invitation is the speaker’s response.

How to read this example

できた here means a restaurant has opened or been established, not “was able to.” んだって shares that news conversationally. 行ってみようよ proposes trying the restaurant; it is separate from the reported content.

Example 04

、がまでくださいって。

Yamakawa, the teacher says to come to the staff room.

Clue The messenger preserves the teacher’s actual request.

How to read this example

山川君 is the person being addressed. 先生が identifies the teacher as the source. ［教員室まで来てください］って relays “Please come to the staff room.” The polite request remains intact inside the casual quotation.

Do not confuse it with…

Casual report ↔ formal message
今日は休むとのことです can deliver an office message. 今日は休むって is conversational. The content can be the same while the style changes.
Quotation って ↔ topic って
日本語って難しいね means “Japanese is difficult, isn’t it?” with 日本語 as the topic. It does not necessarily mean someone said “Japanese.” Look for quoted content and a reporting situation.
だ + って ↔ other uses of だって
先生だって can report “is a teacher.” 私だってできる means “Even I can do it.” Standalone だって can introduce an excuse, like “But / because…”. Read the whole sentence.
Quoted request ↔ carrying out the request
来てくださいって reports a request. It does not say that the listener has already come. Keep the source, the messenger, and the intended recipient separate.
A quoted statement ↔ a better description · Lesson 4
というより chooses a more suitable description. It is not simply a more formal way of saying って; the full ending changes the function.

JLPT approach

  1. Listen for casual contractions. 言ってた is 言っていた with い omitted. んだって combines explanatory んだ with the reporting って.
  2. Keep quoted politeness separate from the messenger’s style. 来てください can appear inside a casual って message. Do not reject it merely because it contains ください.
  3. Read the full ending. Topic って, hearsay って, and other conversational uses can look alike. Who said what, and to whom, is a stronger clue than spelling alone.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

田中さん、あとで電話するって。

たなかさん、あとででんわするって。

Tanaka says they will call later.

The reporting verb is understood. This fits a casual relay of a message.

5. ～という

A compact report in writing: “It is said that…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Sentence-final ～という reports information in a concise written style. It is useful in articles, historical descriptions, and explanatory passages. The sentence can end at という; it is not necessarily missing a following noun.

The book’s examples report an area’s former appearance, a festival’s history, and an account of tofu reaching Japan. A named source is not compulsory, but the ending still frames the information as reported.

The form does not make a historical account certain. Preserve any approximation or uncertainty in the statement. This lesson teaches how the writer presents the account, rather than independently verifying each historical claim.

Think of it this wayLook at the sentence boundary: a complete statement + という + full stop often gives a compact written report. If a noun follows, examine what という is connecting to that noun.

How to form it

Plain verb + という
始まるという／伝わったという／行われてきたという · it is said that something begins / was transmitted / has been held over time. Do not reduce a meaningful compound verb to dictionary form.
い-adjective + という
冬は寒いという · it is said that winters are cold. Keep the adjective’s い.
Noun or な-adjective + だ／だった + という
静かだという／野原だったという · reportedly quiet / reportedly used to be a field. The past state is inside the reported clause.
Negative report
使われていないという · it is said that something is not in use. The negative belongs to the reported situation, not to the fact of reporting.
Read the two layers separately
行われてきた + という · 行う → 行われる is passive; てきた looks back over development or continuation up to the reference point. という then reports that history.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

このは、だったという。

It is said that this area used to be a broad field.

Clue 昔 and だった place the reported landscape in the past.

How to read this example

広い describes 野原, “a broad field.” 野原だった is a complete noun predicate in the past. という reports the former state; it does not say the area is still an open field today.

Example 02

このはでかられてきたという。

It is said that this festival has been held in the village since long ago.

Clue The passive and てきた describe a tradition continuing over time.

How to read this example

行われる means “be held / carried out.” 行われてきた looks back over its continuation. 古くから supplies “since long ago,” while という presents this history as reported information.

Example 03

は1300ぐらいにからにという。

It is said that tofu reached Japan from China around 1,300 years ago.

Clue The account gives an approximate time, an origin, and a destination.

How to read this example

1300年ぐらい前 is “about 1,300 years ago.” 中国から is the origin and 日本に the destination. 伝わった is past “reached / was transmitted”; という attributes the account rather than turning the date into a verified exact figure.

Do not confuse it with…

Written report ↔ casual report
店が閉まるという suits a written report that a shop will close. 店が閉まるって shares that information conversationally. Style changes; the closing is still future or nonpast in the reported clause.
Sentence-final report ↔ naming a noun
「さくら」という店 means “a shop called Sakura.” Here という connects the name to 店. No one needs to have reported that a shop will do something.
Sentence-final report ↔ content before a noun
店が閉まるという知らせ means “news that the shop will close.” という links the clause to 知らせ. With a full stop after 店が閉まるという, という itself finishes the written report.
Reported account ↔ an explanatory conclusion
ということだ can either report information or explain an implication. Do not decide solely from the shared という; read the full construction and surrounding passage.
伝わる ↔ the cause marker から · Lesson 5
中国から日本に伝わった gives an origin and destination: from China to Japan. から can mark a cause elsewhere, but here it marks where the transmission began.

JLPT approach

  1. Read through to the punctuation. という can finish a report or connect to a following noun. This distinction is useful in sentence-order questions.
  2. Keep past and ongoing-history forms intact. だった reports a former state; 行われてきた describes a tradition continuing over time.
  3. Keep approximations in the meaning. ぐらい means “about” in the tofu example. The reporting ending does not turn an approximate account into an exact date.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

この橋は百年前に建てられたという。

このはしはひゃくねんまえにたてられたという。

It is said that this bridge was built a hundred years ago.

A sample sentence in a written historical description. 建てられた is passive past; という marks the report.
