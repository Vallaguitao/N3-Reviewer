Lesson 9: Wishes and requests ～と望む

Separate three kinds of wish: wanting someone else to act, asking for permission to act yourself, and hoping for a favorable outcome. Then distinguish hopes from advice.

1. ～てもらいたい・～ていただきたい・～てほしい

“I want someone to do this” — identify the other person’s action

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～てもらいたい, ～ていただきたい, and ～てほしい express a wish for someone else to do something. In the book, someone listens, checks documents, helps, stays nearby, or refrains from unwanted behavior. The person doing that action may be named, understood, or left unspecified.

てもらいたい presents the action as something the speaker wants to receive from another person. ていただきたい uses the humble counterpart of もらう, showing respect toward the person providing the action. てほしい focuses directly on the action or situation the speaker wants.

When addressed to the person concerned, a wish can function as a request. It can also be a private wish or a comment about absent people. Adding です or using いただきたい does not automatically make any demand gentle. The relationship, wording, and tone still matter; んですが can leave room for a response.

Think of it this wayAsk two questions: “Who wants this?” and “Who will listen, check, help, or stay?” In these examples, the desired action is performed by someone other than the speaker.

How to form it

Verb て-form + もらいたい／いただきたい／ほしい
聞いてもらいたい／見ていただきたい／いてほしい · want someone to listen / would like someone to look / want someone to stay. Build the verb’s て-form first.
Name the person with に
友達に聞いてもらいたい／親に休んでほしい · want a friend to listen / want one’s parents to rest. に identifies the person whose action is wanted in these patterns.
Wish for no action: ない-form + で + ending
行かないでほしい／話さないでもらいたい · want someone not to go / not to speak. Use ないで, not なくて, in this negative-request pattern.
Negate たい: たい → たくない
手伝ってもらいたくない · do not want to receive help. The action 手伝う is affirmative; the wish to receive it is negative.
Negate ほしい: ほしい → ほしくない
無理をしてほしくない · do not want someone to overexert themselves. 無理をしないでほしい expresses the closely related wish that they refrain from it.
Polite or past wish
見ていただきたいです／来てほしかった · would like you to look / wanted you to come. A past wish does not by itself confirm whether it was fulfilled.
Softer introduction: ～たいんですが
確認していただきたいんですが · “I would like you to check this…” The unfinished が invites the listener’s response; it is not necessarily missing information in a dialogue.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

だれかにのをもらいたいとことがあります。

Sometimes I want someone to listen to my worries.

Clue Someone else listens; the speaker wants to be heard.

How to read this example

だれかに leaves the listener unspecified. 自分の悩み refers to the speaker’s worries here. と思うことがあります makes it an occasional feeling, not a request being issued every time.

Example 02

この、ちょっといただきたいんですが。

I would like you to take a look at these documents.

Clue The listener is being asked to look at the documents.

How to read this example

見て is the ordinary て-form, so the requested action belongs to the listener. いただきたい is humble wording for receiving that action. ちょっと and んですが help frame the request; they do not guarantee that it will take only a moment.

Example 03

このはだれにももらいたくない。でやりたい。

I do not want anyone to help me with this job. I want to do it alone.

Clue The speaker rejects help because they want to work alone.

How to read this example

だれにも pairs with the negative to mean “not from anyone.” もらいたくない negates the wish to receive help. 自分一人でやりたい confirms that independence, not lack of available helpers, is the issue.

Example 04

ずっとぼくのそばにいてほしい。へでほしい。

I want you to stay beside me. I do not want you to go far away.

Clue The two wishes describe staying close and avoiding separation.

How to read this example

いてほしい asks for continued presence. 行かないでほしい asks the other person not to go far away. The second clause is a negative action plus でほしい, not an inability to travel.

Example 05

これこののをこわさないでほしい。

I want people not to damage this village's natural environment any further.

Clue The wish is that further damage should not occur.

How to read this example

こわさないでほしい asks people not to damage the environment. これ以上 means “any further.” The people responsible are not named, so this can be a general appeal rather than a direct request to one listener.

Example 06

にはもうをしてほしくない。

I do not want my aging parents to overexert themselves anymore.

Clue The speaker wants the parents to avoid overexertion.

How to read this example

親には makes the parents the people whose behavior is being discussed, with は highlighting them. してほしくない negates the wish for them to overdo things. It is close in this context to 無理をしないでほしい.

Do not confuse it with…

My action ↔ your action
話したい = “I want to speak.” 話してほしい = “I want you/someone to speak.” Do not translate both as the speaker doing the action.
Your action ↔ permission for my action
見ていただきたい asks the other person to look. 見させていただきたい asks to be allowed to look oneself. The extra causative changes the roles.
A wanted object ↔ a wanted action
新しい本がほしい wants a book. 本を読んでほしい wants someone to read. Look for the verb in て-form before ほしい.
An unfulfilled wish ↔ inability · Lesson 8
手伝ってもらいたくない means help is unwanted, not unavailable. 手伝ってもらえるはずがない would instead express a strong judgment that receiving help is impossible.
People ↔ a desired situation
早く暖かくなってほしい expresses a wish for warmer weather. てほしい is not limited to commands addressed to a person, although this lesson’s main examples concern others’ actions.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the actor before translating. In だれかに聞いてもらいたい, someone else listens and the speaker wants that help.
  2. Locate the negative. 行かないでほしい asks for nondeparture; 手伝ってもらいたくない rejects receiving help. Do not treat either as “cannot.”
  3. Distinguish a wish from its fulfillment. たい and ほしい tell you what is wanted. They do not prove that the action happens or that the listener agrees.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

この内容を確認していただきたいんですが。

このないようをかくにんしていただきたいんですが。

I would like you to check this information.

The listener does the checking. いただきたい respectfully presents the action the speaker would like to receive.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 9, printed pages 44–45.

2. ～（さ）せてもらいたい・～（さ）せていただきたい・～（さ）せてほしい

“I would like to be allowed to…” — the speaker wants to act

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Causative て-form + もらいたい／いただきたい／ほしい often asks for permission or an opportunity for the speaker to act. The employees want time to eat slowly; the worker wants permission to leave early; the volunteer wants the chance to make the poster.

Compare 見ていただきたい = “I would like you to look” with 見させていただきたい = “I would like you to let me look.” The person granting permission and the person doing the underlying action have different roles.

The negative examples need special care. 言わせないでほしい can mean “Please do not make me say it.” Here the causative is about being made to act, rather than receiving permission. Use the situation to choose “let” or “make.” Do not translate every causative as force or every negative causative as refusal of permission.

Think of it this waySeparate three roles: the person who wants something, the person who permits or causes it, and the person who actually eats, leaves, speaks, or practices.

How to form it

Group 1: final sound → あ-row + せる
書く → 書かせる → 書かせて；帰る → 帰らせる → 帰らせて · let/make write or return. 帰る is Group 1 even though it ends in る.
Group 1 ending in う: わせる
言う → 言わせる → 言わせて · let/make say. Use わ, not あ: 言わせる, not 言あせる.
Group 2: drop る + させる
食べる → 食べさせる → 食べさせて；見る → 見させる → 見させて · let/make eat or look. The book uses these full causative forms.
Irregular verbs
する → させる → させて；来る → 来させる（こさせる）→ 来させて · let/make do or come. For a する noun: 練習する → 練習させる.
Add the desired permission
帰らせて + いただきたい；作らせて + ほしい · would like to be allowed to leave / want to be allowed to make it. Conjugate the causative first, then add the wish.
Negative causative: せる／させる → せないで／させないで
言わせないでもらいたい／練習させないでほしい · want someone not to make me repeat it / not to make us practice. The negative is inside the causative action.
Make the underlying actor explicit
わたしに作らせてほしい · “I want you/them to let me make it.” Here わたしに marks the person who would do the making. Context determines the omitted permission-giver.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

Example 01

A「がよね。をもっとゆっくりさせてもらいたいね。」B「そうだね。にみよう。」

Clerk A: “Our lunch break is short. I wish we could be allowed to eat more slowly.” Clerk B: “Right. Let's talk to the manager.”

Clue The employees want the workplace to give them enough time to eat slowly.

How to read this example

食べさせてもらいたい refers to the employees doing the eating, not someone feeding them. 昼休みが短い explains the restriction. 店長に言ってみよう proposes raising the issue with the manager.

Example 02

はへばならないので、せていただきたいのですが……。

I have to go to immigration today, so I would like permission to leave early.

Clue The speaker wants permission to leave, not the listener to leave.

How to read this example

帰る becomes 帰らせて before いただきたい. 行かなければならない supplies the obligation to visit immigration, while ので introduces that reason. The unfinished のですが leaves space for the supervisor’s response.

Example 03

それはさっきもしたことだよ。もことをせないでもらいたいよ。

I already explained that. I do not want you to make me say the same thing repeatedly.

Clue The speaker wants to avoid being made to repeat the explanation.

How to read this example

言う → 言わせる → 言わせないで. 何度も and 同じこと identify the unwanted repetition. さっきも説明した explains the irritation; もらいたい here does not make the complaint especially gentle.

Example 04

のポスターはわたしにせてほしいなあ。

I wish they would let me make the cultural festival poster.

Clue わたしに identifies who wants the chance to make the poster.

How to read this example

作る → 作らせて. The speaker wants others to allow or assign the poster-making to them. なあ gives the line a wishful tone rather than confirming that the task has already been assigned.

Example 05

こんなにで４もをさせないでほしいです。

I do not want them to make us practice on the field for four hours on such a hot day.

Clue The unwanted situation is being made to practice for an excessive duration.

How to read this example

練習をさせないでほしい asks those in charge not to impose the practice. ４時間も emphasizes “as much as four hours.” The speaker or group would do the practicing; the hot day explains the objection.

Do not confuse it with…

Other person’s action ↔ my permitted action
作ってもらいたい wants someone to make it for the speaker. 作らせてもらいたい wants the opportunity to make it oneself. The difference is not merely a higher level of politeness.
Want to act ↔ want permission to act
帰りたい expresses a desire to leave. 帰らせていただきたい presents leaving as something the speaker would like the other person to allow. Neither statement itself grants permission.
Positive permission ↔ avoiding compulsion
言わせてほしい can mean “Let me say it.” 言わせないでほしい can mean “Do not make me say it,” as when repetition is burdensome. Context supplies the intended causative meaning.
Wanting to cause ↔ wanting to receive permission
食べさせたい wants to let or make someone eat. 食べさせてもらいたい in the lunch-break example wants permission or circumstances that allow the employees themselves to eat.
Reason for the request · Lesson 5
Reason expressions and the speaker’s stance matter in requests. The book uses ので to explain the immigration-office visit before asking to leave early; the reason is not evidence that permission has already been granted.

JLPT approach

  1. Do the role check before choosing the polite form. 見ていただきたい and 見させていただきたい can both be polite, but ask for different people to look.
  2. Build the causative in stages. 帰る → 帰らせる → 帰らせて → 帰らせていただきたい. This avoids confusing Group 1 verbs ending in る with Group 2.
  3. For a negative, ask what burden is being resisted. Repeating an explanation and practicing four hours in heat point to “do not make me/us,” not “do not let me/us.”

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

この資料を使わせていただきたいのですが。

このしりょうをつかわせていただきたいのですが。

I would like permission to use these materials.

The speaker would use the materials and seeks the other person’s permission. The basic させていただく pattern presents permission and a benefit to the speaker.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 9, printed pages 44–45.

For the permission-and-benefit basis of させていただく, see the Agency for Cultural Affairs, honorific-language guidance.

3. ～といい・～ばいい・～たらいい

Same conditional endings, two jobs: hoping for an outcome or giving advice

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～といい／～ばいい／～たらいい can express either a hope or advice. The conditional presents something as good if it happens. The situation tells you whether the speaker is wishing for that outcome or recommending a course of action.

Hope: “I hope we can meet again” or “I hope I can pass.” The desired outcome cannot be secured merely by deciding to do it. This does not mean the speaker has no influence: studying can help, but wishing to pass is different from announcing a plan to study.

Advice: “You should rest” or “Why not ask the office staff?” The speaker recommends an action the listener could take. The three endings overlap in these uses. ばいい can sound like a simple solution and sometimes sound dismissive; context and tone matter more than a fixed politeness ranking.

Think of it this wayAsk “Is someone wishing for a favorable outcome, or offering a practical action?” Read the subject, the situation, and endings such as なあ, ね, よ, or a following explanation together.

How to form it

といい: plain nonpast clause + といい
会えるといい／雨が降らないといい／天気がいいといい · hope we can meet / it does not rain / the weather is good. For noun or な-adjective predicates, use だ: 晴れだといい／静かだといい.
ばいい: verb ば-form + いい
会う → 会えばいい；できる → できればいい · should meet / hope it is possible, depending on context. Group 1 uses the え-row + ば; Group 2 drops る and adds れば.
Adjectives and negative ば-forms
暖かければいい／行かなければいい／病気でなければいい · hope it is warm / should not go / hope it is not an illness. い → ければ; negative ない → なければ.
たらいい: plain past-shaped form + らいい
会えたらいい／暖かかったらいい／休みだったらいい · hope we can meet / it is warm / it is a day off. The form is built from the past shape, but the hoped-for event can be in the future.
Advice with a controllable action
休むといい／休めばいい／休んだらいい · rest would be a good course of action. The book’s advice uses an action the listener can choose.
Hope about one’s own opportunity or ability
行けるといい／合格できたらいい · hope I can go / pass. Potential forms make clear that the wish concerns the possibility or outcome, rather than just an intention to act.
Common endings
といいですね／たらいいなあ／なければいいが… · a shared or listener-oriented hope / a personal wish / an anxious hope left unfinished. These are contextual clues, not automatic answer-key rules.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the connection.

A. Hope — a favorable outcome is desired

Example 01

【で】「このクラスもでおです。いつかまたみんなでといいですね。」

At graduation, the teacher says: “We part today. I hope we can all meet again someday.”

Clue The teacher hopes that another meeting will become possible.

How to read this example

会える is the potential of 会う. いつか and また place the hoped-for reunion in the future. といいですね shares the hope with the class; it does not fix a date or instruct everyone to meet now.

Example 02

ずっとのが。でなければいいが……。

I have felt unwell lately. I hope it is not a serious illness.

Clue The speaker hopes the feared diagnosis is not the case.

How to read this example

病気でない becomes 病気でなければ before いい. が… leaves the worry hanging. The sentence expresses an anxious hope; it does not establish what the illness is.

Example 03

あしたはだ。がんばろう。できたらいいなあ。

The entrance exam is tomorrow. I will do my best. I hope I can pass.

Clue The exam is tomorrow, so the past-shaped たら expresses a future hope.

How to read this example

合格できる → 合格できたら. The speaker can choose to work hard, as がんばろう shows, but passing is the outcome hoped for. いいなあ adds a personal wishful tone.

B. Advice — a helpful action is suggested

Example 04

いるようですね。あしたはゆっくりといいですよ。

You look tired. You should rest well tomorrow.

Clue Observed tiredness leads to a recommendation to rest.

How to read this example

疲れているようですね gives the reason for the advice. 休む is an action the listener can take tomorrow. といいですよ recommends it; the speaker is not merely hoping that rest happens by chance.

Example 05

その、のならばいいんじゃないですか。

If you do not feel like doing that job, why not decline it?

Clue The proposed solution is not accepting a job the listener is reluctant to do.

How to read this example

気が進まないのなら supplies the condition. 引き受けなければいい suggests nonacceptance. いいんじゃないですか is a suggestion framed as a question, not a command or the obligation なければならない.

Example 06

のがわからなければ、のにみたらいいですよ。

If you do not know how to fill out the application, you should ask the office staff.

Clue The first conditional identifies the problem; the second proposes a solution.

How to read this example

わからなければ means “if you do not know.” 聞いてみたらいい recommends trying to ask the office staff. てみる means trying an action here, and たら does not report that the question has already been asked.

Do not confuse it with…

A wish ↔ advice with the same words
早く帰れるといいですね hopes the person can leave early. 疲れているなら、早く帰るといいですよ advises leaving early. The potential form and surrounding situation help distinguish the two.
Future hope ↔ past regret · Lesson 6
合格できたらいい hopes for a possible result. もっと勉強すればよかった regrets past preparation. The final いい versus よかった and the time context matter.
Negative advice ↔ obligation
引き受けなければいい suggests not accepting. 引き受けなければならない says one must accept. Read the words after なければ; the two expressions point in opposite directions.
Hope ↔ a declared intention · Lesson 11
勉強するつもりだ states a plan to study. 合格できたらいい hopes to pass. A plan concerns intended action; a hope does not announce a guaranteed result.
Hope for an outcome ↔ asking someone to act
手伝ってほしい wants another person’s help. 手伝ってもらえるといい expresses hope that receiving help will be possible. It need not be a direct request to the helper.
Giving advice ↔ asking for advice
どうすればいいですか asks “What should I do?” 聞いてみたらいいですよ supplies a suggestion. The question ending changes the direction of the exchange.

JLPT approach

  1. Classify the sentence before comparing endings. An uncertain exam result suggests hope; a practical solution to a problem suggests advice.
  2. Do not read every たら as past time. Tomorrow’s exam can be followed by 合格できたらいい. Morphological form and event time are different.
  3. Read the complete negative ending. なければいい recommends or hopes for nonaction; なければならない expresses obligation. Stopping at なければ creates a major trap.
  4. Use tone as supporting evidence. なあ often marks a personal wish, but it can appear in other contexts. The meaning of the whole exchange should decide.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

明日、晴れるといいですね。

あした、はれるといいですね。

I hope it is sunny tomorrow.

The speaker hopes for favorable weather, rather than instructing someone to make it sunny.
