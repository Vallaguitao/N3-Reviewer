1. ～はずがない・～わけがない

Reject a possibility strongly: “There is no way…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A はずがない／わけがない means the speaker is convinced A cannot be true or cannot happen. The conviction comes from the speaker’s understanding of the situation: a promise, a phone confirmation, the cost of a house, or the purpose of training.

はずがない often challenges something that conflicts with an expectation. わけがない can sound like a forceful rejection: “There is simply no way.” Their uses overlap. Do not memorize a rigid rule that one is always objective and the other always emotional. Both express the speaker’s judgment, which can be mistaken.

If A itself is negative, the speaker rejects that negative possibility: 来ないはずがない = “There is no way he will not come,” so the speaker strongly expects him to come. Read the inner statement before interpreting the outer denial.

Think of it this wayWrite the statement inside the pattern first. Then put “There is no way that [statement]” around it. Does the resulting belief fit the reason given?

How to form it

Plain verb + はずがない／わけがない
来るはずがない／買えるわけがない · there is no way someone will come / I can buy it. Potential 買える adds ability; the pattern alone does not turn every verb into “can.”
Negative or past clause stays intact
来ないはずがない／忘れたわけがない · cannot fail to come / cannot have forgotten. Keep the meaning of the entire clause before the ending.
い-adjective + ending
易しいはずがない／きびしくないわけがない · cannot be easy / cannot be anything other than demanding. Do not put だ after an い-adjective.
な-adjective: な + ending
簡単なはずがない／簡単なわけがない · cannot be simple. Formal である can also introduce the predicate.
Noun: の + ending
休みのはずがない／学生のわけがない · cannot be a day off / cannot be a student. The book also gives formal である; learn noun の as the basic connection.
Polite and emphatic variants
はずがありません／はずはありません／わけがありません · the denial remains strong. は can emphasize the rejection, as in the shop example; it does not reverse the meaning.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

ちゃんとしたんだから、がはずがない。どうしたのかなあ。

He promised properly, so there is no way he will not come. I wonder what happened.

Clue A firm promise makes the speaker reject the possibility of nonarrival.

How to read this example

Bracket ［彼が来ない］はずがない. The speaker expects him to come and is puzzled by the present situation. どうしたのかなあ adds “I wonder what happened,” rather than canceling that expectation.

Example 02

あのがのはずはありません。でんですから。

There is no way that shop is closed today. I confirmed it by phone.

Clue The phone confirmation is the reason for rejecting “closed today.”

How to read this example

休み is a noun, so use 休みのはず. はありません is a polite, emphatic negative. 電話で確認した explains why the speaker thinks the shop should be open.

Example 03

なのだからはずがない。がんばらなくては……。

It is a national examination, so there is no way it will be easy. I have to work hard...

Clue The speaker’s view of the exam leads to an expectation of difficulty.

How to read this example

易しいはずがない rejects “easy.” 国家試験なのだから supplies the premise. がんばらなくては leaves an obligation such as ならない understood: “I must work hard.”

Example 04

こんなに、わたしにわけがないでしょう。

There is no way I could buy such a large house.

Clue The large house is beyond what the speaker believes they can buy.

How to read this example

買う → 買える is the potential form. ［わたしに買える］わけがない strongly rejects that possibility. でしょう invites agreement here; it does not make the house affordable.

Example 05

にためにいるのだ。がきびしくないわけがない。

We are practicing to win the match. There is no way the practice would not be demanding.

Clue The speaker rejects the idea that serious training would be undemanding.

How to read this example

きびしくない is the inner negative. Rejecting it gives a strong expectation of demanding practice. 勝つために names the goal of winning; this is the purpose use of ために.

Do not confuse it with…

来ないはずだ ↔ 来るはずがない
来ないはずだ expects nonarrival; 来るはずがない strongly rules out arrival. Neither means 来ないはずがない, which rejects nonarrival.
Strong denial ↔ correcting an assumption
買えるわけがない rules out being able to buy it. 買えるわけではない rejects an assumption that one can buy it. がない and ではない do different work.
Strong denial ↔ cautious affirmation
できないことはない admits that something is possible, often with reservations. できないわけがない strongly rejects inability. Two negatives do not always convey the same degree of confidence.
Reason for confidence · Lesson 5
のだから often supplies the reason the speaker treats as established. “I told him last week” supports “He cannot be unaware,” not “He cannot know.”

JLPT approach

  1. Bracket the inner negative. ［知らない］わけがない rejects “does not know”; the speaker is confident the person knows.
  2. Use the preceding reason. A confirmed opening date and a firm promise explain the speaker’s expectation. Match the conclusion to that evidence.
  3. Keep confidence separate from truth. This is a grammar of strong belief, not a guarantee that promises are kept or careful people never get sick.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

本人に確認したのだから、間違いのはずがありません。

ほんにんにかくにんしたのだから、まちがいのはずがありません。

I checked with the person concerned, so it cannot be a mistake.

The speaker treats direct confirmation as the basis for confidence.

2. ～とは限らない

Leave room for exceptions: “Not necessarily…” / “Not always…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A とは限らない means A cannot be assumed to hold in every relevant case. It challenges an absolute claim or an expected result while leaving other possibilities open.

An old song need not be known by everyone. A high price does not guarantee quality. A newspaper report is not automatically true. Words such as みんな, いつも, 必ず, and 絶対 help identify the broad claim being challenged.

It does not assert the opposite as a certainty. “Not everyone knows” does not mean “nobody knows.” If the inner statement is negative, as in けがをしないとは限らない, the speaker leaves injury possible—not certain.

Think of it this wayAsk “What is being treated as guaranteed?” Remove that guarantee, but do not replace it with a guarantee of the opposite.

How to form it

Plain verb + とは限らない
知っているとは限らない／成功するとは限らない · not necessarily know / not necessarily succeed. The whole clause is the claim under discussion.
Negative clause + とは限らない
けがをしないとは限らない · one might get injured. It is not guaranteed that no injury will occur.
い-adjective + とは限らない
高いとは限らない／質がいいとは限らない · not necessarily expensive / good quality. Keep い.
Noun or な-adjective + (だ)とは限らない
本当のこと（だ）とは限らない／安全（だ）とは限らない · not necessarily true / safe. The book permits だ or its omission here; do not substitute noun の or adjective な mechanically.
Common adverbs and polite ending
必ずしも正しいとは限りません · not necessarily correct. 必ずしも works with a negative expression; it does not mean a positive “certainly correct.”

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

このはからわれているが、がみんないるとは。

Although this song has been sung for a long time, it does not mean every Japanese person knows it.

Clue みんな is the part of the knowledge claim that is too broad.

How to read this example

The song’s long history does not guarantee that every Japanese person knows it. The sentence does not claim that no Japanese people know the song.

Example 02

がものががいいとは。

Expensive things are not necessarily always good quality.

Clue A high price is not a guarantee of good quality.

How to read this example

必ず falls within the claim challenged by とは限らない. Some expensive things may be good quality; the price alone is insufficient to assume that result.

Example 03

にけがをしないとは。においたほうがいいですよ。

You might get injured while traveling. It is better to have insurance.

Clue The negative safety claim is not guaranteed, so injury remains possible.

How to read this example

［けがをしない］とは限りません means you cannot rule out injury. 入っておいた adds arranging something in advance in the book’s insurance advice. It does not predict a certain accident.

Example 04

にあることがいつものこと（だ）とは。

What is written in a newspaper is not always true.

Clue いつも is denied, rather than every newspaper statement.

How to read this example

書いてある describes information written in the newspaper. 本当のこと is a noun phrase, with optional だ before とは限らない. The point is “not always true,” not “always false.”

Do not confuse it with…

Possible exception ↔ impossible event
勝てるとは限らない means a win is not guaranteed. 勝てるわけがない rules out being able to win in the speaker’s judgment.
Not guaranteed ↔ not the right explanation
わけではない often corrects an assumption arising from a situation. とは限らない focuses on whether a claim necessarily holds. They can overlap in “not always” contexts, so context matters.
Negative claim with an exception
勝てないとは限らない leaves winning possible. It is weaker than “we will definitely win.” Always identify the negative inside the quotation boundary と.
Widely reported ↔ invariably true · Lesson 7
と言われている reports what people say. A commonly repeated view can still have exceptions; reporting it does not prove universal truth.
Guarantee ↔ sufficient condition · Lesson 6
さえ…ば presents a condition as sufficient from the speaker’s perspective. とは限らない can challenge a presumed guarantee, such as assuming high price always ensures quality.

JLPT approach

  1. Circle the broad word. みんな, 必ず, and いつも often tell you exactly what is too sweeping.
  2. Translate a negative clause in two stages. “They absolutely cannot win” + “not necessarily” leaves a chance of winning.
  3. Read the contrast. “They are not a strong team, but…” prepares a less pessimistic conclusion, not another reason to dismiss them.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

有名な店が、必ずしもおいしいとは限りません。

ゆうめいなみせが、かならずしもおいしいとはかぎりません。

A famous restaurant does not necessarily serve good food.

Fame does not guarantee the quality being discussed; the sentence does not dismiss every famous restaurant.

3. ～わけではない・～というわけではない・～のではない

Correct an assumption: “It is not that…” / “That does not mean…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～わけではない, ～というわけではない, and ～のではない reject a conclusion or explanation someone might assume. Keeping a borrowed book for a long time does not mean forgetting it. Leaving a workplace does not necessarily mean a transfer.

Sometimes the correction limits a broad claim: “I cannot answer the phone at just any time.” Sometimes it rejects the proposed explanation outright: “I am not being transferred; I am quitting.” Do not translate every example as “partly true.” Identify exactly which assumption is being rejected.

These forms often lead into the real situation or a qualification. In this lesson, のではない is explanatory denial; other contexts can give the same-looking ending a different role.

Think of it this wayComplete “You might think A, but that is not the right conclusion; actually B.” Read B before deciding what the negative means.

How to form it

Verb or い-adjective + わけではない
忘れていたわけではない／高いわけではない · it is not that I had forgotten / that it is expensive. Preserve the tense of the assumption.
Noun / な-adjective before わけ
休みのわけではない／簡単なわけではない · it is not that it is a day off / simple. Noun な and formal である also occur; the book lists these alternatives. Learn the complete phrase, not “always use だ.”
Clause + というわけではない
好き（だ）というわけではない／学生（だ）というわけではない · it does not mean liking it / being a student. The book allows だ or its omission with noun and な-adjective predicates here.
Clause + のではない
転勤するのではない／高いのではない · it is not a transfer / not that it is expensive. Nouns and な-adjectives use な before の: 休みなのではない／嫌いなのではない.
Spoken and polite versions
わけじゃない／んじゃない／わけではありません · では → じゃ and explanatory の → ん in conversation. These contractions do not reverse the denial.
Connect to the correction
行かないわけではなく、二日目から参加する · it is not that I will not go; I will join from day two. ではなく connects the denied assumption to what actually applies.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

をおしたままでしたが、いたわけではありません。

I kept your book for a long time, but it is not that I had forgotten it.

Clue Keeping the book a long time could look like forgetting; the speaker rejects that explanation.

How to read this example

お借りしたまま describes leaving the borrowed-book situation unchanged. 忘れていたわけではありません denies forgetting. This sentence alone does not tell us the actual reason for the delay.

Example 02

いつでもにわけではありません。はメールでします。

It is not that I can answer the phone at any time. Please contact me by email.

Clue いつでも makes the assumption too broad.

How to read this example

出られる is the potential of 電話に出る, “answer the phone.” The speaker cannot promise availability at any time and requests email contact. They are not saying they can never answer a phone.

Example 03

このが（だ）というわけではないが、といっしょにができて。

It is not that I like this job, but I enjoy being able to work with him.

Clue Enjoying the company does not necessarily mean liking the job itself.

How to read this example

好き is a な-adjective, followed here by optional だ + というわけではない. The positive point is being able to work with him. Do not replace the nuanced correction with “I hate the job.”

Example 04

するのではありません。をんです。

It is not that I am being transferred. I am leaving the company.

Clue A transfer is denied and resignation is given as the actual explanation.

How to read this example

転勤するのではありません rejects the assumed event, not merely its degree. 辞めるんです explains what is actually happening. This is why “all these patterns mean only partial denial” is misleading.

Example 05

A「いいね。でしょう。」B「これはんじゃないの。での。」

A: Nice hat. It must have been expensive. B: I did not buy this. I made it myself.

Clue The reply corrects the assumption that the hat was purchased.

How to read this example

買ったんじゃないの is conversational explanatory denial here. 自分で作った gives the correction: the speaker made it. The ending の does not make this a question in the supplied dialogue.

Do not confuse it with…

わけではない ↔ わけがない
行くわけではない rejects the assumption that one will go. 行くわけがない strongly rejects the possibility of going. Do not ignore では versus が.
Correcting an assumption ↔ leaving an exception
とは限らない challenges a guarantee. わけではない can explain a specific situation, such as why a borrowed book has not been returned.
のではない ↔ のではないか
転勤するのではありません denies a transfer. 転勤するのではありませんか can ask whether it is a transfer. The final question and tone matter; do not stop reading before the ending.
Explanatory denial ↔ prohibition
ふざけるのではない can be a stern “Do not fool around!” in an instructional context. The book’s transfer and hat examples instead correct what happened. The same spelling does not guarantee the same function.
Admitting A ↔ denying A
読んだことは読んだが explicitly admits reading. 読んだわけではない instead rejects the assumption of having read. Locate what is admitted before translating “but.”

JLPT approach

  1. Use the second clause to identify the correction. “I will join on day two” rejects the idea of not going at all.
  2. Pay attention to words such as いつでも. The speaker may deny unlimited availability without denying the ability to answer any call.
  3. Do not automatically turn “not like” into “hate.” Rejecting an assumption that someone likes a job need not assert the strongest opposite feeling.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

行きたくないわけではありません。今日は時間がないんです。

いきたくないわけではありません。きょうはじかんがないんです。

It is not that I do not want to go. I do not have time today.

The second sentence supplies the actual reason instead of unwillingness.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 8, printed pages 38–39.

4. ～ないことはない

Admit something cautiously: “It is not impossible…” / “I do, to some extent…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～ないことはない negates a negative statement to make a qualified affirmation. The speaker allows that something is possible or true, while sounding less positive than a direct “yes.”

Walking to the station is possible but time-consuming. The curry has some appeal, but the speaker prefers more spice. The exam result does cause some worry, even though the immediate feeling is relief.

The reservation comes from context. It may concern effort, taste, willingness, or degree; it need not mean physical ability. A limitation often follows with が, but an explicit “but…” clause is not obligatory.

Think of it this wayStart with the negative statement—“cannot walk,” “not tasty,” “not worried.” Denying that statement admits the positive with reservations, not with maximum enthusiasm.

How to form it

Verb negative + ことはない
行かないことはない · it is not that I never go / I might go, depending on context. The verb is not automatically potential.
Potential negative + ことはない
歩く → 歩ける → 歩けないことはない · it is not impossible to walk. The potential 歩ける is what introduces ability.
い-adjective: くない + ことはない
おいしい → おいしくないことはない · it is not bad / it does have some appeal. Do not attach ない directly to おいしい.
な-adjective or noun: でない + ことはない
心配でないことはない · I cannot say I am not worried. Use でない before ことはない with a な-adjective or noun predicate; do not use the い-adjective ending くない.
Polite final negative
歩けないことはありません · the inner ない stays plain; the final ない becomes ありません. The meaning remains a qualified affirmation.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

ここからまでことはありませんが、かなりがかかりますよ。

It is not impossible to walk from here to the station, but it will take quite a while.

Clue Walking is possible, but the time required makes it less attractive.

How to read this example

歩けない is negative potential. ことはありません denies that inability. かなり means “quite a lot,” so the following clause gives a substantial time reservation.

Example 02

こののカレーもおいしくないことはないが、わたしはもっとのがだ。

This shop's curry is not bad, but I prefer something spicier.

Clue The speaker grants some merit to the curry without making it a favorite.

How to read this example

おいしくないことはない cautiously admits that the curry tastes good. もっと辛いの means a spicier one. The limitation concerns preference, not an inability to eat the curry.

Example 03

のがでないことはないのですが、はほっとしています。

I cannot say I am not worried about the exam result, but now that it is over I feel relieved.

Clue Some worry remains even though the immediate feeling is relief.

How to read this example

［心配でない］ことはない rejects being completely unworried. 終わってほっとしている gives the current relief. The speaker can experience some worry and relief together.

Do not confuse it with…

Qualified possibility ↔ strong confidence
できないことはない cautiously admits ability. できないわけがない strongly rejects inability. Do not apply a single tone to every double negative.
ないことはない ↔ plain verb + ことはない
行かないことはない can admit going. 行くことはない may mean “there is no need to go” or “never go,” depending on context. The inner ない is essential to this lesson’s double-negative pattern.
Weak affirmation ↔ correction of an assumption
嫌いなわけではない denies the assumption of dislike. 嫌いでないことはない weakly admits dislike. Do not lose track of which feeling the sentence actually allows.
Qualified affirmation ↔ explicit concession
泳げることは泳げるが explicitly grants swimming ability before stating a limitation. ないことはない reaches a qualified affirmation by denying a negative statement.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the exact inner predicate. 歩けない concerns ability; おいしくない concerns taste; 心配でない concerns a feeling.
  2. Look for the reservation after が. “We get along reasonably well, but rarely spend time together” fits a cautious affirmation followed by a limitation.
  3. Keep the strength modest. “Not impossible” does not mean “easy,” and “not bad” does not mean “my favorite.”

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

手伝えないことはありませんが、少し時間がかかります。

てつだえないことはありませんが、すこしじかんがかかります。

I could help, but it will take a little time.

The speaker admits the possibility of helping while setting an expectation about the time involved.

5. ～ことは～が、…

Admit a point, then qualify it: “I do…, but…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A ことは A が admits A and then explains why that fact is not the whole story. The predicate is repeated: “I did read it, but…” or “It is expensive, certainly, but…”.

The second clause carries the qualification: reading did not produce understanding; swimming ability is limited; photographs make an expensive book appealing. The qualification can be positive or negative. が does not automatically require a complaint.

This is a concession, not a denial of the admitted fact. First establish what the speaker grants, then read what matters more in this context.

Think of it this wayTranslate the repeated part as “Yes, A is true.” Then ask what the clause after が adds that changes how we should evaluate A.

How to form it

Repeat the verb or complete verb expression
読むことは読むが／書いてあることは書いてあるが · do read, but / is written there, but. Preserve meaningful parts such as potential forms or てある.
Past admission
読んだことは読んだが · did read, but. The repeated た-form confirms the past action; it is not the experience pattern たことがある.
Plain first part, polite second part
泳げることは泳げますが · I can swim, but. The predicate matches in meaning even though the second occurrence is polite.
い-adjective: repeat the adjective
高いことは高いが · it is expensive, certainly, but. Keep い before こと; do not insert だ.
な-adjective: な before こと, だ before が
大変なことは大変だが · it is difficult, certainly, but. The first occurrence modifies こと, while the second is the predicate.
A fuller explanatory ending
読んだことは読んだんですが · I did read it, but… The added んです gives an explanatory conversational tone. が or けれど can introduce the qualification.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

からのはことはんですが、がよくわかりませんでした。

I did read his letter, but I did not understand the meaning well.

Clue The speaker admits reading but separates it from understanding.

How to read this example

読んだ is repeated, with the second occurrence followed by んですが. 意味がよくわからなかった is the limitation: reading the letter did not result in understanding it well.

Example 02

わたしはことはが、はだめなんです。

I can swim, but I cannot manage long distances.

Clue The ability exists, but it does not extend to long distances.

How to read this example

泳げる is potential and 泳げます is its polite counterpart. The wording does not deny all swimming ability. 長い距離はだめ limits the distance.

Example 03

このはことはが、がてそうだ。

This book is expensive, but it has many photographs and looks enjoyable.

Clue The admitted drawback is followed by an appealing feature.

How to read this example

高いことは高い grants the high price. 写真が多くて supplies the attraction; 楽しめそう suggests that the book looks enjoyable. が here leads to a positive qualification.

Example 04

をのはなことはだが、がでさを。

Raising children is certainly difficult, but their growth is enjoyable and makes you forget the hardship.

Clue The difficulty is acknowledged alongside the joy of seeing growth.

How to read this example

大変な modifies こと, then 大変だ acts as the predicate. 成長が楽しみ explains why the difficulty is not the whole assessment. The speaker does not deny that raising children is demanding.

Do not confuse it with…

Admission ↔ denial of an assumption
読んだことは読んだ admits reading. 読んだわけではない rejects the assumption of reading. These cannot be swapped without changing the message.
Repeated admission ↔ experience
読んだことがある means “have read it before.” 読んだことは読んだ confirms the reading relevant to this situation. Look for the repeated predicate and は.
Admission ↔ two opposing aspects · Lesson 4
反面 and 一方で connect aspects. Repetition with ことは explicitly grants a point before qualifying it; it is more than merely placing two facts side by side.
Admitted ability ↔ a strong guarantee
泳げることは泳げる admits swimming ability but allows a limitation. 泳げないわけがない strongly rejects inability; it is a different stance.

JLPT approach

  1. Match the whole predicate. 書いてある must be echoed as 書いてあります when admitting that an address is on record. Merely repeating 書く is insufficient.
  2. Read after が before choosing. ニュース番組だけ limits what is watched, so the first part must admit watching television.
  3. Check the direction of the contrast. “A little cold, but no heating needed” qualifies the cold. “Cold, so turn on the heat” would support it instead.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

使えることは使えますが、動きが遅いです。

つかえることはつかえますが、うごきがおそいです。

It does work, but it runs slowly.

The speaker admits usability while warning about performance.
