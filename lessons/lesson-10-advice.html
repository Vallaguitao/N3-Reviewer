1. 命令めいれい（しろ）／禁止（～な）

Direct commands and prohibitions: “Do it!” / “Do not do it!”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

The imperative orders an action; dictionary form + な forbids it. 走れ means “Run!” while 走るな means “Do not run!” These compact forms are forceful in direct conversation.

They appear in coaching, urgent warnings, commands to animals, signs, and quoted instructions. The context can be encouragement rather than anger. Do not treat them as ordinary polite requests, or as forms that only one gender can use. Relationship, situation, and tone determine how they sound.

An imperative can also be reported or explained without being directed at the reader: 止まれという意味です explains the meaning of “stop.” Read the quotation boundary と and the reporting verb before deciding who is giving an order to whom.

Think of it this wayFirst identify whether the quoted action must happen or must stop. Then find the original speaker and the intended recipient of the instruction.

How to form it

Group 1 imperative: final sound → え-row
走る → 走れ；止まる → 止まれ；払う → 払え；話す → 話せ · run / stop / pay / speak. These verbs do not add ろ.
Group 2 imperative: drop る + ろ
やめる → やめろ；降りる → 降りろ；見る → 見ろ · stop / get down / look. Standard やめる is Group 2, so not やめれ.
Irregular verbs
する → しろ；来る → 来い（こい） · do / come. せよ is another imperative of する, often seen in formal written directions.
Special form: くれる → くれ
手伝ってくれ · help me. The familiar request is direct and can sound rough; it is not the polite てください.
Prohibition: dictionary form + な
走るな／降りるな／するな／来るな（くるな） · do not run / get down / do / come. The same attachment works across verb groups. Do not add な to the affirmative imperative.
Report the instruction with と
「止まれ」と言った／飲むなと言われた · said “Stop” / was told not to drink. Quotation marks may be omitted; the original imperative can remain.
Compare なさい and な
読みなさい uses the ます-stem and orders reading. 読むな uses dictionary form and forbids reading. Check the verb form and complete ending, not just the presence of な.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

【で】「、！」

During the match, the coach shouts, “Run! Run!”

Clue The coach uses short imperatives during the match.

How to read this example

走る is Group 1: 走る → 走れ. Repetition adds urgency or encouragement. The coach wants running to happen; this is not a prohibition.

Example 02

に「。」と。はに。

I ordered the dog, “Get down.” The dog obeyed.

Clue The dog is the recipient of a direct command.

How to read this example

降りる is Group 2: 降りる → 降りろ. と命令した reports the command, and 従った says the dog obeyed. Here both the instruction and its fulfillment are stated.

Example 03

はというです。

A red light means “stop.”

Clue The sentence explains the instruction associated with a red light.

How to read this example

止まれ is the imperative of 止まる. という意味です means “means…” and explains the quoted content. The whole sentence is not simply a direct order to the reader.

Example 04

をくれとにみよう。

I will try asking a friend, “Help me move.”

Clue The speaker considers asking a friend for help with moving.

How to read this example

手伝ってくれ is a direct request for the friend to help. と marks that request as the content of 頼む. 頼んでみよう expresses trying to ask; it does not say that the friend has already agreed.

Example 05

に「スピードをな！」とある。

The sign says, “Do not speed!”

Clue The sign forbids speeding.

How to read this example

出す is the dictionary form, followed by prohibitive な. 書いてある describes the instruction written on the sign. 出せ would instead order someone to put out or produce something; the actual phrase is 出すな.

Example 06

はにをなとれている。

My father has been told by his doctor not to drink alcohol.

Clue The doctor has specifically instructed the father not to drink.

How to read this example

父 is the recipient and 医者に names the source of the instruction. 飲むな is a quoted prohibition. と言われている does not make this a general rumor about drinking.

Do not confuse it with…

Imperative ↔ potential
払え = “Pay!”; 払える = “can pay.” For Group 1, the similar え sound can hide a crucial difference. The final る matters.
Direct prohibition ↔ a posted instruction
泳がないこと gives an impersonal rule or instruction. 泳ぐな is a blunt prohibition. Either can appear on a sign, so a sign alone does not determine the answer.
Quoted personal instruction ↔ general hearsay · Lesson 7
と言われている can present a circulating view. In 医者に飲むなと言われている, the doctor is a specific source telling the father not to drink. Passive 言われる is not always “people generally say.”
An order ↔ a wish about another person · Lesson 9
行かないでほしい expresses a wish for someone not to go. 行くな directly prohibits going. Neither means the person is physically unable to go.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the verb group first. 走る and 降りる both end in る, but form 走れ and 降りろ differently.
  2. Keep the original instruction’s direction. If A says “Do not laugh,” B’s report needs 笑うな, not 笑え.
  3. Read beyond the quote. と言われても introduces a response to an instruction. The current speaker may be explaining why compliance is difficult rather than issuing another order.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

「ここで待て」と言われました。

「ここでまて」といわれました。

I was told, “Wait here.”

The quote retains the imperative, while the outer reporting sentence is polite. The speaker is reporting an order, not ordering the listener to wait.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 10, printed pages 46–47.

2. ～こと

State a rule or written instruction: “Be sure to…” / “Do not…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Sentence-final dictionary form + こと gives an instruction; negative plain form + こと tells people not to do something. It presents what is required in an impersonal, rule-like way.

This use is common in notices, written directions, school rules, and posted cautions. A teacher can also say it aloud while laying down requirements. Written style does not mean it can never be spoken.

The instruction normally ends at こと. This is a complete use of the pattern, not a missing main verb. Read the setting and the sentence boundary so you do not confuse it with the many other uses of こと.

Think of it this wayAsk “Is this laying down what the reader or group must do?” A posted notice or teacher’s requirement is a strong clue, but the exact action and its negative still decide the form.

How to form it

Positive instruction: dictionary form + こと
出すこと／読むこと／消すこと · submit / read / turn off. Use the action in its dictionary form, not the imperative or past form.
Negative instruction: ない-form + こと
遅れないこと／泳がないこと／入らないこと · do not be late / swim / enter. There is no で between ない and こと in this pattern.
Keep an adverb with the instruction
必ず出すこと／よく読むこと · be sure to submit / read carefully. The adverb describes how the action is to be carried out.
Condition or timing before the rule
部屋を出るときは、電気を消すこと · when leaving the room, turn off the light. The time clause identifies when the instruction applies.
Bare こと at the end
入らないこと。 gives the notice-style prohibition. Adding だ creates a different expression, often advice or an emphatic explanation, rather than this basic posted-instruction form.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

【で】「レポートはにこと。こと。」

At school, the teacher says: “Submit the report next Monday without fail. Do not be late.”

Clue The teacher gives both a required action and a prohibition against lateness.

How to read this example

出すこと requires submission, and 遅れないこと forbids being late. 来週月曜日に names the submission day. 必ず reinforces the requirement; it does not change the formation.

Example 02

をにをよくこと。

Read the instructions carefully before filling out the application.

Clue The order of actions is part of the instruction.

How to read this example

書く前に means “before filling it out.” よく読むこと tells the reader to study the instructions carefully first. 読む stays in dictionary form before こと.

Example 03

【】からこのでこと。

Sign: Do not swim in this river because it is dangerous.

Clue The sign uses a negative verb to state the prohibition.

How to read this example

泳ぐ → 泳がない, followed directly by こと. 危ないから gives the reason. The sentence does not claim that swimming there is physically impossible.

Do not confuse it with…

Rule こと ↔ recommendation ことだ
早く寝ること can be a rule on a list. 早く寝ることだ can advise someone to get to bed early. Do not add だ automatically merely because English needs a complete sentence.
Rule ↔ noun-like こと
泳ぐことが好きだ means “like swimming.” こと has a following particle and predicate there. 泳がないこと。 ends with a prohibition. Read the complete construction.
Do not enter ↔ cannot enter
入らないこと forbids entering. 入れない uses a potential negative of 入る in the “cannot enter” reading; it does not provide the ordinary instruction not to enter.
Instruction ↔ a decision · Lesson 11
行くことにする means deciding to go. The added にする changes the function; not every こと is a rule.
Instruction ↔ admission · Lesson 8
読むことは読むが admits reading before a qualification. The repeated predicate and は are part of a different pattern.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the label and the ending together. 張り紙 or 立て札 supports a written-rule interpretation, and the sentence ends at こと.
  2. Choose the action to perform. “On leaving, turn off the lights” calls for 消す, not a report that they were turned off.
  3. Separate nonaction from inability. A rule tells you not to enter even if entry is physically possible.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

使用後は、元の場所に戻すこと。

しようごは、もとのばしょにもどすこと。

Return it to its original place after use.

A concise written instruction. 戻す is dictionary form before sentence-final こと.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 10, printed pages 46–47.

3. ～べきだ・～べき／～べきではない

Express a judgment about the right action: “Should…” / “Should not…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～べきだ expresses the speaker’s judgment that an action is proper, advisable, or expected. It can concern responsibility, practical priorities, or what someone ought to have done. It is often stronger than a casual suggestion.

～べきではない means “should not do it.” The negative evaluates the action as inappropriate; it does not normally describe inability. ～べきだった looks back with regret or criticism: “should have done it.”

In this lesson, distinguish a judgment from simply reporting a rule. A person can discuss what laws ought to require using べき, but an exercise asking what is legally allowed calls for the wording that states the restriction directly. Do not turn this distinction into “べき can never discuss laws.”

Think of it this wayAsk “Is the speaker judging what is right, describing a fixed restriction, or predicting what will happen?” “Should” in English can hide these different meanings.

How to form it

Verb dictionary form + べきだ
決めるべきだ／来るべきだ（くるべきだ） · should decide / come. Keep the full dictionary form; 来る does not become き or く before べき.
Special option with する
するべきだ／すべきだ；確認するべきだ／確認すべきだ · should do / check. Both are standard; the shortened すべき is common in writing.
Negative: dictionary form + べきではない
借りるべきではない／辞めるべきではない · should not borrow / quit. For the basic N3 pattern, negate after べき rather than writing 借りないべきだ.
Past judgment
始めるべきだった／言うべきではなかった · should have started / should not have said it. Keep the verb in dictionary form and put the past in the final ending.
Before a noun: べき + noun
やるべきこと／守るべきルール · things that should be done / rules that should be followed. Do not insert だ before the noun.
Negative before a noun
言うべきではないこと · something one should not say. The whole negative expression modifies こと.
Polite or softened presentation
話し合うべきです／話し合うべきだと思います · should discuss / I think we should discuss. Politeness and “I think” can soften delivery, but the underlying judgment remains.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

これはなことですから、もうからべきだとよ。

This is important, so I think we should discuss it more before deciding.

Clue The recommended order is discussion first, decision afterward.

How to read this example

話し合ってから supplies the sequence. 決めるべきだ evaluates what should be done, while と思いますよ presents that judgment to the listener. The sentence does not say a decision has already been made.

Example 02

はたくさんあるが、まず、にやるべきことから。

There is a lot of work, but let us start with what should be done today.

Clue べき modifies the noun こと inside the object of the sentence.

How to read this example

［今日中にやるべきこと］ means tasks that should be done by the end of today. ことから here means “starting with those things,” not the Lesson 5 causal pattern “because of the fact that.” 始めよう proposes that priority.

Example 03

せっかくなのだから、にべきではない。

Since you made the effort to join the company, you should not quit so easily.

Clue The speaker judges quitting lightly to be inappropriate.

How to read this example

せっかく入った会社 and なのだから give the reason for the judgment. 辞めるべきではない is negative advice, not a statement that quitting is impossible or legally forbidden.

Example 04

はまでにいるべきではない。

Children should not stay outside late at night.

Clue The sentence expresses a view about appropriate behavior.

How to read this example

いる remains dictionary form before べきではない. 夜遅くまで sets the duration being criticized. The pattern itself supplies a judgment, not evidence of a specific law.

Example 05

あしたまでのレポートがまだ。もっとからべきだった。

I still have not finished the report due tomorrow. I should have started earlier.

Clue The unfinished report prompts regret about starting too late.

How to read this example

あしたまで means the report is due by tomorrow. 始めるべきだった places the past in だった and evaluates an earlier start as what should have happened. The regret does not mean that the speaker never started at all.

Do not confuse it with…

Judgment ↔ expected fact · Lesson 8
来るべきだ says the person ought to come. 来るはずだ expects the person to come based on what is known. One evaluates conduct; the other predicts or infers.
Should not ↔ not allowed
飲むべきではない states a judgment against drinking. 飲めません can state that drinking is not allowed in a rule-defined setting. A potential form can express restrictions as well as ability.
Past regret ↔ conditional regret · Lesson 6
もっと早く始めるべきだった judges an earlier start to have been the right action. もっと早く始めればよかった expresses regret that starting earlier would have been better. The meanings overlap, with different framing.
Strong recommendation ↔ a proposed option
休んだらどうですか offers rest as a suggestion. 休むべきだ makes a stronger judgment that rest is the proper course. Tone and context can affect both.
Should have ↔ glad I did · Lesson 9
～たらいい／～ばいい can express a future hope or advice. ～べきだった looks back at what ought to have been done; ～てよかった instead welcomes an actual event.

JLPT approach

  1. Check whether “should” means expectation or obligation. A timetable supports はず; a judgment about punctuality supports べき.
  2. Put the negative in the right place. The targeted “should not borrow” form is 借りるべきではない.
  3. Check the noun after べき. やるべきこと is one noun phrase, so べき directly modifies こと.
  4. Use the timeline. A missed opportunity followed by べきだった expresses retrospective regret, not a future hope.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

大事なことは、先に確認すべきだと思います。

だいじなことは、さきにかくにんすべきだとおもいます。

I think important matters should be checked beforehand.

確認すべき uses the shorter する → すべき option. と思います presents the judgment as the speaker’s view.

Explanation references

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 10, printed pages 46–47.

4. ～たらどうか

Offer an action to consider: “How about…?” / “Why not…?”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Verb たら + どうか proposes an action to the other person. It asks, in effect, how that option would be. The book suggests a checkup, rest, changing an address, and apologizing.

たらどうでしょうか sounds more formal and can be gentle; たらどうですか is polite; たらどう？ is casual. A polite ending does not guarantee a kind suggestion. When preceded by blame, “Why don’t you apologize?” can sound critical.

The action is being proposed, not reported as already completed. Although たら is formed from the past-shaped verb, these suggestions concern what the listener could do next.

Think of it this wayAsk “Who is expected to take the proposed action?” If the speaker wants permission to use something themselves, that calls for a permission request, not advice to the listener.

How to form it

Verb た-form + ら + どう
休む → 休んだ → 休んだらどう；変える → 変えたらどう · how about resting / changing it? Keep the correct た-form, including だ where required.
Polite and formal versions
聞いたらどうですか／聞いたらどうでしょうか · why not ask / might you consider asking? Tone and context still matter.
Trying an action
聞いてみたらどうですか · why not try asking? てみる becomes てみたら; みる here means trying an action rather than looking.
Suggest a deliberate change
明るくしたらどうですか／静かにしたらどうですか · how about making it brighter / being quieter? い-adjective く + する and な-adjective に + する describe an action or adjustment.
Bare dictionary label versus actual dialogue
～たらどうか names the pattern in a grammar heading. In the book’s conversations, the full forms include どうでしょうか, どうですか, and casual どう？.

Book examples, with clues

Read the Japanese, check the meaning, then use the clue to explain the relationship.

Example 01

のことがなら、をどうでしょうか。

If you are worried about your health, why not have a medical examination once?

Clue The concern is the condition; a checkup is the proposed action.

How to read this example

心配なら introduces the situation prompting advice. 受けたらどうでしょうか suggests having a checkup rather than reporting one already taken. 一度 here is “once / as a first step,” not a claim that only one checkup is ever appropriate.

Example 02

いるみたいですね。らどうですか。

You look tired. Why don't you rest for a while?

Clue The speaker responds to apparent tiredness with a suggestion.

How to read this example

疲れているみたい describes the impression that motivates the advice. 休む → 休んだら. 少し means a while or a little; the listener is being offered a next action.

Example 03

がの？　じゃ、アドレスをどう？

Are you getting a lot of spam? Then how about changing your address?

Clue The casual suggestion follows a problem with spam.

How to read this example

じゃ connects the reported problem to a possible solution. 変える → 変えたらどう？ suggests changing the address. The speaker has not said they will change it for the listener.

Example 04

のはそっちですよ。らどうですか。

You are the one at fault. Why don't you apologize?

Clue The suggestion is critical because the speaker assigns blame first.

How to read this example

悪いのはそっち says the other person is at fault. 一言謝ったらどうですか urges a brief apology. Despite ですか, the context makes this a reproach rather than neutral advice.

Do not confuse it with…

Advice ↔ asking permission
このパソコンを使ったらどうですか recommends that the listener use the computer. このパソコンを使ってもいいですか asks permission for the speaker to use it. The object can be identical while the actor changes.
Suggestion ↔ hope or advice with いい · Lesson 9
聞いてみたらいいですよ also recommends asking. たらどうですか frames it as an option for consideration. A hopeful 晴れたらいい does not invite the listener to make the weather change.
Deliberate action ↔ a state changing
明るくする means make brighter; 明るくなる means become brighter. To suggest an adjustment the listener can make, use する in this exercise context.
Suggestion ↔ past regret · Lesson 6
早く始めたらどうですか suggests starting early. 早く始めればよかった regrets not starting earlier. Do not infer past time from たら alone.
Supportive suggestion ↔ criticism
“You look tired” prepares supportive advice. “You are at fault” prepares a reproach. The same たらどうですか ending can serve either purpose depending on the lead-in.

JLPT approach

  1. Follow the actor. “I need to look something up” normally leads to a request for the speaker’s own computer use, not a suggestion that someone else use it.
  2. Choose an action that addresses the problem. A dark desk calls for making it brighter, not merely describing it becoming bright.
  3. Read the lead-in for tone. Blame can turn a formally polite suggestion into criticism; ですか does not erase that context.

These are reasoning aids, not guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

一度、担当の人に聞いてみたらどうですか。

いちど、たんとうのひとにきいてみたらどうですか。

Why not try asking the person in charge?

A proposed next step for the listener. 聞いてみる emphasizes trying the action.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Lesson 10, printed pages 46–47.
