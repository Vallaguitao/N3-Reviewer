Book overview · intention and result

「する」と「なる」の使い分けの基本

Choose the viewpoint: someone acts, or a change is described.

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

する focuses on someone making or choosing a result. なる focuses on the change or result itself. The same event can be described from either viewpoint: “I made the room clean” and “The room became clean.”

なる does not mean that nobody caused the change. A person may have cleaned the room; the sentence simply puts the resulting change in the foreground. These are the uses compared in this group, not every meaning of する or なる.

Think of it this wayAsk what the sentence tells you: “What did someone do?” or “What changed / what was settled?”

How to build the patterns

Action and its object
人が + 物を + result + する. わたしは部屋をきれいにした: I made the room clean.
Thing undergoing the change
物が / は + result + なる. 部屋はきれいになった: the room became clean.
Past and polite forms
する → した / します / しました; なる → なった / なります / なりました. Past した and なった still preserve the action-versus-result distinction.
Do not decide from は alone
は marks the topic and can replace different particles. Look at the full predicate and the role of each noun, not just the nearest は.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

する: focus on the intentional action

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

する：のなに

する focuses on a person’s intentional action.

Book example

わたしはをきれいにした。

I made the room clean.

Clue を marks the room as the object being changed.

How to read this example

きれいにした highlights the speaker’s action that made it clean.

なる: focus on the change or result

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

なる：の・に

なる focuses on a change or its result.

Book example

はきれいになった。

The room became clean.

Clue The room is the topic undergoing a change.

How to read this example

きれいになった means became clean, not simply was clean. Someone may have caused the change.

Expanded explanation

Use する when an agent deliberately causes or chooses something. Use なる when the sentence presents the resulting state or change, even if a person caused it in the background.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はをした。

My mother made the room bright.

Clue The mother brings about the result.

How to read this example

明るい becomes 明るく before した, while 部屋を marks the room she changes.

Added example

がなった。

The room became bright.

Clue The room is now brighter.

How to read this example

部屋が and なった focus on the changed state without specifying the agent.

Do not confuse it with…

Change vs a static description
きれいになった means became clean. きれいだった means was clean. Translating both as “was clean” loses the change.
Agent vs cause
電気を消したので、暗くなった includes a deliberate cause but describes the room’s resulting darkness. なる is still appropriate.
Personal involvement vs wording
A first-person topic can use なる: わたしは退院できることになった reports a settled possibility. A human noun alone does not force する.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the person acting and the thing changing.
  2. Read any を or が and check the role it gives the following expression.
  3. Choose the intended relationship before checking tense or politeness.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

部屋をきれいにしました。部屋がきれいになりました。

へやをきれいにしました。へやがきれいになりました。

I made the room clean. The room became clean.

Two viewpoints on one result: the first highlights what I did; the second highlights the room’s change.

Book comparison · changing a state

～にする・～くする ／ ～になる・～くなる

Make something a certain way, or become that way.

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

～にする / ～くする means make or change something into a state. The person directs the change: make the room darker, lower the volume, or divide the cake.

～になる / ～くなる means enter a new state. The sentence tells you what has changed. It can name the cause too; the change need not be spontaneous.

Think of it this wayMatch the result word first: nouns and な-adjectives use に; い-adjectives change their final い to く.

How to build the patterns

Noun + に
半分にする / 半分になる = make it half / become half. The noun names the result.
な-adjective + に
静かにする / 静かになる; きれいにする / きれいになる. きれい is a な-adjective despite ending in the sound い.
い-adjective: い → く
暗い → 暗くする / 暗くなる; 小さい → 小さくする / 小さくなる. Do not use 暗いにする.
いい has the よい stem
いい → よくなる = get better; よくする = improve something. This matters in the child’s recovery question.
Negative change
釣れる → 釣れない → 釣れなくなる = become unable to catch fish. The final い of the negative changes to く before なる.
Request an action
短くしてください asks someone to make something shorter. 短くなって describes becoming shorter and does not request the hairdresser’s action on your hair.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

する: change the state of something

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～にする・～くする
⇒を

にする / くする: make or change something into a state.

Book explanation

Means to change a state or situation.

Book example

わたしはケーキをにした。

I halved a large cake.

Clue 半分 is the chosen result.

How to read this example

Noun + にする describes what the speaker did to the cake.

Book example

を、をにしたい。

I want to strengthen my bones by eating small fish.

Clue The speaker states an intended result for their own action.

How to read this example

丈夫 is a な-adjective, so it becomes 丈夫にしたい. The example illustrates a stated aim, not a guarantee of its effect.

Book example

を、をしてください。

Turn off the lights and darken the room.

Clue The request asks someone to change the room.

How to read this example

暗い becomes 暗く, and してください asks for the action that brings about that state.

なる: describe the changed state

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～になる・～くなる
⇒が

になる / くなる: describe a change in state.

Book explanation

Describes a change in state; the sentence may still name an intentional cause.

Book example

ケーキがになった。

The large cake was reduced to half.

Clue The cake is described as having become half.

How to read this example

が marks the thing changing; the sentence does not name who divided or reduced it.

Book example

をよくので、がになった。

After regularly eating small fish, my bones became stronger.

Clue The sentence presents stronger bones as a result.

How to read this example

丈夫になった describes the change, while ので supplies the cause claimed in the example.

Book example

をので、がなった。

I turned off the light, so the room became dark.

Clue A deliberate action can be the cause of a なる sentence.

How to read this example

Turning off the light explains why the room became dark; the final clause focuses on the room.

Book example

、このではがあまりなった。

Lately it has become difficult to catch many fish in this river.

Clue The changed condition is negative potential.

How to read this example

釣れる becomes 釣れない, then 釣れなくなった. あまり with that negative means not much, rather than no fish at all.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

をしてください。

Please turn the sound down.

Clue The listener is asked to lower the volume.

How to read this example

小さい becomes 小さく before してください; 音を identifies what is changed.

Added example

がなった。

The sound became quieter.

Clue The sound itself is the changing subject.

How to read this example

小さくなった reports the quieter result.

Added example

をにした。

I made the meeting room quiet.

Clue Someone deliberately makes the meeting room quiet.

How to read this example

静か is a な-adjective and takes に before した.

Added example

がになった。

The meeting room became quiet.

Clue The meeting room becomes quiet.

How to read this example

静かになった reports the state change rather than the action that caused it.

Do not confuse it with…

をする side vs がなる side
音を小さくした: someone lowered the volume. 音が小さくなった: the sound became quieter. The same result has different grammatical participants.
State change vs ability change
読めなくなった = became unable to read. 読めるようになった = became able to read. Look inside the changed state for the negative or potential meaning.
Transitive and intransitive pairs
火を消す = put out the flame; 火が消える = the flame goes out. The one-point lesson uses these verb pairs to distinguish controlled actions from system results.

JLPT approach

  1. Check whether the result word is a noun, a な-adjective, or an い-adjective.
  2. Use the object or changing subject to choose する or なる.
  3. Read negatives in full: できなくなった is loss of ability, not a new decision not to act.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

もう少し小さくしてください。

もうすこしちいさくしてください。

Please make it a little smaller / turn it down a little.

Context supplies what is changed, such as size or volume. The request targets the other person’s action.

Book comparison · decision and settled result

～にする・～ことにする ／ ～になる・～ことになる

“I decided…” or “It has been settled that…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

にする / ことにする presents a choice as someone’s decision. Choose a noun option with にする; choose an action or non-action with ことにする.

になる / ことになる reports the settled outcome or arrangement. It often follows a discussion, circumstances, or an organizational decision. The sentence leaves the decision-maker in the background; it does not prove the speaker had no say.

Think of it this wayA chosen option or action points to する. A report of what has been arranged points to なる.

How to build the patterns

Noun choice + にする
そばにする = choose soba; ８月３０日にする = set it for August 30. しよう means “let’s choose / I’ll choose.”
Verb dictionary form + ことにする
行くことにした = decided to go. Keep 行く inside the plan even though the decision itself is past.
Verb ない-form + ことにする
吸わないことにした = decided not to smoke. The decision is positive; the chosen action is negative.
A settled option / plan
金曜日になった = was set for Friday; 出張することになった = it has been arranged that I will go on a business trip.
Passive and potential plan contents
捨てられることになった reports a decision that things will be discarded. 退院できることになった reports that discharge has become possible under the arrangement; it does not mean simply “I decided to discharge myself.”

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

する: make a choice or decision

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～にする・～ことにする
⇒

にする / ことにする: choose an option or decide on an action.

Book explanation

Decide (with person as agent).

Book example

のは３０にしよう。

Let's set the departure date for the trip as August 30th.

Clue しよう proposes choosing the date.

How to read this example

August 30 is a noun-like date option, so にしよう is enough; no こと is needed.

Book example

にハワイにことにした。

I decided to go to Hawaii for the holidays.

Clue The speaker presents a personal decision.

How to read this example

行く remains dictionary form inside the plan; した places the decision in the past.

Book example

もうたばこはことにした。

I decided not to smoke anymore.

Clue The content of the decision is not smoking.

How to read this example

吸わない is the negative action, followed by positive ことにした, “decided.”

なる: report what has been settled

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～になる・～ことになる
⇒

になる / ことになる: report the settled option or arrangement.

Book explanation

Result in, be settled (without human agent being explicitly stated).

Book example

のは３０になった。

The departure date for the trip has been set for August 30.

Clue The date is presented as settled.

How to read this example

になった reports the outcome; it does not mean the date changed by itself without a decision.

Book example

、することになった。

It has been arranged that I will go on a business trip next month.

Clue The business trip is an arrangement being reported.

How to read this example

ことになった leaves the decision-maker in the background and should not automatically be translated “I decided.”

Book example

ははことになった。

It has been decided that there will be no company trip this year.

Clue The settled plan is not to hold the trip.

How to read this example

行わない stays negative inside ことになった; it is the cancellation or non-occurrence that has been arranged.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はそばにした。

I decided on soba for lunch.

Clue The chosen lunch option is soba.

How to read this example

Noun + にした reports the speaker’s choice.

Added example

はになった。

The meeting was set for Friday.

Clue Friday is the settled meeting day.

How to read this example

になった presents the arrangement, not the chooser.

Added example

ことにした。

I decided to walk every morning.

Clue The walking is a recurring action within a decision.

How to read this example

毎朝 describes the plan; ことにした reports choosing that plan.

Added example

からでことになった。

It has been decided that I will work in Osaka starting next month.

Clue The Osaka work assignment is presented as arranged.

How to read this example

来月から gives the future start, while なった says the arrangement has already been settled.

Do not confuse it with…

ことにした vs ことになった
Lesson 11 contrasts deciding with reporting the settled outcome. A first-person subject can occur with either.
Decision vs conscious effort
買うことにした = decided to buy. 買うようにしている = make a point of buying as a practice. A one-time bicycle purchase is a decision in the dialogue.
Plan vs behavioral change
住むことになった = arranged to live somewhere. 住むようになった = came to live there as a change in circumstances. Future dates and the conversation’s setup help identify the intended meaning.
Further uses of ことになる
In other contexts it can also express a resulting implication, “that would mean…”. This section and its dialogue primarily practice settled decisions and arrangements.

JLPT approach

  1. Read who is presenting the choice and whether the sentence reports their decision or the outcome of arrangements.
  2. Keep the planned verb in dictionary or ない-form; tense usually appears on する / なる.
  3. Do not translate every ことになった as “I decided.”

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

こちらにします。

こちらにします。

I’ll take this one.

A useful ordering or shopping expression. The chosen option is a noun-like こちら, so no こと is needed.

Book comparison · continuing patterns

～にしている・～ことにしている ／ ～になっている・～ことになっている

A personal rule you follow, or an arrangement already in effect.

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

にしている / ことにしている describes maintaining a choice or personal rule. “I make it a rule to write in my diary” emphasizes a decision that guides repeated behavior.

になっている / ことになっている describes an established arrangement, rule, or schedule. Uniform requirements and tomorrow’s flight can both use it. It reports what is set, not a guarantee that everyone always follows the rule or that every plan will happen.

Think of it this way“What I choose to keep doing” versus “what the rule or schedule says.”

How to build the patterns

Noun + にしている
昼食はパンにしている = make bread the usual lunch choice. Here している maintains a preference, not an action happening only at this instant.
Vる / Vない + ことにしている
日記を書くことにしている / 袋をもらわないことにしている. Positive and negative personal rules both fit.
Noun + になっている
会場は桜公園になっている = Sakura Park is the established venue. The focus is the current arrangement.
Vる / Vない + ことになっている
制服を着ることになっている = wearing a uniform is the rule. 行くことになっている = is scheduled to go.
Embedded prohibitions and inability
通学できないことになっている means the rules do not allow commuting that way here. してはいけないことになっている reports an established prohibition.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

している: maintain a personal choice or rule

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～にしている・～ことにしている
⇒ことをいる

にしている / ことにしている: keep following a chosen preference or personal rule.

Book explanation

Continue with a pre-established pattern of action.

Book example

はいつもパンにしています。

I make bread my usual choice for lunch.

Clue Bread is a maintained preference.

How to read this example

いつも and にしています make it a usual lunch choice, not bread being made at this moment.

How to read this example

寝る前に gives the timing and 必ず reinforces the regular commitment.

Book example

レジはもらわないことにしている。

I make it a rule not to accept shopping bags.

Clue The speaker follows a rule of not accepting bags.

How to read this example

もらわない is negative; ことにしている means that decision remains part of the speaker’s practice.

なっている: an established rule or schedule

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～になっている・～ことになっている
⇒ことがいる（・）

になっている / ことになっている: an established arrangement, rule, or schedule is in effect.

Book explanation

An established rule, arrangement, or schedule remains in effect.

Book example

、のはになっています。

Sakura Park is the established venue for the cherry-blossom gathering every year.

Clue The annual venue is an established arrangement.

How to read this example

毎年 describes recurrence; になっています reports what is set.

Book example

このではをことになっている。

This company requires you to wear a uniform.

Clue Wearing a uniform is the company’s rule.

How to read this example

ことになっている describes the established requirement, not the speaker’s personal preference.

Book example

はでできないことになっている。

Students are not allowed to commute to school by car.

Clue できない expresses lack of permission in this rule.

How to read this example

Book example

、は９のでにことになっている。

Tomorrow, the president is scheduled to go to China on a 9 o'clock flight.

Clue Tomorrow’s flight is a schedule already in place.

How to read this example

ことになっている can describe a future one-time event; it does not always mean a repeated rule.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はにしている。

I usually choose fruit for breakfast.

Clue Fruit is the speaker’s usual chosen breakfast.

How to read this example

にしている maintains a preference.

Added example

はコーヒーをことにしている。

I make it a rule not to drink coffee at night.

Clue The negative action is a personal rule.

How to read this example

飲まないことにしている means the speaker chooses not to drink coffee at night.

Added example

はになっています。

The reception desk is set up on the first floor.

Clue The first floor is the assigned reception location.

How to read this example

になっています reports the present arrangement.

Added example

ではをことになっている。

The rule is that phones are not used in the library.

Clue Not using phones is the established library rule.

How to read this example

The negative clause is the content of ことになっている.

Do not confuse it with…

Decided then vs still in effect
ことにした reports making the decision. ことにしている describes following that decision. ことになった reports the arrangement being settled; ことになっている reports its current status.
Personal rule vs careful effort
ようにしている emphasizes making an effort or taking care. ことにしている emphasizes the personal rule. Both can describe an established habit; effort does not necessarily mean failure.
Scheduled behavior vs system function
電気がつくようになっている explains how a setup works. 電気を消すことになっている reports a rule or arrangement about switching it off.
Does ている always mean “right now”?
No. These patterns describe maintained choices or arrangements. 毎年 and 明日 can appear naturally because the arrangement is in force now.

JLPT approach

  1. Look for a personal practice, an institutional rule, or a schedule.
  2. Identify what ている maintains: someone’s decision, or the settled arrangement.
  3. Read the embedded negative before translating the rule. “Not allowed” may be the contextual meaning of できない here.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

ここでは靴を脱ぐことになっています。

ここではくつをぬぐことになっています。

The rule here is to take off your shoes.

This explains an established practice. It does not mean the speaker personally decided to remove everyone’s shoes.

Book comparison · purpose, arising change, and design

～ようにする A ／ ～ようになる・～ようになっている

Create the conditions, describe the change, or explain the setup.

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

ようにする A: someone takes action so that a desired state becomes possible or an unwanted result is prevented. Moving a desk can make the view visible; making a hole can let a cat pass through.

ようになる: a new ability, behavior, or state develops. It describes what changed, even when someone’s deliberate action caused that change.

ようになっている: in the design use here, something is arranged or functions so that a result occurs or is possible. It describes a door, room, device, or built-in function.

Think of it this way“I arranged it so…” → ようにする. “It became possible / started happening…” → ようになる. “This is how it works…” → ようになっている.

How to build the patterns

Desired state + ようにする
見えるようにする = arrange things so the view is visible; 通れるようにした = made it possible to pass through. Potential verbs are normal in this purposeful-arrangement use.
Negative desired result
触られないようにしている = keep arranging things so they will not be touched. 触られない is passive negative: not be touched.
Ability or behavior + ようになる
読めるようになった = became able to read; 手伝うようになった = started helping. The first uses potential; the second describes a new behavioral pattern.
Function + ようになっている
押すと開くようになっている = works so that it opens when pushed. と often gives the trigger and the following verb gives the resulting function.
Choose the verb inside the pattern
火を消す describes someone extinguishing a flame; 火が消える describes the flame going out. In a device-function question, identify whose action or result the clause expresses.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

A. ようにする: create or maintain the desired conditions

Book wording and English gloss
Book form

～ようにする　A
⇒あるのためにを

ようにする A: take action to bring about or maintain a desired state.

Book explanation

Cause a change for a specific purpose.

Book example

のを、でものがようにしよう。

Let’s move the desk so that we can see the scenery outside even while working.

Clue Moving the desk is the deliberate means.

How to read this example

見える describes the desired visibility, and ようにしよう proposes arranging it.

Book example

ドアにを、ねこがようにした。

I cut a hole in the door so the cat could pass through.

Clue The hole enables the cat to pass through.

How to read this example

通れる is potential; ようにした focuses on making that ability possible through the action on the door.

Book example

なはいつものに、にようにしている。

I always keep important things on the shelf so that the children cannot touch them.

Clue The speaker maintains conditions that prevent touching.

How to read this example

触られない is passive negative, referring to the important things not being touched by the children.

B. ようになる: describe a new ability or behavior

Book wording and English gloss
Book form 1

～ようになる
⇒が

ようになる: a new state, ability, or behavior develops.

Book explanation

Describes a new ability, behavior, or state; it may result from deliberate action.

Book example

のをので、でものがようになった。

Since I changed the position of my desk, I can now see the outside scenery while working.

Clue The same desk move is viewed through its result.

How to read this example

見えるようになった means the view has become visible; it does not describe a decision to look.

Book example

がそうにをしていれば、もようになる。

If parents appear to enjoy doing housework, their children will also start helping willingly.

Clue Helping develops into a new behavior.

How to read this example

手伝う is not potential here; ようになる describes the children starting to help willingly.

C. ようになっている: explain a design or function

Book wording and English gloss
Book form 2

～ようになっている
⇒あるのためにそういる

ようになっている: something is built or arranged to allow a particular result or function.

Book explanation

Means done or arranged in such a way for a specific purpose.

Book example

このはにもがあって、のがからもようになっている。

This house also has windows on the roof, allowing sunlight to enter from above.

Clue The windows explain the house’s function.

How to read this example

Do not confuse it with…

Purpose A vs habit B
読めるようにした: made reading possible. 読むようにしている: make a point of reading. Do not apply the habit’s deliberate-action shortcut to every ようにする sentence.
Action vs resulting ability

Design vs an acquired behavior
A machine with ようになっている commonly describes its setup. A person with ようになった commonly describes a change in behavior or ability. These are useful contextual tendencies, not a rule that human subjects can never use なる.
Purpose connection in Group D
Group D’s ように explains how an action supports a desired result. Here the final する / なる adds the viewpoint of arranging, changing, or functioning.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the subject of the clause before ように, not just the topic at the start of the sentence.
  2. Check potential, passive, and negative endings before selecting する or なる.
  3. Use triggers such as 押すと or 自動で as evidence of a function, then verify the whole sentence.
  4. Read ようになっている in context: the design reading is the focus here, not its only possible use in Japanese.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Book grammar · habitual care

～ようにする B・～ようにしている

“Make a point of…” / “Take care to…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Vる / Vないようにする B means take care to do, or avoid doing, an action. With ようにしている, the speaker describes a conscious ongoing practice: replying promptly or avoiding driving on snowy days.

It emphasizes attention and effort, not an automatic mechanism. It can describe a successful, regular habit; “try to” does not necessarily imply that the speaker often fails. ようにしましょう and ようにしてください can encourage or request careful behavior.

Think of it this wayFind the action the person can deliberately choose: eat, reply, carry, look, or avoid doing something.

How to build the patterns

Dictionary verb + ようにする
返信するようにする = make a point of replying. Use dictionary form before ように; the final する takes tense or politeness.
Negative verb + ようにする
運転しないようにする = take care not to drive; 遅れないようにしている = make an effort not to be late.
Maintain the practice
読むようにしています = I make a point of reading. This is the polite form of the ongoing practice.
Encourage / request
取るようにしましょう = let’s make sure to take…; 動かすようにしてください = please make a point of moving…. The intended action remains in plain form.
Do not confuse deliberate avoidance with inability
見ない = choose not to look; 見えない = not be visible / cannot see. The television-listings question concerns deliberate avoidance.

「～ようにしている」と「～ようになっている」

7 questions · conscious practice or designed function? Find the subject of the action before ように. Compare deliberate actions such as 食べる with abilities or results such as 食べられる and 消える.

2　うちの台所は、長い時間ガスを使うと（a 火を消す　b 火が消える）ようになっている。

3　ホテルでは、お風呂に入っていても（a 電話をかける　b 電話がかけられる）ようになっている。

5　健康のためになるべく（a 体を動かす　b 体が動く）ようにしてください。

6　このドアは内側からは外が見えるが、外からは（a 中を見ない　b 中が見えない）ようになっている。

7　新聞のテレビ番組表を見るとテレビが見たくなるので、試験前は（a 見ない　b 見えない）ようにしているんです。

Who acts, and what is enabled?

ようにしている: a conscious practice
魚を食べる describes choosing to eat fish. 体を動かす describes deliberately moving the body. 見ない describes choosing not to look. The person makes a point of doing or avoiding the action.
ようになっている: a function or arrangement
火が消える describes a flame going out. 電話がかけられる describes the ability to make a call. 中が見えない describes the inside not being visible. These clauses explain the result or possibility provided by the setup.

Check the pair, not only を / が: 消す / 消える, 動かす / 動く, and 見る / 見える have different meanings. Read the noun’s role and the verb together.

Keep this rule in its context: the comparison is habitual care versus a thing’s function. Earlier purpose A also uses ようにする with potential clauses, as in 通れるようにした. “Potential always means なる” would be an incorrect shortcut.

Review purposeful arrangement and resulting change, or review deliberate habits. A third-person subject by itself does not determine the answer.
