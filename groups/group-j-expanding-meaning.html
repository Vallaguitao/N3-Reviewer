Book table A · action stage

ある動作のどの段階かを表す

Action stages: starting, unfinished, completed, or sustained to the end

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

These endings attach to a verb’s ます stem and add information about its progress. Ask where the action is on its timeline, then check whether the speaker emphasizes completion or persistence.

かける / かけだ · underway but unfinished
The action is beginning or has begun without reaching completion. It can be interrupted, but the ending alone does not mean it has stopped permanently. An emerging state such as understanding can also be described this way.
きる · complete the whole action
Focus on reaching the endpoint or using the whole amount/capacity. Its potential negative, きれない, means cannot finish it all.
通す（とおす） · keep going throughout
Focus on maintaining an action or stance through the whole period, often despite difficulty or pressure.
出す（だす） · begin / burst into
Focus on the onset, often sudden or spontaneous: a person starts laughing or a machine starts moving. Learn established combinations; some verbs ending in 出す have other meanings.

Think of it this wayAsk “Has it started? Is it complete? Did it continue throughout?” A time word helps, but the whole situation decides the answer.

Forms and attachment

Build the ます stem
書きます → 書き; 読みます → 読み; 食べます → 食べ; します → し; 来ます → 来（き）. Remove ます, not simply the final る of the dictionary form.
かける versus かけだ
読みかける → 読みかけた (began reading / read partway). 読みかけだ describes an unfinished state. Before a noun: 読みかけの本. Before ので: 読みかけなので.
きる and ability
読みきる → 読みきった (finished reading it all). Potential: 読みきれる → 読みきれない → 読みきれなかった (could not finish it all). Do not confuse きらない “do not finish” with きれない “cannot finish.”
通す / 出す conjugate at the end
やり通す → やり通した / やり通して; 泣き出す → 泣き出した / 泣き出して. Keep the first verb’s stem unchanged.

Book introduction
Book overview

やにのをつけると、そのやのをことができます。

Adding certain endings to verbs and nouns lets you express more specific meanings, such as progress, difficulty, or a characteristic.

かける / かけだ · begun but not finished

Book wording and formation

～かける
～かけだ

～しいない

An action is beginning or has begun but is not yet complete.

ます ＋かける・かけだ

Verb ます stem + かける / かけだ. Use かけの before a noun, and かけなので before a reason.

Book example

はかかけて、しまった。

She started to say something, then fell silent.

Clue The silence follows the start of speaking.

How to read this example

言いかけて links the unfinished attempt to 黙ってしまった. Here the context supplies the interruption; かける alone does not always mean a permanent stop.

Book example

はのをおいて、に。

My mother put down the book she was partway through and went shopping.

Clue The book is only partly read.

How to read this example

読みかけの modifies 本: an unfinished reading, not a book that has just been fully read. の connects the unfinished state to the noun.

きる · finish the whole amount

Book wording and formation

～きる

／に～する

Do the whole action or carry it out completely.

ます ＋きる

Verb ます stem + きる. The potential form きれる becomes きれない when the whole action cannot be completed.

Book example

40キロのをきった。

I ran the entire long distance of 40 kilometers.

Clue The entire 40 kilometers is the endpoint.

How to read this example

走りきった emphasizes completing the whole distance, including its demanding length.

Book example

こんなにたくさん、ではきれない。

I cannot eat all this by myself.

Clue The problem is finishing the whole quantity alone.

How to read this example

食べきれない is a potential negative: cannot eat it all. It does not say the speaker cannot eat any of it.

通す · persist throughout

Book wording and formation

～通す

まで～し

Keep doing something through to the end or throughout the whole period.

ます ＋

Verb ます stem + 通す（とおす）. Conjugate 通す at the end: 通した / 通して.

Book example

でことはまでやり。

Let’s follow through to the end on what we have decided for ourselves.

Clue The speaker urges persistence to the end.

How to read this example

やり通そう is volitional: let’s carry it through. 最後まで supports maintaining the commitment.

Book example

はをも。

He remained silent no matter what he was asked.

Clue He maintains silence despite every question.

How to read this example

黙り通した can describe sustaining a state throughout; it need not be a visibly active task. 何を聞かれても means no matter what he was asked.

出す · the moment an action begins

Book wording and formation

～出す

（・に）～し

Begin an action, often suddenly or spontaneously.

ます ＋

Verb ます stem + 出す（だす）: 出した for the past; 出して to connect to what follows.

Book example

はわたしのをと、。

When he saw my face, he suddenly started laughing.

Clue 突然 points to the onset of laughter.

How to read this example

笑います → 笑い出した. The sentence tells us when laughter began, not when it ended.

Book example

を、おもちゃのがに。

When I replaced the batteries, the toy car suddenly started moving.

Clue The changed batteries trigger movement.

How to read this example

動き出した means started moving; 急に reinforces the sudden onset. The toy’s final stopping point is not described.

Expanded explanation

かける leaves an action beginning or unfinished; the context may show an interruption. きる emphasizes full completion, 通す emphasizes persistence throughout, and 出す marks the onset, often sudden. Completion and persistence can describe the same event with different emphasis.

Added examples

Apply the same meanings in these additional situations.

Added example

かけのメールをした。

I saved the email I had started writing but had not finished.

Clue Saving does not mean the email is finished.

How to read this example

書きかけの identifies a draft whose writing is still incomplete. の connects the state with メール.

Added example

をできった。

I finished reading the entire long novel in one night.

Clue The whole novel was finished within one night.

How to read this example

読みきった is completion, and 一晩で tells us the time needed to reach that endpoint.

Added example

はまでのを。

She stuck to her opinion right to the end.

Clue Her opinion is maintained right to the end.

How to read this example

言い通した highlights holding to what she said throughout, rather than merely finishing one sentence.

Added example

がに。

The baby suddenly started crying.

Clue The baby’s crying begins suddenly.

How to read this example

泣き出した focuses on onset. It does not tell us how long the crying continued.

Do not confuse it with…

きる versus 通す
読みきる highlights finishing the whole text; 読み通す highlights reading through to the end. Both can describe a completed reading. Decide which emphasis the context supports, rather than assuming they never overlap.
かけだ versus たばかりだ
読みかけだ is unfinished. 読んだばかりだ means the reading was recently completed from the speaker’s perspective. Review Group F: ばかり.
出す versus 始める
Both can mark a beginning. 出す often makes the onset feel sudden or spontaneous; 始める is a broader way to describe starting. 急に is a useful clue, not a requirement in every 出す sentence.
かける versus ところだ
読むところだ is just about to read; 読んでいるところだ is in the middle of reading. 読みかけだ specifically leaves the reading unfinished. Review Lesson 1: time and stages.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the base action before judging its ending. 実力を出しきる means use one’s ability fully; the second part is きる, even though the base verb is 出す.
  2. Read endpoint clues such as 全部 and 最後まで with the reason given. Too little time often explains きれなかった; maintaining a stance throughout a lifetime points toward 通す.
  3. Do not reject かける because the action is mental. わかりかけてきた describes understanding that has begun to develop.

Use clues to predict the meaning, then check the complete phrase. A clue is not an automatic answer rule.

Useful expression

最後までやり通そう。

さいごまで やりとおそう。

Let’s see it through to the end.

通そう is the volitional form of 通す. The focus is persistence.

Book table B · characteristics and condition

特徴・状態・様子を表す①

Ease, difficulty, discomfort, and doing too much

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

First decide what is being evaluated: how easily an action happens, how difficult it feels, or whether it exceeds a suitable limit. These are different questions even when the same verb comes before the ending.

やすい · easy to / prone to
書きやすい means easy to write with; 破れやすい means tears easily. The action can be intentional or happen naturally. “Easy” does not automatically mean desirable.
にくい · hard to / resistant to
Describes difficulty or something that does not happen readily. 食べにくい is hard to eat; 割れにくい is resistant to breaking, which can be a useful quality.
づらい · difficult or uncomfortable to do
Often highlights physical, emotional, or social difficulty for the person doing the action. It is not limited to embarrassment: 見づらい can describe poor visibility.
すぎる / すぎだ · too much / too…
The action or quality goes beyond a suitable amount. The standard depends on context: too small for these feet, too technical for this listener, or too much work for one’s health.

Think of it this wayFor やすい / にくい, ask how readily it happens. For づらい, ask what makes doing it burdensome. For すぎる, identify the exceeded limit.

Forms and attachment

Verb stem + やすい / にくい / づらい
書きます → 書きやすい; 覚えます → 覚えにくい; 話します → 話しづらい. Use づ in づらい.
These endings act like い-adjectives
書きやすい → 書きやすく / 書きやすかった / 書きやすくない. 話しづらい → 話しづらかった. Before a noun: 割れにくいカップ.
Verb or adjective + すぎる
食べます → 食べすぎる; 小さい → 小さすぎる; 静か（な）→ 静かすぎる. Remove ます, the final い, or な/だ as appropriate. いい uses よ: よすぎる.
Changing the whole compound
大きくなる → 大きくなりすぎる → 大きくなりすぎて. The く belongs to 大きい + なる; the ます stem なり receives すぎる.
すぎ + だ / です
飲みすぎだ → 飲みすぎです. If です is already printed after the blank, use すぎ, without an extra だ. 食べすぎて links the excessive action to what follows.

Book examples by pattern

Read the Japanese, then use the English and clue. Open the extra guidance when you need the form or distinction explained.

やすい · easy or likely to happen

Book wording and formation

～やすい

に～できる
に～してしまう

An action is easy to do, or an event happens readily. This can be helpful or undesirable.

ます ＋やすい

Verb ます stem + やすい. The resulting word behaves like an い-adjective.

Book example

のはわかりやすい。

Mr./Ms. Tanaka’s explanations are easy to understand.

Clue The listener can follow the explanation easily.

How to read this example

わかります → わかりやすい. Here やすい evaluates understandability, not low price.

Book example

やすいだからをつけて。

Be careful: this paper is thin and tears easily.

Clue The thin paper may tear readily.

How to read this example

破れやすい uses the event verb 破れる “tear.” 気をつけて shows why this easy occurrence is undesirable.

にくい · difficulty or resistance

Book wording and formation

～にくい

に～できない
には～しない

An action is difficult to do, or an event does not happen readily.

ます ＋にくい

Verb ます stem + にくい. Before a noun, keep にくい: 割れにくいカップ.

Book example

このはにくい。

This meat is tough and difficult to eat.

Clue The meat’s toughness makes eating difficult.

How to read this example

食べにくい does not say eating is impossible. The sentence identifies a practical obstacle.

Book example

でにくいカップはありませんか。

Do you have a sturdy cup that does not break easily?

Clue Resistance to breaking is the desired quality.

How to read this example

割れにくい modifies カップ directly. にくい can express a benefit when the event being resisted is unwanted.

づらい · an action that feels difficult

Book wording and formation

～づらい

～するのが

Doing something is difficult or uncomfortable for the person involved; the difficulty can be physical, emotional, or social.

ます ＋づらい

Verb ます stem + づらい. For the past, use づらかった.

Book example

なことなのでのにはづらい。

Since it is a personal matter, I find it awkward to ask people at work for help.

Clue The personal nature of the matter makes asking awkward.

How to read this example

頼みづらい highlights social discomfort. Do not infer that づらい is limited to emotional difficulty in every sentence.

Book example

ここはのがです。

From this position, the writing on the blackboard is hard to see.

Clue The viewing position makes the board difficult to see.

How to read this example

見づらい describes a physical difficulty here, showing why an emotion-only rule for づらい would fail.

すぎる / すぎだ · beyond a suitable limit

Book wording and formation

～すぎる
～すぎだ

なを
～だ・～する

The action or quality exceeds what is appropriate: too much, or too…

ます・イい・ナ ＋すぎる・すぎだ

Verb ます stem / い-adjective without its final い / な-adjective base + すぎる. Nounlike expressions such as 言いすぎ also take だ / です.

Book example

このはわたしにはすぎる。

These shoes are too small for me.

Clue The shoes exceed the speaker’s tolerance for smallness.

How to read this example

小さい loses its final い: 小さすぎる. わたしには supplies the person-relative standard.

Book example

おいしかったので、すぎてしまった。

It was so delicious that I ended up eating too much.

Clue The delicious taste led to eating beyond a suitable amount.

How to read this example

食べます → 食べすぎて, followed by しまった to present the excessive eating as an unfortunate result.

Book example

、それはすぎだよ。

Hey, that is going too far with what you are saying.

Clue The speaker judges the remark as going too far.

How to read this example

言いすぎ is nounlike here and takes だ. It can criticize harsh or excessive words, not only the number of words spoken.

Expanded explanation

にくい and づらい overlap: both can describe physical difficulty, while づらい often emphasizes the burden felt by the person. やすい can be helpful (“easy to understand”) or cautionary (“tears easily”). すぎる evaluates the amount as excessive.

Added examples

Apply the same meanings in these additional situations.

Added example

このペンはやすい。

This pen is light and easy to write with.

Clue The pen’s lightness makes it comfortable to use.

How to read this example

書きやすい describes writing with this pen; it does not mean the pen writes by itself.

Added example

このはがいてにくい。

This kanji is hard to remember because its shape resembles other characters.

Clue Similar shapes make memorization difficult.

How to read this example

覚えにくい concerns learning or remembering the character, rather than finishing a quantity of writing.

Added example

にはそのをづらかった。

I found it difficult to tell the teacher about that mistake.

Clue Telling the teacher about the mistake felt difficult.

How to read this example

話しづらかった is the past of an い-adjective ending. Replace い with かった, rather than adding だった.

Added example

このはでもやすい。

This road is easy to walk along even at night.

Clue The road remains easy to walk along even at night.

How to read this example

夜でも adds an even-in-that-situation meaning; it does not change the form 歩きやすい.

Added example

がすぎてわからない。

The explanation is too technical for me to understand.

Clue Technical detail exceeds what the listener can understand.

How to read this example

専門的 is a な-adjective base: 専門的すぎて. Do not insert な before すぎる.

Added example

すぎないようにしてください。

Please take care not to work too much.

Clue The request aims to prevent overworking.

How to read this example

働きすぎない negates working excessively, so the speaker is not asking the listener to avoid all work. ようにしてください expresses the request.

Do not confuse it with…

にくい versus づらい
Their meanings overlap. にくい often describes a practical obstacle or resistance; づらい often emphasizes difficulty felt by the person. Both can describe physical difficulty, and にくい can also express reluctance. Read the situation, not a rigid “objective versus emotional” rule.
やすい versus がち / っぽい
すべりやすい describes a tendency to slip under the conditions. 遅れがち describes recurring lateness or a tendency toward that state. 忘れっぽい describes a forgetful disposition. Compare tendency and qualities.
Hard to do versus cannot do
食べにくい says eating is difficult, not impossible. 食べきれない says the whole amount cannot be finished. Review きる and its potential negative.
すぎる versus ほど / くらい
すぎる evaluates the degree as excessive. ほど / くらい can indicate extent or comparison without automatically saying it is too much. See Lesson 3: degree.

JLPT approach

  1. For a warning, identify the risky event. 雪道 + 気をつけて suggests slipping readily, not difficulty slipping.
  2. Check the base verb: 切れにくい uses 切れる “snap/break” → 切れ. Do not mistake it for a statement that a person cannot cut something.
  3. Read the noun or connector after the blank. A correct meaning still needs the right form: すぎて, すぎです, やすいから, or づらかった.

Use clues to predict the meaning, then check the complete phrase. A clue is not an automatic answer rule.

Useful expression

ちょっと聞きづらいんですが…。

ちょっと ききづらいんですが…。

This is a little awkward to ask, but…

A conversational lead-in to a sensitive question. んですが softens the opening and leaves room for the question.

Book table B · tendency, image, resemblance, abundance

特徴・状態・様子を表す②

Tendencies, typical qualities, resemblance, and unwanted abundance

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

These endings describe different relationships. A person may tend to do something, behave as expected of their role, have a noticeable quality, resemble something else, or be surrounded by too much of something.

がちだ · tends to / often in a state
Often describes an unwanted tendency: 遅れがち, 忘れがち. Some combinations describe a manner, such as 遠慮がち “hesitant/reserved,” rather than simply counting repeated events.
らしい · typical of / befitting
The quality fits the image or expectations associated with the noun: 春らしい weather, 社長らしい behavior. This use is different from inference/hearsay らしい, “apparently.”
っぽい · -ish / having a quality / prone to
水っぽい is watery; 子どもっぽい is childish; 忘れっぽい is forgetful. It often expresses criticism, but it is not always negative or a claim that something is fake.
のようだ / みたいだ · like / resembling
Compare a feature with something else: scenery like a painting or bread like cake. The comparison itself does not assert identity. みたいだ is more conversational.
だらけだ · full of / covered in
Highlights an abundance, usually of something unwanted: mistakes, rubbish, or injuries. With people, it can emphasize crowding; it does not make those people inherently bad.

Think of it this wayAsk “A recurring tendency? A fitting quality? A noticeable property? A comparison? An abundance?” Then inspect the word immediately after the blank.

Forms and attachment

がちだ · noun or verb stem
遅れます → 遅れがちだ; 遠慮 + がちだ. Before a noun: 遅れがちな人. To describe manner: 遠慮がちに聞く. Learn natural combinations; these endings do not attach freely to every word.
らしい · acts like an い-adjective
N + らしい: 春らしい日. Before an action: 社長らしく行動する. Negative: その人らしくない. No の is added between the noun and らしい.
っぽい · noun or selected verb stems
水 + っぽい; 忘れます → 忘れっぽい. Forms: 水っぽくて; 忘れっぽくなる; 子どもっぽい人. Memorize established words, not a rule allowing every noun or verb.
のようだ · three positions
Nのようだ ends the description; Nのような + noun; Nのように + action/quality. 絵のようだ / 絵のような景色 / 絵のように美しい.
みたいだ · same positions, no の
Nみたいだ / Nみたいな + noun / Nみたいに + action/quality. ケーキみたいに甘い. Do not transfer the の from のようだ.
だらけだ · noun-based
間違いだらけだ → 間違いだらけだった. Before a noun: 間違いだらけの作文. Before なる: 傷だらけになる.

Book examples by pattern

Read the Japanese, then use the English and clue. Open the extra guidance when you need the form or distinction explained.

がちだ · tendencies and a reserved manner

Book wording and formation

～がちだ

～のになるがある

There is a tendency toward an action or state, often an unwanted one.

・ます ＋がちだ

Noun or verb ます stem + がちだ. Common forms include がちな before a noun and がちに for manner.

Book example

のためはだ。

The construction tends to fall behind because of the rain.

Clue Rain repeatedly pushes the work behind schedule.

How to read this example

遅れがち describes a tendency toward delay, not necessarily one already completed delay.

Book example

ににをみた。

I hesitantly asked Yuka her age.

Clue The question is asked in a hesitant manner.

How to read this example

遠慮がちに modifies 聞いてみた. This use describes reserved behavior, not simply how often the person asks questions.

らしい · characteristic or fitting

Book wording and formation

～らしい

～のイメージとだ

The quality fits the image or expectations associated with the noun: typical of / befitting.

＋らしい

Noun + らしい. Use らしく before an action, and らしくない for the negative.

Book example

はらしいだった。

Today was warm, a day typical of spring.

Clue The warmth fits the expected character of spring.

How to read this example

春らしい modifies 一日. This is typical-quality らしい, not a report that the day is apparently spring.

Book example

いつものなリーさんらしくないですね。

You do not seem like your usual lively self, Lee.

Clue Lee’s current manner differs from the usual lively self.

How to read this example

らしくない is the negative of characteristic らしい. The speaker is not questioning Lee’s identity.

っぽい · a noticeable quality or disposition

Book wording and formation

～っぽい

～のような・がある

Something has a noticeable quality or a person has a characteristic tendency.

・ます ＋っぽい

Noun or selected verb ます stem + っぽい. This behaves like an い-adjective: っぽく / っぽくて.

Book example

はわがままばかり、っぽいだ。

She is childish, always insisting on having things her own way.

Clue Repeated selfish demands support the criticism of childishness.

How to read this example

子どもっぽい evaluates behavior. ばかり emphasizes the repeated one-sided behavior in the reason.

Book example

はあきっぽくて、でやめてしまう。

My younger brother gets bored easily and gives up on everything partway through.

Clue Giving things up partway illustrates a tendency to get bored.

How to read this example

あきっぽい comes from あきます. っぽくて connects this characteristic to the following behavior.

のようだ / みたいだ · resemblance

Book wording and formation

～のようだ
～みたいだ

～によくいる

Something resembles something else in a particular way.

＋のようだ・みたいだ
＋のような・みたいな＋

Noun + のようだ / みたいだ. Before another noun: のような / みたいな. Before an action or quality: のように / みたいに.

Book example

ここからはのようだ。

The scenery visible from here looks like a painting.

Clue The scenery is compared with a painting.

How to read this example

絵のようだ ends the description. The comparison is about its appearance, not whether the landscape is literally painted.

Book example

このパンはケーキみたいに。

This bread is sweet and soft like cake.

Clue Cake supplies the comparison for sweetness and softness.

How to read this example

みたいに comes before the qualities 甘くて軟かい. Before a noun, the form would instead be みたいな.

Book example

ガラスみたいなをで。

We played by stepping on glasslike ice with our shoes.

Clue The ice resembles glass.

How to read this example

ガラスみたいな modifies 氷, so な is needed. The thing being stepped on remains ice.

だらけだ · abundant, usually unwanted contents

Book wording and formation

～だらけだ

～（いやなもの）がたくさんある

There is a large amount of something, usually something unwanted.

＋だらけだ

Noun + だらけだ. Use だらけの before a noun and だらけに before なる.

Book example

きたテストはだった。

The test I got back was full of mistakes.

Clue The returned test contains many mistakes.

How to read this example

間違いだらけだった gives a past state of unwanted abundance. It need not mean every answer was wrong.

Book example

サッカーをしていたら、になった。

After playing soccer every day, I ended up covered in cuts and scratches.

Clue Repeated soccer playing has left many injuries.

How to read this example

傷だらけに combines with なった to show a resulting state. だらけ is especially natural with unwanted marks or contents.

Expanded explanation

らしい here expresses a characteristic or fitting quality; っぽい describes a noticeable property or disposition. ようだ／みたいだ compares features, while だらけ highlights abundance, usually unwanted. Context distinguishes this らしい from “apparently” らしい.

Added examples

Apply the same meanings in these additional situations.

Added example

とをがちだ。

When I am busy, I tend to skip breakfast.

Clue Busy periods are linked to repeatedly missing breakfast.

How to read this example

抜きます → 抜きがちだ. This describes a tendency, not a decision to skip breakfast every time.

Added example

はらしいだ。

Today is a cool day typical of autumn.

Clue The coolness fits autumn’s expected character.

How to read this example

秋らしい modifies 涼しい日. Read らしい as typical here, not as uncertain hearsay.

Added example

このスープは。

This soup is a little watery.

Clue The soup has a noticeable watery quality.

How to read this example

水っぽい is an established adjective; 少し limits the degree. っぽい does not require the soup to be literally water.

Added example

あのはのように。

That cloud looks like an animal.

Clue The cloud’s appearance suggests an animal’s shape.

How to read this example

動物のように modifies 見える. This is resemblance, not a purpose or instruction.

Added example

はだった。

The station was packed with tourists.

Clue だらけ emphasizes the crowding of tourists.

How to read this example

The wording can suggest that their presence feels excessive or overwhelming. It does not claim tourists are inherently undesirable.

Added example

のはっぽく。

His explanation sounded rather like an excuse.

Clue The explanation gives the impression of an excuse.

How to read this example

言い訳っぽい becomes 言い訳っぽく before 聞こえた. This describes how it sounded, not proven dishonesty.

Do not confuse it with…

Typical らしい versus apparently らしい
春らしい日 describes a day with springlike qualities. 学生らしい can mean “apparently a student.” Both uses can follow nouns, so the preceding word alone cannot decide the meaning. Review Lesson 7: evidence and hearsay.
らしい versus のようだ / みたいだ
子どもらしい服 is clothing suited to children; the clothes are not literally children. 京都らしい物 represents Kyoto. In the book’s seasonal examples, actual spring versus out-of-season springlike weather is a useful clue, not a universal rule that resemblance requires different identities. Practice the one-point distinction.
がち versus っぽい
忘れがち focuses on a tendency to forget, often in particular circumstances; 忘れっぽい presents forgetfulness as a characteristic. The readings can overlap. Wateriness is 水っぽい, not a freely invented 水がち.
Resemblance ように versus purpose ように
ケーキみたいに甘い compares sweetness; 忘れないようにメモする states the aim of making a note. Read what is being compared or intended. See Group D: よう.
だらけ versus ばかり
だらけ emphasizes abundant presence, often unwelcome; ばかり can emphasize that the contents or repeated actions are mostly one kind. Neither automatically means every single item without exception. See Group F: ばかり.

JLPT approach

  1. For らしい, identify the speaker’s purpose: a fitting characteristic, or a report/inference? In 社長なら社長らしく, the speaker expects behavior appropriate to the role.
  2. Check the next word: 行動する needs らしく; こと needs みたいな; 甘い needs みたいに; a sentence ending can use のようだ.
  3. Learn 雨らしい雨 as “proper/substantial rain.” NらしいN can mean an example worthy of the name, rather than reporting uncertainty about N.

Use clues to predict the meaning, then check the complete phrase. A clue is not an automatic answer rule.

Useful expression

今日は春らしいですね。

きょうは はるらしいですね。

It feels like a proper spring day today.

Here らしい comments on typical seasonal qualities. It does not report that the season might be spring.

A better way to distinguish the meanings

Characteristic or appropriate → らしい
Ask whether the quality fits the image of a role, place, season, or category. 子どもらしい服 means clothes appropriate to children; it does not identify the clothes as children.
A comparison of features → のようだ / みたいだ
Ask what the thing resembles. The book uses weather outside the named season and liveliness without an actual festival to make this contrast easy to see. These are clues for those examples, not universal identity rules.
Finish the grammar
Before a noun: らしい / のような / みたいな. Before an action: らしく / のように / みたいに. At the end: らしい / のようだ / みたいだ. Review the complete forms.
Watch the other らしい
“Apparently” らしい expresses a report or inference. A noun before らしい does not by itself prove which use you have. In these exercises, focus on characteristic qualities and comparisons.
Book distinction

～らしい

に～だ。～のイメージとだ。

In the following book examples, the season or event really is the one named, and its qualities fit expectations. More broadly, らしい expresses a fitting or characteristic quality.

Book example

はらしいだった。（はだ。）

Today’s weather was typical of spring. (It really is spring now.)

Clue The book explicitly says it really is spring.

How to read this example

らしい identifies weather that fits that season’s expected qualities. Apply this clue to this example, not as a rule that every noun before らしい must literally identify the subject.

Book example

はおらしいにぎやかさだった。（におだ。）

The town had the lively atmosphere of a proper festival. (A festival really is taking place.)

Clue There really is a festival in this example.

How to read this example

お祭りらしい modifies にぎやかさ: the liveliness appropriate to a festival.

～のようだ・～みたいだ

に～ではないが、～によくいる。「～みたいだ」は。

In the following book examples, the season or event is different from the one named, but resembles it. みたいだ is conversational. Resemblance itself does not assert identity.

Book example

はのようなだった。（はではない。）

Today’s weather was like spring. (It is not spring now.)

Clue The book explicitly places this weather outside spring.

How to read this example

のような compares the weather with spring and modifies 天気. The example’s seasonal contrast helps choose the intended expression.

Book example

はおみたいなにぎやかさだった。（にはおではない。）

The town was as lively as a festival. (There is not actually a festival.)

Clue No festival is actually taking place in this example.

How to read this example

お祭りみたいな compares the town’s liveliness with a festival. みたいな is the conversational noun-modifying form.
