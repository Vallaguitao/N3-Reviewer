Book grammar · こと only

「こと」だけを使う場合（「の」は使わない）

Use こと to state the content of a goal, or as part of a fixed expression

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

こと and の can turn a clause into a noun-like unit. For example, 日本語を勉強する means “study Japanese”; 日本語を勉強すること means “studying Japanese.” The whole unit can then be a topic, subject, or object.

Sometimes both work: 絵を描くことが好きです and 絵を描くのが好きです both express liking to draw. “こと is abstract; の is concrete” can suggest a tendency, but it is not a reliable answer rule by itself. The construction and the following predicate matter more.

This topic groups the cases where the book selects こと. First, Xは…ことだ states what a dream, feature, difference, or similar idea consists of: “My goal is to pass N3.” Second, expressions such as ことがある and ことはない have their own meanings and attachment patterns. Learn each whole expression.

Read the example notation carefully. In a contrast example, こと（の） means use こと; the parenthesized の is the rejected alternative. Likewise, の（こと） in the next topic means use の. These parentheses do not mean both answers are acceptable.

Think of it this wayFirst identify the whole expression. Is it stating what a goal consists of, reporting an occasional event, or saying an action is unnecessary?

How to build the patterns

A clause becomes a noun-like unit
［N3に合格すること］が目標です. The が belongs to the whole bracketed clause, not just to the verb 合格する. The nominalizer itself does not conjugate.
Define the content: Xは + clause + ことだ
夢は漫画家になることです. Use a plain verb inside the clause; です gives the whole sentence its polite ending. Do not put ます directly before こと.
Keep the required plain-clause ending
行かないこと／忙しいこと／静かなこと. A present affirmative な-adjective takes な. A formal noun predicate can use であること: Bが肉料理であること, as in the lunch example.
Occasional events: V dictionary / ない + ことがある
帰ることがある = sometimes go home; 返事をしないことがある = sometimes do not reply. The negative belongs inside the event being reported.
Past occasions: V dictionary + こともあった
遅く帰ることもあった = there were also times when I went home late. あった places those occasions in the past; も adds “also / on some occasions.”
No need: V dictionary + ことはない
心配することはない = there is no need to worry. Polite: 心配することはありません. This use reassures someone that the action is unnecessary.
Experience is a different pattern: Vた + ことがある
日本へ行ったことがある = have been to Japan. The た before こと is the key difference from 行くことがある, “sometimes go to Japan.”

Book examples by use

Follow the labeled uses in order. In こと（の） and の（こと）, the form in parentheses is the rejected alternative.

Book wording and English gloss
Book introduction

「こと」「の」はようにと、ばならないがあります。

In some cases, こと and の may be used interchangeably, but in others they have separate, distinctive uses.

A. Explain what a goal, feature, or difference consists of

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

a）「～は…ことだ」ので、～のを…でとき

Use Xは…ことだ when the clause explains the content of X: what the dream, feature, or difference consists of.

Book example

・わたしののは、になること（の）です。（の＝になること）

・My dream for the future is to become a manga artist. (Future dream = becoming a manga artist)

Clue Use こと: becoming a manga artist is the content of the dream.

How to read this example

The structure equates 将来の夢 with 漫画家になること. The parenthesized の is rejected in this sentence pattern.

Book example

・サッカーというスポーツのは、にをはいけないこと（の）だ。

・One feature of soccer is that, as a basic rule, players must not use their hands.

Clue The feature being identified is a rule: players basically must not use their hands.

How to read this example

手を使ってはいけない forms the whole clause before こと; the negative is part of that content.

Book example

・AランチとBランチのは、AがでBがであること（の）だ。

・The difference between Lunch A and Lunch B is that A is a fish dish and B is a meat dish.

Clue こと packages the full difference between the two lunches.

How to read this example

魚料理で and 肉料理である are noun-predicate connections; formal である is retained before こと.

B. ことがある: an event happens sometimes

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

b）「こと」を

Fixed grammatical expressions that use こと.

Book formation

～ことがある　⇒「たまに～のがある。」
　／ない　＋ことがある

V dictionary form / Vない + ことがある: something happens sometimes, including an action sometimes not happening.

Book example

①はぼくがもをしないことがある。

①My wife sometimes doesn't respond even when I talk to her.

Clue Not replying is what happens sometimes.

How to read this example

しない is inside the clause, while ことがある reports occasional occurrence. It does not say that the wife never replies.

Book example

②はが、12にこともあった。

② I used to have so much work that there were also times when I got home around twelve.

Clue あった places the occasional late returns in the past.

How to read this example

帰る stays in dictionary form before こともあった; this differs from the experience pattern 帰ったことがある.

C. ことはない: there is no need to do it

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

～ことはない　⇒「～するはない・～しなくてもいい。」
　　＋ことはない

V dictionary form + ことはない: there is no need to do it; doing it is unnecessary.

Book example

①のはですよ。そんなにすることはありませんよ。

① The interview questions are simple. There is no need to worry so much.

Clue 心配することはありません means there is no need to worry so much.

How to read this example

The speaker is offering reassurance, not saying worrying is forbidden or predicting that the listener will never worry.

Book example

②があるが、ただのかぜだろう。すぐににことはない。

② I have a slight fever, but it is probably just a cold. There is no need to go to the hospital straight away.

Clue The speaker judges an immediate hospital visit unnecessary.

How to read this example

行くことはない means no need to go in this reassurance context, not simply “I will not go.” だろう keeps the explanation about the cold tentative.

Related fixed patterns: ということだ / とのことだ (reported information, Lesson 7); ないことはない (limited possibility, Lesson 8); ことは…が (qualified concession, Lesson 8); sentence-final こと (written rules, Lesson 10); ことにする / ことにしている (decisions and personal practices, Lesson 11 and Group G); ことになる / ことになっている (decided or arranged situations, Group G).

Added examples

Check which construction is doing the work in each sentence.

Added example

のはN3にすることです。

My goal is to pass N3.

Clue Passing N3 is the content of the goal.

How to read this example

合格する is dictionary form before こと; です makes the completed sentence polite.

Added example

でもをすることがある。

There are times when I work even on weekends.

Clue Working at weekends happens sometimes.

How to read this example

週末でも extends the statement to weekends, while ことがある gives the occasional frequency.

Added example

まだがあるから、ことはない。

There is still time, so there is no need to hurry.

Clue The remaining time is the reason the speaker says there is no need to hurry.

How to read this example

急ぐことはない removes a need; it does not forbid rushing.

Added example

なのはずつことだ。

What matters is continuing a little every day.

Clue Both nominalizers have different jobs in the same sentence.

How to read this example

大切なのは sets up “what matters”; 続けること supplies the activity that matters. The な connects 大切 to の.

Do not confuse it with…

Content ↔ focused information
目標は合格することだ states the content of the goal. 合格したのは去年だ focuses on when someone passed. Do not choose solely because the sentence contains は or だ.
No need ↔ must not · Lesson 10
～てはいけない prohibits an action. ～ことはない in the reassurance pattern says it is unnecessary. “You do not need to hurry” does not forbid hurrying.
No need ↔ limited possibility · Lesson 8
～ないことはない cautiously allows a possibility: “it is not that I cannot…”. Keep both negatives; it is different from dictionary-form + ことはない.
Other fixed こと expressions
ということだ / とのことだ reports information; ことは…が makes a qualified concession; sentence-final こと gives a written rule. Do not exchange こと for の inside those patterns.
Decision ↔ arranged outcome
ことにする / ことにしている concerns a decision or personal practice. ことになる / ことになっている concerns what is decided or arranged. Their meaning comes from the complete expression.
An event ↔ an object
食べること refers to eating; 食べる物 refers to something to eat. Both may become “thing” in loose English, so identify the actual referent.

JLPT approach

  1. Look to the right of the blank first. がある, はない, にする, and になる help identify fixed こと expressions. Then check the verb form before the blank.
  2. Track where the negative is. 返事をしないことがある means that not replying happens sometimes. 心配することはない means there is no need to worry in the reassurance context.
  3. Keep experience separate from frequency. 行ったことがある is past experience; 行くことがある is occasional occurrence. 行くこともあった places occasional occurrences in the past.
  4. Read the instruction as well as the sentence. Some book questions allow more than one choice. Do not force a single answer when the construction permits both forms.

Clues help you interpret a sentence. They are not guaranteed shortcuts based on a single word.

Useful expression

わからなければ、遠慮することはありません。

わからなければ、えんりょすることはありません。

If you do not understand, there is no need to hesitate.

An encouraging expression. ことはありません removes a perceived need to hold back; it does not predict whether the listener will hesitate.

Book grammar · の only

「の」だけを使う場合（「こと」は使わない）

Use の for a perceived event, a purpose evaluation, or focused information

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

の can package a clause as an event you directly perceive. In 子どもが遊んでいるのが見える, what is visible is the scene of the child playing. The book selects の for this direct sight, sound, or sensation.

The same の also appears in other constructions. Vるのに便利だ evaluates something as useful for doing an activity. ～のは…だ sets up an event, then highlights a time, reason, or other important detail. These are separate uses; neither is explained by “something you can see.”

Two fixed patterns complete this topic. ～というのは introduces a term for definition or explanation. ～のではないか / ～のではないだろうか presents a tentative thought: “Could it be that…?” or “I suspect that…”. The negative-looking question ending does not automatically deny the thought inside it.

Think of it this wayAsk what the sentence is doing: perceiving an event, evaluating usefulness for a purpose, highlighting a detail, defining a term, or proposing a tentative idea?

How to build the patterns

Direct perception: clause + の + を / が + perception verb
子どもが遊んでいるのを見る／遊んでいるのが見える. 見る takes を for what you watch; 見える takes が for what is visible. The choice of particle comes from the final verb.
Sound or sensation
赤ちゃんが泣いているのが聞こえる／家が揺れるのを感じる. ている presents an ongoing event; a dictionary-form verb can also present the event being sensed. Do not require ている in every perception sentence.
Purpose evaluation: V dictionary + のに（は）
覚えるのに便利だ／生活するのに必要だ. の packages the activity; に relates usefulness or necessity to that activity. は can add topic emphasis or contrast.
Focus: plain clause + のは + focused information + だ
会ったのは５年前だ／遅れたのは電車が止まったからだ. Read after は to find the highlighted time or reason. A past event keeps its past verb form before の.
Define a term: N / quoted expression + というのは
正三角形というのは…／「アクセスする」というのは…. The quoted expression can itself be a verb phrase. 名 in the book’s formation label means noun, not “name.”
Tentative thought: plain clause + のではないか
知っているのではないか = could it be that they know? Use な after a present affirmative noun or な-adjective: 学生なのではないか／必要なのではないか. Past forms retain だった: 必要だったのではないか.
More tentative or polite endings
～のではないだろうか adds a reflective “I wonder”; ～のではないでしょうか is polite. The clause before の can itself be negative: よくないのではないか = I suspect it is not good.

Book examples by use

Follow the labeled uses in order. In こと（の） and の（こと）, the form in parentheses is the rejected alternative.

A. Direct perception: see, hear, or feel an event

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

a）でとらえたやなどをとき（「・・・・など」のを。）

Use の to package an event perceived directly through sight, sound, or sensation, with verbs such as 見る, 見える, 眺める, 聞こえる, and 感じる.

Book example

・このからでがいるの（こと）が。

・I can see children playing in the garden from this window.

Clue Use の for the visible scene of children playing.

How to read this example

遊んでいる presents the ongoing activity; が fits 見える, “can be seen.” The children are not merely a fact someone knows about.

Book example

・あの、がの（こと）を。

・That day, I felt the house shake violently.

Clue The speaker directly felt the house shake.

How to read this example

の packages the sensed event and を marks what was felt. 揺れる does not need ている to describe an event perceived directly.

B. Purpose evaluation: useful or necessary for doing

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

b）「～のに（は）…」ので、～というについての（だ・など）を…でとき

In Vるのに（は）…, the activity is a purpose against which usefulness or necessity is evaluated, for example with 便利だ or 役に立つ.

Book example

・はこのでするの（こと）にどうしてもなのだ。

・A car is absolutely necessary for living in this village.

Clue 生活するのに identifies the activity for which a car is necessary.

How to read this example

This is a purpose evaluation, not “although one lives here.” The final なのだ is a separate explanatory ending.

Book example

・でをするの（こと）にはファストフードがやはりだ。

・Fast food is convenient when you want to eat in a short time.

Clue のには introduces the purpose of eating in a short time.

How to read this example

は adds emphasis or contrast to のに; 便利だ evaluates fast food as convenient for that purpose.

C. Focus: highlight a time, reason, or other detail

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

c）「～のは…だ」ので、したいを…でとき

In ～のは…だ, the phrase after は supplies the information being emphasized, such as the time or reason for an event.

Book example

・にの（こと）は５である。（５にに。）

・It was five years ago that I first met her. (I met her five years ago.)

Clue ５年前 is the information being highlighted.

How to read this example

会ったのは first sets up the event of meeting her; the rest identifies when it happened. である is a formal equivalent of だ.

Book example

・の（こと）はがあったからだ。

・The reason I came home late was that I had to work overtime.

Clue The reason for returning late comes after は.

How to read this example

の packages the late return, and 残業があったからだ explains why. This is not a statement defining what overtime is.

D. というのは: define a term or expression

Book wording and English gloss
Book explanation

d）「の」を

Fixed grammatical expressions that use の.

Book formation

～というのは…だ　⇒「～のは…だ。」
　＋というのは…だ

Noun / quoted expression + というのは…だ: introduce a term and explain what it means.

Book example

①というのはのさがのことである。

① An equilateral triangle is a triangle whose three sides are the same length.

Clue というのは introduces 正三角形 as the term being defined.

How to read this example

The later 三角形のこと refers to that type of triangle; one sentence can contain の and こと in different roles.

Book example

②「アクセスする」というのはどんなですか。

② What does "access" mean?

Clue The quoted verb アクセスする is the expression whose meaning is requested.

How to read this example

というのは can introduce a quoted phrase as well as a noun label.

E. のではないか: a tentative thought

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

～のではないか・～のではないだろうか　⇒「～と。」
（ナ　だ－な・　だ－な）＋のではないか・のではないだろうか

Plain clause + のではないか / のではないだろうか: tentatively propose an idea, “I suspect…” or “Could it be that…?”. Present affirmative nouns and な-adjectives take な before の.

Book example

①こんなにはによくないのではないか。

① I suspect food this salty is not good for your health.

Clue The tentative claim is that the food is not good for the body.

How to read this example

よくない stays negative inside のではないか. Do not interpret the whole ending as an assertion that the food is good.

Book example

②もしかしたらヤンさんはのことをいるのではないでしょうか。

② Could it be that Yang actually knows the truth?

Clue The speaker tentatively suggests that Yang knows the truth.

How to read this example

もしかしたら signals possibility and のではないでしょうか softens the proposal; the ending does not mean Yang lacks that knowledge.

Added examples

Check which construction is doing the work in each sentence.

Added example

のでがいるのが。

I can hear a baby crying in the next room.

Clue The sound of the baby crying is directly audible.

How to read this example

の packages the event, and が goes with 聞こえる. This differs from simply being told that a baby is crying.

Added example

をのにこのアプリはだ。

This app is useful for memorizing kanji.

Clue Memorizing kanji is the activity the app is useful for.

How to read this example

覚えるのに便利だ is a purpose evaluation; it does not mean “although I memorize kanji.”

Added example

がをのはのときだ。

It was in high school that I began Japanese.

Clue The time at high school is the highlighted information.

How to read this example

始めたのは sets up the past event, then 高校生のときだ identifies when it happened.

Added example

ほうがいいのではないか。

Wouldn't it be better to rest a little?

Clue The speaker tentatively suggests taking a short rest.

How to read this example

休んだほうがいい supplies the advice; のではないか makes it a tentative proposal, not a rejection of resting.

Do not confuse it with…

Perceiving an event ↔ knowing a fact
の is the book choice when a scene or sound is directly perceived. Do not apply that rule mechanically to 知っている, 覚えている, or information reported by someone: these can use こと or の depending on the sentence.
Useful for doing ↔ although · Lesson 4
のに can also mean “although”: 勉強したのに忘れた, “I studied, but I forgot.” In 覚えるのに便利だ, the sentence instead evaluates usefulness for an activity. Read the relationship, not just the identical spelling.
Purpose evaluation ↔ purpose of an action · Lesson 5
ために can state the purpose of an action: 日本語を勉強するために学校へ行く, “go to school to study Japanese.” This lesson’s のに connects an activity to what is useful or necessary for doing it. A purpose word in the English translation does not make the forms interchangeable.
Focus ↔ the content of a goal
夢は漫画家になることです tells what the dream is. 漫画家になったのは去年です focuses on when it happened. In 大切なのは続けることだ, の frames “what matters” and こと supplies the activity; both can occur together.
Define a term ↔ report information · Lesson 7
Nというのは… explains what a term means in this use. ～ということだ can report information. という does not by itself decide which construction follows.
Tentative idea ↔ denial · Lesson 8
Pのではないか often tentatively proposes P. A plain statement Pのではない can deny an explanation. ～わけではない rejects or limits a conclusion. Read the entire ending and its context.
の as “one” is another use
赤いのをください means “Please give me the red one.” Here の stands for an understood item. That is different from nominalizing the event in 赤ちゃんが泣いているのが聞こえる.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the complete construction. A perception verb, のに便利だ, and のは…からだ point to different uses of の.
  2. Read both sides of は. In a focus sentence, the clause before の sets the scene and the phrase after は supplies the key information.
  3. Do not cancel negatives mechanically. よくないのではないか suggests that something is not good. The outer question ending softens the proposal; it does not turn よくない into “good.”
  4. Treat “only の” as a rule for the illustrated construction. It does not mean the same verb or word can never occur with こと in any other meaning or structure.

Clues help you interpret a sentence. They are not guaranteed shortcuts based on a single word.

Useful expression

「見直す」というのは、どんな意味ですか。

「みなおす」というのは、どんないみですか。

What does “minaosu” mean?

Use this expression to ask about a word. The quoted verb is the term being defined; というのは introduces it.

Book one-point lesson

「物」と「こと」

Book check

◎（　）の中に「物」か「こと」を書きなさい。

1　由美が作る（　）はいつもとてもおいしいね。

2　おいしい料理を作る（　）はとても楽しい。

3　子どもを育てるという（　）をわたしは大切に考えています。

4　これは何という（　）ですか。触ってみてもいいですか。

5　子どものころ祖母にいつも言われていた（　）を思い出した。

6　友だちに「持ってきてね」と言われていた（　）を家に置いてきてしまった。

7　この作文は先週自習の時間に書いた（　）です。

8　先週作文に書いた（　）は、全部本当です。

9　今日やりたいのは、ここに置いてある（　）を片付ける（　）です。

物 or こと: what are you referring to?

物 · an item
Something to eat, an object you can show, or a keepsake you still have. A clause before 物 describes which item: 食べる物 = something to eat.
こと · content or an event
What someone said, the activity of eating, or the remembered event of receiving a gift. 食べること = eating.

Useful test: Are you pointing to the item, or talking about what happened / what was said? An event can be visible too, so “Can I see it?” alone is not enough.

Compare the souvenir examples below: keeping the gift focuses on 物; remembering someone bringing it focuses on こと. English “thing” can hide this distinction.

Book one-point reference: printed page 69. This comparison concerns literal 物 and content/event こと; other fixed expressions with もの have their own meanings.

Book explanation

：がある、でに

物 refers to a physical item or entity. In these examples, focus on the thing shown, eaten, or kept.

Book example

・ちょっとください。これ、きのうです。

・Please take a look. This is what we talked about yesterday.

Clue 物 refers to a particular item that can now be shown.

How to read this example

The item was the subject of yesterday’s conversation; it is not the content of what was said.

Book example

・かはありませんか。

・Do you have anything to eat?

Clue 食べる物 means something to eat, such as food.

How to read this example

食べること would mean the activity of eating. Identify whether the speaker seeks an item or names an action.

Book example

・、おじができてくれたをでもにしている。

・I still cherish the things my uncle bought for me in a foreign country a long time ago.

Clue The speaker still cherishes the item or items the uncle brought.

How to read this example

物 is the object kept; 買ってきてくれた describes what the uncle did for the speaker.

Book explanation

こと：がなく、で

こと refers to content, a fact, an activity, or an event, rather than a physical item.

Book example

・きのうわたしがことはですよ。

・Everything I said yesterday is true.

Clue こと refers to the content of what the speaker said.

How to read this example

全部本当 evaluates those statements as true; it does not evaluate physical objects.

Book example

・わたしたちだけでおいしいをことは、にはいようね。

・Let’s not tell Dad that we are eating delicious food without him.

Clue 物 and こと refer to different things here.

How to read this example

おいしい物 is the food; that food being eaten by the speakers without Dad is the event packaged by 食べること. だけ limits who joins in.

Book example

・、おじがからおをきてくれたことをよくいる。

・I clearly remember my uncle bringing me a souvenir from abroad a long time ago.

Clue The speaker remembers the event of the uncle bringing a souvenir.

How to read this example

こと refers to that remembered happening; compare 物 when the focus is the keepsake itself.
