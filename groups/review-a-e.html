ApproachQuestionsSubmit / scoreReview guide

A four-step approach to mixed questions

  1. Read the situation. Who acts? Is the speaker explaining, deciding, comparing, warning, or reporting something?
  2. Predict the relationship. Look for a target, purpose, result, time, degree, or intention. Translate the message before choosing a familiar-looking ending.
  3. Build the full phrase. Check the verb form and the words on both sides of the blank. Keep any printed ない, です, んです, noun, or particle in your answer.
  4. Test the alternatives. A choice may be valid Japanese elsewhere but change the intended meaning here. Explain why your answer fits both the form and the context.

JLPT practice tip: a clue narrows your options; it does not replace reading the whole sentence. After reviewing a mistake, name the distinction you missed before retrying.

Open the grammar comparison guide
Target, viewpoint, or topic?
Use Nに対して for a directed action or attitude; Nにとって for an evaluation from that standpoint; Nについて for a topic of knowledge or discussion. In a question containing a person, the person’s role matters more than simply being human. Review Group B.
Decision, repeated effort, or occasional event?
Vる / Vない + ことにしている is a maintained personal rule. Vる / Vない + ようにしている is an ongoing effort or practice. Vる + こともある describes something that sometimes happens. Fixed expressions do not allow こと and の to be exchanged freely. Review Group C: こと and Lesson 11.
Understand a reason, deny an assumption, or refuse an action?
わけだ: “That explains it.” わけではない: “It is not the case that…” わけにはいかない: “I cannot responsibly do that.” For someone else’s permitted action, check causative formation: 歩く → 歩かせる. Review Group E.
Purpose or method?
Result + ように, action states the outcome the action aims for. Action + ことで, result gives a means or cause. First check the verb’s roles: 意味が伝わる versus 意味を伝える. Purpose ために often accompanies a deliberate action; its use is not determined by one isolated English translation. Review Group D: purpose.
Small amount, minimum, or insufficient basis?
ぐらいなら can frame a limited amount as a condition. Nぐらい can mark a basic minimum in a suitable context. だけでは…ない says that alone is insufficient; だけでも includes even that small amount. さえ highlights a notable or unexpected included case. Review ぐらい and だけ.
Definition, confirmation, or tentative explanation?
XというのはYという意味だ defines an expression. Vた + のではないですか can suggest a possible explanation; the ない does not automatically deny it. Vた / Vていた + とおり links a result with a prior action or expectation. Review Group C: の and Lesson 2: とおり.

Useful expression

なるほど、そういうわけですね。

なるほど、そういう わけですね。

I see—that explains it.

A useful response when new information makes a situation understandable. It expresses a conclusion, not an obligation.

  2. Explain the correct answer in one sentence, then say what the nearest distractor would mean.
