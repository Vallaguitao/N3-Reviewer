Book grammar · resemblance

～（かの）ようだ・～のようだ・～（かの）ように…・～のように…

Describe something as if it were something else

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

In this use, よう compares a person, thing, or situation with an image that helps describe it. Working ロボットのように means working like a robot; it does not identify the worker as an actual robot. まるで…かのようだ makes an imagined “as if…” comparison especially vivid.

The ending depends on what よう connects to. ようだ is a predicate, ような describes a following noun, and ように connects the comparison to an action or state. The meaning alone does not tell you which ending fits.

A comparison can resemble an inference in English. “He works like a robot” compares his manner to a robot; “He seems tired” judges his condition from evidence. This topic starts with the comparison use. Always read the full situation before translating よう as “seems.”

Think of it this wayFirst ask what is being compared. Then look immediately after the blank: a noun, an action/state, or the end of a predicate?

How to build the patterns

Noun + の + よう
ロボットのようだ／ロボットのように働く／ロボットのような動き. Keep の after a noun; the book’s 名 means noun.
Plain clause + よう
春が来たようだ／バケツをひっくり返したような雨. Keep the past or negative form needed inside the comparison. Do not attach a normal ます form directly in this comparison pattern.
A vivid comparison: clause + かのよう
何もなかったかのように = as if nothing had happened. かの belongs together; do not omit の when the printed sentence already ends in か.
Noun predicate + であるかのよう
王様であるかのように振る舞う = behave as if one were a king. This formal clause pattern differs from the simpler noun + のように.
ような + noun
雲のような犬／見てきたかのような話し方. な links the description directly to the noun.
ように + action or state
魚のように泳ぐ／眠っているかのように静かだ. The following predicate may be a verb or an adjective.
ようだ is a predicate, even inside a longer sentence
春のようです／春のようだった. A plain predicate can continue into ようだから or ようだと言われた. ようだ does not have to be the last word of the whole sentence.

Book introduction
Book introduction

「よう」をは「いることをもの」と「すること・することをもの」ののに。

There are two main uses for よう: to express similarity, and to express expectation of or a demand for something.

A. Resemblance and imagined comparisons

Book wording and English gloss
Book meaning

⇒よくいるものに

Used to illustrate how something resembles something else, or to illustrate by metaphor.

Book formation

の ＋ようだ・ように
だ－である ＋かのような・かのように
　 ＋（かの）ようだ・（かの）ように

Noun + のようだ / のように; noun predicate + であるかのような / であるかのように; plain verb clause + （かの）ようだ / （かの）ように. Use ような before a noun, as in example ③.

Book example

①は、まるでがかのようだ。

①It's warm today, and it feels like spring has arrived.

Clue まるで and かのようだ create an “as if” comparison.

How to read this example

来た is past inside the imagined situation of spring having arrived; the sentence describes the warmth now.

Book example

②からまでロボットのように。

②I worked like a robot from morning till night.

Clue The robot describes the manner of working.

How to read this example

ように connects that comparison to 働いた; it does not make the speaker a literal robot.

Book example

③バケツをようなだった。

③ It rained as if someone had tipped out a bucket of water.

Clue 雨 is the noun being described, so the form is ような.

How to read this example

The image is water pouring out of an overturned bucket, not rain feeling like the bucket itself.

Expanded explanation

Quick form check: ようだ is a predicate, including inside a quotation or reason clause; ような modifies a noun; ように connects to an action or state. かの makes an imagined comparison explicit.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はのように。

He swims quickly like a fish.

Clue A fish is the comparison for the way he swims.

How to read this example

の connects 魚 to よう, and に connects the phrase to 速く泳ぐ.

Added example

がいるかのようにだった。

It was quiet as if the entire town were asleep.

Clue The whole town being asleep is an imagined comparison.

How to read this example

かのように connects it to the state 静かだった; ように can lead to an adjective, not only a verb.

Added example

のようながきた。

A white dog like a cloud came running.

Clue ような modifies the noun 犬.

How to read this example

The whiteness or fluffy appearance supplies the cloud comparison; the dog is not being identified as a cloud.

Do not confuse it with…

Resemblance ↔ a real example
にんじんのような野菜 names carrots as a real example of vegetables. ロボットのように働く creates an image of how someone works. Ask whether the named item belongs to the category or supplies a comparison.
ような ↔ ように
Compare 雲のような犬, “a cloud-like dog,” with 雲のように白い, “white like a cloud.” The next word determines the connection, even though the comparison is similar.
ようだ ↔ appearance そうだ
飲みそうなお酒 means liquor someone looks likely to drink. 飲むかのような… sets up an “as if drinking” comparison. The exercise contrasts a likely action with an imagined situation; do not choose by a shared English “looks like.”
Comparison よう ↔ volitional よう · Lesson 11
食べようとする uses the volitional form 食べよう. 食べるように uses dictionary-form 食べる plus the separate word よう. The extra る changes the grammar.

JLPT approach

  1. Check the attachment first. A noun immediately after the blank points to ような; an action or state often takes ように; a complete predicate takes ようだ.
  2. Continue past the blank. から and quotation と can follow a completed ようだ predicate. Do not reject ようだ simply because more words follow.
  3. Keep かの together. If the stem already ends in か, choose the option that supplies のように or のような as needed.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

まるで夢のようです。

まるでゆめのようです。

It feels just like a dream.

A vivid reaction to something surprising or wonderful. The comparison does not establish that the event is literally a dream.

Book grammar · comparison and examples

～ように…

Show a shared feature, or give a representative example

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Use Aように、B when B is similar to A in the relevant respect: “just as A…, B….” Humans need nutrients, and plants do too. The statement compares a shared feature; it does not say that plants look human.

Nのような… can also give an example of a larger category. Carrots and peppers exemplify deeply colored vegetables; a kind person exemplifies the kind of companion the speaker wants. English “such as” or “someone like…” can be more helpful than a mechanical “seems.”

The book also includes “the kind of relationship you imagine.” The clause describes a type; ではありません then denies that the actual relationship matches it. Keep the final negative in the interpretation.

Think of it this wayIs the sentence saying “B shares this feature with A,” or “N is an example of this kind of thing”? Then check whether よう modifies a noun or connects clauses.

How to build the patterns

Shared feature: noun + のように
人間のように、植物にも…. の connects the noun to よう. The later も helps show that the same feature applies to the second group too.
Shared feature: plain clause + ように
母親が明るい人だったように…. だった stays past: the clause recalls what the mother was like. Negative and ている forms also retain the meaning required by context.
Adjective and noun-predicate connections
い-adjectives connect directly. Present affirmative な-adjectives take な or formal である before よう. A noun used as the comparison standard takes の; a formal noun-predicate clause can take である.
Example before a noun: Nのような + category
にんじんのような野菜／林さんのような人. The following noun names the category or kind being described.
Example introducing a description: Nのように、…
日本語のように、使う文字が３種類もある言語…. 日本語 is the example; the following clause describes the relevant feature.
Clause + ような + noun
想像しているような関係／子どもでも食べられるようなメニュー. The clause describes what kind of relationship or menu is meant. Choose な for the noun connection.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

A. Compare a shared feature

Book wording and English gloss
Book meaning A

⇒であることを

Indicates that something is roughly the same.

Book formation

の・（ナ だ－な／－である・ だ－である）＋ように

Noun + のように, or a plain clause + ように. Present affirmative な-adjectives use な or である; formal noun predicates use である. Use ような to modify the noun in example ③.

Book example

①のように、にもがだ。

① Just like humans, plants also need nutrients.

Clue Humans and plants share a need for nutrients.

How to read this example

The later にも reinforces that the same feature applies to plants too; this is not a claim that plants resemble human bodies.

Book example

②がだったように、そのもが。

② Just as their mother was a cheerful person, her daughters also have cheerful personalities.

Clue The daughters share their mother’s cheerful character.

How to read this example

だった keeps the description of the mother in the past; ように connects the comparison to the daughters.

Book example

③わたしたちはあなたがしているようなではありませんよ。

③We don't have the kind of relationship you imagine.

Clue ような connects the imagined kind to 関係.

How to read this example

The final ではありません denies that their relationship matches the listener’s idea; do not drop that negative.

B. Give an example or describe a kind

Book wording and English gloss
Book meaning B

⇒を

Cite as an example.

Book formation

の ＋ように

Noun + のように introduces an example with a following description. Before a noun, use のような, as in examples ② and ③.

Book example

①のように、が３もあるは。

① Languages like Japanese, which use as many as three types of characters, are unusual.

Clue Japanese is the example of a language using several character types.

How to read this example

３種類も emphasizes as many as three; the point is an example plus its relevant feature.

Book example

②わたしはにんじんやピーマンのようなのがだ。

②I like dark-colored vegetables like carrots and green peppers.

Clue Carrots and peppers are examples of the vegetables liked.

How to read this example

のような modifies 野菜; や shows a non-exhaustive list.

Book example

③は。のようなと。

③ Hayashi is kind. I would like to be with someone like Hayashi.

Clue Hayashi’s kindness is the feature the speaker wants in a companion.

How to read this example

のような人 means a person like Hayashi; つき合いたい concerns associating with or dating such a person, depending on context.

Expanded explanation

The first use compares two cases as broadly alike. The second presents one noun as a representative member of a larger category.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

がように、もをいる。

Just as my father runs every morning, I also keep exercising.

Clue The father’s routine and the speaker’s routine are broadly parallel.

How to read this example

ように compares their continuing exercise; 私も reinforces the shared feature.

Added example

のように、がを。

I want to visit a city with many old buildings, such as Kyoto.

Clue Kyoto supplies an example of a city with many old buildings.

How to read this example

ように introduces the feature, while 訪れたい states what the speaker wants to do.

Added example

のようななに。

I want to learn from a kind teacher like Mr. Tanaka.

Clue Tanaka is the example of a kind teacher.

How to read this example

ような modifies 先生; に identifies the person from whom the speaker wants to learn.

Do not confuse it with…

Shared feature ↔ vivid metaphor
ロボットのように働く uses a robot as an image. 人間のように植物にも栄養が必要だ compares two groups’ actual need. The same form can serve different comparisons.
Examples ↔ exhaustive lists · Group A
など and Nのような… can present examples. にんじんやピーマン names sample vegetables; it need not mean those are the only vegetables liked.
Similarity ↔ the same procedure · Lesson 2
とおりに emphasizes following a model or instruction as given. ように can describe a broad similarity; it does not always require an exact match.
An example ↔ a typical quality
ケーキやあんパンのような甘い物 presents examples of sweet foods. らしい can mean “typical of” in other contexts, but it does not replace this example-to-category link.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the category noun. 人, 野菜, 関係, 問題点, and メニュー can follow ような. Read their modifying clause as one unit.
  2. Look for a shared feature. A later も often reinforces “A is like this, and B is too.” It is evidence, not an automatic answer rule.
  3. Do not lose a negative ending. 想像しているような関係ではない denies that particular kind of relationship.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

京都のような町に住んでみたいです。

きょうとのようなまちにすんでみたいです。

I would like to try living in a town like Kyoto.

京都 supplies the model or example; な connects it to 町. The exact feature admired depends on context.

Book grammar · known information and hoped result

～ように…

Refer back to information, or look ahead to a desired result

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Known-information ように means “as…”. 前にも話したように refers back to an earlier explanation; 表からわかるように points to what a chart shows. The statement after ように is presented as consistent with that information.

Desired-result ように means “so that…” or “in a way that…”. The speaker takes an action to make a state possible or prevent an unwanted event. だれにでもわかるように説明する means explain in a way everyone can understand; the understanding is the intended result.

Potential forms, naturally occurring states, and negative outcomes are common in this result use: 読める, 聞こえる, 忘れない. Another person can also perform the desired action: 子どもが野菜を食べるように. The key is the result being aimed at, not a rule that every verb must lack volition in every context.

Think of it this wayDoes the first clause identify information already available, or a result that the later action aims to achieve? Follow the direction of the meaning.

How to build the patterns

Known information: V dictionary / た / ている + ように
表からわかるように／前に話したように／表示されているように. Choose tense according to the evidence: a past message uses あった or 話した, while a current display can use ている.
A set expression: ご存じのように
“As you know.” Keep this conventional noun-like connection with の. It does not follow by simply adding ように to a polite verb.
Desired ability: potential verb + ように
読む → 読めるように; 覚える → 覚えられるように; する → できるように. The goal is being able to perform the action.
Desired state: dictionary form + ように
聞こえるように／見えるように／治るように. These describe what should become audible, visible, or recovered, rather than a deliberate act of listening, looking, or curing.
Prevent an event: Vない + ように
忘れないように書く／目を覚まさないように音を小さくする. The negative belongs to the unwanted event being prevented.
Different actors can appear
子どもが食べるように、親が料理を工夫する. The child eats; the parent adjusts the cooking. With ように, the desired event and the supporting action need not have the same subject.
Do not confuse form with intended meaning
生活するのに便利だ evaluates usefulness for living; 読めるように練習する aims to enable reading. The whole pattern determines how the activity and the result relate.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

A. Refer to known or available information

Book wording and English gloss
Book meaning A

⇒…のがすでにいるとする

Used at the front of a sentence, indicates that something is already understood or known.

Book formation

　／た／ている ＋ように

V dictionary / Vた / Vている + ように: refer to information as known or available. ご存じのように in example ③ is a conventional expression with a different connection.

Book example

①にもように、はわたしはにいません。

①As I said before, I won't be in Japan next week.

Clue The earlier statement is the reference point.

How to read this example

話したように means “as I said”; the speaker is not trying to make the earlier statement happen.

Book example

②のにあったように、はのできがいいらしい。

② As reported in this morning’s newspaper, the rice crop seems to be good this year.

Clue The newspaper provides the information being referred to.

How to read this example

書いてあった is past, and らしい keeps the rice-crop statement as reported or inferred information.

Book example

③のように、はがです。

③As you know, Japan is a country with many typhoons.

Clue ご存じのように is the set phrase “as you know.” It respectfully treats the listener as already knowing the following information; memorize its の connection.

B. Aim for a result or prevent an event

Book wording and English gloss
Book meaning B

⇒することを。

Express a desired result: so that a state becomes possible, an event happens, or an unwanted event is prevented.

Book formation

　／ない ＋ように

V dictionary / Vない + ように. In this use, the clause presents a desired state or result, often with a potential verb, a non-volitional verb, or another person as the subject.

Book example

①よくようにワインを。

①I drank some wine so that I could sleep well.

Clue 眠れる is the potential form: being able to sleep is the desired result.

How to read this example

飲んだ reports what the speaker did with that aim; ように does not establish that the method worked.

Book example

②はをだれにでもわかるようにする。

② Mr. Ikeda explains difficult social issues in a way that anyone can understand.

Clue Understanding is the intended result of the explanation.

How to read this example

わかる describes comprehension, and だれにでも extends it to anyone; ように means “in a way that…” here.

Book example

③がをようにテレビのをした。

③ I turned down the sound on the TV so that the baby wouldn't wake up.

Clue The baby not waking is the desired state.

How to read this example

The person who adjusts the volume and the baby who might wake are different participants.

Expanded explanation

Known-information ように points backward to shared knowledge. Result-oriented ように points forward to a state the speaker wants to make possible or prevent.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

がしたように、このはでよく。

As the teacher explained, this form is often used in writing.

Clue The teacher’s earlier explanation is the reference point.

How to read this example

Explained content is being recalled, not expressed as a future goal.

Added example

にされているように、はしました。

As shown on the screen, registration has ended.

Clue The screen already displays the relevant information.

How to read this example

表示されている is the current displayed state; 受付は終了しました states what that information says.

Added example

ように、をカレンダーに。

I put the event on my calendar so that I would not forget.

Clue The calendar entry is intended to prevent forgetting.

How to read this example

忘れない is the desired negative outcome, and 入れた is the supporting action.

Added example

のにもように、でください。

Please speak loudly so that people in the back can hear.

Clue 聞こえる describes sound reaching the people in the back.

How to read this example

大きな声で話してください requests the action that would make that result possible.

Added example

がように、をに。

I put the medicine up high so that the child would not touch it.

Clue The child’s not touching the medicine is the desired outcome.

How to read this example

The speaker controls where it is placed, while the child is the subject of 触らない.

Added example

でニュースがように、をしている。

I study kanji every day so that I can read the news in Japanese.

Clue 読める marks a reading ability the speaker aims to develop.

How to read this example

Daily kanji study supports that aim; the sentence does not yet say the ability has been achieved.

Do not confuse it with…

ように ↔ ために · One-point lesson
ように / ために: compare “so the dirt comes off” with “in order to remove the dirt.” In the book contrast, ように frames a desired state or ability; ために frames an intended action as the goal.
Desired result ↔ reported instruction
忘れないように言われた reports someone’s instruction not to forget. 忘れないようにメモした describes a note-taking action meant to prevent forgetting. Read the main predicate.
Purpose evaluation · Group C
Vるのに便利だ / 必要だ evaluates something as useful or necessary for an activity. It differs from explaining how an action is taken to bring about a result.
An effort or habit · Lesson 11
ようにする / ようにしている describes making an effort or maintaining a practice. In 読めるように勉強する, the concrete supporting action is 勉強する.
A desired change ↔ a change that happens
ようになる reports a change in ability or behavior. 読めるように勉強している gives the aim; 読めるようになった says that ability has developed.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the evidence or goal. A chart, prior announcement, or earlier explanation supports “as.” An action plus the state it is meant to enable supports “so that.”
  2. Separate paired verbs. 見る / 聞く are deliberate actions; 見える / 聞こえる describe visibility or audibility. Their similar English translations can hide the test point.
  3. Do not turn an aim into a confirmed result. ～ように…した tells us what someone wanted to achieve; it does not prove that the result occurred.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

忘れないように、メモしておきます。

わすれないように、めもしておきます。

I’ll make a note so I don’t forget.

The desired result is not forgetting; ておきます presents the note as preparation for later.

Book grammar · demand and request

～ように…・～ようにと…・～よう…

Report instructions, make a formal request, or express a wish

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Vる / Vない + ように + a reporting verb conveys what someone is asked or told to do. 出すように言った means told someone to submit it. 話さないように注意された means was warned not to speak. The requested action is inside the ように clause.

Here deliberate-action verbs are natural. 買う and 書く can be things someone is instructed to do. Do not extend the purpose lesson’s preference for states and abilities into a ban on these verbs in reported requests.

The book also includes formal requests and prayers. ご返信くださいますようお願い申し上げます is a formal request for a reply. 合格できますように is a wish for being able to pass. A wish is not a report that somebody issued a command, even though the book groups these expressions together.

Think of it this wayFind the main action: told, asked, warned, requested, or prayed. Then identify who is supposed to do what, or which outcome is being wished for.

How to build the patterns

Reported positive instruction
V dictionary + ように言う / 頼む: 宿題を出すように言う. Keep the action in plain form inside the report.
Reported negative instruction
Vない + ように注意する: ここに置かないように注意する. Negative ない belongs to the action being discouraged.
Variants: ように / ようにと / よう
話さないようにと注意された／置かないよう注意された. The optional と marks the reported content; よう without に is common in formal wording.
Track the people through a passive verb
頼む → 頼まれる; 注意する → 注意される. 頼まれた means the subject received the request. It does not tell us the requester’s gender or identity when they are omitted.
Formal request: ～ますよう（に）お願いする
ご返信くださいますようお願い申し上げます. Unlike ordinary resemblance forms, this formal request can use the ます form before よう.
Prayer or wish: ～ますように
合格できますように／早く治りますように. A final 祈っています may be stated or left understood. The hoped-for person can be the speaker or someone else; context identifies them.
Deadline words still matter
金曜日までに出す = submit by Friday. までに marks the deadline; ように marks the content of the instruction. Do not confuse the two に phrases.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

A. Report requests and warnings

Book wording and English gloss
Book meaning

⇒することを

Express the content of an instruction or request. The examples also include a formal request and a prayer.

Book formation

　／ない ＋ように・ようにと・よう

Formation: dictionary or negative verb form + ように, ようにと, or よう. Polite requests such as examples 3 and 4 may also use the ます form.

Book example

①をくるように。

① I was asked to buy a magazine and bring it back.

Clue 頼まれた means the speaker was asked to do something.

How to read this example

買ってくる includes going to buy the magazine and bringing it back; the requester is not identified in the sentence.

Book example

②のではでようにとされた。

②I was warned not to talk on my cell phone on the train.

Clue 話さない is the content of the warning.

How to read this example

ようにと introduces that content, and 注意された makes the subject the person who received the warning.

B. Make a formal request

Book example

③にごくださいますよう、。

③ I would be grateful if you could reply by the end of this week.

Clue This is a formal request for the other person to reply.

How to read this example

The polite くださいます before よう is allowed in this request formula; 今週中に gives the deadline.

C. Express a prayer or wish

Book example

④にできますように。（ときの）

④ May I pass the exam. (A prayer or wish; the person meant depends on context.)

Clue できますように expresses a prayer for success.

How to read this example

It is not an assertion of ability or a guarantee of passing; whose success is meant depends on the situation.

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はにをまでにように。

The teacher told the students to submit the homework by Friday.

Clue The teacher gives the instruction and the students are to submit the homework.

How to read this example

出すように marks the instruction’s content; 金曜日までに marks the deadline.

Added example

からここにをようされた。

An attendant warned me not to leave luggage here.

Clue The attendant is the source of the warning.

How to read this example

置かないよう omits に in a more formal version of 置かないように; the negative instruction remains unchanged.

Added example

がによう、。

We sincerely pray that everyone can return safely.

Clue The speaker wishes for everyone’s safe return.

How to read this example

帰れます is potential plus politeness, while お祈り申し上げます formally expresses the prayer.

Do not confuse it with…

Request ↔ intended result
聞こえるように話す means speak so people can hear. 大きな声で話すように言う means tell someone to speak loudly. The first gives an aim; the second reports an instruction.
Reported instruction ↔ direct command · Lesson 10
出せ / 出すな are direct command/prohibition forms. 出すように / 出さないように言う reports the content through another verb.
Formal request ↔ respectful forms · Lesson 12
くださいます respectfully refers to the other person’s action. お願い申し上げます humbly presents the speaker’s request. The roles remain distinct.
Wish ↔ certainty
できますように hopes for success. It neither guarantees success nor says the action has already happened. A polite ending does not change a wish into a fact.
Volitional form · Lesson 11
出そうとする uses the volitional 出そう, “try / be about to submit.” 出すように言う contains plain 出す plus よう, “tell someone to submit.”

JLPT approach

  1. Read beyond ように. 言う, 頼む, 注意する, and their passive forms identify reported instructions; 祈る identifies a wish.
  2. Keep the perspective straight. 頼まれた reports receiving a request. Identify the performer of the requested action separately from the person who requested it.
  3. Recognize the polite exception. ますように is natural in wishes and formal requests. Do not reject it by applying the plain-form rule for comparisons.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

ご確認くださいますよう、お願いいたします。

ごかくにんくださいますよう、おねがいいたします。

I would be grateful if you could check this.

A formal written request. くださいます respects the recipient’s checking; お願いいたします presents the speaker’s request.

「～ように」と「～ために」

10 questions · ように or ために? Start with the verb before the blank, its subject, and the result or action it describes. Similar English translations can hide different Japanese structures.

Hints are optional. Submit all ten answers to see the reasoning, then compare the four book examples below.

◎（　）の中に「ように」か「ために」を書きなさい。

1　汚れをきれいに落とす（　）、特別な洗剤を使ってみた。

2　汚れがきれいに落ちる（　）、特別な洗剤を使ってみた。

3　この本は、簡単に漢字が覚えられる（　）、説明が工夫されている。

4　漢字をしっかり覚える（　）、一つの漢字を何度も紙に書いた。

5　覚えた漢字を忘れない（　）、ときどき復習している。

6　かぜが早く治る（　）、ビタミンCをたくさんとっています。

7　自分の店を持つという夢を実現する（　）、会社を辞めた。

8　太陽の光が部屋いっぱいに入る（　）、カーテンを大きく開けてください。

9　大学に入る（　）、わたしはたくさんの準備をしなければならなかった。

10　子どもがいたずらをしない（　）、書類をきちんと片付けておこう。

Choose from the Japanese goal, not the English “for”

ように · a desired state, ability, or event
読めるように = so that someone can read; 忘れないように = so as not to forget. The supporting action aims to enable or prevent an outcome. Its subject may differ from the subject of the desired event.
ために · an intended action as the goal
読むために = in order to read. In the textbook’s purpose contrast, the person taking the supporting action also intends to carry out the goal action.

Watch the verb pair: 汚れを落とす means remove dirt; 汚れが落ちる means dirt comes off. Also distinguish 覚える from potential 覚えられる. Identify who acts and what changes.

Do not overextend the shortcut: ように can describe another person’s desired deliberate action. A negative verb does not universally rule out ために in all contexts. These exercises teach the stated action-versus-result contrast.

Keep purpose separate from cause: ため（に） in Lesson 5 can also explain a cause. Read whether the sentence gives a goal or explains why something happened.

Book comparison: printed page 73. An intended result is not proof that the chosen action achieves it.

Book explanation · ～ように

そうなってほしい。のを（・のの・がになるなど）につく。の・ないにつく。

ように presents a state or outcome someone wants to bring about. Dictionary and ない forms are used, commonly with states, abilities, or another person’s desired action. The desired event need not be directly controlled by the person taking the supporting action.

Book example

・試合でいい成績が残せるように、みんながんばって練習している。

Everyone is practicing hard so that they can achieve good results in the match.

Clue 残せる is potential: being able to achieve a good result is the desired outcome.

How to read this example

ように connects practice to that ability-oriented aim.

Book example

・子どもがたくさん野菜を食べるように、料理をいろいろ工夫している。

I vary the cooking in different ways so that the children will eat plenty of vegetables.

Clue The children eat, while the speaker adjusts the cooking.

How to read this example

ように presents another person’s desired behavior, even though 食べる can be a deliberate action.

Book explanation · ～ために

そうしようというの。のをのにつく。

ために presents an intended action as the purpose. In this use, a verb in dictionary form names what the actor plans to do; the supporting action is taken to achieve that goal.

Book example

・試合でいい成績を残すために、みんなががんばって練習している。

Everyone is practicing hard in order to achieve good results in the match.

Clue 残す presents achieving a good result as an intended action.

How to read this example

In this comparison, ために introduces that action as the purpose of practicing.

Book example

・食材をむだなく食べるために、料理をいろいろ工夫している。

I vary the cooking in different ways in order to use the ingredients without wasting any.

Clue 食べる presents an intended action by the people planning the cooking.

How to read this example

ために links their preparation to eating the ingredients without waste.
