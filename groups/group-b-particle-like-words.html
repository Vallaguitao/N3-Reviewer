Book grammar · Topic

1. ～について…

Name what the information or discussion is about

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Nについて means “about N / concerning N.” N is the subject matter of thinking, asking, explaining, researching, writing, or knowing. In 学校について調べる, the school is what you investigate; it is not necessarily where you investigate.

The person you speak to and the topic can appear together: 先生に試験について質問する means ask the teacher about the exam. 先生に identifies the listener; 試験について identifies the content.

Do not translate the book’s formation label 名 as “name”: here it abbreviates noun. These expressions attach to a noun or noun phrase, and the main predicate supplies tense and politeness.

Think of it this wayAsk “About what?” Then keep that topic separate from whom the speaker addresses and where the action happens.

How to attach it

Noun + について + predicate
教育について書く／手続きについて聞く. Typical predicates deal with information or thought; the noun itself does not conjugate.
Noun + についての + noun
作品についての感想／教育についてのレポート. Add の when the phrase directly describes the following noun.
A clause can already modify the noun
文化について書いてある資料 = materials in which something is written about culture. 書いてある forms the modifying clause, so do not insert の before that verb.
Add emphasis after the expression
この件については知っています／その点についても説明します. は and も focus or include the topic without changing “about.”
Negative and polite endings
その人について何も知りません. The negative is on 知る; について itself remains unchanged. 何も + negative means know nothing about that person.
Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　＋について

Formation: noun + について.

Book explanation

「～の・～にがあることを…する。」～はを。…は「・・・・・・する・いる」などのの。

Introduce the topic or content of thinking, speaking, asking, researching, writing, explaining, or knowing.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the relationship.

Book example

①すみません、についてのですが……。

①Excuse me, I would like to ask you about the admission procedure...

Clue 入学手続き is the topic of the question.

How to read this example

聞きたいのですが politely introduces the wish to ask; it does not yet give the question itself.

Book example

②、わたしののについてレポートをいます。

②I am currently writing a report about education in my country.

Clue The report concerns education in the speaker’s country.

How to read this example

について identifies its subject matter, while 書いています describes the current writing activity.

Book example

③このについてのをいただけませんか。

③Could you tell me your thoughts about this work?

Clue についての links the work to the noun 感想.

How to read this example

話していただけませんか respectfully asks the other person to share those impressions.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

のについています。

I am researching Japanese festivals.

Clue The festivals are what the speaker researches.

How to read this example

について does not identify where the research takes place or whom the speaker asks.

Added example

にについてしました。

I asked the teacher about the exam.

Clue 先生に gives the person asked; 試験について gives what the question concerns.

How to read this example

Both relationships can coexist.

Do not confuse it with…

Topic ↔ target
親に対して文句を言う directs a complaint to the parents. 親について文句を言う makes the parents the complaint’s subject matter; the listener could be someone else.
Topic ↔ perspective
私にとって難しい evaluates difficulty for me. 私について調べる investigates information about me. “For / about” are not interchangeable.
Topic ↔ direct object
この作曲家が好きです says I like the composer. この作曲家について何も知りません says I know nothing about them. Do not attach について just because a person is mentioned.
Additional focus · Group A
についてさえ can emphasize “even about.” The added particle does not erase the underlying topic relationship.

JLPT approach

  1. Use the predicate as evidence. 調べる, 説明する, 書く, and 知る often need a topic. They are clues, not a rule that every occurrence requires について.
  2. Check the next word. についての感想 connects to a noun; について書く connects directly to a verb.
  3. Separate the two participants. In 先生に試験について聞く, the teacher is asked and the exam is discussed.

Use clues to reason from context; a single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

試験の申し込みについて質問があります。

しけんのもうしこみについてしつもんがあります。

I have a question about registering for the exam.

The registration process is the topic. The sentence does not yet identify whom the speaker will ask.

Book grammar · Target and contrast

2. ～に対して…・～に対する

Direct an action or attitude toward a target

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Nに対して marks the target of an action, reaction, request, or attitude. A company asks residents for understanding; staff use polite language toward guests; people oppose an opinion. The target may be a person, group, proposal, or other thing being responded to.

It can often be translated as “toward,” “to,” or “against,” depending on the predicate. It does not itself mean opposition. 親切だ gives a kind attitude; 反対する gives opposition.

The book also recalls a separate contrast use: Aのに対してB, “whereas A…, B….” The rainy-month example compares two facts rather than directing an attitude toward a month.

Think of it this wayAsk “Toward whom or what is the action or attitude directed?” If two complete situations are compared, check the contrast use instead.

How to attach it

Noun + に対して + predicate
客に対してていねいだ／住民に対して理解を求める. The predicate identifies the action or attitude aimed at the target.
Before a noun: に対する + noun
政府に対する批判 = criticism directed at the government. Use に対する when directly modifying 批判. Memorize the pair: に対して + predicate / に対する + noun.
Polite or past predicates
利用者に対して説明しました. The final verb changes for tense or politeness; the linking expression stays に対して.
Add は / も / まで
家族に対しては親切だ／友だちに対してまで敬語を使う. Added particles mark contrast, inclusion, or unexpected extension.
Contrast: plain clause + のに対して
3月は多かったのに対して、4月は少なかった. For noun / な-adjective predicates in this clause pattern, use なのに対して: 静かなのに対して. Keep this distinct from a noun target + に対して.
Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　＋
　＋＋

Formation: noun + に対して; noun + に対する + noun.

Book explanation

「～に…する・…というを。」…は～へのやなどを（・を・する・きびしい・だなど）。

Identify the target toward which an action or attitude is directed. The predicate supplies the action or attitude, such as asking, complaining, thanking, being strict, or being kind.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the relationship.

A. Target of an action or attitude

Book example

A①ホテルのはにていねいなを。

A① Hotel staff use very polite language towards guests.

Clue The guests receive the staff’s polite language.

How to read this example

に対して points toward those guests; the predicate supplies the polite attitude.

Book example

A②ののみんながした。

A②Everyone in the family opposed my father's opinion.

Clue The father’s opinion is the target of opposition.

How to read this example

It is the opinion, not necessarily the father as a person, that the family opposes.

Book example

A③をため、はのを。

A③In order to build a new factory, the company sought the understanding of the local residents.

Clue The nearby residents are asked for understanding.

How to read this example

工場を作るため gives the purpose; に対して marks the people addressed.

Book example

A④、がなっている。

A④Recently, there has been growing criticism of the government.

Clue に対する directly modifies 批判.

How to read this example

The government is the target of the criticism; 大きくなっている describes its growth.

B. Contrast between two situations

Book cross-reference

B　、はのがの、は。

B This year, there were many rainy days in March, but there were fewer in April.

→第１部 ４課－①

Clue Two months are compared through their rainy-day counts.

How to read this example

多かったのに対して uses a complete clause plus の, introducing contrast rather than a target.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はするがある。

The company has a responsibility to explain things to its users.

Clue The users are the people to whom an explanation is owed.

How to read this example

説明する責任がある describes the company’s responsibility toward them.

Added example

はいつもだ。

My younger brother is always kind toward the family.

Clue The family receives the brother’s kindness.

How to read this example

親切だ supplies a positive attitude, showing that に対して need not mean against.

Do not confuse it with…

Target ↔ topic
先生についてうわさする means gossip about the teacher. 先生に対して失礼だ means be rude toward the teacher. The teacher need not hear gossip about them.
Target ↔ evaluation for someone
利用者にとってありがたい evaluates a service as welcome for users. 利用者に対して行う directs the service to users. Both can contain the same people.
Contrast · Lesson 4
～のに対して compares differing facts. Look for the clause boundary and a second contrasting situation.
Respectful language · Lesson 12
敬語 supplies respectful wording. 客に対して simply identifies whom the wording is directed toward; it is not itself an honorific conjugation.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the direction, not an automatic “against.” Gratitude, kindness, criticism, and demands all have targets.
  2. Check noun modification. に対する批判 has a noun immediately after the expression; に対して批判する has a verb.
  3. Watch for the printed の. A clause ending in の before the blank can lead to the contrast construction のに対して.

Use clues to reason from context; a single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

先生に対する感謝の気持ちを伝えたいです。

せんせいにたいするかんしゃのきもちをつたえたいです。

I want to express my gratitude to my teacher.

に対する links the target to the noun 感謝. The attitude is gratitude, not opposition.

Explanation reference

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Group B, printed pages 62–63. English guidance and extra expressions support the existing Japanese examples.

Book grammar · Agent and cross-references

3. ～によって…

Identify an agent, or recognize dependence, cause, and means

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

The main use here is the agent in a passive sentence: “by N.” In a novel written by an author or a building designed by an architect, によって identifies who performed the action. The work or result is often the sentence topic or subject.

によって does not create the passive. 書かれた, 作られた, and 設計された supply the passive verb form. A sentence can contain an active main verb while a smaller clause contains the passive: everyone sings “the song created by an unknown person.”

The book links to two other uses: dependence (“varies depending on the person”) and cause (“decreased because of the fare increase”). The exercises also use means: wind power turns a turbine. Read the noun and the predicate together to choose the relationship.

Think of it this wayAsk four different questions: “By whom?” “Depending on what?” “Because of what?” or “By means of what?” The spelling alone does not decide.

How to attach it

Agent: noun + によって + passive
作者によって書かれた. Group 1 書く → 書かれる; Group 2 建てる → 建てられる; する → される. Past and polite endings follow normally.
A passive clause before a noun
ある人によって作られた歌. The whole clause modifies 歌. A later active verb does not cancel the passive inside this clause.
Dependence: noun + によって
人によって違う／時代によって変わる. The person or period is the factor across which the result varies.
Cause or means: noun + によって
値上げによって利用者が減る／風の力によって風車を回す. A change can be a cause; a resource or method can be the means. These need not have passive predicates.
Before a noun: による + noun
値上げによる影響／専門家による説明. による directly modifies the noun; the relevant relationship still comes from context.
Personal passive agents often use に
リーさんに誘われた／先生にほめられた. Ordinary personal invitations and praise usually use に. によって is not a universal replacement whenever a passive appears.
Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　＋によって

Formation: noun + によって.

Book explanation

…は。～はそのをする。にのものがになるので、をするをときに。

In this use, によって identifies who performs the action in a passive clause. The thing created or affected is often inanimate; the verb, not によって itself, supplies the passive form.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the relationship.

A. Agent in a passive clause

Book example

A①『』というは1906にによって。

A① The novel “Botchan” was written by Natsume Soseki in 1906.

Clue The novel is the work written; Natsume Soseki is the writer.

How to read this example

書かれた supplies the passive, while によって names the agent.

Book example

A②このなはからこののによってきた。

A② Local people have preserved this traditional festival since long ago.

Clue The local people preserve the festival.

How to read this example

守られてきた combines passive preservation with continuity from the past up to now.

Book example

A③あるのによってこのを、ではみんながいる。

A③ Everyone now sings this song, which was created by an unknown person.

Clue The passive is inside 作られたこの歌, which describes the song.

How to read this example

The main clause is active: everyone now sings it. The unknown creator is still the agent of the earlier creation.

B. Dependence: varies according to…

Book cross-reference

B　によってが。

B Perceptions vary from person to person.

→第１部 ２課－②

Clue This is dependence, not a passive agent.

How to read this example

Different people feel or perceive things differently; 感じ方 means way of perceiving.

C. Cause: because of…

Book cross-reference

C　タクシーのによってが。

C. The number of users has decreased due to an increase in taxi fares.

→第１部 ５課－②

Clue The taxi-fare increase causes fewer people to use taxis.

How to read this example

減った means decreased and is intransitive, so no passive agent is being named.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

このはなによってされた。

This building was designed by a famous architect.

Clue The architect designs the building.

How to read this example

設計された is passive; the building, not the architect, is the thing being described as designed.

Added example

このはによって。

This piece was composed by a young composer.

Clue The composer performs the creating.

How to read this example

作られた presents the piece as the result of that action.

Do not confuse it with…

Agent ↔ role
代表として参加する states a participant’s role. 建築家によって設計された states who designed the building. “Architect” can be a role noun, but the passive relationship decides here.
Dependence · Lesson 2
人によって違う compares outcomes across people. It does not mean the differences were passively made by those people.
Cause · Lesson 5
値上げによって利用者が減った links a cause and its result. The intransitive 減った is not passive.
Passive ↔ respect · Lesson 12
～れる / られる can also express respect in other sentences. An identified agent and a created work support the passive reading here.

JLPT approach

  1. Check the whole clause. A passive may be inside the phrase modifying a noun, even when the final verb is active.
  2. Do not force “by a person” onto a change or resource. 値上げ is a cause; 風の力 is a means.
  3. A personal passive is a useful trap. The invitation exercise expects リーさんに誘われた. Choose ordinary usage in context, not the longest expression.

Use clues to reason from context; a single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

この資料は専門家によって作られました。

このしりょうはせんもんかによってつくられました。

These materials were created by an expert.

The materials receive the action; the expert performs it. 作られました supplies the passive.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Group B, printed pages 62–63. English guidance and extra expressions support the existing Japanese examples.

Book grammar · Standpoint

4. ～にとって…

Say for whom something is valuable, difficult, necessary, or meaningful

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Nにとって introduces the person or group whose situation, interests, or perspective determines an evaluation. Something is difficult for me, valuable for the family, or necessary for a learner. The sentence often ends in an adjective or an evaluative noun such as 問題 or 喜び.

The person named does not have to be speaking or consciously making the judgment. A narrator can explain what sleep means for growing children. にとって is not a general-purpose translation of every English “for.” It does not simply identify the recipient of an explanation or the topic of research.

にとっては can emphasize a contrast: a stone may look ordinary to others but be a precious keepsake to its owner. The value belongs to the perspective supplied by 私にとっては.

Think of it this wayAsk “For whom is this important, easy, welcome, or a problem?” Then find the actual evaluation in the predicate.

How to attach it

Person / group noun phrase + にとって
留学生にとって／家族にとって. A longer noun phrase such as 日本に住む留学生 still attaches as a unit.
An evaluative predicate follows
私にとって難しい／子どもにとって大切だ／親にとって喜びだ. Adjectives are common, but evaluative noun predicates work too.
Add は or も
私にとっては思い出の品だ／私にとっても大切だ. は can contrast perspectives; も includes another person who shares the evaluation.
Before a noun: にとっての + noun
私にとっての宝物 = a treasure for me. の connects the perspective phrase to the noun.
Negate the evaluation where needed
私にとって簡単だとは言えない = I cannot say it is easy for me. Do not drop the negative ending when translating.
Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　＋にとって

Formation: noun + にとって.

Book explanation

「～のからと…だ。」…はをが。

Evaluate something in relation to the named person’s or group’s situation, interests, or perspective. Adjectives and evaluative noun predicates are common.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the relationship.

Book example

①ににとってはなだ。

①The strong yen is a serious problem for international students living in Japan.

Clue にとって identifies students living in Japan as the group facing the problem.

How to read this example

The example’s situation assumes the stronger yen makes their finances harder; the phrase itself does not say that every student is affected equally.

Book example

②にとってはことです。

②Shopping is fun for young women.

Clue The sentence presents an evaluation for the named group.

How to read this example

Study that perspective structure; the broad statement is not a claim that every young woman shares the preference.

Book example

③これはただのですが、わたしにとってはのです。

③ This is just a stone, but to me it is an unforgettable keepsake.

Clue The stone has personal value for the speaker even if it looks ordinary.

How to read this example

にとっては emphasizes that perspective; 思い出の品 means a keepsake.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

わたしにとってのが。

For me, Japanese conversation is the most difficult.

Clue Conversation is evaluated as the hardest part for the speaker.

How to read this example

にとって identifies whose learning experience is being assessed.

Added example

にとってがだ。

For the family, health is more important than anything.

Clue The family’s interests frame the judgment.

How to read this example

何より大切 means more important than anything else in that evaluation.

Do not confuse it with…

Perspective ↔ target
住民に対して説明する directs an explanation to residents. 住民にとってわかりやすい evaluates how understandable it is for them.
Perspective ↔ topic
この問題について考える names what you think about. この問題は私にとって難しい evaluates the problem in relation to you.
Perspective ↔ role
教師として話す means speak in the role of a teacher. 教師にとって大切だ means important for teachers. Role and affected perspective are different relationships.
Judgment with qualification · Lesson 8
～とは限らない can limit a generalization. にとって simply specifies whose situation the evaluation concerns; it does not guarantee that everyone shares it.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the evaluation after the blank. 大切, 必要, 難しい, 面白い, and ありがたい are useful clues.
  2. Do not select only by the noun. A mayor can be a topic, a target, or the person facing difficulty; the following predicate resolves it.
  3. Read negative judgments to the end. 簡単だとは言えません rejects calling it easy; it does not assert that it is easy.

Use clues to reason from context; a single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

私にとって、毎日の復習が大切です。

わたしにとって、まいにちのふくしゅうがたいせつです。

Daily review is important for me.

にとって identifies whose learning situation the evaluation concerns.

Explanation reference

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Group B, printed pages 62–63. English guidance and extra expressions support the existing Japanese examples.

Book grammar · Role or capacity

5. ～として…

Identify a role, capacity, status, or intended use

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Nとして means “as N / in the role or capacity of N.” A person comes as a scholarship student or attends as a representative. An object can also have a role: a coffee cup is used as a vase.

The role need not be a profession or a freely chosen job. The exercises include being born as the eldest son and treating coffee or tea as medicine. The noun after “as” is the status or function assigned in that context.

Do not turn every として into “from someone’s viewpoint.” A role can influence an opinion, but として identifies the capacity in which someone acts; にとって identifies whose situation an evaluation concerns.

Think of it this wayAsk “As what?” or “In what role / for what use?” Make sure the preceding noun names that role rather than the actor or topic.

How to attach it

Role noun + として + predicate
代表として参加する／通訳として働く. The final predicate carries tense and politeness; として links the role.
Object + を + role + として使う
カップを花びんとして使う. The cup is the object used; vase is the function it serves.
Passive use: として使われる
この部屋は会議室として使われている. The room is being used; meeting room is the role. A passive ending does not automatically call for によって.
Before a noun: としての + noun
教師としての責任／代表としての意見. の connects the role to the following noun.
としては / としても
代表としては反対です can frame the response in that role. Context matters: this is different from the conditional ～たとしても, “even assuming that…”.
Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　＋として

Formation: noun + として.

Book explanation

「～という、、、で…」

Identify a role, status, capacity, or use: as a representative, as a student, or as a vase.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the relationship.

Book example

①リーさんはとしてに。

①Mr. Lee came to Japan as a government-sponsored international student.

Clue The role in which Lee comes is government-sponsored international student.

How to read this example

として does not mean that the student caused someone else to come.

Book example

②はこののとして「を」にする。

② Mr. Oyama will participate in the ``Conference to Think about Peace'' as a representative of this school.

Clue Oyama participates in the capacity of the school’s representative.

How to read this example

The conference is the event entered; 代表 is the role.

Book example

③わたしはコーヒーカップをとしています。

③I use a coffee cup as a vase.

Clue The object remains a coffee cup, but it serves the function of a vase.

How to read this example

を marks the object used; として marks its use.

Book example

④このはのとしてです。

④This region is famous for its tea production.

Clue The region is known as a tea-producing area.

How to read this example

産地 is the status for which it is famous.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

このはとしている。

This room is used as a meeting room.

Clue The room’s assigned use is a meeting room.

How to read this example

使われている is passive, but として answers “as what?”, not “by whom?”.

Added example

はとしてにした。

Mr. Tanaka took part in the meeting as an interpreter.

Clue Tanaka attends in the role of interpreter.

How to read this example

The sentence identifies the capacity of participation, not merely a topic discussed at the meeting.

Do not confuse it with…

Role ↔ agent
医者によって書かれた identifies the doctor who wrote something. 医者として書いた identifies the writer’s capacity. Read whether the noun answers “by whom?” or “as what?”.
Role ↔ perspective
家族にとって大切だ evaluates importance for the family. 家族の代表として話す means speak as the family’s representative.
Role ↔ topic
教師について話す means talk about teachers. 教師として話す means speak as a teacher. The same noun has different work in the sentence.
A limited claim · Group A
としてだけ can limit the role involved. The extra particle adds a restriction without changing the core “as” relationship.

JLPT approach

  1. Roles include objects’ uses. Vase, meeting room, and medicine are functions, not people performing an action.
  2. Do not let a passive distract you. 会議室として使われる states what the room is used as; 専門家によって作られる names who creates something.
  3. Distinguish capacity from value. “As a representative” points to として; “important for the representative” points to にとって.

Use clues to reason from context; a single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

このノートを復習用として使っています。

このノートをふくしゅうようとしてつかっています。

I use this notebook for review.

復習用 identifies the notebook’s assigned use.

One person, three relationships

について · about whom?
The person is the topic. Complaining about your parents does not tell us whom you speak to.
に対して · directed toward whom?
The person is the target. Complaining to your parents identifies whom you address; the complaint can concern something else.
にとって · for whom?
The person’s situation frames an evaluation. A child’s growth is a joy for the parents.

JLPT habit: Do not choose from the noun alone. Follow it to the predicate: what is discussed, who is addressed, or for whom something matters?

Book explanation

～について：～はにのある（・・・など）のを。

について identifies the subject matter of thought or communication: what someone thinks, writes, speaks, or asks about.

Book example

　についてを。（のはにがあること）

Example: Complain about one’s parents. The complaint concerns the parents; it may be addressed to someone else.

Clue The parents are the subject matter of the complaint.

How to read this example

The person hearing it may be someone else; について alone does not identify that listener.

Book explanation

～：～は（する・など）や（だ・きびしいなど）がを。

に対して identifies the target toward which an action or attitude is directed.

Book example

　を。（をは）

Example: Complain to one’s parents. The parents are the people being addressed.

Clue The complaint is addressed to the parents.

How to read this example

The original parenthetical identifies whom the speaker complains to, not necessarily what the complaint is about.

Book explanation

～にとって：～はやをするを。

にとって identifies whose situation or perspective matters to an evaluation.

Book example

　にとってのはのだ。（だとは）

Example: For parents, seeing their child grow is the greatest joy. The joy is evaluated from the parents’ position.

Clue The joy is evaluated from the parents’ position.

How to read this example

にとって identifies those for whom the child’s growth is especially meaningful.
