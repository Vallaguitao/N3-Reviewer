Book grammar · natural conclusion

～わけだ・～というわけだ

Connect the facts: “That means…” or “Now I see why.”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

わけだ presents something as an understandable consequence of the facts. You may calculate a result, explain how a decision followed from the circumstances, or recognize why an observed situation makes sense.

というわけだ packages a whole conclusion: “So, what that means is…” It is especially useful for a summary or restatement. It is not simply the dictionary meaning “reason.”

Think of it this wayFacts → a conclusion that follows. New information → “That explains it.” Look for the relationship, not just a word such as だから or それで.

How to build the patterns

Verb / い-adjective: plain form
着くわけだ・遅れたわけだ・寒かったわけだ. Keep tense and negatives inside the clause: 来ないわけだ means “that explains why someone is not coming,” with no obligation implied.
な-adjective: な before わけ
上手なわけだ. Formal である also works. Do not use direct 上手だわけだ.
Noun: の / な / である
休みのわけだ・休みなわけだ・休みであるわけだ. The book lists these noun connections; の is a useful basic form to learn. Natural choice also depends on the noun and context.
With という: preserve the statement
着くというわけだ・上手だというわけだ. For nouns and な-adjectives, the book allows だ before という to be included or omitted: ４連休というわけだ.
Polite and explanatory endings
わけです / わけですね / わけなんですね. ね invites agreement or checks understanding; なんですね adds an explanatory tone. These endings do not change the core logical connection.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

（ナ だ－な／－である・ だ－の／－な／－である）＋わけだ
（ナ（だ）・（だ））＋というわけだ

Before わけだ, use plain verbs and い-adjectives; な-adjectives take な or である. The book lists の / な / である for nouns. Before というわけだ, use a plain clause; noun and な-adjective だ may be included or omitted.

Book explanation

「からと、～というになる。（①②③）」「からと～というができる。（④⑤）」

Examples 1–3 present a conclusion that follows from the circumstances. Examples 4–5 show that new information makes an existing fact understandable.

A. Draw or explain a conclusion

Book example

①ここからまで１か。じゃ、からば９にはわけだ。

① An hour and a half from here to Tokyo Station? Then, if I leave now, that means I will arrive by nine.

Clue The travel time supports a calculation.

How to read this example

じゃ introduces the conclusion; ９時には means by nine here. A future result can use わけだ when presented as following from the stated facts.

Book example

②ほかにいるがなかったから、そのにことにしたわけです。

② There were no other days when we were free, so that is how we came to decide to meet that day.

Clue No other date was available.

How to read this example

ことにした reports the decision; わけです explains how the circumstances led to it. Compare decision-making in Lesson 11.

Book example

③はが、はだ。つまり、４というわけだ。

③ There are no classes on Friday, and Monday is a public holiday. In other words, that makes a four-day break.

Clue Count Friday through Monday.

How to read this example

Friday off plus the weekend plus Monday’s holiday gives four consecutive days. つまり and というわけだ summarize this particular arrangement, not every Friday and Monday.

B. Understand why an existing fact makes sense

Book example

④にがんですね。それで、きのうのあんなにわけですね。

④ Oh, it snowed during the night? That explains why it was so cold last night.

Clue New information explains the cold night.

How to read this example

The speaker already experienced the cold; learning about the snow makes it understandable. それで marks that realization.

Book example

⑤のはですか。それで、もがだというわけなんですね。

⑤ Her father is a painter? Ah, that explains why she is good at drawing too.

Clue The speaker connects two pieces of information.

How to read this example

上手だ remains a complete predicate before という. The father’s occupation is treated as an explanation for the daughter’s skill; the grammar does not prove that connection.

Expanded explanation

Use this when the earlier facts make the conclusion natural. It can also show “now I understand why” after receiving an explanation.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

したのか。になるわけだ。

You practiced three hours every day. No wonder you improved.

Clue The amount of practice explains the improvement.

How to read this example

上手になる describes becoming skillful; わけだ expresses “no wonder.” Do not mistake plain なる for a command or a promise.

Added example

はだから、はというわけです。

Today is a holiday, so naturally the bank is closed.

Clue The holiday explains the bank closure.

How to read this example

休みというわけです summarizes a noun predicate with だ omitted before という, as allowed in the book’s formation.

Added example

がいたんですか。それでわけですね。

The train had stopped? So that explains why you were late.

Clue The train disruption explains the lateness.

How to read this example

遅れた stays past before わけですね; ね checks the newly understood explanation.

Added example

にいるから、でになるわけだ。

There are ten in each bag, so three bags make thirty.

Clue Ten per bag multiplied by three gives thirty.

How to read this example

This is the calculation use of わけだ. The point is the result that follows from the quantities.

Do not confuse it with…

わけだ vs はずだ
わけだ: “That follows / that explains it.” はずだ: “I confidently expect it to be so.” Both can concern past, present, or future. Compare the one-point lesson; tense alone will not decide.
わけだ vs から / ので
から and ので connect a reason to another clause. わけだ presents the resulting understanding. They can appear together: 原因だから、結果になるわけだ.
わけだ vs negative わけ patterns
来ないわけだ = that explains the non-arrival. 来ないわけにはいかない = cannot avoid coming. The words after わけ change the judgment.
A conclusion can be mistaken
The speaker treats the connection as making sense. This does not make every inference objectively proven, especially when explaining someone’s abilities or motives.

JLPT approach

  1. Identify the facts and the conclusion separately. In a calculation, work out the result before considering the ending.
  2. For “now I understand,” find what was observed and what new information explains it.
  3. Check attachment after choosing the meaning: 上手なわけだ but 上手だというわけだ.
  4. Do not eliminate わけだ just because the result is in the future. The station example draws a conclusion from a travel-time calculation.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

それで、そういうわけだったんですね。

それで、そういうわけだったんですね。

Oh, so that explains it.

Use this after learning the background to an event. It acknowledges the explanation rather than demanding a reason.

Book grammar · restrained action

～わけにはいかない

“I cannot bring myself to / cannot responsibly do that.”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Vるわけにはいかない rules an action out because of the circumstances, responsibility, or personal reservations. Doing it would go against a promise, another person’s trust, or what the speaker considers acceptable.

The focus is the reason for holding back, rather than a lack of skill or physical ability. It often concerns the speaker’s own situation; “usually first person” is a usage tendency, not a ban on every other subject.

Think of it this wayName the action before わけ. Then ask: “What makes doing this unacceptable in this situation?”

How to build the patterns

Dictionary form + わけにはいかない
行く → 行くわけにはいかない; 食べる → 食べるわけにはいかない; する → するわけにはいかない; 来る → 来るわけにはいかない. The blocked action stays in dictionary form.
Polite form changes the final ending
飲むわけにはいきません. Keep 飲む before わけ; do not change it to 飲みます. Past: 飲むわけにはいかなかった = I could not allow myself to drink then.
Causative actions also fit
待たせるわけにはいかない = cannot keep someone waiting. 待たせる is already the dictionary form of “make / let someone wait.”
Do not insert potential automatically
読めない describes inability to read. 読むわけにはいかない describes a reason not to read, such as respecting someone’s diary. The exercise tests this distinction.
Watch the negative before わけ
行くわけにはいかない = cannot go. 行かないわけにはいかない = cannot skip going → must go. The second pattern has its own topic.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　 ＋わけにはいかない

Verb dictionary form + わけにはいかない: circumstances make doing that action unacceptable.

Book explanation

「があるので、～することはできない。」などがでできないのではなくて、～したいが、に・があるなどのがあってできないというで。はふつう。

This expresses a reason for holding back, such as social expectations or personal reservations, rather than inability. The speaker may want to act but feels unable to do so acceptably. The subject is usually the speaker.

Book example

①がをほしいといる。のをわけにはいかない。

① My best friend is asking me to lend them money. I feel I cannot turn down my best friend’s request.

Clue Friendship makes refusal feel unacceptable.

How to read this example

The blocked action is 断る, “refuse,” so the speaker feels bound to help. てほしい earlier reports the friend’s request; it is a separate construction.

Book example

②はでんです。おをわけにはいきません。

② I came by car today, so I cannot have any alcohol.

Clue Driving gives the speaker a reason to refrain.

How to read this example

飲む is the blocked action, and いきません makes the ending polite. The sentence is not saying the speaker lacks the physical ability to drink.

Book example

③かぜをひいてしまったが、ながあるから、をわけにはいかない。

③ I have caught a cold, but there is an important meeting, so I feel I cannot miss work.

Clue The meeting creates a felt responsibility.

How to read this example

The speaker sees missing work as unacceptable despite being ill. This explains the speaker’s judgment in the example.

Expanded explanation

The action is physically possible, but responsibility, relationships, safety, or social expectations make it unacceptable.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はだから、わけにはいかない。

I have an exam tomorrow, so I cannot allow myself to go out tonight.

Clue Tomorrow’s exam is the reason to hold back.

How to read this example

遊ぶわけにはいかない rejects going out tonight; it does not mean the speaker must go out.

Added example

をわけにはいきません。

We cannot keep the customer waiting.

Clue The action is making someone else wait.

How to read this example

待たせる is causative and remains in dictionary form before わけ. The service situation motivates the restraint.

Added example

なので、なくすわけにはいかない。

It is a borrowed book, so I must not lose it.

Clue Borrowing the book creates responsibility for it.

How to read this example

なくす is the action to avoid. The expression can reject an unacceptable outcome even when no one wants that outcome.

Added example

した、でやめるわけにはいかない。

Since I promised, I cannot quit halfway.

Clue A promise makes quitting unacceptable.

How to read this example

約束した以上 means “now that I have promised”; the obligation is the reason the speaker cannot quit halfway.

Do not confuse it with…

vs Vられない / potential negative
字が見えなくて読めない: cannot read because the writing is not visible. 友だちの日記だから読むわけにはいかない: cannot read it because of privacy.
vs わけがない
読むわけにはいかない: I cannot appropriately read it. 読めるわけがない: there is no way I can read it. Lesson 8 explains strong rejection of a possibility.
vs てはいけない / べきではない
てはいけない states a prohibition; べきではない gives a judgment about what one should not do. わけにはいかない highlights the circumstances that prevent this action. Compare Lesson 10’s advice and judgments.
vs ないわけにはいかない
断るわけにはいかない = cannot refuse. 引き受けないわけにはいかない = cannot avoid accepting. Different verbs can describe the same practical obligation; inspect the actual action.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the reason clause: a promise, responsibility, or relationship supports a restraint reading.
  2. Identify the blocked action. A negative English translation does not mean the Japanese verb before わけ must be negative.
  3. Eliminate ability-based distractors only after checking why the action is impossible in this sentence.
  4. Read the entire ending. がない, ではない, and にはいかない are different constructions.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

今、帰るわけにはいきません。

いま、かえるわけにはいきません。

I cannot leave right now, given the circumstances.

A contextual refusal. Giving a short reason explains why leaving is not an acceptable option for the speaker.

Book grammar · unavoidable action

～ないわけにはいかない

“I cannot leave this undone” → “I have to do it.”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Vないわけにはいかない says that NOT doing an action is unacceptable, so the speaker feels compelled to do it. A relationship, personal duty, or practical circumstances leave no acceptable option to skip it.

Reluctance or inconvenience is common, but the key is the pressure of the situation. The textbook contrasts this with merely stating a rule. The expression emphasizes the speaker’s felt obligation.

Think of it this wayKeep the two layers separate: 行かない = not go; わけにはいかない = cannot accept that option. Together: “I cannot NOT go.”

How to build the patterns

Godan: a-row + ない
買う → 買わない → 買わないわけにはいかない. 帰る → 帰らない; 話す → 話さない. Verbs ending in う use わない.
Ichidan: remove る + ない
食べる → 食べないわけにはいかない. 着る（きる）→ 着ない. Do not use 着らない for “not wear.”
Irregular verbs
する → しないわけにはいかない; 来る → 来ない（こない）わけにはいかない. The ない belongs to the verb’s negative form.
Polite and past endings
返さないわけにはいきません = I have to return it. 返さないわけにはいかなかった = I had to return it then. Keep the inner negative in both.
Two negatives, different jobs
The first ない makes the action negative. The final いかない rejects that option. This is obligation, not a claim that the action is physically impossible.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　ない ＋わけにはいかない

Verb ない-form + わけにはいかない: not doing the action is not an acceptable option, so one has to do it.

Book explanation

「があるので、～しなければならない。」などでいて～しなければならないのではなくて、あまり～したくないが、・があってそうするがあるというで。はふつう。

This expresses necessity arising from social expectations or a personal sense of duty, often despite reluctance. The focus is felt obligation in the circumstances, rather than simply citing a rule. The subject is usually the speaker.

Book example

①このではがないとやはり。わけにはいかない。

① It really would be difficult to manage in this town without a bicycle. I have no choice but to buy one.

Clue Managing without a bicycle is presented as impractical.

How to read this example

買う becomes 買わない, and rejecting that option produces “must buy.” This is situational necessity, not an uncontrollable urge to shop.

Book example

②けど、もわけにはいきませんよね。

② It is really hot, but we cannot go without wearing anything, can we?

Clue The negative clause means wearing nothing.

How to read this example

何も + 着ない forms that idea; わけにはいきません rejects it. The outcome is that some clothing is necessary despite the heat.

Book example

③このCD、ずっといたいけど、のだからわけにはいかない。

③ I would like to keep this CD forever, but it belongs to the library, so I have to return it.

Clue The CD belongs to the library.

How to read this example

図書館の uses の to stand for the library’s item. 返さない is “not return”; the outer ending rejects keeping it unreturned.

Book cross-references
Book cross-references

～わけがない　→１８－①
～わけではない・～というわけではない　→１８－③

Review Lesson 8: わけがない rejects a possibility strongly; わけではない / というわけではない denies a conclusion or qualifies a claim.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

されたので、わけにはいかない。

I was invited, so I cannot avoid going.

Clue The invitation creates a felt obligation.

How to read this example

行かない is the option the speaker cannot accept. The sentence communicates this speaker’s sense of duty, not a universal rule about invitations.

Added example

のはだから、わけにはいかない。

I made the mistake, so I have to apologize.

Clue Taking responsibility for the mistake requires an apology.

How to read this example

謝る becomes 謝らない; the ending rejects leaving the apology undone.

Added example

みんながいるから、もわけにはいかない。

Everyone is helping, so I have to work too.

Clue Everyone else’s effort creates social pressure.

How to read this example

働かない means not working. 私も shows that the speaker feels obliged to contribute too.

Added example

がなので、レポートをわけにはいかない。

The deadline is today, so I have no choice but to submit the report.

Clue Today’s deadline makes leaving the report unsubmitted unacceptable.

How to read this example

出す becomes 出さない. The ending highlights the speaker’s practical necessity to submit it.

Do not confuse it with…

vs なければならない / ないといけない
Both can translate as “have to.” These broadly state necessity or a requirement; ないわけにはいかない emphasizes why the speaker cannot accept leaving the action undone.
vs ないわけではない
行かないわけにはいかない = I must go. 行かないわけではない = it is not that I will not go (a qualified denial). Lesson 8: わけではない.
vs ないわけがない
来ないわけがない = there is no way someone will fail to come → strong expectation of arrival. 来ないわけにはいかない = someone feels obliged to come. Belief and obligation are different.
vs ずにはいられない
笑わずにはいられない = cannot help laughing: an impulse or response is hard to suppress. Here, 買わないわけにはいかない means circumstances require buying, rather than an irresistible shopping urge.

JLPT approach

  1. Locate ない before わけ; then read the ending after わけ. Do not just count how many negatives appear.
  2. Restate the result positively: 買わないわけにはいかない → must buy. Check whether that action fits the reason.
  3. Watch fragments in blanks: 話さ + ないわけにはいかない creates 話さない; し + ない creates しない.
  4. Separate “must do it” from “surely will do it.” The former concerns obligation; the latter is an expectation.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

お世話になったので、お礼を言わないわけにはいきません。

おせわになったので、おれいをいわないわけにはいきません。

They helped me, so I really must thank them.

The speaker feels a personal obligation to acknowledge the help. 言う becomes 言わない, not 言あない.

Book one-point lesson · page 77

「～はずだ」と「～わけだ」

6 questions · はず or わけ? Identify whether the speaker is expressing an expectation or explaining something that now makes sense. Read the follow-up sentence, not just the tense.

Try the questions before opening the comparison below. Two optional hints are available for each item; submit all six for detailed reasoning.

◎（　）の中に「はず」か「わけ」を書きなさい。

1　実験のやり方を変えてみたんです。今度はきっと成功する（　）です。期待していてください。

2　実験のやり方を変えてみたんです。だから、こんなにいい結果が出た（　）です。

3　A「はさみはどこ？」
B「いつもの引き出しの中にある（　）だよ。よく探してみて。」

4　彼は「必ず行くよ。」と言っていたのだから、来る（　）ですよ。もう少し待ちましょう。

5　山川さんもこの大会の準備係ですか。それで、こんなに早く来た（　）ですね。

6　A「今日、川口さんも会に出席するでしょうか。」
B「え？　川口さんは今、日本にはいない（　）ですよ。おとといアメリカに行ったんです。」

Expectation or understood conclusion?

はずだ · “It should be so”
A confident expectation based on information such as a promise, a usual location, or someone’s travel plans. The speaker need not have checked that the expected state is true.
わけだ · “That follows / that explains it”
The speaker recognizes a consequence, works out a result, or accepts an explanation for a known fact. New information makes the situation understandable.

Read the next line: “Look for it” or “let’s wait” supports an unconfirmed expectation. “That explains why the result was so good” explains an observed result.

Avoid the tense trap: はず can describe the present or past, and わけ can describe a future consequence. Both may sound reasonable in some contexts; choose the stance supported by the full passage.

Keep confidence separate from obligation: 来るはずだ = I expect someone to come; 来ないわけにはいかない = someone feels compelled to come. English “should” can hide this difference.

Review はずがない / わけがない in Lesson 8 for strong rejection of a possibility.

Book comparison: printed page 77. The following treatment examples illustrate the speaker’s expectation or understanding.

Book explanation · ～はずだ

のなを。のある。

はずだ expresses the speaker’s confident expectation or inference, based on what they know.

Book example

・いい薬を使ったから、きっとすぐ治るはずですよ。

We have used a good medicine, so you should recover soon.

Clue The speaker predicts recovery from the treatment information.

How to read this example

治るはず expresses an expected result, not a confirmation that recovery has happened. きっと reinforces the confidence.

Book example

・この商品は人気があるから、十日ぐらいで売り切れるはずだ。

This product is popular, so it should sell out in about ten days.

Clue Popularity is the basis for a sales forecast.

How to read this example

売り切れるはずだ predicts selling out in about ten days. That result is still an expectation.

Book explanation · ～わけだ

ではなくて、であるとしたことを。

わけだ presents something as making sense or following naturally from the circumstances: “that means…” or “that explains it.”

Book example

・なるほど、この薬を使えば早く治るわけですね。じゃあ使ってみましょう。

I see—so using this medicine means a quicker recovery. Then let’s try it.

Clue なるほど signals newly understood information.

How to read this example

The speaker accepts that using this medicine would explain a quick recovery and then proposes trying it. This example shows why “future always means はず” is unreliable.

Book example

・この薬は１日１袋飲むのですから、十日間で10袋になるわけですね。

Since this medicine is taken at one packet a day, that means ten packets over ten days.

Clue One packet a day for ten days gives ten packets.

How to read this example

The speaker is confirming the calculation with わけですね. The sentence demonstrates the grammar’s arithmetic-conclusion use.
