A four-step approach to mixed questions

  1. Read the situation. Who acts? Is the speaker explaining, deciding, comparing, warning, or reporting something?
  2. Predict the relationship. Look for a target, purpose, result, time, degree, or intention. Translate the message before choosing a familiar-looking ending.
  3. Build the full phrase. Check the verb form and the words on both sides of the blank. Keep any printed ない, です, んです, noun, or particle in your answer.
  4. Test the alternatives. A choice may be valid Japanese elsewhere but change the intended meaning here. Explain why your answer fits both the form and the context.

JLPT practice tip: a clue narrows your options; it does not replace reading the whole sentence. After reviewing a mistake, name the distinction you missed before retrying.

Review the distinctions behind your mistakes

Use this guide before practice if you need a refresher, or afterward to identify what to study next. The question feedback explains which reading applies in each specific context.

Open the grammar comparison guide
Limited contents, added information, or recent completion?
Nばかり emphasizes contents dominated by one kind and can have an affirmative ending. しか requires negation in the “only” use. Vるばかりでなく…も adds another action. Vたばかり is recent completion; Vてばかり is repeated behavior. The word before ばかり changes the meaning. Review Group F.
Imminent event, emphatic denial, or an eager wish?
Recognize meaningful pairs: 今にも + Vます stem + そうだ for imminent appearance; 決して…ない for emphatic negation; ぜひ…たい for an eager wish. どうか often makes an earnest appeal, while おそらく expresses probability. The ending and the speaker’s intention must both fit. Review Group I.
Past outcome, discovery, or a hypothetical condition?
Vていたら…た can narrate what happened after a continuing activity. Vると…ていた can introduce a state discovered afterward. These patterns are not restricted to unreal “if” situations. For a deliberate next action, Vてから directly expresses sequence. Review Group H: results and discoveries and sequence.
Completion, excess, or an action just finished?
Use the ます stem: 使いきる means use it all; 使いすぎる means use too much. Potential 書ききれる → 書ききれない means cannot finish the whole writing. Vたところ locates an event immediately after completion; it does not describe how much of an object was consumed. Review Group J: action stages and Lesson 1: timing.
Personal choice, established rule, or changed state?
ことにする presents a decision; ことになっている describes an established arrangement or rule. ようにしている describes a maintained effort. For adjectives, い → く + なる describes change, while く + する means make something that way. A past resulting state can use なっていた. Review Group G.
Two real qualities or a comparison?
Aも…ばBも… can list two existing qualities: the ば is additive here. With いい, use よければ. Nらしい + noun expresses a characteristic or fitting quality; Nのような / Nみたいな + noun compares features. “Apparently” らしい is a separate context-dependent use. Review Group H: both…and and Group J: qualities.

Make the next attempt more useful
  1. Classify the mistake: meaning, attachment, actor, time, or speaker intention.
  2. Explain the correct answer in one sentence, then say what the nearest distractor would mean.
  3. Open the linked lesson, study the relevant distinction, and return for a fresh attempt with hints closed.
