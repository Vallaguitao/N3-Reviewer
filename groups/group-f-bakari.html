Book grammar · limitation

～ばかり…

“Nothing but…” / “All I do or can do is…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

ばかり narrows the focus to one kind of thing or one activity. The speaker sees little variety or no other meaningful action: always the same food, only the same conversation partners, or simply watching without helping.

It often conveys criticism, regret, or frustration, but that tone comes from the context. 祈るばかりです means “all I can do is pray” and need not criticize anyone. “Nothing but” can express the speaker’s impression rather than a precise claim that there are zero exceptions.

Think of it this wayIdentify what comes immediately before ばかり. Is the focus a thing, a person plus particle, or an entire action?

How to build the patterns

Noun + ばかり
カップラーメンばかり食べる = eat nothing but cup noodles. The focus is the food. In this example, ばかり takes the position where a simple object marker might otherwise appear.
Noun + particle + ばかり
同じ国の人とばかり話す = talk only with people from the same country. と keeps its “with” role; ばかり limits the conversation partners. Do not remove a particle needed to show the relationship.
Dictionary verb + ばかり
文句を言うばかり = do nothing but complain; 祈るばかり = all one can do is pray. Interpret the activity in context; this form is not always a future action.
Vている + ばかり
泣いているばかり = just be crying, with nothing else happening. The whole ongoing activity is the focus.
Connection to another clause
ばかりで connects the limited activity to another fact: 文句を言うばかりで、手伝わない. Adding なく reverses the limitation: ばかりでなく means “not only.”

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

（＋）・　／ている ＋ばかり

Noun (with a particle where needed), verb dictionary form, or Vている + ばかり. The phrase before ばかり is the focus of the limitation.

Book explanation

「いつも～だけで、ほかのもの・ことはない。」よくないといることをが。

Only this kind of thing or activity, with little or nothing else. Often used when the speaker disapproves of the lack of variety or other action.

Book example

①はあきずにカップラーメンばかりいる。

① My younger brother eats nothing but cup noodles every day, without getting tired of them.

Clue The food is the focus.

How to read this example

カップラーメンばかり describes a diet dominated by cup noodles; 毎日 and あきずに reinforce the repeated pattern.

Book example

②ではのとばかり、いろいろなのとしたほうがいい。

② In the dormitory, you should talk with people from various countries instead of only with people from your own country.

Clue と marks the conversation partners.

How to read this example

The advice is to widen the circle of people, not to stop talking to compatriots altogether. 話さないで leads into the suggested alternative.

Book example

③ありがとうございます。いつもいただくばかりで、もできなくてすみません。

③ Thank you. I am sorry that I am always on the receiving end and have not been able to give anything in return.

Clue いただく means humbly receiving here.

How to read this example

The speaker apologizes for always receiving without reciprocating. It does not mean “I am always giving you things.”

Book example

④はただいるばかりで、があったのかわからなかった。

④ The child just kept crying, so I could not tell what had happened.

Clue Crying is the only observable response.

How to read this example

泣いているばかり explains why the observer could not understand what had happened; the sentence does not say the child lacked that knowledge.

Book example

⑤こののはどうしているのでしょう。のをばかりです。

⑤ I wonder how the girl in this photo is doing now. All I can do is pray for her happiness.

Clue Praying is presented as all the speaker can do.

How to read this example

This is a sincere limited-action reading, not criticism of praying too much.

Expanded explanation

The form narrows attention to one repeated item or action. Context often gives it a critical tone, though an action such as 祈るばかり can simply mean “all one can do is pray.”

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

のはばかりいる。

On my days off, I watch nothing but videos.

Clue Videos are what the speaker mostly watches.

How to read this example

Noun + ばかり focuses on the content, while 見ている describes the activity.

Added example

をばかりで、も。

He only complains and does not help at all.

Clue The other clause reveals what is missing.

How to read this example

Complaining occurs, but helping does not. 言うばかりで links that limitation to 何も手伝わない.

Added example

ばかりほうがいい。

You should not eat only sweets.

Clue The advice concerns eating only sweets.

How to read this example

甘い物 is the restricted food category. The sentence encourages variety rather than banning every sweet.

Added example

あとはをばかりです。

All we can do now is wait for the result.

Clue あとは identifies the remaining step.

How to read this example

This overlaps with the readiness use: once the earlier work is done, waiting for the result is all that remains.

Do not confuse it with…

vs だけ
Both can mean “only.” だけ often states a simple restriction; ばかり often highlights concentration or repetition, sometimes with dissatisfaction. Do not assume every ばかり sentence is negative.
Object focus vs action focus
動画ばかり見ている focuses on videos as the main thing watched. 動画を見てばかりいる focuses on spending one’s time watching them. The meanings can overlap.
Limitation vs readiness
祈るばかり = all I can do is pray. 準備が終わって、出発するばかり = ready, with only departure left. Preparation context gives the second meaning.
Other ばかり meanings
A quantity such as 三日ばかり can mean “about three days.” Do not apply the “nothing but” reading automatically when a number and counter come before it.

JLPT approach

  1. Locate the scope: the word or phrase being limited.
  2. Read the other clause: 手伝わない or お返しができない can show what is missing.
  3. Distinguish “only A” from “not only A”: check for でなく after ばかり.
  4. Use the reason and tone to separate criticism, apology, and “all I can do” acceptance.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

お礼を言うばかりです。

おれいをいうばかりです。

All I can do is thank you.

The limitation can convey gratitude or humility. It does not automatically mean repeated criticism.

Book grammar · repeated behavior

～てばかりいる

“Keep doing it” / “Do little else but…”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Vてばかりいる describes doing an activity repeatedly or spending one’s time on it while neglecting other things. It commonly expresses the speaker’s dissatisfaction: constantly checking a clock, playing instead of studying, or watching instead of helping.

The pattern describes a repeated or dominant activity, not a steadily changing degree. It can also describe unwelcome repeated events such as rain; it does not require deliberate behavior.

Think of it this wayRead てばかりいる as one pattern. The action comes in て-form; tense and negation usually appear on the final いる.

How to build the patterns

Vて + ばかりいる
見る → 見てばかりいる; 遊ぶ → 遊んでばかりいる; 悩む → 悩んでばかりいる; する → してばかりいる. The て-form includes voiced で where the verb requires it.
Polite / past
見てばかりいます = keep watching; 遊んでばかりいた = used to do nothing but play. Keep the first verb in て-form when the final いる changes.
A request to stop the exclusive habit
見てばかりいないで、手伝ってください = do not just watch; help. いないで negates the “doing nothing else” habit, not every possible act of watching.
Connect to another clause
話してばかりいて、聞かない = keep talking and do not listen. The final いる becomes いて to connect the clauses.
Compare the order
泣いているばかり and 泣いてばかりいる can both describe little besides crying. With printed ばかりいる after a blank, the expected missing piece is 泣いて, not 泣く or 泣いた.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

　て ＋ばかりいる

Verb て-form + ばかりいる. For example, 見る becomes 見てばかりいる, and 遊ぶ becomes 遊んでばかりいる.

Book explanation

「ほかのことはしないで、よく～する。」ので。

Do an activity repeatedly or spend one’s time on it while doing little else. Commonly said with criticism or dissatisfaction.

Book example

①はばかりいる。

① My grandfather has been getting angry a lot lately.

Clue The complaint concerns repeated anger.

How to read this example

怒る becomes 怒って before ばかりいる. 最近 sets the recent period in which the behavior is frequent.

Book example

②のころはばかりいた。しなかったことをはにいる。

② Around the age of twenty, I did nothing but have fun. Now I regret not studying.

Clue いた makes the repeated habit past.

How to read this example

遊んで remains in て-form. The regret about not studying explains the speaker’s critical view of that earlier period.

Book example

③ただばかりいないで、はくださいよ。

③Don't just watch, please help me a little.

Clue The speaker asks for help instead of passive watching.

How to read this example

見てばかりいないで means “do not just watch”; 少しは requests at least some contribution.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はゲームをしてばかりいる。

My brother does nothing but play games.

Clue The activity takes up too much of the brother’s time.

How to read this example

してばかりいる focuses on repeated gaming, rather than on a game that has just finished.

Added example

してばかりいないで、まずしよう。

Do not just worry; let us act first.

Clue The negative request targets unproductive repetition.

How to read this example

心配してばかりいないで urges moving beyond worry; 行動しよう supplies the proposed alternative.

Added example

、がばかりいる。

Recently it has been raining constantly.

Clue Rain is the unwelcome recurring event.

How to read this example

降ってばかりいる shows that this pattern can describe events rather than a person’s chosen habit.

Added example

はのをしてばかりいて、のを。

He keeps talking about himself and does not listen to others.

Clue いて connects the repeated habit to the neglected action.

How to read this example

He keeps talking about himself and fails to listen; the final いる becomes いて between clauses.

Do not confuse it with…

vs Vたばかりだ
見てばかりいる = keep looking; 見たばかりだ = have just looked. One describes repetition, the other a recent completed action.
vs change Vるばかりだ
怒ってばかりいる = keep getting angry. 悪くなるばかりだ = keep getting worse. Continuing change emphasizes a direction or degree, not merely recurring behavior.
vs an ordinary ongoing action
見ている can simply mean “is watching.” 見てばかりいる adds the impression that this occupies the person too much or excludes other activities.
Advice following the complaint
行動してみたらどうですか suggests trying action. The complaint identifies the habit; the second clause proposes an alternative. Compare Lesson 10’s suggestions.

JLPT approach

  1. Inspect the printed ending first. ばかりいる calls for て-form.
  2. Read ばかりいた as the past of a repeated habit, not as “just did.”
  3. After ばかりいないで, find the alternative action being requested.
  4. Repeated events need not be intentional: repeated rain can be described this way.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

考えてばかりいないで、やってみよう。

かんがえてばかりいないで、やってみよう。

Let’s stop just thinking about it and give it a try.

This urges action instead of spending all one’s time thinking. It is not a general instruction never to think.

Book grammar · addition

～ばかりでなく…

“Not only A, but also B.”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

AばかりでなくB adds another fact rather than restricting the sentence to A. A is true, and B is true as well. The second part often contains も, but the addition can be clear from the whole sentence without it.

Both positive and negative facts can be added. The form itself does not express disapproval. First identify the two things being added, then check how A attaches to ばかりでなく.

Think of it this wayRemove “not only / but also” mentally: you should be left with two compatible facts, A and B.

How to build the patterns

Noun (+ particle) + ばかりでなく
英語ばかりでなく、中国語も = not only English but Chinese too. As a noun predicate, the formal form である can also appear: 学生であるばかりでなく… .
Verb plain form + ばかりでなく
案内してくれたばかりでなく = not only showed me around. Keep past or negative meaning when it belongs to the first fact.
い-adjective: keep the plain form
面白いばかりでなく / 安いばかりでなく. Do not add だ after the い-adjective.
な-adjective: な / である
便利なばかりでなく / 便利であるばかりでなく. Both alternatives in the exercise are valid. 便利に is an adverbial form and does not complete this attachment.
でなく vs でない
ばかりでなく connects to the added fact. ばかりではない can end a statement meaning “it is not only…”; the placement in the sentence matters.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation

（＋）・（ナ だ－な／－である・ だ－である）＋ばかりでなく

Noun (with a particle where needed) or a plain clause + ばかりでなく. Present な-adjectives take な or である; noun predicates can use である.

Book explanation

「～だけでなくて、そのほかにも～。」

Not only A, but B as well: the second part adds another fact.

Book example

①ばかりでなく、のがエネルギーにをいる。

① Not only Japanese people but people all over the world are interested in energy issues.

Clue The group of interested people expands.

How to read this example

世界中の人 adds people beyond Japanese people. The first group is included, not excluded.

Book example

②このは、ばかりでなく、さまざまなことが。

② This program is not only interesting; you can also learn all kinds of things from it.

Clue Interest and learning value are both asserted.

How to read this example

面白い attaches directly to ばかりでなく. The second benefit is clear even without an explicit も.

Book example

③はをしてくれたばかりでなく、こののもごちそうしてくれた。

③He not only showed me around the town, but also treated me to local cuisine.

Clue Two completed acts of kindness are added.

How to read this example

案内してくれた stays past before ばかりでなく; も marks the extra kindness of the meal.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はばかりでなく、も。

She speaks not only English but also Chinese.

Clue Chinese is added to English.

How to read this example

英語ばかり would restrict the languages; でなく opens the statement to another language.

Added example

このはばかりでなく、サービスもいい。

This store is not only inexpensive but also has good service.

Clue The store has two positive features.

How to read this example

安い needs no だ before ばかりでなく, and サービスも adds the second feature.

Added example

はをばかりでなく、もしてくれた。

Mr. Tanaka not only prepared the materials but also gave the presentation.

Clue The first action remains past in an addition pattern.

How to read this example

作ったばかりでなく does not mean “only just prepared” here; 発表も supplies the additional action.

Added example

はばかりでなく、にもいい。

Exercise is good not only for the body but also for the mind.

Clue も adds the mind as another beneficiary.

How to read this example

心にも keeps に for what exercise benefits, with も meaning “too.”

Do not confuse it with…

vs だけでなく
Both express “not only.” ばかりでなく often suits a more formal explanation. Learn the addition relationship rather than inventing a strict difference in factual meaning.
vs ばかりで
寝るばかりで、何もしない = only sleep, do nothing else. 寝るばかりでなく、運動もする = not only sleep, also exercise. The small なく changes the relationship.
vs Vたばかり
案内したばかり = have just guided someone. 案内したばかりでなく、食事も… = not only guided someone, but also a meal… . A た-form alone does not prove recent completion.
Addition vs contrast
“A, and B too” adds information. 反面 in Lesson 4 balances contrasting aspects. Decide whether the second fact adds to or contrasts with the first.

JLPT approach

  1. Read past the blank to find the second fact.
  2. Check も, but do not require it in every addition sentence.
  3. Match the first fact’s tense to context: a completed visit uses a past verb; a general instruction may use dictionary form.
  4. For な-adjectives, choose な or である in this present-tense attachment. A past form is possible in other contexts, but should not be inserted without a past-time meaning.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

勉強ばかりでなく、休むことも大切です。

べんきょうばかりでなく、やすむこともたいせつです。

Resting is important too, not just studying.

も highlights the added activity. The sentence does not reject studying; it expands what is important.

Shin Kanzen Master N3 Grammar, Group F, printed pages 80–81. English notes explain the source constructions and add related comparisons; the Japanese examples retain their wording and order.

Book grammar · change and readiness

～ばかりだ

Two uses: a change keeps going, or only one step remains.

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

A. Continuing change: Vるばかりだ presents a change as continuing in one direction: prices keep rising or a relationship gets worse and worse. The verb describes change, and the trend is often unwelcome.

B. Readiness: preparation is complete, and only the final action remains: “All that remains is to leave.” The remaining action has not yet been completed.

The Japanese shape is the same. The verb and surrounding facts decide the meaning, so each use has its own example heading below.

Think of it this wayChange verb + a developing trend → “keeps…”; completed preparation + あとは → “only … remains.”

How to build the patterns

A. Dictionary form of a change verb
増えるばかりだ / 悪くなるばかりだ / 忘れていくばかりだ. In the last example, 忘れていく as a whole ends in dictionary-form いく and describes a progressing change.
B. Dictionary form of the remaining action
出発するばかりだ / 食べるばかりだ / 送るばかりだ. Preparation is over; leaving, eating, or sending remains.
Polite and connected forms
出発するばかりです; 弱くなるばかりで心配です. The connected で allows another clause to follow; it does not itself mean addition.
Ready-state expressions
食べるばかりになっている = everything is now ready for eating. 送るばかりというとき = at the stage when only sending remained.
The verb form changes the time relationship
出発するばかり = only departure remains. 出発したばかり = have just departed. Check whether the action is pending or completed.

Book examples by use

Read the Japanese and translation, then use the clue to identify the form and its function.

A. A change continues in one direction

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation A

　 ＋ばかりだ

Formation: verb dictionary form + ばかりだ.

Book explanation A

「～というにがいく。」～はを（なる・など）。よくないのが。

A change continues in one direction. Use a verb of change, such as getting weaker or decreasing; the trend is often unwelcome.

Book example

A①けんかしてから、とはがなるばかりだ。

A① Ever since that argument, my relationship with her has only been getting worse.

Clue 悪くなる describes a worsening direction.

How to read this example

The argument is the starting point, and the relationship continues to deteriorate. 彼女 here means “her”; the Japanese does not specify that she is a girlfriend.

Book example

②はいつもいなければいくばかりだ。

② If you do not keep using a foreign language, you will only forget more and more of it.

Clue 忘れていく describes progressive loss over time.

How to read this example

The whole expression ends in dictionary-form いく before ばかりだ. This is a continuing trend, not a single recent instance of forgetting.

Book example

③、はがなるばかりでです。

③ Lately my grandmother has been getting weaker and weaker, which worries me.

Clue 弱くなる is a change of degree.

How to read this example

ばかりで connects the trend to the speaker’s worry. It does not mean the grandmother has only just become weak.

B. Preparation is complete; one action remains

Book wording and English gloss
Book formation B

　 ＋ばかりだ

Formation: verb dictionary form + ばかりだ.

Book explanation B

「が、はただ～するだけのだ。」

Preparation is complete, and only the final action remains to be done.

Book example

B①のはできました。もうするばかりです。

B① Everything is ready for the trip. All that remains is to leave.

Clue The preparations are already finished.

How to read this example

出発する names the one remaining action; it has not yet happened.

Book example

②のが、もうばかりになっている。

② The meal preparations are finished, and everything is ready for us to eat.

Clue The meal is ready for eating.

How to read this example

食べるばかりになっている describes the prepared state, not continuous overeating.

Book example

③パーティーのが、はにばかりというときになって、ミスが。

③ The party invitations were finished, and just when all that remained was to send them to the guests, a mistake was found.

Clue The mistake is discovered at the final preparation stage.

How to read this example

送るばかりというとき means the moment when only sending remained; it does not say the invitations had already been sent.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はばかりだ。

Prices just keep rising.

Clue 上がる describes continuing price increases.

How to read this example

With ばかりだ, the speaker presents the trend as going in that direction without relief.

Added example

のはばかりで、がだ。

The river water keeps decreasing, so rain is needed.

Clue 減る supplies the downward direction.

How to read this example

ばかりで connects the decrease to the need for rain. Repeated decreases form a trend, not recent completion.

Added example

は。あとはするばかりだ。

Everything is loaded; all that remains is to depart.

Clue Loading is complete but departure is pending.

How to read this example

積んだ establishes the finished preparation; あとは出発するばかり identifies the final step.

Added example

はして、するばかりになっている。

The presentation materials are complete and only need to be printed.

Clue The materials are complete and await printing.

How to read this example

印刷する stays in dictionary form because printing is still to be done.

Do not confuse it with…

vs Vてばかりいる
食べてばかりいる = keep eating, doing little else. 準備が終わって、食べるばかりだ = ready to eat. Similar English words can hide different situations.
vs “all I can do” limitation
ただ見守るばかりだ can mean all I can do is watch over the situation. It does not require completed preparation. Use the limitation reading when the point is having no other useful action.
vs Vるところだ
出発するところだ locates the action at the “about to leave” stage. 出発するばかりだ emphasizes that preparation is complete and departure is the only step left.
vs intention or attempt
出発しようとする describes trying or being about to act. Readiness ばかりだ specifically highlights the absence of other remaining preparation.

JLPT approach

  1. Classify the verb: does it describe change, or a final action?
  2. Find the setup. 準備ができた, 終わった, and あとは often establish readiness.
  3. For a trend, keep dictionary form even if the trend began in the past.
  4. Do not force every Vるばかり into these two uses; a sentence may simply mean “do nothing but” or “all one can do.”

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

準備はできました。あとは始めるばかりです。

じゅんびはできました。あとははじめるばかりです。

Everything is ready. All that remains is to begin.

This reports readiness. It does not say that the activity has just begun.

Book grammar · recent completion

～たばかりだ

“Have only just…” — recent from the speaker’s perspective.

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Vたばかりだ presents an action as recently completed. The speaker treats the time since it happened as short for the situation. That can mean a few minutes, last month, or the early period after joining a company.

“Recent” helps explain a current situation or an expectation: still being unfamiliar, or feeling surprised that something has already broken. The action is completed; ばかり does not mean it is still in progress.

Think of it this wayA completed event is still treated as new. Look for まだ, もう, のに, or ので and ask why the event’s recency matters.

How to build the patterns

Vた + ばかりだ
買う → 買ったばかりだ; 食べる → 食べたばかりだ; 来る → 来た（きた）ばかりだ; する → したばかりだ.
て-form and た-form share sound changes
聞いて → 聞いた; 読んで → 読んだ; 会って → 会った; 話して → 話した. Replace て / で with た / だ in the corresponding forms.
Describe a noun with の
買ったばかりのおもちゃ = a toy just bought. 習ったばかりの言葉 = a word just learned. Keep の before the noun.
Give a reason or contrast
結婚したばかりなので = because I have only just married; 食べたばかりなのに = even though I have only just eaten. The connection is ばかりなので / なのに.
Use で to continue
来たばかりで、まだ慣れていない = have only just arrived and am not used to things yet. This で connects the recent state to the following fact.

The action has only just happened from the speaker’s perspective. The amount of elapsed time depends on the situation; compare the one-point lesson.

Do not confuse it with…

vs Vたところだ
たところ places you at the immediate post-action stage. たばかり emphasizes recency as relevant to the situation. Both can describe something that literally just happened; context determines the preferred focus.
Subjective recency is not unlimited
先月結婚したばかり can be natural because adjusting to married life takes time. The elapsed time must still make sense as “recent” in that context.
Check what follows ばかり
買ったばかりの本 = a recently bought book. 買ったばかりでなく、読んだ = not only bought it, but read it. The addition ending changes the function.
vs Vるばかりだ
始まったばかり = has only just started. 始まるばかり = only starting remains, if readiness is established. The small tense change is central to the exercise.

JLPT approach

  1. Confirm that the action has already happened.
  2. Use the full time frame: １か月前 can fit たばかり, even though it does not fit an immediate “just this moment” report.
  3. Check the word after the blank: の + noun supports たばかりの in this recent-completion use.
  4. Treat 今 as a clue, not a universal ban on ばかり. For an immediate scene report, ところ is the textbook’s preferred answer.

Use clues to interpret the whole sentence. A single word is not a guaranteed answer shortcut.

Useful expression

入ったばかりなので、まだよくわかりません。

はいったばかりなので、まだよくわかりません。

I have only just joined, so I do not know much about it yet.

Recency explains unfamiliarity. The missing context supplies what you joined, such as a company or group.

Book one-point lesson · page 83

「～たばかり」と「～たところ」

7 questions · たばかり or たところ? Both can describe a completed action. Decide whether the sentence emphasizes recentness or the immediate scene, then inspect the particle after the blank.

Two optional hints are available for each question. Submit all seven for reasoning, including where the alternative could work with a different emphasis.

◎（　）の中に「ばかり」か「ところ」を書きなさい。

1　うちには生まれた（　）の子犬が３匹います。

2　会議が始まった（　）に高橋さんが入ってきた。

3　もしもし、今新幹線に乗った（　）です。そちらに８時に着くと思います。

4　このパソコンはまだ買った（　）なのに、調子が悪い。

5　彼は１か月前に日本に来た（　）だそうです。でも、日本語が上手ですね。

6　好きな曲を聞き終わった（　）で、ちょうど昼休みが終わった。

7　この４月に入社した（　）なので、まだ会社の人間関係がよくわからない。

Recent in context, or right at that stage?

たばかり · the event still feels new
先月結婚したばかり: last month is still recent when discussing adjustment to married life. Recency often explains まだ… or makes もう…なのに surprising. Use たばかりの to describe a noun: 生まれたばかりの子犬.
たところ · the immediate post-action scene
今、着いたところです: a report made just after arriving. たところに / へ / を can connect that scene to another event. In the exercise, たところで also marks the point at which another event occurs.

Use two checks: first the time and viewpoint, then the connection after the blank. A sentence about a month ago favors ばかり; a new event entering the just-completed scene favors ところ.

Do not make 今 an absolute rule: both 今着いたばかり and 今着いたところ can be natural. The textbook prefers ところ for an immediate phone update and ばかり when the event’s newness explains a current state or surprise.

Keep the scope of the particle rule clear: the book compares these recent-completion constructions. Its rule does not ban every appearance of ばかりに or ところの in Japanese; other grammatical constructions have different meanings.

Review ところ and scene particles in Lesson 1. In these questions, the Japanese connection can be a stronger clue than a shared English translation such as “just.”

Book explanation · ～たばかり

のだといるときに。にでなくても。に「の」をつけて、「～たばかりの」のでこともできるが、「に・へ・を」をつけてことはできない。

Use たばかり when an event still feels recent, even if some time has passed. In this recent-completion use, たばかりの can modify a noun. It does not take に / へ / を to mark the immediate scene in the way たところ does.

Book example

・でき上がったばかりのケーキをみんなで食べた。

We all ate the cake that had just been finished.

Clue の directly links the recent completion to ケーキ.

How to read this example

でき上がったばかりのケーキ means a cake that has just been finished, using the noun-modifying pattern taught here.

Book explanation · ～たところ

のだとときに。にでなければ。に「に・へ・を」をつけてこともできるが、「の」をつけて「～たところの」のでことはできない。

Use たところ to locate an event at the stage immediately after an action. に / へ / を can connect that scene to another event. For a recently completed action modifying a noun in this lesson, use たばかりの rather than たところの.

Book example

・もしもし、今、駅に着いたところです。

Hello, I have just arrived at the station.

Clue The call reports the immediate post-arrival stage.

How to read this example

今 and もしもし frame a current update. ところ emphasizes “I am at the stage just after arriving.”

Book example

・ケーキができ上がったところへ子どもたちが帰ってきた。

The children came home just as the cake was finished.

Clue へ marks the scene into which the children return.

How to read this example

The cake has just been finished when their arrival coincides with that moment. This is a scene connection, not a noun description.
