Book table · negation and completion

否定と完了を決める副詞

Check whether the sentence denies something or says it is already done

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Learn the adverb and the expression it supports as a pair. 全くわからない means “do not understand at all”; めったに行かない means “rarely go.” These are different kinds of negative meaning: degree, frequency, and strong refusal are not interchangeable.

すでに means “already.” It points to an event or state established by the time being discussed. The ending can show the completed event, the resulting state, or a preparation someone has made.

The book groups useful pairings for these meanings. It does not claim that each adverb has only one use: 全く can also mean “completely,” and そんなに can occur in an affirmative question. Interpret the sentence you are given.

Think of it this wayFirst look for negation or an already-established result. Then choose the meaning: not at all, not that much, hardly ever, never, or already.

Forms and matching expressions

全く／少しも + negative
全くわからない = do not understand at all; 少しも変わっていない = has not changed even a little. These emphasize the completeness of the denial.
そんなに／そう + negative
そんなに難しくない = not that difficult; そう心配する必要はない = no need to worry so much. They deny the degree suggested by the situation. そう here means “so / that much,” not hearsay そうだ.
たいして + negative
たいして重要ではない = not particularly important. This is a mild evaluation, often “not very…,” rather than a claim of absolute zero.
決して + negative
決して許さない = will never forgive; 決して言わないでください = please do not tell anyone under any circumstances. It adds strong resolve or prohibition.
めったに + negative
めったに食事しない = rarely eat out. This is frequency, so it allows occasional exceptions; it does not mean “never.”
Build the negative correctly
分かる → 分からない; 行く → 行かない; する → しない; 来る → 来ない（こない）. い-adjective: 面白い → 面白くない → 面白くなかった. な-adjective: 重要だ → 重要ではない → 重要ではなかった.
すでに + た／ている／てある
解決した = has already been resolved; 準備ができている = the preparations are already complete; 予約してある = a reservation has already been made and is in place. ている is not always “is doing.”
Negation can sit inside a longer ending
そんなに役に立たないだろう = probably will not be that useful. The negative ない still supplies the pairing even though the sentence continues with だろう.

Expanded explanation

Learn these pairings as meaningful chunks: 決して～ない for emphatic denial, めったに～ない for low frequency, and すでに～た／ている／てある for an already-established event or state. Check the meaning as well as the form; these adverbs are not interchangeable.

How to read this example

Do not confuse it with…

決して ↔ めったに
決して行かない is a strong “will never go.” めったに行かない means “hardly ever go.” Both use negation here, but one expresses resolve and the other low frequency.
全く／少しも ↔ たいして／そんなに
“Not at all” is stronger than “not particularly / not that much.” Do not treat every negative pairing as a total denial.
すでに ↔ もう／まだ
すでに is often a more formal “already”; もう is common in conversation and has additional uses. まだ～ていない means “have not yet….” A past reference point can take すでに～ていた: review already-existing discoveries in Group H.
A negative form ↔ a negative-sounding word
面白くなかった is the negative past of “interesting.” つまらなかった usually evaluates something as “boring.” In the book exercise, たいして面白くなかった gives “was not very interesting.” Read the conjugation, not just the English feeling. Review Lesson 8’s negation patterns.

JLPT approach

  1. Find the full ending, including ない inside ないでください or ないだろう. Do not stop at the final ください or だろう.
  2. If two choices seem negative, compare degree, frequency, and speaker attitude. The whole message decides between “rarely,” “never,” and “not very.”
  3. For すでに, locate the reference time. すでに出ていた means departure was earlier than another past event, not necessarily earlier than today.

The adverb is a clue, not an automatic answer. Check meaning, attachment, time, and the speaker’s intention together.

Useful expression

その件については、すでに確認してあります。

そのけんについては、すでにかくにんしてあります。

That matter has already been checked.

してあります presents the check as completed and ready to rely on. It is useful when reporting preparations.

Book table · change and time

変化と時間を表す副詞

Separate gradual change, an eventual event, and something about to happen

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

少しずつ, 次第に, and ますます describe development. 少しずつ emphasizes small steps; 次第に describes a gradual progression; ますます means the degree or trend increases further, “more and more.” The change can be favorable or unfavorable.

そのうち and 今に look ahead. They suggest that something will happen eventually or before long, without giving an exact time. 今にも is different: the event looks close to happening right at the moment being described.

Think of it this wayAsk whether you see a trend, expect an event later, or see signs that it could happen at any moment.

Forms and matching expressions

少しずつ + progress or small steps
少しずつ大きくなる = grow a little at a time. ずつ can also distribute amounts, as in 毎日少しずつ読む, “read a little each day”; its uses are broader than change alone.
次第に + a changing predicate
次第に色づく = gradually change color; 次第に小さくなる = gradually become quieter/smaller. A static adjective alone does not state the development.
ますます + increasing degree or trend
ますます増える = increase more and more; ますます便利になる = become increasingly convenient. It intensifies the development; it is not just another word for “sometimes.”
そのうち／今に + an expected event
そのうちできるようになるだろう = will probably become able to do it before long. 今に…時代が来る = a time will come when…. In this forward-looking use the main event normally has a nonpast ending. 今に can sound confident, including “you’ll see.”
今にも + Vます-stem + そうだ
降り出します → 降り出しそうだ; 泣きます → 泣きそうだ. This appearance そうだ means “looks about to…,” not reported information.
Before a noun: そうな
今にも泣きそうな顔 = a face that looks about to cry. Change そうだ to そうな before 顔. 泣いた顔 would describe a face after crying.
Conjugations that express change
大きい → 大きくなる; 便利だ → 便利になる; 話せる → 話せるようになる. ～てきた traces development up to the reference point; ～ていった follows it onward or away.

Do not confuse it with…

そのうち／今に ↔ 今にも
“It will happen before long” differs from “it looks about to happen now.” The extra も matters. 今にも often pairs with a visible imminent event such as rain beginning or someone crying.
今にも…そうだ ↔ hearsay そうだ
泣きそうだ uses the verb stem: looks about to cry. 泣くそうだ follows a plain clause: I hear that someone cries / will cry. Review appearance and hearsay in Lesson 7.
次第に ↔ ～次第
The adverb 次第に means “gradually.” In other constructions, 次第 can mean dependence or an immediate next step. Do not transfer those meanings to this fixed adverb.
A changing ability ↔ the ability alone
話せる states “can speak”; 話せるようになってきた describes developing that ability. Review Group G: ようになる.

JLPT approach

  1. Look for a verb of change, such as 上がる or 増える, rather than selecting a static 高い just because the topic is temperature.
  2. Check both sides of a blank: 今にも predicts an imminent appearance, while a following noun requires そうな.
  3. Do not confuse the time of the story with “now.” 今にも泣きそうな顔をしていた reports an imminent-looking event seen at a past moment.

The adverb is a clue, not an automatic answer. Check meaning, attachment, time, and the speaker’s intention together.

Useful expression

そのうち leaves the timing open. It encourages an expectation, not a guaranteed deadline.

Book table · possibility and judgment

可能性と判断を表す副詞

Match a possibility, a probable judgment, or an impression with its ending

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

These adverbs show how the speaker presents a judgment. もしかしたら／もしかすると raise a possibility; おそらく gives a probable judgment; どうも suggests an impression that something seems to be the case.

The ending works with the adverb: かもしれない leaves a possibility open, だろう or と思う presents a judgment, and ようだ／らしい can express an inference. These words do not assign fixed probability percentages.

Some pairings overlap, especially with のではないか. Learn the book’s typical combinations, then check whether the speaker is suggesting, predicting, or drawing an impression.

Think of it this wayFinish the thought in English first: “Maybe…,” “Probably…,” or “It seems….” Then check the form attached to the Japanese predicate.

Forms and matching expressions

もしかしたら／もしかすると + かもしれない
雪になるかもしれない = it might snow; 道を間違えたかもしれない = we might have taken the wrong road. A plain past or negative clause can also express the possibility.
おそらく + だろう／と思う
できないだろう = probably cannot finish/do it; 皿だと思う = I think it is a plate. と思う quotes a full plain clause: keep だ after a present affirmative noun or な-adjective.
Conjecture with のではないか
うそをついているのではないか = could they be lying? ～のではないだろうか is a more tentative, reflective version. The rhetorical negative question does not simply mean the proposition is false.
Attachment before かもしれない／だろう
Verb and い-adjective plain forms attach directly. Drop present affirmative だ after nouns and な-adjectives: 雨かもしれない／雨だろう, 静かかもしれない. For のではないか, use 雨なのではないか／静かなのではないか.
どうも + ようだ／らしい
間違っているようだ = seems to be wrong; 好きではないらしい = apparently does not like it. どうも supplies a sense of “from what I can tell / somehow it seems.”
ようだ and らしい have different noun connections
Verb plain form + ようだ; い-adjective + ようだ; noun + のようだ; な-adjective + なようだ. For this inference use of らしい, use 雨らしい／静からしい, without の or な.

Do not confuse it with…

もしかしたら ↔ もしも
もしかしたら suggests something may be true and pairs naturally with かもしれない. もしも introduces an “if” condition for a following consequence. Compare hypothetical conditions.
おそらく ↔ ぜひ
おそらく…だろう expresses a probable judgment about what will happen. ぜひ行きたい expresses a strong wish. Wanting something is different from predicting it. Compare wishes and requests.
どうも ↔ どうか
どうも…ようだ offers an impression. どうか…ください makes an earnest request. どうも also occurs in thanks and greetings; those are different uses.
Inference ようだ ↔ resemblance ようだ
鍵をなくしたようだ = it seems I lost the key. 夢のようだ = it is like a dream. Review Group D’s よう patterns and compare まるで.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the ending before deciding on the adverb. だろう, かもしれない, and ください express different kinds of message.
  2. Do not turn “maybe” into a confirmed fact in your translation. Preserve negative clauses too: できないだろう predicts inability, not success.
  3. Recognize のではないか as a possible conjecture. In うそをついているのではないか, the speaker suspects lying; they are not simply denying it.

The adverb is a clue, not an automatic answer. Check meaning, attachment, time, and the speaker’s intention together.

Useful expression

もしかすると、別の場所に置いたかもしれません。

もしかすると、べつのばしょにおいたかもしれません。

Maybe I put it somewhere else.

Useful when considering an explanation for a missing object. The past 置いた is inside the present possibility judgment.

Book table · resemblance and requests

比況と願いを表す副詞

Distinguish “as if,” an eager wish, finding a way, and an earnest plea

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

まるで and ちょうど can introduce resemblance. A doll seems as if it were alive; dots look just like eyes. This is a comparison, not proof that the object really is what it resembles.

ぜひ, なんとかして, and どうか express different attitudes. ぜひ adds enthusiasm or a strong wish; なんとかして says “find some way to…”; どうか adds an earnest “please.” Choose by what the speaker is trying to do, not by treating them all as intensifiers.

Think of it this wayIs the speaker comparing two things, encouraging an action, trying to overcome a difficulty, or pleading with someone?

Forms and matching expressions

まるで／ちょうど + ようだ／みたいだ
まるで生きているようだ = as if alive; ちょうど目みたいだ = just like eyes. The resemblance is figurative or based on appearance.
Attach a noun to the comparison
Noun + のようだ: 王様のようだ. Noun + みたいだ: 王様みたいだ. Do not add の before みたいだ.
Modify a noun or an action
王様のような人 = a person like a king; 王様のように命令する = give orders like a king. ように modifies the behavior, while ような modifies a noun. Verb plain past + ように can mean “as if one had done…”: 勝ったように喜ぶ.
ぜひ + てください／たい／てほしい
ぜひ来てください = please do come; ぜひ見てみたい = I would really like to try seeing it. ぜひ encourages or expresses desire; it does not predict that something will certainly happen.
なんとかして + a desired or intended action
なんとかして成功させたい = I want to find a way to make it succeed. The emphasis is accomplishing the goal despite difficulty, without specifying how yet.
どうか + a request or wish for someone’s action
どうか許してください = please forgive me; どうか教えてほしい = I really want you to tell me. This is an earnest appeal, stronger than a neutral request.
Who performs the desired action?
Vます-stem + たい: I want to do it, 読みたい. Vて + ほしい: I want someone to do it, 読んでほしい. Vないで + ください: ask someone not to do it, 忘れないでください.

Do not confuse it with…

まるで…ように ↔ …らしく
勝ったように喜ぶ can describe celebrating as if they had won, even while the game continues. 勝ったらしく suggests that apparently winning explains the behavior. Distinguish comparison from inferred fact.
ぜひ ↔ 必ず
ぜひ来てください warmly urges a visit. 必ず来てください insists that the person be sure to come. ぜひ is especially common with wishes and invitations; it does not mean certainty in a weather prediction.
なんとかして ↔ なんとか
なんとかして emphasizes finding a way or means. なんとか終わった can report that something was managed somehow. Do not reduce both to a vague “very.”
たい ↔ てほしい; どうか ↔ どうも
Keep track of whose action is wanted. どうか appeals to someone; どうも offers an impression in the previous topic. Review Lesson 9’s wishes.

JLPT approach

  1. If a noun follows the blank, test ような／そうな. If an action follows, test ように. Meaning and attachment must both fit.
  2. A preceding report can explain a wish: hearing that a sunrise is beautiful makes someone want to see it. The earlier そうだ does not make the next sentence another report.
  3. Read encouragement such as がんばろう as context. It can support a determined goal with なんとかして…たい.

The adverb is a clue, not an automatic answer. Check meaning, attachment, time, and the speaker’s intention together.

Book table · condition and concession

条件・譲歩・限定を表す副詞

Read the link: “if,” “even if,” “despite the effort,” “since we have the chance,” or “only”

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

もしも sets up a hypothetical condition. 万一／万が一 focus on an unlikely eventuality, often something to prepare for. They do not state a literal one-in-ten-thousand probability.

どんなに／いくら…ても mean “no matter how much….” たとえ…ても means “even if….” The main conclusion remains true despite the imagined condition.

せっかく highlights effort or a valuable opportunity. のに or ても can show it being wasted; のだから gives a reason to make the most of it. ただ…だけ limits the statement to one thing or activity.

Think of it this wayDecide how the clauses connect before selecting the word: a condition, an unchanged conclusion, a wasted opportunity, a reason to use the opportunity, or a limit.

Forms and matching expressions

もしも + a conditional form
下がらなければ = if it does not go down; 選べるなら = if choosing were possible; なったら = if it turns out that…. もしも is the opening signal; the predicate still needs an appropriate conditional ending.
万一／万が一 + 場合は or another condition
問題がある場合は = if there is a problem. A verb clause attaches directly to 場合; nouns use の場合, and な-adjectives use な場合.
万一／万が一 + ても／でも
お金が足りなくても = even if money is insufficient; 大雨でも = even in heavy rain. This keeps the unlikely-event framing but says the result still holds.
どんなに／いくら + ても／でも
どんなに忙しくても = however busy; いくらがんばっても = no matter how hard one tries. Here いくら concerns degree or amount, not a question asking the price.
たとえ + ても／でも
たとえ水中に落としても = even if it is dropped into water. The condition need not have happened. A bare cause ending such as から does not give this “even if” connection.
Conjugate the concession
Verb: がんばる → がんばっても; negative: 足りない → 足りなくても. い-adjective: 忙しい → 忙しくても. Noun／な-adjective: 大雨でも／不便でも.
せっかく + のに／ても
やったのに = although I did it; 作っても = even if I make it. The surrounding result shows the effort or opportunity failing to pay off.
せっかく + のだから
来たのだから = since we have come all this way / now that we have this chance. The second clause often proposes or expresses a wish to use the opportunity. Do not select disappointment automatically.
ただ + a limited item or clause + だけ
ただ一つだけ = just one; ただ見ていただけ = was just looking. The adverb introduces the limitation, and だけ states its scope. ただ can mean other things elsewhere, including “however” as a connector.

Do not confuse it with…

もしも ↔ もしかしたら
もしも…なったら asks you to imagine a condition and its consequence. もしかしたら…かもしれない suggests an uncertain explanation. Review possible judgments.
ても／でも ↔ のに
Even if A happens, B still holds: たとえ雨でも行く. Although A is a given fact, B is unexpected: 雨なのに行く. Both can contrast ideas, but the hypothetical/factual framing differs. Review たとえ.
せっかく…のに ↔ せっかく…のだから
The first says the effort or opportunity was wasted; the second invites making use of it. Look at whether the ending reports disappointment or proposes something worthwhile.
ただ…だけ ↔ しか…ない
ただ時間だけ emphasizes only time. 時間しかない also limits what is available, but しか requires a negative predicate. Do not insert a negative into a だけ sentence just because English uses “only.” Review Group A’s particles.
The condition marker ↔ the sentence ending
もしも does not decide every later ending by itself. The action/state distinction still matters when choosing ば or たら. Review Group H’s conditional guide.

JLPT approach

  1. Follow the sentence beyond the blank. 写真を撮ろう is a proposal to use the camera, which favors an opportunity-based reason with のだから.
  2. Check attachment: a noun takes でも in the “even if” pattern. 大雪でも and 大雪では are different connections.
  3. Do not choose by the adverb alone. せっかく can lead to disappointment or a positive proposal; 万一 can introduce either “if” or “even if,” depending on the ending.

The adverb is a clue, not an automatic answer. Check meaning, attachment, time, and the speaker’s intention together.

Useful expression

せっかくの機会だから、ぜひ参加したいです。

せっかくのきかいだから、ぜひさんかしたいです。

It’s such a good opportunity that I’d really like to take part.

せっかくの + noun describes a valuable opportunity. ぜひ adds the speaker’s eagerness to use it.
