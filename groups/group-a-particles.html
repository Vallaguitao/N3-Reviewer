Book table · Emphasis

1. こそ

Single out the exact person, thing, time, or reason that matters

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

こそ puts a spotlight on the phrase immediately before it: “this very one,” “precisely,” or “this time for sure.” The speaker distinguishes it from alternatives. 今度こそ suggests renewed determination after other occasions; この資料こそ identifies the exact material sought.

With からこそ, the spotlight falls on a reason: “precisely because…” The parent example presents being a parent as the reason for strict scolding. The particle emphasizes the speaker’s explanation; it does not prove that the conduct is justified.

Think of it this wayFind what comes immediately before こそ. Is the speaker singling out a time, an item, a person, or the reason for a result?

How to attach it

Noun / time expression + こそ
今度こそ／この資料こそ／私こそ. The particle itself does not conjugate. The final verb supplies tense, desire, or intention.
Keep a needed relationship particle
あなたにこそ話したい = you are precisely the person I want to tell. に retains the recipient relationship; こそ adds emphasis.
Reason clause + からこそ
努力したからこそ成功した = succeeded precisely because of the effort. Keep the plain past form when the effort is already completed.
Noun / な-adjective + だからこそ
親だからこそ／大切だからこそ. だ connects the noun or な-adjective to から. An い-adjective connects directly: 難しいからこそ.
A useful response
こちらこそ = it is I who should say that / likewise. In an exchange of thanks or apologies, it returns the emphasis to the speaker’s side.
Book introduction

にいろいろなをにはのようなものがあります。

These particles add meanings such as emphasis, limitation, and the speaker’s attitude to a sentence.

Book examples by use

Each heading identifies the use illustrated below. Open the original wording if you want to compare it with the English explanation.

A. Emphasis: this very one / precisely because

Book wording and English gloss

ほかとはっきりしてする

Single out an item clearly from alternatives and emphasize it.

Book example

こそしたい。

This time, I really want to win.

Clue 今度こそ highlights this attempt against other occasions.

How to read this example

したい expresses the wish to win; こそ adds determination, not certainty of success.

Book example

このこそいたものだ。

This is exactly the material I have been looking for for so long.

Clue この資料 is singled out as the exact material sought.

How to read this example

探していた describes the earlier ongoing search; ものだ identifies what it was.

Book example

だからこそのをきびしくしかるのだ。

It is precisely because they are parents that they scold their children harshly.

Clue 親だから is the reason being emphasized.

How to read this example

自分の子 refers to the parents’ own children. Read this as the speaker’s explanation of the behavior.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

こそを。

Today, for sure, I will finish all of my homework.

Clue 今日こそ focuses determination on today.

How to read this example

終わらせる means bring the homework to completion, while こそ emphasizes the occasion.

Added example

このこそがいただ。

This method is precisely the solution we were looking for.

Clue この方法 is identified as the particular solution sought.

How to read this example

こそ adds “precisely,” not the claim that no other useful method exists.

Do not confuse it with…

こそ ↔ だけ
だけ limits the set (“only this”). こそ identifies or emphasizes the decisive member. “This is exactly what I need” need not mean nothing else is needed.
こそ ↔ も
も includes another member (“this too”). こそ singles one out as especially relevant. Read whether the speaker adds an item or puts it in focus.
からこそ ↔ ordinary reason · Lesson 5
のだから presents a reason as a basis for a conclusion or advice. からこそ especially stresses why this particular reason matters.
Determination ↔ decision · Lesson 11
ことにする states a decision. 今日こそ adds emphasis to today; it does not itself express deciding.

JLPT approach

  1. Read the emphasized phrase before translating. 今度こそ is about the occasion; 親だからこそ is about the reason.
  2. Do not turn emphasis into exclusion. こそ is not a general replacement for だけ.
  3. Use the reply as evidence. In an apology exchange, 私の方こそ shifts responsibility toward the speaker; it is not the addition “me too.”

Use the clues to reason from context, not as guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

こちらこそ、ありがとうございます。

こちらこそ、ありがとうございます。

It is I who should thank you.

A natural response to thanks. The emphasis returns to your side without claiming that only you are grateful.

Book table · Extreme example and suggestion

2. でも

An extreme example, or one possible suggestion

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Use 1: “even.” でも selects a case that might seem least likely to fit, then says that it fits too. If even children know something, the speaker implies it is widely known. In 小さなミスでも見落としてはいけない, the rule reaches even small mistakes.

Use 2: “something like…” In a suggestion, intention, or request, でも offers one possible example without insisting on that particular choice. お茶でも飲みましょうか suggests tea or something similar. It does not dismiss tea as worthless.

The sentence ending is a useful clue: ～ましょうか, ～ようかな, and ～てください can support the suggestion use. They are clues to meaning, not automatic rules that erase context.

Think of it this wayAsk whether the item is an unexpectedly included case (“even X”) or a possible choice (“X or something”).

How to attach it

Noun + でも
子どもでも知っている／お茶でも飲む. The same connection can carry different meanings; recover the meaning from the whole sentence.
Preserve に and と when needed
机の上にでも置く = put it on the desk or somewhere suitable. 初めて会った人とでも仲よくなる = get along even with a person just met. The preceding particle still marks place or companion.
Suggestion with a volitional ending
映画でも見ようかな. 見る → 見よう is the volitional form; かな adds wondering to oneself.
Question words + でも
だれでも／何でも／いつでも can mean anyone / anything / anytime. Compare negative だれも…ない in the も topic.
Distinguish the other でも forms
Sentence-opening でも can mean “but.” Verb て-form + も, as in 雨が降っても, forms “even if / even though.” Do not apply the noun-suggestion explanation to every spelling of でも.

Book examples by use

Each heading identifies the use illustrated below. Open the original wording if you want to compare it with the English explanation.

A. Even an unlikely case fits

Book wording and English gloss

なを、ほかはだと

Give an extreme example to imply that other, less surprising cases fit too.

Book example

そんなことはでもいる。

Even children know this.

Clue Children are the supposedly least knowledgeable group in this comparison.

How to read this example

Even they know it, so the speaker treats the fact as widely known.

Book example

ミスでもはいけない。

Don't overlook even small mistakes.

Clue The prohibition extends to small mistakes too.

How to read this example

見落としてはいけない means must not overlook; でも does not itself create the prohibition.

Book example

はとでもすぐなる。

My sister quickly becomes friends with people even when she meets them for the first time.

Clue と marks the person with whom the sister becomes friendly.

How to read this example

でも includes even someone just met, emphasizing how readily she makes friends.

B. Suggest one possible option

Book wording and English gloss

・・などのでを

Offer one possible example in a suggestion, intention, or request.

Book example

でもか。

Shall we have some tea?

Clue Tea is a possible choice in an invitation.

How to read this example

飲みましょうか supplies “shall we”; でも keeps the choice open.

Book example

でもかな。

Maybe I should watch a movie.

Clue 映画 is a possible activity.

How to read this example

見ようかな means “maybe I will watch”; it is an intention being considered, not a command to another person.

Book example

はのにでもおいてください。

Please leave your luggage on the desk or somewhere suitable.

Clue に marks a possible place for the luggage.

How to read this example

にでも offers the desk as one suitable location. 置いておいて asks that it be placed and left there.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

そのはでも。

Even an elementary-school student can read that kanji.

Clue The elementary-school student is the extreme example of someone able to read the kanji.

How to read this example

読める is potential, meaning can read.

Added example

でもは。

I eat breakfast even on busy days.

Clue Busy days are included despite the circumstances.

How to read this example

This is “even on busy days,” not a suggestion to choose a busy day.

Added example

はでもしませんか。

Would you like to take a walk in a park or somewhere this weekend?

Clue The invitation is to take a walk.

How to read this example

公園でも can be heard as including the park as a possible setting; 公園で散歩でもしませんか more clearly places suggestion でも on the activity.

Added example

でもください。

If you are tired, please take a short break or something.

Clue A break is suggested as a possible response to tiredness.

How to read this example

取ってください is the request; でも makes the activity an example.

Do not confuse it with…

でも ↔ さえ
さえ strongly highlights an unexpected extreme. でも can also mean even, but it additionally makes soft suggestions. お茶でもどうですか is not replaced by お茶さえどうですか.
でも ↔ など / なんか
など・なんか can also offer an example. These overlap in suggestions; judge the whole sentence rather than treating every multiple-choice item as a rigid one-to-one English match.
Noun example ↔ concession · Lesson 6
たとえ…ても says the result stands even under a condition. お茶でも names one possible drink; it does not mean “even if tea.”
Suggestion ↔ advice · Lesson 10
～たらどうですか proposes an action. でも selects an example inside a proposal. They operate at different places in the sentence.

JLPT approach

  1. Look at the final predicate. 知っている can support “even”; 飲みましょうか can suggest a possible activity.
  2. Keep the roles marked by に and と. 人とでも still means with that person; 机の上にでも still gives a possible location.
  3. Do not equate all “even” particles. Check whether the item is an extreme example, an unexpected addition, or merely an option.

Use the clues to reason from context, not as guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

コーヒーでも飲みませんか。

コーヒーでものみませんか。

Would you like to have coffee or something?

Coffee is offered as one option. Another drink can still fit the invitation.

Book table · Strong negation and extreme example

3. も

Include something too; with the right negative pattern, exclude even the minimum

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

も starts from inclusion: “also / too.” When the included item is striking in context, English may use “even.” 難しい漢字も書ける includes difficult kanji among what the child can write. The surprising force comes from the situation as well as the particle.

A minimum quantity + も + negative gives zero: 1円も残っていない means not even one yen remains. A question word with も and a negative similarly excludes every relevant member: だれにも見せない means show it to no one.

Other negative sentences can deny even a basic action: 立つこともできない means cannot even stand. Do not assume every noun + も + negative means zero of everything; the scope depends on the phrase and context.

Think of it this wayFind the item included by も, then read the final positive or negative ending. Is it an addition, a surprising case, or a minimum unit denied?

How to attach it

Noun + も
難しい漢字も書ける. も commonly takes the place of が or を for the focused noun. Do not mechanically add it after every case particle.
Keep relationship particles
山の上にも店がある／だれにも見せない. に remains before も to mark a location or recipient.
One unit + も + negative
1円もない／一つもない／一日も休まない. The minimum unit is denied, giving none / not a single one in that context.
Question word + も + negative
だれも来ない／何もない／どこにも行かない. A necessary particle may appear between the question word and も.
Action as a noun + も
立つこともできない. Dictionary form + こと turns standing into the action being denied even as a possibility.
A larger quantity + も can express “as much as”
一時間も待った can emphasize a surprisingly long wait. 一時間も眠れなかった, with a negative and a threshold reading, means could not sleep even an hour. The ending and context matter.

Book examples by use

Each heading identifies the use illustrated below. Open the original wording if you want to compare it with the English explanation.

A. Deny even the minimum / exclude everyone

Book wording and English gloss

「～ない」とする

With the appropriate negative pattern, deny even the minimum: not one / not anyone.

Book example

１もに。

I went to school without missing a day.

Clue One day is the minimum unit denied.

How to read this example

休まないで means without taking a day off; the main verb 通った reports attending school.

My wallet is empty. I don't have a single yen left.

Clue The empty wallet confirms the zero reading.

How to read this example

1円も + 残っていない means not even a single yen remains.

Book example

この、だれにもくださいよ。

Please don't show this photo to anyone.

Clue だれにも + 見せない excludes every possible recipient.

How to read this example

The requested action is not showing the photo to anyone.

B. Include a striking case too

Book wording and English gloss

なを、ほかもだと

Include an extreme case too; context can give the meaning “even.”

Book example

がこともできない。

My legs hurt and I can't even stand.

Clue Standing is treated as a basic ability that the pain prevents.

How to read this example

立つこと turns the action into a noun phrase before もできない.

Book example

このはもうも。

This child can already write even difficult kanji.

Clue Difficult kanji are included among what the child can write.

How to read this example

もう suggests the ability has already developed; も can be translated as “even” in this context.

Book example

こんなににもがある。

There is a store even on top of such a high mountain.

Clue にも retains the location に while including an unexpectedly high place.

How to read this example

店がある says there is a shop there.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

きのうはも。

I could not sleep even one hour yesterday.

Clue The negative potential 眠れなかった means could not sleep.

How to read this example

On the intended threshold reading, even one hour was not reached.

Added example

にはがもない。

There is not even one egg in the refrigerator.

Clue 一つ is one egg as the minimum count.

How to read this example

一つもない therefore means no eggs at all in the refrigerator.

Added example

もこのアプリをに。

Beginners can also use this app easily.

Clue も includes beginners too.

How to read this example

The sentence can suggest that beginner accessibility is striking, but “even” is not forced by も alone.

Added example

からはも。

You can also see the sea from the mountain.

Clue も adds the sea to the visible sights.

How to read this example

見える means is visible / can be seen; whether the sea is surprising depends on the situation.

Do not confuse it with…

も…ない ↔ しか…ない
One-point comparison: 一人もいない = not even one person; 一人しかいない = only one person. Both end negatively, but the quantities differ.
も ↔ さえ
さえ explicitly highlights an unexpected extreme. も may simply add an item; translate it as “even” only when the context supports that emphasis.
も ↔ まで
まで highlights the surprising reach of a set or action. 大人も means adults too; 大人まで emphasizes that it extends even to adults.
No one ↔ not necessarily everyone · Lesson 8
～とは限らない limits certainty or a generalization. だれも来ない denies anyone’s coming in the relevant situation; it is a stronger claim.

JLPT approach

  1. Read past the particle. The negative ending is essential to the zero-quantity pattern.
  2. Use the minimum-unit test. 一人も…ない denies even one; 一人しか…ない leaves one as the exception.
  3. Avoid automatic “even.” 初心者も can simply mean beginners too. Surprise needs support from the context.

Use the clues to reason from context, not as guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

一つも忘れないように、確認します。

ひとつもわすれないように、かくにんします。

I will check so that I do not forget a single item.

一つも + negative denies even the smallest unit. The final 確認します is positive; the relevant negative is 忘れない.

Book table · Unexpected extreme and minimum condition

4. さえ

Even this surprising case; or this one condition is enough

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Use 1: “even.” さえ singles out an extreme case to emphasize surprise. If even a scholar cannot solve a problem, the speaker presents it as exceptionally difficult. If not even eggs are in the refrigerator, eggs are treated as a basic item one would expect.

Use 2: “as long as this one condition is met.” With a conditional ending, さえ can present something as sufficient for the result. 雨さえ降らなければ focuses on avoiding rain as the condition for fireworks.

“Sufficient” means that the speaker presents it as enough in the situation. It does not mean the condition is objectively the only requirement in the real world. 自分さえよければ… questions a selfish attitude: treating one’s own well-being as all that matters.

Think of it this wayCheck for a conditional ending such as あれば, なければ, or すれば. Then decide whether the sentence highlights surprise or focuses on a sufficient condition.

How to attach it

Noun + さえ
卵さえない／誕生日さえ忘れた. The noun is the extreme item; positive and negative predicates are both possible.
Particle + さえ
北極にさえ行った／学者でさえ解けない. に retains the destination; でさえ gives the “even a scholar” form.
Noun + さえ + conditional predicate
パスポートさえあれば／雨さえ降らなければ. Keep the negative condition where intended: no rain, not rain occurring.
ます-stem + さえ + すれば
聞きます → 聞きさえすれば／練習します → 練習しさえすれば. Remove ます and add さえすれば, not dictionary-form 聞くさえすれば.
Read the whole condition
自分さえよければ uses いい → よければ. さえ does not itself conjugate; the adjective or verb after it supplies the conditional form.

A. A surprising extreme

Book wording and English gloss

There aren't even eggs in the refrigerator.

さえ + 入っていない emphasizes their surprising absence; it does not prove that absolutely nothing else is inside.

How to read this example

Clue A scholar is expected to solve difficult questions, so failure even at that level marks a very hard problem.

How to read this example

B. One condition presented as enough

Book wording and English gloss

を　→１ ６-③

Present one condition as enough for a result; as long as that condition holds.

Is it enough that you yourself are all right?

Clue よければ is the conditional of いい.

How to read this example

The question criticizes treating one’s own well-being as sufficient, without considering other people.

Book example

さえば、ができる。

As long as it doesn't rain, we can have fireworks.

Clue The important condition is negative: rain must not fall.

How to read this example

The speaker presents that as enough for the fireworks to go ahead in this situation.

Book example

As long as you ask someone for directions, you won't get lost.

How to read this example

聞きさえすれば presents asking directions as the sufficient step, rather than guaranteeing that people can never get lost.

Added examples

Apply the same distinction to these additional situations.

Added example

はのさえいた。

He had forgotten even his own birthday.

Clue One’s own birthday is something usually expected to be remembered.

How to read this example

さえ makes forgetting it a striking example.

Added example

I was so busy that I could not even eat lunch.

Clue Lunch is treated as a basic activity, yet the speaker could not manage it.

How to read this example

Added example

As long as you have a passport, you can complete the procedure.

Clue The speaker presents having a passport as enough for this procedure in context.

How to read this example

This is a grammar example, not a statement of every real procedure’s requirements.

Added example

As long as you practice for ten minutes every day, you will improve.

Clue 練習し is the stem before さえすれば.

How to read this example

The intended translation takes 十分 as ten minutes (じゅっぷん); the same spelling read じゅうぶん means enough. The grammar presents the daily practice as sufficient for improvement.

Do not confuse it with…

さえ ↔ でも
でも can give an extreme example too. Unlike さえ, it can also casually suggest tea or another option. Meaning and sentence ending decide.
さえ ↔ まで
まで emphasizes an unexpected extension, such as sending cards even to strangers. さえ emphasizes the surprising extreme itself. They overlap in some contexts, not all.
Sufficient condition · Lesson 6
～さえ…ば is the same minimum-condition family studied in Lesson 6. Keep track of which single condition the speaker focuses on.
Sufficiency ↔ deliberate effort · Lesson 11
練習するようにしている reports maintained effort. 練習しさえすれば presents practicing as enough for a result. One is a habit report; the other is conditional.

JLPT approach

  1. Check polarity inside the condition. 雨さえ降らなければ requires rain not to fall.
  2. Use the full construction. A blank before すれば may need a ます-stem + さえ, not a dictionary form.
  3. Read the speaker’s evaluation. A scholar is a surprising failure case; an egg can be a surprisingly absent basic item. The extreme depends on the sentence.

Use the clues to reason from context, not as guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

時間さえあれば、もう一度確認できます。

じかんさえあれば、もういちどかくにんできます。

As long as I have time, I can check it once more.

The speaker presents time as the remaining condition in this situation.

Book table · Unexpected extent

5. まで

The action reaches further than expected

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

まで here means “even,” with a sense of unexpected extension. The affected people, places, things, or actions reach beyond the expected range. When the baby cries, even the speaker feels like crying. New Year’s cards go even to people the speaker has never met.

借金までして adds a more extreme step: going so far as to borrow money. The sentence then says that step is unnecessary. まで itself does not mean the action is good, bad, or completed; read the rest of the sentence.

Think of it this wayAsk “What ordinary range is being extended, and what surprising extra person, place, or step is included?”

How to attach it

Noun + まで
私まで／大人まで／休みの日まで. The noun is the unexpected extension, not necessarily a physical endpoint.
Keep a needed particle
会ったことがない人にまで年賀状を出す. に marks the recipients; まで extends the recipient group even that far.
Action noun + まで + する
借金までして. Borrowing money is the extra step taken or considered. して is the て-form of する.
Action て-form + まで
借金をしてまで買う also expresses going so far as borrowing to buy. Notice the changed connection; do not mix the word order with 借金までして.
Separate the familiar endpoint use
5時まで = until five; 駅まで = as far as the station. In this topic, まで often describes an unexpected extension rather than a literal endpoint.

Book examples by use

Each heading identifies the use illustrated below. Open the original wording if you want to compare it with the English explanation.

A. An unexpected extra person, recipient, or step

Book wording and English gloss

ののをする

Emphasize that the range extends further than expected.

Book example

How to read this example

Clue Borrowing is the extra step that goes further than necessary.

How to read this example

Book example

Clue に marks card recipients.

How to read this example

Added examples

Added example

Clue だけでなく establishes children as the first group; まで then adds adults as a surprising further group.

How to read this example

Do not confuse it with…

まで ↔ ぐらい
ぐらい can treat something as a small or basic amount: greetings at least. まで treats it as reaching further than expected: even difficult tasks. This contrast applies to those uses, not every use of either particle.
まで ↔ も
も simply includes another member unless context adds emphasis. まで makes the extension itself prominent.
まで ↔ さえ
さえ spotlights an extreme case. まで often suggests adding a further case to an already understood range. Read the implied comparison, not only the English word “even.”
Until ↔ by a deadline
5時まで待つ means wait until five. 5時までに出す means submit by five. Deadline までに is different from the unexpected-extension use here.

JLPT approach

  1. Look for an added step or member. 自分のだけでなく followed by someone else’s material supports an unexpected extension.
  2. Do not interpret every まで as a time limit. 人にまで is about recipients in the card example.
  3. Keep the final judgment separate. 借金までして…なくてもいい says there is no need to go that far. It does not report that the borrowing already happened.

Use the clues to reason from context, not as guaranteed answer-key shortcuts.

Useful expression

休みの日まで仕事のことを考えてしまいます。

やすみのひまでしごとのことをかんがえてしまいます。

I end up thinking about work even on my days off.

The thought extends beyond the expected working days. てしまう adds the sense that this happens despite the speaker’s wishes.

6. ぐらい・くらい

A small/basic amount, or a benchmark for degree

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Use 1: “at least / something as basic as / just a little.” ぐらい・くらい can present the preceding item as modest or minimal. Greetings are treated as a basic skill; Sunday is treated as a reasonable minimum day of rest.

Use 2: “about the size/level of / enough that…” An egg can be a size benchmark, and being affordable can be a price benchmark. The item is a measuring point, not necessarily a tiny amount.

ぐらい and くらい are usually interchangeable in these uses. The particle does not always mean “at least”: quantity expressions such as 十分くらい commonly mean about ten minutes.

Think of it this wayDecide whether the speaker judges something as modest/basic or uses it to measure size, amount, time, or degree.

How to attach it

Noun + ぐらい / くらい

Quantity + くらい
十分くらい = about ten minutes. With なら, 十分くらいなら means if it is around ten minutes / that much, I can manage it.
Noun + ぐらいの + noun
卵ぐらいの大きさ／猫ぐらいの大きさ. の connects the benchmark to the following noun.
Plain clause + くらい
私にも買えるくらいの値段／小学生でも解けるくらい簡単だ. Potential 買える / 解ける gives a measure of affordability or ease.
ぐらいは / ぐらいなら
名前ぐらいは marks a minimum expectation; あいさつぐらいなら limits the claim to a modest task. A request with ぐらい can sound impatient or belittling depending on context.

Book table · Suggestion and dismissive tone

7. など・なんか

Offer an example, dismiss something, or speak modestly about yourself

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

In a suggestion, など / なんか offers an example among alternatives. Fruit, Hawaii, or a particular outfit is one possibility, not the only choice. なんか is more conversational; など fits more neutral or formal wording.

In dismissive or modest speech, the item is treated as unimportant or insufficient. お礼など要りません waves away thanks; そんなうわさなんか rejects the rumor. 私なんか lowers the speaker’s own standing in a modest or self-critical way.

The particle itself is not a negative ending. A suggestion can be positive, and the dismissive force comes from the situation and predicate. Avoid using なんか toward another person if you do not intend to sound dismissive.

Think of it this wayAsk whether the item is a friendly option, something being brushed aside, or the speaker’s modest description of themself.

How to attach it

Noun + など / なんか
果物など／ハワイなんか／私なんか. No verb conjugation is needed on the particle itself.
Suggestions with different levels of formality
この服などいかがですか is a polite suggestion. ハワイなんかどう is a casual version with どう and an omitted polite ending.
Action noun + など + する
ダイエットなどしたくない. する → したい → したくない means do not want to do it. など dismisses dieting; したくない supplies the negative desire.
Keep a relationship particle where needed
あの人となんか会いたくない = do not want to meet someone like that. と marks the meeting partner; なんか adds dismissal.
Do not confuse two spellings of なんか
私なんか uses the example/dismissal particle. なんか食べたい can be conversational 何か食べたい, “I want to eat something.” The position and meaning differ.

Book table · Limitation and range

8. だけ

Limit the set, or reach the available/wanted extent

MeaningFormsExamplesComparisonsJLPT tips

Meaning in plain English

Use 1: “only / just.” だけ limits the relevant person, thing, or action. 母にだけ selects the mother as the only recipient; 聞くだけ limits the action to listening. Read exactly what is restricted.

Use 2: “as much as / all that…” 好きなだけ allows the full amount desired. やれるだけのこと refers to everything within one’s ability. The amount has a boundary, but it is not necessarily small.

だけ can occur with affirmative or negative predicates. In へびだけはいやだ, snakes are singled out as the disliked exception. That is different from saying the speaker dislikes only animals generally.

Think of it this wayFind the boundary: only which person/action, or as much as which desire/ability permits?

How to attach it

Noun + だけ
へびだけ／必要な人だけ. The noun phrase identifies the limited set. は can add contrast: へびだけは.
Keep the recipient relationship
母にだけ／家族にだけ. に marks whom the information is told to; だけ limits that recipient set.
Plain verb + だけ
聞くだけ／買っただけ. Dictionary form or past form changes the action’s timing; だけ itself stays the same.
な-adjective / desire + だけ
好きなだけ／言いたいだけ. 好き needs な before だけ in this construction; the たい-form connects directly.
Potential verb + だけの + noun
やれるだけのこと／できるだけの仕事. The potential sets the available capacity, and の links that extent to the following noun.
できるだけ as an adverbial expression
できるだけ早く = as early as possible. Compare 今日できるだけの仕事, which describes the amount of work possible today.

5　母は30年も前の細かいこと（　）よく覚えている。
